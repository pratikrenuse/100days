# C.L.E.A.R. UI Principles (quick reference)

Apply all five pillars to any screen, then score each 0–5 (25 total). Fix the lowest first. It's a loop: after a change, re-check earlier pillars.

**C — Copywriting.** Make the benefit obvious in 1–2 seconds (WIIFM). Cut any sentence that breaks nothing if removed. Sound like a person, not a brochure. Specific, active verbs; reassure near friction.

**L — Layout.** Use Gestalt grouping: same role = same look (Similarity); tight spacing within groups, loose between them (Proximity); remove meaningless styling (Simplicity); commit to a 4px grid and align edges (Alignment); group with cards/sections (Common Region); one clear top-left-to-bottom scan path (Continuity).

**E — Emphasis.** Tune six dials — Size, Color, Space, Placement, Visualization, Motion. Lead with 1–2; let the rest support. Squint Test: the key element must dominate even when blurred. If everything shouts, nothing stands out.

**A — Accessibility.** Design for permanent, temporary, and situational limits. Every screen: visible without searching, operable without precision, actionable without guessing. Big tap targets (Fitt's Law), ≥4.5:1 text contrast, never color alone, prevent errors (disable invalid, allow undo, confirm irreversible).

**R — Reward.** Deliver an emotional outcome matched to the moment: Control (safety, certainty, undo), Competence (progress, "done" states, streaks), or Recognition (badges, roles, reactions). The screen must answer at least one of "Am I safe? Did I do well? Am I recognized?" Keep rewards proportional.

**Copy guardrails:** no hype, no emojis, no em dashes — direct and clear.
