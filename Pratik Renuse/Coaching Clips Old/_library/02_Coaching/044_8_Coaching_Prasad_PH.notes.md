# 044_8_Coaching_Prasad_PH · notes
Who: Pratik in a peer call with Prasad, a head of marketing at a developer tools startup who led its Product Hunt launch ("PH" = Product Hunt)
Summary: Pratik reaches out after seeing a strong Product Hunt launch and Prasad walks him through the whole plan: segmented manual outreach, investor and team networks, internal mobilisation and paid ads, which won product of the day and week and lifted signups several times over. Pratik reflects on why the outreach copy worked, on planning versus execution, and shares his view on European B2B marketing budgets and his fintech work in Spain.
Topics: Product Hunt launch playbook, cold outreach on Twitter and LinkedIn, non salesy outreach copy, internal mobilisation, community and WhatsApp groups, channel saturation, launch results, B2B marketing budgets in Europe, fintech and UPI, advising vs consulting
Publishable: partly (the launch playbook and numbers belong to Prasad's company; use only Pratik's own observations, and anonymise Prasad)

## Pratik's best moments (clip and site candidates)
- [08:37 to 09:40] Why the outreach message worked: it did not push or ask directly, it felt elegant rather than salesy, and the link made it easy to act. Pushing too hard too early makes people feel sold to.
- [17:09 to 17:43] Launch success needs both a plan and the hustle to execute it; planning is easy, contacting all those people is the real work.
- [17:43 to 18:28] Product Hunt is getting saturated: everyone now runs the same playbook, so ranking, especially for product of the week, is much harder than it used to be.
- [20:20 to 21:30] Turn a successful launch into content or a playbook, and use the first big win to set how the company sees you as a new marketing head.
- [22:55 to 25:10] The fintech opportunity in Spain: banking is still offline and slow, and India's UPI is the case study for what instant QR payments could do in Europe.
- [25:17 to 26:08] Pratik's read of B2B marketing budgets: cuts hit startups that were burning money, but early and mid stage companies have not reduced marketing hiring or budgets much.
- [27:26 to 27:50] Advising founders (one or two hour calls weekly through accelerators) fits around other work far better than consulting, which takes eight to ten hours a week.
