## Pratik's best moments (clip and site candidates)
- [25:31 to 26:46] When starting out, Pratik did not charge early career coaching clients. Money is just an exchange of value, and what he needed first was testimonials, then referrals: "if this helped you, don't pay me, just pass it on."
- [26:46 to 28:27] Going from zero to one: one off fees are small, but building the first community creates lifetime value, because the same client comes back for different problems over a lifetime (the Tony Robbins and Marc Benioff example).
- [28:43 to 30:16] Build the base and regular clients first, then start charging; go for lifetime value rather than a single transaction.
- [37:55 to 39:16] Pay what you want pricing: people default to the lowest option, so if nobody pays it is a human bias, not proof that your session lacked value.
- [40:07 to 40:33] Group coaching and one to one coaching are different businesses. Try both and see which fits your goals.
- [45:04 to 45:40] In a crowded coaching niche, decide how you are different and build credibility and proof before people will trust you.
- [49:52 to 50:45] Do not pick a niche too early. Run sessions, see where the sweet spot is; for Pratik's career coaching it turned out to be master's and MBA graduates.
