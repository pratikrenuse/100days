# 035_214_Session_5_22_April · notes
Who: Pratik teaching an in person growth metrics class to a TETR College of Business cohort; student teams behind Not Cultured (clothing), Sinful Cookies (no added sugar cookies), a pickleball equipment brand and others join the discussion.
Summary: Pratik recaps funnel metrics, then teaches the North Star metric in practice: three criteria, common pitfalls (vanity versus actionable, flow versus stock, leading versus lagging), and the hockey stick curve. A Pranamat case shows how a stalled company aligned all teams around NPS and a promoter referral engine. He then workshops North Star metrics live for three student startups, with side advice on online versus offline pricing, website hero copy, session recordings and testimonials.
Topics: North Star metric, vanity versus actionable metrics, flow versus stock metrics, leading versus lagging metrics, weekly growth rate, hockey stick growth, NPS, referral programs, Pranamat case, long term customer engagement, community, dynamic pricing, website hero section, Microsoft Clarity, testimonials, repeat purchase rate
Publishable: partly (strong teaching segments; the opening minutes are empty room audio and the student startup critiques should stay private)

## Pratik's best moments (clip and site candidates)
- [10:25 to 13:31] What a North Star metric does: captures customer value, aligns every team, and tracks growth
- [16:43 to 18:15] Two metric pitfalls: vanity metrics like follower counts, and stock metrics like lifetime revenue that hide what is happening now
- [19:03 to 22:07] Leading versus lagging metrics: why weekly growth rate predicts the hockey stick and last quarter's revenue does not
- [28:40 to 31:45] Pranamat case: growth stalled because every team had its own success metric and no one knew what made customers recommend the product
- [31:45 to 38:53] Short term versus long term feedback loops: re-engage customers a year later, with The Whole Truth's content engine and WhatsApp communities as the example
- [41:20 to 46:20] Pranamat's fix: NPS as North Star plus an automated promoter referral program, which turned 32% of promoters into advocates
- [01:03:08 to 01:05:31] Online pricing is flexible: how quick commerce apps quietly raise delivery fees for their heaviest users, and why that is a values decision for founders
- [01:08:45 to 01:11:30] Website advice: install Microsoft Clarity or Hotjar first, watch what visitors skip, and put the value proposition in the hero section
- [01:22:12 to 01:25:23] For frequently bought products with low barriers to entry, loyalty is the moat, so repeat purchase rate becomes the North Star
