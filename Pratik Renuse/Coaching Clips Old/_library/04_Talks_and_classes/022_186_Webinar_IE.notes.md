# 022_186_Webinar_IE · notes
Who: Pratik giving a webinar to IE Business School students, hosted by Shubham (IE student), with questions from students including Amrita; a Startup Lab professor attended
Summary: A 50 minute talk on going from idea to startup in four parts: checking the idea fits your values and strengths, validating with an MVP, reaching product market fit while networking with investors, then fundraising, fair equity splits, processes, hiring and culture. Pratik uses his own story of scaling a physical toy startup from zero to seven figures in about 10 and a half months, a cold LinkedIn message that led to coffee with a large Amsterdam VC, and consulting cases about equity fights. Q&A covers whether values change over time, remote founding teams, investor location and MVPs for physical products. The first 11 minutes are pre-call setup chat.
Topics: startup ideas and personal values, strengths, MVP, product market fit, networking with investors, LinkedIn outreach, copywriting, fundraising options, equity split, slicing the pie, processes, hiring, company culture, remote founding teams
Publishable: yes (a public talk; skip the first 11 minutes of setup)

## Pratik's best moments (clip and site candidates)
- [17:11 to 18:35] "Which mountain should you climb?": success without fulfillment is the ultimate failure, so the most crucial stage is checking the startup idea fits your values, not the MVP or fundraising
- [20:46 to 21:42] Scaling a startup from zero to seven figures in 10 and a half months, then realising it was not aligned with his values and leaving: "It's like seeing your baby grow. You have to leave it"
- [31:09 to 32:30] "Raise money when you don't need it, because when you need it, it's too late", and networking as authentic relationship building, not trading what each side brings to the table
- [35:00 to 36:30] The short, honest LinkedIn message that got him coffee with one of Amsterdam's biggest VCs (60 plus seed investments), and why it worked
- [47:25 to 48:55] The wrong way to split founder equity (50 50 by headcount or googling what a CMO gets) and the resentment it builds a year later
- [49:48 to 50:37] Consulting story: an investor ended up with close to 70 percent because the co-founders did not understand equity, and it pushed founders out of their own startup
- [53:02 to 53:50] Build processes early: not doing it cost them a lot of effort six to eight months later
- [57:28 to 58:40] Free lunches and ping pong tables do not motivate a team; the non tangibles (vision, mission, values) do, with the SpaceX example
