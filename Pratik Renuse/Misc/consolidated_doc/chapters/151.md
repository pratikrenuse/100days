# Disagree and commit 

Disagree and commit solves a common execution problem: decisions get made, but the team doesn’t push them forward with full urgency and effort when they disagree with the outcome

Instead they:.

  - Keep arguing after the decision is made
  - Don’t prioritize the work
  - Don’t take ownership of execution

This happens when people disagree with a decision and don’t commit to it.

Disagree and commit fixes this by setting a clear standard: once a decision is made, there’s no partial buy-in.

There are only three options:

  - **Agree and commit**. You support the decision and fully own execution.
  - **Disagree and commit**. You disagree with the decision, but still fully own execution.
  - **Disagree and quit**. You disagree so strongly that you would leave if the decision is executed.

What is not an option:

  - Disagree and not commit. No half-support, no dragging your feet, no undermining the decision.

If you’re not at the level of “I would leave over this,” then your job is to disagree in the room and fully commit once the decision is made.

## How to implement it

Start at the top, on the founder or executive team. Then spread it down the organization. 

### **1) Explain the standard**

At every level, define it clearly:

  - “If you disagree, state your argument in the decision meeting.”
  - “After the decision-maker decides, you share which of these options you will do:
      
      - Agree and commit
      - Disagree and commit
      - Disagree and quit  You need to commit to the decision
  - “You’ll respect the authority of the decision-maker. Commitment means you agree to full responsibility for your actions coming out of the decision.”

Also define what is not acceptable:

  - Raising the disagreement after the meeting
  - Telling others you disagreed
  - Showing hesitation or doubt in execution

### **2) Set the preconditions** 

  - There is a single, named decision maker
  - The decision maker asks for and listens to input (see [Decision-making: separate decision from implementation](https://docs.google.com/document/d/18VkFRbhDTeU0nBEiot8CXiK37UUEZp0D-sT4-IN02Hs))
  - The decision maker clearly states the decision

### **3) Surface disagreement in the meeting**

Each participant must:

  - State their view clearly and directly
  - Say their disagreement in the meeting, not after
  - Stop once they have shared their input and allow the decision owner to decide

### **4) State the decision**

The decision owner must:

  - Clearly say what will be done and end the discussion
      
      - “We’re doing X.”
  - Move directly into execution
      
      - Define next steps
      - Assign owners
      - Confirm timelines

### **5) Commit after the decision**

From that moment, everyone must:

  - Present the decision as their own
  - Drive execution
  - Ensure their team follows it
  - Not express disagreement anywhere

Commitment is behavior.

### **6) Enforce the standard**

Leaders must actively enforce this when it is not followed:

  - If someone raises a concern after the meeting: “Was this raised in the decision meeting?”
  - If someone signals doubt or does not follow through: “The decision is made. I need you to support and execute it.”

Do not allow side conversations, re-discussion, or partial execution to continue. Address it immediately.

### **7) Apply disagree and commit at every level**

Once leaders are consistently doing this themselves, they should:

  - Explain the standard to their direct reports
  - Expect their direct reports to run decisions the same way in their own teams
  - Make sure any reopened decision or lack of follow-through is addressed
