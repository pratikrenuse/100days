Before You Hire

A decision-making framework for increasing output without increasing headcount

[AI has expanded what a company can produce without adding people](https://leanaileaderboard.com/). The CEO's job is no longer simply allocating headcount; It is designing leverage across systems, AI, and people. [Hiring should be the last step](https://docs.google.com/document/d/1YEH0EWZOXu0nptlOCXM9etBiLVO2qDWuS_JIwK7go9w/edit?tab=t.0), not the first.

When a team needs to move faster, work through these levels in order. Stop when you have the speed you need.

Most companies jump directly to step six. The fastest companies work through the earlier steps first. Every premature hire at a scaling company costs months of ramp time, management overhead, and cultural risk.

# Step 1: Identify the Real Bottleneck

When teams feel slow, leaders often assume the problem is execution capacity. Often it is not.

Common constraints include:

Unclear [priorities](https://docs.google.com/document/d/1GoiH4pwsph5rwSM_wMMiMJkX6-qFVwO0SVYkodNZhwg/edit?tab=t.0)

Slow [decision-making](https://docs.google.com/document/d/1hPdWQjYfdK0SB2i3ViZ9XsMPjTYEi7Do1WI22ofmEdo/edit?tab=t.0)

Unclear [ownership](https://docs.google.com/document/d/1v72Mozz6Zr-3Knzcd9yNcs1E8-QKk5NkTVmR6pIQj98/edit?tab=t.0)

Lack of [accountability](https://docs.google.com/presentation/d/1foUj9i_K3X-0c5WTQrggSyH93-FflNxzdlrgAUvARSQ/edit?slide=id.g200d4b09bbd_2_94#slide=id.g200d4b09bbd_2_94)

If the constraint is not execution, adding people or AI will only increase complexity. First identify the real bottleneck.

# Step 2: Remove Unnecessary Work

Before asking how to do work faster, ask: should this work exist at all?

Many recurring tasks exist only because they historically required human effort. Examples: manual reports, recurring research, internal status updates, data entry, information retrieval. AI can often generate these automatically.

The highest form of leverage is not doing work faster. It is removing the work entirely.

Find Work to Automate

Repeated Work

Ask: What's being done the same way each week?

Example: A manager rebuilds the same weekly report from scratch every Friday.

"Only Takes a Few Minutes"

Ask: What are people doing that "only takes a few minutes" but happens often?

Example: A manager checks multiple tools every day to see what needs attention.

Shouldn't Exist

Ask: If we were starting from scratch, would we keep doing this?

Example: The team produces a weekly report that no one uses.   

# Step 3: Redesign Workflows Around AI

This step redesigns your process. AI can now do real work: write code, draft documents, analyze data, and generate reports. The question is no longer whether it can. It is how you structure your workflows around it.

## Example: engineering pipeline

Traditional: engineer writes code → tests → submits pull request → engineer reviews.

AI-native: engineer writes spec → AI generates code and tests → second AI agent reviews for style, coverage, and bugs → low-risk changes merge automatically → high-risk changes escalate to a human → critical changes escalate to senior engineers.

Previously, most of the value came from doing the work itself, so teams focused on execution. That made sense. Now, execution is largely handled by AI. The leverage shifts to defining the work upfront and reviewing it at the end, not doing it in the middle.

## The review hierarchy

AI dramatically increases the volume of work that can be generated. The new constraint is evaluation.

Level 1: AI generation. AI produces the output.

Level 2: AI validation. A second agent checks for known failure modes. If the output passes predefined thresholds, it ships automatically.

Level 3: Human review. Higher-risk work escalates to a human.

Level 4: Senior oversight. Critical decisions escalate to senior leaders.

This pattern applies beyond engineering. Any function where AI generates output at scale needs a designed validation layer.

Companies that design [strong review hierarchies](https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/p/the-non-technical-pms-guide-to-building-with-cursor) can safely scale AI output. Companies that rely only on human review will bottleneck.

# Step 4: Deploy AI Agents

The next layer of leverage is autonomous AI workers. These agents operate continuously and perform defined responsibilities:

Pull request review agents

Competitor monitoring agents

Analytics summary agents

Lead qualification agents

Support triage agents

A small team [supported by AI agents](https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/p/this-week-on-how-i-ai-pms-who-use) can perform the work of a much larger organization. Every agent needs a directly responsible individual (DRI) human who owns the agent's output, monitors its failure modes, and is accountable when something goes wrong.

# Step 5: Redesign Roles Around AI

Once processes and agents are in place, look at each role and ask: what is this person actually doing every day, and how much of it can AI now handle?

Engineers should be designing systems and reviewing AI-generated output, not writing every line. Support leads should be managing AI agents that handle tickets, not answering tickets themselves. If someone's day-to-day hasn't changed, you haven't finished the earlier steps.

How to lead AI adoption

Don't tell your teams to "use more AI." That creates vague pressure and scattered efforts. Do make it an explicit objective in your OKRs.

Define a company-level goal to redesign how work gets done with AI

Define key results for each department. Each leader should own specific outcomes, like reducing cycle time, increasing output per person, or shifting a percentage of workload to automation.

Use an [OKR tracker](https://docs.google.com/spreadsheets/d/1_TgYK7fHvUXWrcIhGgUeeq8OoFi0LQ6usQm3r7owZA0/edit) to hold leaders accountable

Obstacles

The biggest obstacle here is usually [fear](https://docs.google.com/document/d/1sDWHUm6zwEGiPBQH2Ig6DDvVL-IEQSlfNeQnR2mhjmE/edit?tab=t.0). When you redesign roles, people hear "we are looking for ways to replace you with AI." Name this directly. Make it clear that the goal is to make every person on the team dramatically more capable.

Another adoption obstacle we hear directly from our clients: "I'm a creator. I want to code." Acknowledge it and be clear about the expectation: the role is changing. Support people in finding new forms of creativity in this model. Not everyone will make that shift- some will leave, and others you'll need to let go. This is a normal and healthy part of change.

# Step 6: Hire AI-Proficient Talent

Only after working through the earlier levels should a company hire. When hiring, prioritize candidates who design workflows using AI, automate repetitive tasks, experiment rapidly with new systems, and manage and validate AI output.

The goal is to hire operators who naturally multiply themselves with AI.

## When hiring is the right answer

If your team is fully AI-leveraged and the constraint is domain expertise AI cannot replicate (deep technical knowledge, regulatory experience, relationship capacity), then hire. If the work requires judgment in a high-stakes domain where AI validation is insufficient, hire. If the work requires trust, presence, or persuasion, hire.
