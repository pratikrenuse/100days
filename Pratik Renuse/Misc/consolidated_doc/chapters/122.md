**Fear: Tactics to neutralize fear-based decision-making**

**CEOs**: When a decision, conversation, or standard keeps slipping, it’s usually because a leader on your team is afraid of a specific outcome if they act.

Your job is to remove fear as an execution bottleneck so the business can move. Here’s a practical way to do that.

### **1. Name The fear**

Fear stays powerful when it’s vague. Leaders stuck in fear will often explain the delay in reasonable-sounding language:

  - “The timing isn’t quite right.”  
      
  - “I want to be careful about how I handle this.”  
      
  - “I’m not sure this is the moment.”  
      

Don’t debate the logic. Push for specificity. Ask:

  - “What are you worried will actually happen if you do this?”

You’ll usually get something more concrete:

  - “They’ll get really upset.”  
      
  - “If we lose them, Q1 is at risk.”  
      
  - “I don’t know what we’ll see if we look at the numbers.

Now you know what they’re worried about.

  

### **2. Be specific, drive to action**

With the concern surfaced, narrow in on the exact moment that’s stopping them from acting. Keep asking:

  - “And if that happens, what are you worried about?”

Example:

Leader: “They’ll get really upset.”

You: “And if they get upset?”

Leader: “They might cry.”

You: “And if they cry?”

Leader: “I’ll freeze and won’t know what to say.”

That’s the moment. This was never about whether to have the conversation. It was about not knowing what to do if they cry. Once that’s clear, the problem becomes much smaller and solvable.

### **3. Mirror it back to them**

Name it directly:

  

“So this isn’t about giving feedback.

It’s about not knowing what to do if they cry.”

  

This removes the fog. What feels overwhelming becomes concrete and workable.

Most leaders feel immediate relief here - not because the situation is easy, but because it’s now clear.

### **4. Define the action**

Decide what kind of fear it is, and help them prepare for that moment. For example:

  - **Fear of navigating a hard moment** (e.g. ‘I don’t know what to say if they cry’)

**Action:** 

Write down:

  - The specific scenario you’re worried about
  - **The sentence or two you will say if it happens**
  - Rehearse once.
  - Have the words visible during the conversation if needed.

See also: [Difficult Conversations: how to have them](https://docs.google.com/document/d/11pGR1sc5IlFXxXE9ve_RrvE1MVWpoYR7CxSyPL6wHQk/edit?tab=t.0)

  

  - **Fear of losing control of the conversation** (e.g. being pulled into a debate)

**Action:**

Before the conversation, define the decision boundary. Write down:

  - What is decided and will not change
  - What, if anything, is open for discussion
  - Prepare one sentence you will use to restate the boundary if the conversation is drifting.

  
  

  - **Fear of downstream consequences.****  
    ****Action**: 

Before the conversation, make the tradeoff explicit. Write down:

  - The most likely downside of acting now
  - The cost of delaying or avoiding the action

See also:[Decision-making: separate decision from implementation](https://docs.google.com/document/d/18VkFRbhDTeU0nBEiot8CXiK37UUEZp0D-sT4-IN02Hs/edit)  
  

So, pick the category, prepare for that moment, and have the leader confirm what they intend to do and when specifically they plan to take the action.
