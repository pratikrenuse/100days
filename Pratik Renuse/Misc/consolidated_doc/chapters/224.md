# A Perfect Fundraising Process Takes Time

Teams need ~1 month to prepare the core materials before their first investor meeting.
Teams need 2 - 6 weeks to execute a fundraising process from first meeting to signed term sheet.
Unless you are Semisonic, closing time is ~1 month from a signed term sheet to money in the bank.

Preparation | Execution | Closing

# When to Fundraise...Timing Matters!

**Holidays and Vacation**
The more money a VC firm manages, the more likely they are to be on vacation. As silly as this sounds, you do not want to be fundraising during summer vacations, Thanksgiving to Christmas or ski week in February (apparently this is a thing…).

**Quarter Ends**
You want to avoid being in the thick of your conversations with investors at the quarter end. If you are close to the quarter end, most investors will want to wait to see the results. If you miss your quarter badly, then your process will die.

**Seasonality**
If your business has a strong seasonality, then you will want to time your fundraise for the tail end of strong periods and avoid raising during the weak periods.

**Milestones and Metrics**
You strategically want to fundraise either right before or right after big milestones or hitting important metrics. For example, it would be worth timing a fundraise after signing a key channel partner, releasing a new product or signing a key hire.

**Zero Cash Date**
Given a 3 month fundraising cycle, you will want to start a fundraise at least 6 months prior to your zero cash date. Factor venture debt runway at 50% to be conservative.

# Target Investor List: Finding Investor / Company Fit

**Target List Size**
The average Series A process targets ~30 investors.
The average Series B process targets ~20 investors.

**Timing**
Allow for 2 weeks advance notice to schedule VC meetings.

**Right Partner**
Find the right partner (stage, expertise, availability) at each firm and identify the strongest warm intro to them.

**Optimize**
Understand what outcome you want to optimize for: specialist vs. generalist, expertise, brand, check size / price, the individual.

**Competition**
Eliminate VCs with competitive investments.

# The Perfect Pitch Deck

**Length**
The perfect Series A pitch deck is ~20 slides.
The perfect Series B pitch deck is ~25 slides to account for additional data slides.

**Time**
For an in-person meeting, be prepared to fit your pitch into as little as 30 minutes.
For an emailed pitch, an investor will scan a deck in less than 3 minutes so make sure the key points are obvious.

**Storytelling Matters**
The best pitches follow a story arc where you grab someone's attention, introduce a conflict and build toward a gradual resolution and ultimately your ask.
Whereas seed pitches are all about selling vision, Series A is centered around proving product/market fit and then extrapolating GTM.

**Lead the Witness**
Be self-aware of your weaknesses and the questions VCs will immediately ask about your business. Tackle the hard questions before they are asked with slides that let you frame the perspective.

**Track Engagement**
Never email your pitch deck directly. Always use a web service like Docsend so you can track engagement.
Eliminate friction so avoid password requirements and make the deck downloadable.

# Data Deck: Lead the Witness by Framing Your Data

**GPs are Lazy**
Most GPs are too lazy, too busy or not capable of doing the work to uncover secrets in your data. Thus, you should do it for them.

**Associates are Not**
GPs may ask Associates to analyze your data. Associates want to look smart and the best way to do that is to find flaws and pick your data apart.

**Tell Your Story**
When you assemble a data deck, you control the narrative with charts and graphs. You control the story and you get to lead investors to the conclusions that you want them to draw.

**Lies, Lies and Statistics**
Your data deck gives you the opportunity to highlight your strengths and explain your weaknesses. Investors will find your weaknesses so you may as well explain them and your plans to ameliorate them.

**Keep the Process Moving**
Giving every investor the same data deck prevents your fundraising process from slowing down based on their available resources to analyze your data

# References and Legal Diligence: Checking the Box

**Use Customers Strategically**
Even your best customers will get annoyed if you ask them to do 20 references. Find 10-15 customers that will give investor references and spread them out to investors who are at the finish line. Save your most valuable references for your preferred investors.

**Consider Recording**
If you have a customer reference that every investor wants to call, then consider having one of your insiders record a Zoom customer reference and then sharing the video. This can speed up the process and prevent overtaxing a valued relationship.

**Give References Talking Points**
Often times, the customer reference is from a senior person, not necessarily the person who is closest to the details so help them out. Remind them of the size of their contract, their engagement metrics, ROI and any upsells they've done.

**Use References to Align Process**
Customer references and management references can be used to align the timing of the various investors in a process. If one investor is too far ahead of the others, then references are an easy way to slow their process while you let others catch up.

**Shorten the Risk Window**
Legal diligence is a necessary evil but you want to prepare as much of the required materials in advance of signing a term sheet so you can make the time to close as fast as possible. The longer the window, the more risk your deal falls apart.

# Execute Like Your Startup's Life Depends on It

**Block Time**
Block time on your calendar weeks in advance for fundraising. If you are not in Silicon Valley, then it is even more important to block time and schedule meetings two weeks in advance.

**Learn and Iterate Quickly**
Refine your pitch after every meeting and write down the questions you received and the areas people were skeptical. Remember that you are not simply selling, but also qualify the investor so ask questions about their process, their expertise and their fit.

**Ask for Help**
As the philosopher Mick Jagger once said, "You can't always get what you want." Treat every meeting with an investor as an opportunity to ask for something: a customer intro, a talent recommendation or simply ask for their opinion. You just might get what you need.

**Keep the Ball in Their Court**
In a fundraising process, you want to keep the pressure on the investor (which also keeps their attention from waning). So it is incumbent upon you to answer questions quickly, send materials quickly and generally drive follow up.

**Manage Your Pipeline**
It is important to manage your pipeline like a sales funnel. Qualify out investors that aren't interested quickly (so you can focus on higher probability conversions) and try to drive all the interested parties on roughly the same timeline.

# ABC: Always Be Closing

**Know When to Go All-In**
VCs can conduct diligence forever and drag out a decision. The key is to understand each investors' decision making process and try to align them to finish at roughly the same time. Then, you push all-in when everyone is almost finished.

**Reverse Diligence**
Ask investors for a list of all their CEOs and then reach out to a few. You'll want to pick both successful companies and failures to understand how your future board member behaved in good and tough times.

**Selection**
If you only have one term sheet, then this is simple! If you have more than one term sheet, then we always recommend ranking your term sheets based on preferred investor regardless of terms.

**Negotiation**
We recommend starting with your top choice and giving them the option to close the deal at the terms the market dictated. Nearly every time, this results in the your top choice investor signing at close to the best terms you were offered.

**Legal**
If you have all of your legal diligence prepared, then sprinting from a term to a close should be easy (and possible within 2-3 weeks). Cap the legal expenses and guide counsel to a deadline so you have a margin of error.

# Remember the Five P's for a Perfect Fundraise

**Prepare Materials** - Preparation is the key to a smooth fundraising process. Allow yourself at least one month to prepare your materials.

**Practice the Pitch** - Practice with your team, practice with your friends, practice with your investors. Then practice some more. You only get one shot to make a great first impression and polish, confidence and presence matters.

**Precise Targets** - Focusing on the right investors and getting a strong, direct introduction will save you time and increase the probability of success.

**Pipeline Management** - Make sure you have enough investors moving through your pipeline at the same stages so you have a competitive process.

**Patience Make Perfect** - Once you receive term sheets, you are in the driver's seat. Take your time and choose the right investor that is aligned with your company's long term success.
