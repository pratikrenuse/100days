In June 2021, Marc Lou was so low that he dug 4 holes in the wall. Two years later he made a million dollars in a year, alone.

Before it worked

Five years where almost nothing went right.

Restaurant floor
2016
2016 01 / 06

He finished a computer science degree and spent a year building a dating app for people who love sports. It went nowhere. He paid the bills waiting tables for about $10 an hour.

Scroll through the years
2017 02 / 06

He moved to Seoul for an AI startup that predicted flight prices. Angel investors put in $100,000. About nine months later it had made nothing and it was over.

He lived in a 12 square metre flat with cockroaches in the bathroom. By 27 he was broke, back at his parents' place, and taking acting gigs for $200 each.

2018 03 / 06

Then something finally worked. VirallyBot was a marketing tool for escape rooms, at $199 a month. He got his first customer on a 42-minute sales call after cold emails, and it made about $100,000 in total.

When COVID shut the escape rooms, it went to zero in a week.

March 2021 04 / 06

He put $100,000 he had inherited into Tesla stock. He lost $36,000 in two weeks.

9 June 2021 05 / 06

He broke down. In his own words, he "dug 4 holes in the wall."

Around the same time, a PDF guide he made called The Golden Plumber earned $4 in two months.

November 2021 06 / 06

He took a job paying $9,000 a month, and got fired in November.

That's when he found the people on Twitter who build their products in public, and started doing the same.

From there he shipped small products, one after another, and posted every win and every flop on Twitter.

What survived

A few small ones paid a little, and he sold one of them, MakeLanding, for $35,000. The ones that still pay him today are ShipFast, CodeFast, DataFast and TrustMRR.

The first one that really worked was ShipFast.

The 36 and the 90% are his own numbers. Which square stands for which product is for illustration.

The turning point

He told his wife they'd be lucky to make $100.

Photo: Bernd Dittrich / Unsplash

Every time he started a new product, he wrote the same code again: login, payments, emails. In 2023 he spent about a week packing all of it into one starter kit for Next.js apps, priced at $199. He called it ShipFast and launched it on 1 September 2023.

Scroll to launch

It made $6,000 in the first 48 hours.

About $40,000 by the end of the first month. He says it grew because he kept tweeting about it and buyers posted their own results.

By November 2023 it was making $65,400 in a single month, at around 91% margin.

In April 2024 it hit $133,000 in one month. He gives the credit for that jump to his YouTube videos.

How he got users

No ads. He built in public and let people watch.

His Twitter account had only 1,000 followers in July 2022. In early 2023 it grew by about 50 a day, and by the ShipFast launch more than 40,000 people were watching what he built. Every product launched to the same crowd.

Twitter, every day

Revenue posted in public

He shared his numbers, his launches and his failures. When he launched something, people already trusted him.

Launch day

Product Hunt, Twitter and Hacker News together

ShipFast went out on all three on the same day, plus a short demo video on Reddit's r/SideProject.

Free tools

Free stuff that points to paid stuff

A free habit tool brought about 30,000 visitors in two months to his paid habit app. He also built a free logo maker with a ShipFast banner.

Videos

Funny launch skits

He made short comedy videos for launches. People shared them, so each launch spread on its own.

What didn't work

Ads and cold calls

Facebook ads for the gloves sold nothing. Cold calls failed for VirallyBot. Cold emails worked.

The warning

AI made his best product almost free to copy.

ShipFast dropped from about $126,000 a month in 2024 to about $2,000 a month in 2026. He blames AI. People can now get the same setup code written for them.

He saw it coming and moved to products that charge every month. DataFast went from $300 a month to $30,831 a month in under two years.

Two points per line. What happened in between isn't drawn. The ShipFast 2026 figure comes from a weaker secondary source.

His biggest earner today

TrustMRR: a page where founders prove their revenue.

Founders connect Stripe and their real revenue shows on a public page nobody can fake. Others browse it to find startups to buy. He built the first version in about six hours, coding with AI in Cursor.

One idea to steal

Turn the work you keep repeating into a product.

ShipFast was code Marc had already written dozens of times for himself. Look at what you keep redoing for every client or project. Someone else is probably redoing it too, and would pay to skip it.
