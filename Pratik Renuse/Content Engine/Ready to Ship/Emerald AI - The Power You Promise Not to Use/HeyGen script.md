# Emerald AI - The Power You Promise Not to Use -- HeyGen script

The fastest way to get electricity for a data centre right now is to promise you'll give some of it back. That's it. That's the trick.

Getting hooked up to the grid in America is now a queue you measure in years. Four to seven of them in places like Phoenix and Dallas. Texas stopped taking new data centre connections altogether back in August.

And the reason is almost boringly simple. Your electricity company plans around your worst moment, not your normal one. So if you tell them you'll pull five hundred megawatts flat, forever, no flexibility, they have to go find five hundred brand new megawatts before they can say yes.

But a lot of AI work isn't actually in a hurry. A training run can ease off for twenty minutes. Overnight jobs can wait. Only some of it truly can't.

Emerald AI is twenty two months old and that's all it sells. Software that works out, in seconds, what can be slowed down and by how much.

And this month it co-founded an industry alliance with Google and Nvidia. Nineteen partners, including Anthropic and National Grid. You don't build a trade body unless there's a negotiation to win, and this one isn't about price. It's about who gets connected first.

The proof so far? One blistering afternoon in Phoenix. The grid strained, and the site dropped a quarter of its power and held it there for three hours. One site, one afternoon, mind. That's a demonstration, not a habit.

Everyone else answers the power problem by building more power. This one answers it by using less, for twenty minutes, at the exact moment it's expensive. Megawatts keep getting built. What gets scarce is the promise attached to them.
