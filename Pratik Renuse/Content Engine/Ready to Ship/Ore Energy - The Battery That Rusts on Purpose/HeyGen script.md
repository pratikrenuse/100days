# Ore Energy - The Battery That Rusts on Purpose -- HeyGen script

A Dutch company just raised forty three million dollars for a battery that works by letting iron rust. And no, that's not a joke about old technology. Rusting is how it works.

Here's why it matters. Your normal grid battery, the lithium kind, runs about four hours and then it's flat. Fine for taking the edge off an evening. Useless for a data centre, which needs the same power on hour forty as it did on hour one.

This one, they say, holds twenty four to a hundred hours.

And you know what that changes? Nothing about how much power we make. Everything about what it's worth. Wind and solar were never short of energy. They were short of timing. Stretch storage from four hours to several days and that stops being a reason to burn anything.

It's called Ore Energy. Iron, water and air.

Now, iron batteries have been promised before and not turned up. Their real test is twenty twenty eight.

But every giant AI site being planned gets its own gas plant attached, because connecting to the grid takes four or five years.

Today, running out of power means building a power station. Tomorrow, a shed full of rust.
