A Dutch company just raised 43 million dollars for a battery that works by letting iron rust.

That is not a joke about old technology. Rusting is the mechanism.

..

The reason it matters is the number beside it. A normal grid battery, the lithium kind, runs for about four hours and then it is empty. Perfectly good for taking the sting out of an evening peak. Useless for a data centre, which needs the same power on hour 40 as it did on hour one.

Ore Energy says its cells hold 24 to 100 hours.

..

Every answer to the AI power problem so far has been the same answer. Build more. More gas turbines, ordered years ahead. More reactors, one of which reached first criticality in Texas this week. Eventually fusion.

This is a different sentence entirely. It adds no generation at all. It changes what the generation you already have is worth.

Because wind and solar were never short of energy. They were short of timing. Cheap enough to put almost anywhere, and unable to promise you next Tuesday evening. Stretch storage from four hours to several days and that timing gap stops being a reason to burn anything.

The thing being sold here is not electricity. It is the ability to say when.

- 37.3 million euros raised on Wednesday, led by Plural and HV.
- The cell is built from iron, water and air. No lithium, no cobalt, none of the rare metals that sit behind today's export controls.

..

The honest part: iron-air has been announced before and has not arrived. Commercial production is targeted for 2028, and that is the year to judge this on, not this one.

But look at what it would compete with. The giant AI campuses being planned now arrive with their own gas plants attached, because the wait to connect to the grid runs four to five years and nobody wants to spend it queueing. Panels and storage go up in months, on land nobody is fighting over.

That is the whole race. Not who makes the most power. Who can put firm power somewhere before the decade turns.

Today, running out of power means building another power station. Tomorrow it means building a warehouse of rust beside the field you already had.
