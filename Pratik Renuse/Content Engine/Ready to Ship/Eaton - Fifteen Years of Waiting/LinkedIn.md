America has fifteen years of data centre construction already on order.

Not planned. Not forecast. Ordered, by people who have committed enough money that someone quoted them the electrical equipment.

..

The number comes from Eaton, which makes the gear sitting between a power station and a server rack. Switchgear, transformers, backup power. Unexciting boxes, and nothing gets switched on without them.

Because Eaton has to quote all of that before a building exists, its order book is a measurement of the buildout that nobody else has. On last Friday's earnings call, its chief executive put the total American data centre backlog at 307 gigawatts. At the rate the industry actually built in 2025, that is roughly fifteen years of work.

Three months earlier the same figure was twelve years.

..

Almost everything else in this argument is a projection. How much power AI needs by 2030. How fast reactors arrive. Whether turbines free up.

This is not a projection. It is a tally of things people have already decided to pay for.

And what it says is that the constraint was never demand and never capital. It is the physical speed at which an industry can pour concrete, wind copper and connect substations.

..

The honest caveat, and Eaton gave it itself: only about a fifth of that backlog turns into deliveries any time soon. The rest lands in 2028 and later. A queue is a queue, not a promise, and some of those projects will quietly never get built.

That cuts both ways though. If the queue holds, pricing power stays with the boring equipment makers for a decade. If it does not, a great many announced gigawatts are about to evaporate. Watching the conversion rate is the cheapest way to find out which.

Today the scarce thing in AI is a chip you wait months for. Tomorrow it is a place in a line that already runs fifteen years deep.
