The most back-ordered product in the entire AI boom isn't a chip.

It's a gas turbine.

Yeah, a gas turbine.

The same boring machine sitting in a power plant may be the real thing deciding how fast AI can grow.

And I couldn't figure out why. So I went digging.

..

It comes down to one thing: power.

Every AI data center needs an enormous, steady supply of it, the instant you switch it on.

Solar stops at night. Nuclear takes a decade to build.

So for now, that power comes from burning gas. And the turbines that burn it are brutally hard to make.

Only a handful of companies on Earth can build them.

..

The biggest is GE Vernova, the power business spun out of GE just two years ago.

And it's completely sold out.

..

- Its turbines are booked through 2029, with orders into 2031
- Some buyers pay triple the price just to hold their spot

And money can't speed it up. The four biggest tech firms will spend 725 billion dollars on AI this year, up 77%. None of it buys a turbine any faster.

..

That's when it clicked for me.

The AI race isn't really about the smartest model. It's about electricity.

By 2030, the world's data centers will use about as much power as all of Japan.

Solar, wind, the first small reactors, none of it scales fast enough.

So for years, this one boring machine sets the speed limit on AI.

..

Can anyone build around it? A few are trying.

Fusion startups are aiming for the early 2030s. And some want to leave Earth entirely: Starcloud already trained an AI model in orbit, and even SpaceX is building data center satellites.

But none of it is ready this decade.

..

Sooo…

For the next five to ten years, the thing holding AI back isn't brains. It's power.

The company that decides how fast AI grows won't be a lab in Silicon Valley. It'll be whoever builds electricity fastest.

Right now, that's a turbine maker most people have never heard of.
