# Impulse Space - The Rocket Lets Go -- HeyGen script

A rocket does one thing. It throws you incredibly hard in roughly the right direction, and then it lets go.

And where it lets go is almost never where you needed to be.

Every story about putting data centres in space gets priced the same way, in dollars per kilo to get up there. That's the taxi to the airport. It is not the ride home.

See, a satellite has to reach one exact orbit out of thousands of nearby ones. Then it has to stay there. And staying isn't free, because even that high up there's still a whisper of air dragging you down, so you're burning fuel for years just to stand still. Then it gets moved. Then somebody has to bring it down safely at the end.

None of that is the rocket's problem. And none of it shows up in the numbers anyone publishes.

Impulse Space just took another three hundred and eight million dollars, which makes that one round eight hundred and eight million. It was started by Tom Mueller, the first person SpaceX ever hired, the man who built the engines on every Falcon nine.

One of its craft is already up there, moving things around. The bigger one flies in twenty twenty seven. Hasn't flown yet, so, grain of salt.

A hundred thousand heavy satellites, each burning fuel to hold its spot for a decade. Launch is a one off. Staying put is a subscription.

Getting off Earth is becoming a commodity. Staying exactly where you meant to be isn't. In shipping, in telecoms, in parcels, the long haul went cheap and the last mile kept the money.
