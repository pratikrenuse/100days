# Positron - The Chip That Uses Phone Memory -- HeyGen script

There's a chip company with eight hundred and seventy five million dollars of new money, and its whole plan is to not use the most fought-over part in AI.

Every AI chip runs into the same wall. Its limit isn't how fast it thinks. It's how fast it can read.

A model is basically a giant pile of numbers, and the chip has to pull those numbers out of memory to do anything at all. So memory sets the speed. Not the maths.

The industry's answer is a premium memory, stacked right beside the processor. And it's sold out into twenty twenty seven. You basically can't buy it.

So a company called Positron went the other way entirely. Its chip runs on the ordinary memory that's already in your phone. Cheap, mass produced, actually available.

The chip doesn't exist yet, mind you. It goes into production late twenty twenty seven.

But five weeks earlier, a London company raised three hundred and twelve million to escape the exact same memory by going faster instead of cheaper. One went up, one went down. Both are betting the shortage is a choice, not a law.

Today, an AI machine is defined by its most expensive part. Tomorrow, it might be defined by the part it was built without.
