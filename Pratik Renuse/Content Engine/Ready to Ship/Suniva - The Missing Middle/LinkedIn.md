America puts up a lot of solar panels. It barely makes the part inside them that turns light into electricity.

..

A panel is not one thing. Sand becomes purified silicon, the silicon becomes a block, the block is sliced into wafers, the wafers become cells, and the cells are laminated into the panel you see on a roof.

The cell is the piece that does the physics. Everything after it is packaging.

For years American policy funded the last step, because assembly lines are cheap and quick to stand up and they make a good photograph. So the country built a lot of panel capacity out of cells it imported. The headline number described the wrapper.

..

On 9 September a company called Suniva closed 835 million dollars to fix its own share of that. The money funds a 4.5 gigawatt cell plant in Laurens County, South Carolina, taking Suniva from 1 gigawatt of cell capacity to 5.5.

Backers include Goldman Sachs Alternatives and I Squared Capital. The building shell is already standing. Construction finishes in 2027, production starts in 2028.

..

It matters because of what the AI buildout is actually short of, which is not ambition but speed. The queue for the electrical gear that connects a data centre to the grid now runs to roughly fifteen years of work at current build rates. Gas turbines are sold out into 2029.

Solar paired with storage is the fastest thing anyone can put up in America. That advantage only holds if the cells exist.

Two honest caveats. Nothing finished in 2028 helps the crunch happening now, and Suniva is building the mature cell design rather than the newest one.

..

Rare earths taught this exact lesson one industry over. The West had the mines and not the refining. In solar it had the assembly and not the cells.

A country keeps measuring its industrial strength at the end of the line it can see. Today the shortage everyone talks about is electricity. Tomorrow the question is who still makes the middle of anything.
