Nvidia just put two billion dollars into land in Texas.

Not a factory. Not a research campus. A thousand acres outside Abilene, and the electrical connection that comes with it.

..

The company is Lancium. It does the unglamorous work of a power developer: find a site, secure a grid connection, get permission to build. Its Abilene campus is the first operational site of Stargate, the enormous data centre programme behind OpenAI and SoftBank.

Nvidia is reportedly taking about 20 percent, with another billion tied to getting more power hooked up. The whole business is valued around ten billion dollars.

..

Two weeks earlier, the same company did something related but importantly different. Nvidia was reported to be arranging roughly 250 billion dollars of credit support so OpenAI could rent a different campus, because OpenAI may not carry the rating to borrow that much alone.

That was lending. This is owning. Lending can be unwound. Ownership cannot.

..

The part that keeps getting missed about this buildout is which input is actually scarce.

Chips get manufactured. Buildings get poured. Money is not the problem. What nobody can produce on demand is a site that already has a grid connection, water, permits, and neighbours who will tolerate the noise.

The wait to connect a large new load to the American grid now runs years. Which means a piece of land that is already plugged in stopped being real estate a while ago. It is an option on the next five years of AI capacity, and Nvidia just bought a fifth of one.

..

There is a risk hiding in that. If one company's balance sheet ends up underwriting the chips, the racks, the credit and the ground, then the single point of failure for the whole industry is not a factory or a power station. It is a credit rating.

Today Nvidia sells you the computers. Ten years out, the sharper question is how much of the ground beneath the industry it quietly ended up holding.
