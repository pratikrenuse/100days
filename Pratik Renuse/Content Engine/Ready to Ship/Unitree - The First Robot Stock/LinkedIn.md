The best-selling humanoid robot maker on Earth is not Tesla. It is not even American.

It is a nine-year-old company from Hangzhou called Unitree. And on July 3, China cleared it to become the first big humanoid robot stock in history.

I read the IPO filing. The numbers stopped me cold.

..

Most robot companies burn investor money on demos. Unitree sells robots the way a phone maker sells phones.

- Revenue hit about 1.7 billion yuan in 2025, up 335% in a single year. In 2022 it was 123 million. Roughly 14x in three years.
- Profit rose nearly 8x, to around 600 million yuan. A robot company. Profitable.
- Humanoids were 1.9% of its sales in 2023. By late 2025, 51.5%. At a 63% gross margin.

It delivered about 5,500 humanoid robots last year, more than anyone else on the planet. Its cheapest model costs under six thousand dollars.

..

Regulators cleared the listing in 73 days, a record. The raise is about 619 million dollars, at a valuation near 6 billion.

So why does a stock matter more than another viral backflip video?

Because a listing gives the whole sector a price. Public numbers, quarterly proof, and a benchmark every rival gets measured against. Electric cars had their moment like this too, one real stock, then a flood of capital into the entire supply chain.

..

That is the part I keep chewing on. The humanoid race will not be won by the smartest robot. It will be won on the factory floor, by whoever rides the cost curve down fastest.

An average humanoid cost about 35 thousand dollars to build in 2025. By 2030, forecasts put it near 17 thousand, cheaper than a small car. Morgan Stanley's long bet: a billion of these machines at work by 2050, a five trillion dollar workforce.

..

Today, a humanoid robot is a video you watch on your phone. Tomorrow, it is a line item in a public company's earnings report. And the day after, a machine in your building that cost less than the car outside it.

The demos were the trailer. The filing was the movie starting.
