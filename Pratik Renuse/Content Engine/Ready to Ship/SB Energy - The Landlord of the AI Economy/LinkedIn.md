The largest data centre project ever announced is going on an old uranium enrichment site in southern Ohio. The company that would own it is called SB Energy, and it is SoftBank's energy arm.

On Monday the Wall Street Journal reported that Nvidia is in talks to guarantee around 250 billion dollars so OpenAI can rent the place. Talks, not a signature.

..

Two things in that plan are worth slowing down for.

- 10 gigawatts of computing, and beside it its own gas power station, 9.2 gigawatts, on site.
- The whole build, chips included, could pass 500 billion dollars.

The power plant is there because the grid cannot connect them in time. Large transformers in the US are now quoted four to five years out, and medium voltage switchgear is sold out through 2028 in much of the market. So they are building around the grid rather than waiting in it.

..

The second thing is stranger. OpenAI may not have a strong enough credit rating to borrow at that scale. Under the reported plan, SB Energy borrows against Nvidia's credit instead.

The chip seller co-signs the loan on the building that will house its chips.

..

That is the shape of the whole industry right now, in one deal. A software company cannot get the mortgage. A hardware company puts its name on the paperwork. And a Japanese telecom's energy subsidiary ends up owning the land underneath all of it.

The scarce thing was never intelligence. It is a substation and a signature.

..

First phase is roughly 800 megawatts, targeted for 2028, and only if the financing, generation and transmission all land.

Meanwhile GE Vernova's turbine order book hit 116 gigawatts this month, guiding to 125 by year end. Nobody is short of demand for power. Everybody is short of the ability to underwrite it.

By 2030 the firms that look most like AI winners will have balance sheets that read like utilities: land, generation, twenty year contracts, a credit rating.

Today the AI race is won by whoever trains the best model. Tomorrow it is won by whoever can be underwritten.
