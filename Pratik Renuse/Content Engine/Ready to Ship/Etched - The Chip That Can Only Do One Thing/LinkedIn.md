A chip company was just valued at 10.3 billion dollars for building something that deliberately cannot run tomorrow's AI. Not a flaw. The entire product.

The company is Etched. Its chip is called Sohu and it runs exactly one design, the transformer, the thing sitting under ChatGPT and Claude and Gemini. Feed it a model built any other way and it does nothing at all.

..

Every other AI chip has to hold space in reserve for whatever comes next, because nobody knows what the next big model will look like. That reserved space is insurance, and you pay for it in performance on the models you actually run today.

Etched cancelled the insurance and spent the space on transformers. It claims roughly 20 times the speed of Nvidia's H100 on them. Their figure, no independent test published.

- 300 million raised on Thursday, led by Sequoia. Reported as the highest valuation Sequoia has ever led a Series C at.
- The company was worth 5 billion in December. Seven months later, 10.3 billion.
- Around a billion dollars of customer demand reported before it ships at volume.

..

One name on the cap table sharpens it. SK Hynix is in the round. The memory maker that supplies Nvidia just bought a slice of the company built to take Nvidia's inference business.

..

Computing has run this play before. Graphics got a dedicated chip. Video encoding got one. Bitcoin mining got one. The pattern each time: a job stops changing, and the money leaves the flexible machine for the one that does that job and nothing else.

So the real bet here is not about chips at all. It is that AI models have stopped changing shape. If a genuinely new architecture takes hold, Etched is a museum piece by the following Tuesday. That is a real risk, and 10.3 billion says a lot of serious people are discounting it.

Today the winner is the chip that can do anything, because we are still guessing what comes next. Tomorrow it is the chip that stopped guessing, because the guessing was over and only one company noticed early.
