Everyone's obsessed with how smart AI is getting.

Almost nobody asks the dumb question: where does all that heat go?

So this week, I followed the heat. And it led somewhere strange.

Not to a chip. To a company that basically air-conditions computers. Coolers, for a machine. I had to see it for myself.

..

Here's the part nobody tells you.

An AI chip runs so hot it would cook itself in seconds. And it's not one chip. A single AI rack today throws off as much heat as a hundred microwaves running at once.

For years, fans were enough. Just blow air over the chips.

But the new AI machines got so hot that air quit working. To cool them with fans, you'd need hurricane winds screaming through the box.

So they gave up on air. Now they run liquid, actual water, right up against the chips.

..

And almost nobody builds that plumbing at scale. Except one company: Vertiv.

You've probably never heard of it. Wall Street has.

- Its order backlog just hit about 15 billion dollars, up roughly 80% in a single year
- This year it will sell around 14 billion dollars of gear
- Its stock went from about 12 dollars in 2020 to near 328, roughly 27x, now a 126 billion dollar company

All of that, for a company that makes coolers.

..

And there it was, the thing I'd missed.

We keep score on AI by how clever it gets. Wrong scoreboard. The real one is heat.

..

And it only gets hotter from here.

Today's racks already run as hot as a hundred microwaves. By 2027, Nvidia's own roadmap points to four, even five times more.

Water won't be enough. So the chips get dunked, fully bathed in special liquid. And the cooling market roughly triples, toward 15 billion dollars by 2030.

..

So here's what I keep coming back to.

The company quietly winning the AI race today keeps chips cool with water. Tomorrow, it'll be whoever can drown a chip in liquid and keep it running.

Because building a genius is one thing. Keeping it from melting is the real race.
