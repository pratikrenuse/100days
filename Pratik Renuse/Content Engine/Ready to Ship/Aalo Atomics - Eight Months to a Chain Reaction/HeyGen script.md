# Aalo Atomics - Eight Months to a Chain Reaction -- HeyGen script

At twenty past midnight on July 4th, a new nuclear reactor came alive in the Idaho desert. It was eight months old. Yeah, eight months from breaking ground to a live chain reaction. America hasn't built one that fast in eighty years.

The company is called Aalo Atomics, and barely anyone knows the name. But that same week, a heatwave pushed the biggest US grid past a record from 2006, and for the first time ever, data centers were ordered onto backup generators. The machines are ready. The electricity isn't. And the gas turbines everyone needs? Sold out into 2030.

So Aalo builds reactors like products, not projects. Small ten megawatt units, made factory-style, bundled into fifty megawatt pods that sit right next to a data center. Their second reactor is already going up, and that one will power a real data center on site.

Here's the future I'd watch. Twenty two gigawatts of small reactors are in development across America, and Amazon wants five just for itself. Today, a data center waits years for the grid. Tomorrow, the reactor comes with the building.
