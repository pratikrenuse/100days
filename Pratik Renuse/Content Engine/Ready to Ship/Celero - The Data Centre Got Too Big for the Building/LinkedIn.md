The biggest AI computers no longer fit inside one building. They are being stitched together across a hundred miles of glass.

..

The reason is not clever engineering. It is that nobody can get enough electricity in one place.

Microsoft said last week it plans to go from about 12 gigawatts of data centre capacity to more than 38 by 2032, and the reason given was that shortages have already forced it to turn customers away. Texas paused new data centre grid connections outright in August, pending an audit.

So if you cannot make one site bigger, you make several sites behave like one. You run fibre between them and treat four buildings in four counties as a single machine.

Which quietly makes the fibre part of the machine.

..

That link is held together by a component almost nobody outside the industry has heard of. It sits at each end of a long fibre and does three jobs.

- It turns electricity into light, and light back into electricity
- It works out exactly when data was sent, without a second cable to carry the timing
- It repairs the errors that appear as light dims and smears over distance

Over a hundred miles, all three get hard.

..

On 8 September, a company called Celero raised 275 million dollars at above 3 billion, led by Google's growth fund, after validating what it says is the first chip of this kind built on a 2 nanometre process. It handles up to 3.2 terabits a second per port, well ahead of what AI networks run today.

Small company. Irvine, California. Around 400 million dollars raised in total, against a market that did not exist at this scale two years ago.

..

Every argument about AI is about which model is smartest and which chip is fastest. Neither question mentions geography.

Today an AI data centre is a building you can point at. Tomorrow it is a region, and what decides the winner is the quality of the glass running between the counties.
