Buyers are paying three times the price for a part that costs a fraction of a penny.

..

Scattered across every AI server board are tens of thousands of tiny ceramic blocks called capacitors. The job is dull and essential. When a processor suddenly gulps power, the capacitor smooths the flow so the chip does not stumble.

They are the size of a grain of sand. They cost almost nothing. Nobody outside an electronics purchasing department has ever spent a thought on them.

This year they became the third biggest line item in an AI server, behind only the processors and the memory.

..

The reason has nothing to do with the material. Ceramic is everywhere. The physics is a century old.

It is the factory. The high grade capacitors an AI accelerator needs come off a small number of specialised lines, and those lines are running at 90 to 95 percent. Building a new one takes 18 to 24 months, and it takes that long regardless of what anyone offers.

So buyers do the only thing available. Trade sources say they are paying two to three times the market price and signing agreements running from six months to several years, purely to guarantee they get any.

..

The biggest name in it is Yageo, from Taiwan. In July it raised capacitor prices for its direct customers for the first time, around 50 percent across the board.

Murata, Samsung and Taiyo Yuden are all building new lines. None of that arrives in volume before late 2027.

..

What I keep turning over is the shape of it. A trillion dollar industry, held up by something you could lose in a carpet, because the machine that presses ceramic takes longer to install than the product it feeds takes to go obsolete.

One honest flag: capacitor shortages have happened before and every one of them ended in a glut. Late 2027 is the date to check this against.

Today the thing you cannot get is the chip. Tomorrow it is whatever sits beside it and takes two years to make.
