# TSMC Packaging - The Bottleneck Wears a Record Quarter -- HeyGen script

The world's biggest chipmaker just posted the most profitable quarter in its history. Forty billion dollars in three months. And it spent the announcement pointing at its own weak spot.

Not the chips. The gluing.

See, an AI chip isn't really one chip. It's a processor plus stacks of memory, bonded onto one slab of silicon so they can talk fast enough. That bonding step is called packaging. It has its own factories, its own machines, and TSMC says it's now the thing slowing down the entire AI buildout. Nvidia waits in that queue. So does everyone else.

So TSMC is growing that capacity eighty percent a year, and the day after earnings it raised its American spending plan to 265 billion dollars, with four factories aimed at this one step. Even then, supply ends the year about ten percent short.

And you know what? It's a pattern. First chips were scarce. Then memory. Now the stitching between them. By 2030, the next squeeze is connections made of light.

Today, the AI race runs on glue. Tomorrow, on lasers. The boring layer always wins.
