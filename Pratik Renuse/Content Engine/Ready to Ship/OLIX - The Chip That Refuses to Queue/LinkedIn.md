Someone just raised 312 million dollars to build an AI chip that does not need either of the two things the whole industry is short of.

Not a compromise. The entire plan.

..

Here is the shortage everyone is living inside. The special memory that sits beside an AI processor is sold out through 2027. Not scarce. Sold out, with buyers wiring deposits before there is a contract to sign. Ordinary computer memory got roughly 60% more expensive in one quarter, according to market trackers. And the bonding step that fuses those memory parts onto the processor is booked solid too.

So everybody is standing in the same two queues.

..

A two-year-old London company called OLIX decided not to stand in either.

Its design, and this is the company's account of it, keeps the model inside the processor itself, so it needs none of that special memory. It also skips the bonding step entirely. They say the reason out loud: those are the parts nobody can get, so we built something that does not want them.

- 270.5 million euros raised on Monday, at a 2.8 billion euro valuation.
- Arm invested. So did Reed Hastings, who co-founded Netflix.

..

Their way of framing the problem is what stuck with me. A data centre is a factory, and the thing coming off the line is a sentence. Producing one sentence takes hundreds of separate operations. Any real factory would give each stage its own machine. This industry runs every stage on the same one.

So they built a different machine for each stage, and connected them with light instead of copper.

..

It may not work. Nothing has shipped. Customers do not get chips until late 2027, and no outsider has tested a single number.

But sit with what is being bet. For three years the money in AI has gone to whoever owns the part you cannot buy. Every quarter, a list of companies nobody could name turned out to be holding the industry's throat. Very few people were asking the other question, which is whether the part was necessary at all.

Today the winner owns the scarce thing. Tomorrow the winner is whoever quietly designed the scarce thing out of the machine.
