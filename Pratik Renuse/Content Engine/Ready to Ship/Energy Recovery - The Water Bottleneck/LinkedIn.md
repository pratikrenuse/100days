The next thing the world runs short of won't be oil.

It'll be water.

And this week I found the small company quietly keeping that crisis at bay. You've never heard of it.

..

Here's the problem. We can already make fresh water from the sea. It's called desalination, and it's how cities from Dubai to San Diego drink.

But there's a catch: pushing seawater through filters takes a monstrous amount of energy. For decades, that made fresh water painfully expensive.

Then a company called Energy Recovery cracked it. They built a simple ceramic device, about the size of a can, that catches the energy the process throws away and feeds it straight back in.

The result? It cuts the energy needed by up to 60%. That one part is what makes desalination affordable at all.

..

And most of the world's biggest plants now run on it.

- Their devices save desalination plants around 7.2 billion dollars of energy every year
- One small part, sitting quietly inside the world's water supply

A company most people can't name has become a hidden gatekeeper of who gets to drink.

..

So the water crisis isn't really about the planet running dry. It's about the cost of making more. And that cost is the thing to watch.

By 2030, as heat rises and cities swell, the world will lean on desalination like never before. And Energy Recovery is already taking the same trick into cooling and factories, anywhere energy gets wasted under pressure.

..

Today, the quiet winner turns seawater into something a city can afford. Tomorrow, it wants to squeeze wasted energy out of everything.

Because you can live without a lot of things. Water isn't one of them.

- Company states its devices save customers ~$7.2 billion in energy costs per year, globally; it is the dominant supplier for large seawater desalination plants.

- Expanding the same tech into CO2 refrigeration / industrial uses.
