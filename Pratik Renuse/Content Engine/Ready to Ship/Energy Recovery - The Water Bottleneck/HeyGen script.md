# Energy Recovery -- HeyGen script

The next thing the world runs short of won't be oil. It'll be water. And this week I found the small company quietly keeping that crisis at bay.

Here's the problem. We can already make fresh water from the sea. It's called desalination, and it's how cities from Dubai to San Diego drink. But pushing seawater through filters takes a monstrous amount of energy, and for decades that made fresh water painfully expensive.

Then a company called Energy Recovery cracked it. They built a simple ceramic device, about the size of a can, that catches the energy the process throws away and feeds it straight back in. It cuts the energy needed by up to 60%. That one part is what makes desalination affordable at all.

And most of the world's biggest plants now run on it, saving them around 7.2 billion dollars of energy every year. A company most people can't name has become a hidden gatekeeper of who gets to drink.

So the water crisis isn't about the planet running dry. It's about the cost of making more. And by 2030, as heat rises and cities swell, the world will lean on desalination like never before.

Today, this quiet company turns seawater into something a city can afford. Because you can live without a lot of things. Water isn't one of them.
