We spent 3 billion dollars just to read the first human genome.

Today, reading one costs about a hundred bucks.

But reading DNA was never the hard part. The value is in writing it. Printing a strand from scratch, then growing that into something real.

One company does this better than anyone, and hardly anyone outside biotech knows the name. Twist Bioscience. It prints DNA on a chip.

This is already how a lot of medicine gets made. You design a drug on a screen, print the DNA, and let living cells build it. It's how the COVID vaccine came together in days.

Now the strange part. DNA is so dense that one gram could hold a room full of hard drives, and it survives for thousands of years. So Twist spun off a company, backed by Jeff Bezos and the CIA's venture fund, to store real data inside it.

For two decades, biology was about reading the code of life. The next two are about writing it. And the company that owns the printer could matter more than the ones building the models.

Honest question: does this sound like the future to you, or a science fair project that got out of hand?
