# Cerebras - Reading and Writing Split Apart -- HeyGen script

AMD just admitted its own machines are good at only half the job. And it is the reading half.

Because when you ask an AI something, two opposite things happen. Reading your question is bulk work, thousands of words all landing at once. Writing the answer cannot work like that. It makes one word, reads back what it just wrote, then makes the next. One at a time, every time.

Two jobs, one machine, and it was never good at both.

So last Thursday, AMD partnered with a company called Cerebras. AMD reads the question. Cerebras writes the answer.

And Cerebras is an odd one. It refused to make chips a normal size. Its processor is a single unbroken slice about as wide as a dinner plate, so between one word and the next, the data barely moves. Together they claim up to five times more words per second for the same electricity.

That is how one market quietly turns into two.

Today everyone is chasing one chip. Tomorrow there are two races, and almost nobody is watching the second one.
