The company that wins the humanoid robot race might be a Japanese gearbox maker founded in 1944.

..

Every robot joint has the same problem. The motor spins fast and weak. The joint has to move slow and strong. Something in between does the conversion, and in a walking robot that something carries the entire weight of the machine through every single step.

That part is a gearbox called a cycloidal reducer. Roughly 60 percent of the world's supply comes from one company, Nabtesco.

Its operating profit rose 60 percent last year on robot and automation orders. It is now doubling capacity.

..

The detail that genuinely surprised me is that this is not one market with a leader. It is two.

Hips and legs need cycloidal reducers, built to carry load. Arms, wrists and hands need a completely different device called a harmonic drive, built for precision. They are not interchangeable. And about 85 percent of harmonic drives come from one other Japanese company.

So the two hardest parts of a humanoid come from two near monopolies, sitting in different halves of the same robot.

..

Actuators, meaning the motor and gearbox packages that drive those joints, are somewhere between 40 and 60 percent of what a humanoid costs to build, and the reducer is the heart of an actuator. Which means the price of a robot only falls when gearboxes get cheaper, and gearboxes only get cheaper when volume shows up.

The squeeze is already visible. Unitree, China's volume leader, cut one of its robots from nearly 40,000 yuan to under 30,000. That reduction did not come from nowhere. It came out of the parts list.

..

The obvious risk: nobody wants to buy 85 percent of anything from a single supplier, so every customer is quietly funding a second source. Chinese and European challengers are being built for exactly this.

Still, the argument everyone is having is about which robot brand wins. In ten years the answer may be that none of them especially did, and the money went to whoever made the hip.

Today the robot has a logo on the outside. Tomorrow the name worth knowing is the one stamped inside the knee.
