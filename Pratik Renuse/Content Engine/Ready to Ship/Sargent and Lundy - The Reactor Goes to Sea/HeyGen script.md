# Sargent and Lundy - The Reactor Goes to Sea -- HeyGen script

A Korean shipbuilder just agreed to develop nuclear power plants that float. And the clever bit is not the floating.

It is that you can order the plant before you decide which reactor goes inside it.

Nuclear was never really held up by the science. It was held up by the order you have to do things in. Find the land. Win the permits. Join the queue for the grid. Then pick a reactor company and hope the regulator approves that exact design. Every step waits for the one before it, and any of them can send you back to the start. That is why a power plant takes a decade and a half while the data centre waiting on it takes months.

Build it on a hull and most of that just disappears. Made in a shipyard, on a shipyard's schedule, then towed to whoever needs it.

The drawings come from a Chicago firm called Sargent and Lundy. They have been designing America's power plants for over a hundred years, quietly.

And the pull is already there. Nearly ten gigawatts of offshore nuclear has been committed to AI data centres.

Today, power is something you wait in line for. Tomorrow, it gets delivered.
