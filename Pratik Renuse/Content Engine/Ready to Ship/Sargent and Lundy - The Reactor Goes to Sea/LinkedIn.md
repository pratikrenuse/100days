Last Tuesday a Korean shipbuilder and a Chicago engineering firm agreed to develop nuclear power plants that float. The interesting part is not the water. It is that the hull will take whichever reactor you pick later.

The engineering firm is Sargent and Lundy. It has been designing American power plants for over a century without ever needing you to know that.

..

Nuclear's real obstacle was never the physics. It was the order of operations.

- Find a site. Win the local permits. Join the grid queue.
- Then choose a reactor company, and hope the design you chose is the one the regulator ends up approving.

Each step has to finish before the next can start, and any one of them can reset the clock. That is why a power plant is measured in a decade and a half while the data centre waiting for it is measured in months.

..

A floating plant removes the site, most of the local permitting, and much of the grid queue. It gets built inside a shipyard, on a shipyard's schedule, then towed to where the demand is.

The reactor agnostic hull removes the last one. You can start welding while the reactor market is still arguing about which design wins, and decide at the end.

The plant stops being a construction project and becomes a product with a delivery date.

..

The pull is already visible. As of May, thirteen announced offshore nuclear projects had committed more than 9.8 gigawatts specifically to AI data centres. Not to this platform, which has no orders yet, but to the idea of moving reactors offshore.

The wall they are all trying to get around is on land: GE Vernova's gas turbine order book hit 116 gigawatts this month and it is guiding to 125 by year end.

By the mid 2030s the first gigawatt of AI power that beats the grid to the customer will most likely arrive by sea, welded by a shipyard rather than poured by a utility.

Today power is something you queue for. Tomorrow it is something that gets delivered. And the money will not go to the famous name on the reactor. It goes to whoever owns the drawing of the box it sits in.
