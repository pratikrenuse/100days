A company was founded on Wednesday whose product is not an app, a model or a chip.

Its product is the scientific method, running without anyone watching it.

..

The name is Discovery Loop. The loop is the one every scientist has run since the seventeenth century. Have an idea worth testing, test it, look hard at what came back, choose the next thing. Repeat until something works or you retire.

Machines already do the testing. Laboratories exist that mix and measure faster than a person can read the results.

The part nobody has handed over is the choosing. Someone always sits at that junction deciding what deserves the next attempt, and everything downstream waits on them.

That seat is what this company is built to empty.

..

Now look at where they are aiming it first, because it is the most revealing line in the whole announcement.

Not cancer. Not batteries. They are pointing it at AI research itself, with the company as its own first customer, before it goes near a laboratory bench.

They picked the one field where an experiment costs almost nothing, the answer comes back in hours, and what comes out the other end is a better version of the thing that asked the question.

That is not a science company yet. That is a company teaching itself to be one.

- Four people leaving Google to start it, one of them after 27 years.
- Radical Ventures and Khosla are co-leading the seed. The round has not closed and Google itself is an investor.

..

It could go nowhere. A system that only ever marks its own homework is a very expensive hall of mirrors, and nothing here has been demonstrated, published or reproduced by anyone outside the building. The real test is the first result someone else can repeat.

But if that loop ever closes properly, the ceiling on human discovery stops being how many gifted people we can train and starts being something far more ordinary.

Today the pace of discovery is set by how many good scientists are alive. Tomorrow it is set by how many laboratories we can build, and how much electricity we can feed them.
