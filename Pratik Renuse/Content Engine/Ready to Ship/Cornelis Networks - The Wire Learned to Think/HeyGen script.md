# Cornelis Networks - The Wire Learned to Think -- HeyGen script

Half the AI hardware on the planet is sitting there doing nothing right now. Idle. Powered up, paid for, and waiting. That's the industry's own estimate, by the way.

I couldn't let that go, so I looked up why. Turns out an AI doesn't run on one chip. It runs on thousands, and they all have to keep agreeing with each other. Each one works out a little piece, sends it off, and then just waits for the total to come back. Millions of times a day. That waiting is where the time goes.

And the network in between? It carries all those little pieces and does absolutely nothing with them. The chips do the adding up, at both ends.

Cornelis Networks just raised two hundred and five million dollars to put a bit of computing power inside the network itself, so it adds the numbers up while they're already moving. One finished answer lands instead of thousands of scraps.

Their marketing chief said it perfectly. The payload does not arrive the way it left.

Then I hit the money side. By their own reckoning, the network is about fifteen percent of what one of these machines costs. And it decides how much you get out of the other eighty five. Their numbers, mind, and their simulations.

But if they're even close, nobody's really short of chips. They're short of chips that are actually busy. Right now you buy AI by the chip. Soon you'll buy it by the hour it actually works.
