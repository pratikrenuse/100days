# Orbital - The Land Grab Above Us -- HeyGen script

A company with six employees just asked the US government to let it build one hundred thousand data centers in space. Six people. Five months old.

The company is called Orbital, and its filing asks for ten gigawatts of computing in orbit. That's about ten big nuclear plants' worth. Sounds absurd, right? Except look who filed before them. SpaceX wants up to a million compute satellites. And Starcloud, backed by Nvidia, already has an AI chip running in orbit right now. It trained the first AI model in space last December.

Why space? In the right orbit, the sun never sets, so a solar panel earns up to eight times more than on Earth. And the vacuum cools everything for free. No water. No cooling towers.

The catch is the rocket ride. This only works when launch costs drop to around two hundred dollars a kilogram, and that needs Starship flying like an airline.

So watch the paperwork, not the hardware. Today, data centers fight for land and power. Tomorrow, they fight for spots in the sky. And the winners are filing their claims right now.
