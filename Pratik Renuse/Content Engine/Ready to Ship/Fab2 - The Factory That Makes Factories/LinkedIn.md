A company that has never sold a chip just raised 500 million dollars to sell chip factories instead.

..

The hard part of building a new processor was never the design. Plenty of people can design one. The hard part is finding somewhere to make it.

Only a handful of places on Earth can fabricate advanced silicon, and all of them are full. So a small team with an unusual idea does not get a slot, the unusual idea never gets made, and the industry quietly settles on the same few designs.

Fab2 is going after that, and the approach is genuinely odd. Instead of building one enormous factory, it builds small ones and then mass-produces them. A factory that makes factories.

It designs and builds every machine inside itself, from the pumps and valves and gas lines up to the lithography and the vacuum chambers that hold it. Then it assembles the machines into a fab, and copies the fab.

..

The round closed on 9 September at a 3.7 billion dollar valuation, the first outside money the company has ever disclosed. Three years ago it was worth around 100 million.

The founders are Jim Keller, who designed AMD's Zen and the chips in the early iPhones, and Sam Zeloof, who made working chips in his parents' garage while he was still at school. The garage is not a fun fact. It is the entire argument: a fab can be small.

..

The catch is real and worth saying. Their method draws each pattern directly with a beam of electrons rather than projecting it through a mask, and that is slow. Hours per chip, not wafers per minute. Today it suits prototypes, not volume. Nothing here competes with TSMC.

A few miles away in Austin, Tesla is building the opposite answer, a single megafab that could cost up to 119 billion dollars. One giant building against thousands of small ones.

..

There has only ever been one answer to a chip shortage, and it is build more fab. Which quietly puts the machines that build fabs underneath every bottleneck anyone has ever complained about.

Today the scarce thing is a slot in someone else's factory. Tomorrow it might be the factory itself, delivered on a truck.
