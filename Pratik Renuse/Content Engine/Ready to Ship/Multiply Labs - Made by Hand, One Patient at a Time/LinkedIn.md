The most advanced cancer treatments on earth are made by hand, one patient at a time, by a person standing in a clean room.

..

A normal pill gets stamped out by the million from a single batch, which is why it costs pennies.

A cell therapy cannot work that way. They take your own cells out, modify them, grow them for weeks, check them, and put them back into you. Every batch is a batch of one, and it is assembled by highly trained people doing delicate work slowly.

That is a large part of why these treatments carry price tags in the hundreds of thousands, and why some patients wait for supply rather than for science.

..

Multiply Labs raised 75 million dollars on 17 September to hand that work to robots. It has been at this for ten years, which is unusual in a field full of two year old companies.

The clever call is what it did not do. The robots operate the pharmaceutical equipment that is already installed, rather than replacing the factory. In an industry where changing anything triggers a fresh round of regulatory approval, that choice is most of the business case.

Its own figures, and they are its own: 74 percent off the cost per dose, and up to a hundred times the throughput of people doing it manually.

Two of the cheques came from AstraZeneca's venture arm and from Legend Biotech, which makes one of the few cell therapies actually selling today. The customers funded the supplier, which usually means the pilot is real.

..

Most of the attention in this field goes to AI designing medicines faster, and it genuinely is getting faster.

But a drug you can design in a month and then build by hand in six weeks per person is a six week drug. The wait does not disappear. It relocates, into the clean room. It is the same trick this industry keeps pulling: solve the famous problem and the bill moves to the dull one underneath it.

Which means the molecule stops being the expensive part of medicine. Twenty years from now the search is close to free, and the money sits with whoever owns the machine that builds your dose.
