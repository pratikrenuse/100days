# Video Script (HeyGen) -- adapter (LOCKED)

Turns one Story Brief into a talking-head script your HeyGen avatar reads. Same story and same voice as the LinkedIn post, reshaped for the ear. Also the voiceover base for future Reels and Shorts.

Built from the two gold-standard stories, GE Vernova and Astera Labs. Both finished scripts are embedded below. Copy the beats and the spoken rhythm, never the sentences.

---

## The one production rule that changes everything

**HeyGen reads aloud every word you give it.** So the script must be *pure spoken words*. Never put stage directions, stat cards, [brackets], overlays, or delivery notes inside the file, the avatar would read them out.

**Deliver the script and nothing else.** Just a title line and the spoken words. No on-screen overlay list, no delivery notes, no runtime block, no "note" section. Pratik pastes the file straight into HeyGen. Keep it clean.

---

## How a spoken script differs from a post (shape changes, voice does not)
- **Written for the ear.** Shorter sentences. No bullet lists read aloud. Numbers spoken naturally ("nearly ten times", not "10x"; "over 700 billion dollars", not "$725B").
- **A cold-open first line** that stops the scroll in two seconds. Often the hook plus its answer right away ("Everyone thinks the AI race is about chips. It's not.").
- **Pace ~150 words per minute.** So ~100 words is about 40 seconds. Note the target runtime at the top.
- **Be human, out loud.** Spoken video wants MORE warmth than a post. Sprinkle real reactions and everyday asides where the surprises land: "Yeah, a gas turbine.", "Cables!", "But get this.", "And you know what?", "Now here's the wild part.", "you basically can't buy one." They make the avatar sound like a person, not a newsreader. Still don't put one on every line, but lean warmer than the LinkedIn post.
- **End on the shift close.** No "follow" tag needed unless it fits.

## The beats (same eight as every channel)
1. Cold-open hook (surprise, spoken, answered fast)
2. Reaction + the promise ("so I went digging")
3. The plain explanation (analogy if abstract, spoken as a mini-story)
4. The hidden player, named once
5. The one or two strongest numbers, spoken, not listed
6. The realization ("we all think X, nope, it's Y")
7. The concrete forecast (real 2030 number or named player)
8. The shift close (today X, tomorrow Y, the pattern) + soft follow line

## Same hard rules as every channel
Write for a normal person, zero jargon, no em dashes, no emojis, say each point once, real reactions used sparingly. If it doesn't sound like the GE post read aloud, it's wrong.

**Flow, don't announce.** Same rule as the LinkedIn master template. Don't start each part with a stock announcement ("here's the thing", "that's when it hit me", "here's where it gets strange", "the thought I can't shake"). One such signal in a whole script is plenty; let the rest flow from the line before as a consequence or a continuation. A reaction inside a beat ("Cables!") is fine; an announcement that opens a beat is not.

## Fixed beats, fresh words (read this before every script)
The eight beats never change. The exact words must change every single time. **Never reuse a signature line across videos.** If GE closes with "So here's my bet," Astera must not. If one opens with "So this week I went digging," the next opens differently. A viewer who sees two of your videos should never feel they're watching a template with the nouns swapped.

Rotate these especially, they are the ones that go stale fastest:
- **The hook / "I went looking" line:** "So this week I went digging into..." / "So I went looking for..." / "So I spent a day chasing down..." / "It made me curious, so I dug in."
- **The reaction:** "Wild, right?" / "Cables!" / "I had to know why." / "That stopped me."
- **The realization:** "That's when it hit me..." / "That's when it clicked..." / "And here's the twist..."
- **The forecast opener:** "Now here's the future." / "And it only gets bigger." / "Here's where it goes next."
- **The close:** "So here's my bet..." / "So here's where it's heading..." / "So here's what I think happens..." / "Mark this down..." Then land the shift (today X, tomorrow Y) and the pattern in fresh words too.

The two examples below deliberately share zero signature lines. Copy the beats, never the sentences.

## Length (three cuts per story)
Write the ~60s first (it's the full story), then trim down for the shorter platforms. Pace is ~150 words per minute.

- **~60s, ~150 to 160 words (default):** the full cut. Personal "went digging" hook, full mechanism or analogy, every number, and the 2030 forecast. Best for LinkedIn-native video and YouTube Shorts, where slightly longer is tolerated and substance wins.
- **~45s, ~115 words (dense short):** drop the hook flourish and the forecast, compress the mechanism. Keep the number burst. Best all-rounder for Reels.
- **~40s, ~100 words (leanest):** also drop one data point. Best for TikTok and fast-scroll Reels.

**Pack it with data, not filler.** Group the proof into one confident run: three or four dated figures back to back (GE: sold out to 2029, buyers pay triple, stock ~10x now ~$300B, then the $725B kicker). That burst is what makes a short script feel substantial. The completion-rate sweet spot across platforms is 30 to 45s, so treat 60s as the ceiling, reserved for the platforms that reward depth.

## Worked example 1: GE Vernova

> Everyone thinks the AI race is about chips. It's not.
>
> So this week, I went digging into what's really slowing it down. And you know what it turned out to be? A gas turbine. Yeah, a gas turbine. And right now, you basically can't buy one.
>
> See, every AI data center is a hungry machine. It needs huge, steady power the second you turn it on. And solar? Stops at night. Nuclear? Ten years to build. So for now, it runs on gas, and only a few companies on Earth can build those turbines.
>
> The biggest is GE Vernova, and barely anyone knows the name. But get this. It's sold out until 2029. Some buyers pay triple just to hold a spot. And its stock? Up nearly ten times, now worth 300 billion dollars. Meanwhile, Big Tech will spend 725 billion on AI this year, and none of it buys a turbine any faster.
>
> So here's my bet. This race was never about the smartest model. It's about electricity. Today, the winner makes gas turbines. Tomorrow? Whoever cracks fusion.

The reactions ("Yeah, a gas turbine.", "But get this.", "And its stock?") are what keep the numbers from feeling like a report. Full file: `Ready to Ship/GE Vernova - Power is the Ceiling/HeyGen script.md`

## Worked example 2: Astera Labs

> Ask anyone about AI, you think of Nvidia. But this week, I went looking for the winner nobody talks about. And it's a weird one. It doesn't make chips. It makes cables. Cables!
>
> So here's the thing. An AI is really thousands of chips. And you know what? It works a lot like your brain. What matters is how fast they all talk to each other. Right now, they talk through copper wires. But AI got so hungry for data that copper just can't keep up.
>
> So the real winner? It's not the chip. It's the wire. And almost nobody builds them like Astera Labs. Barely anyone knows it. But get this. This June, it joined the Nasdaq-100. Its sales jumped 93% in a year. And its market is racing toward 20 billion dollars.
>
> Now here's the wild part. Today these machines link tens of thousands of chips. Soon? Millions. And copper just gives up. So the wires switch to light, real lasers firing data between the chips. By 2030, a third could run on light, and a startup called Ayar Labs is already building it.
>
> So here's where it's heading. Today, the race is won with cables. Tomorrow, with light. Because the real winners? They always hide in the boring stuff.

Notice the close opens differently from GE's "So here's my bet" on purpose. Same beat, fresh words. And it stays warm ("Cables!", "And you know what?", "But get this.", "Now here's the wild part.") while still carrying every number and a concrete, named future. Full file: `Ready to Ship/Astera Labs - The Bottleneck Slides Down/HeyGen script.md`
