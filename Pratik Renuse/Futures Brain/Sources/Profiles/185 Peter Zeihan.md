## Who they are
Geopolitical strategist and founder of the consulting firm Zeihan on Geopolitics (founded 2012), previously a vice president at the intelligence firm Stratfor. He analyzes the world through the lens of geography, demographics, and energy, and is a New York Times bestselling author known for blunt, contrarian forecasts.

## Their future thesis (one line)
The US-secured era of globalization is ending, and as American security guarantees recede, an aging and shrinking global population plus broken supply chains will trigger deglobalization, regional fragmentation, and the relative decline of export- and import-dependent powers, especially China.

## Key future-relevant pieces
- "The End of the World Is Just the Beginning: Mapping the Collapse of Globalization" (2022), https://zeihan.com/the-end-of-the-world-is-just-the-beginning/
  Argues that globalization was an artificial, US-underwritten order that is now unwinding, and that the next decades bring deglobalization, broken supply chains, and falling living standards in countries dependent on trade. Pairs this with a demographic collapse argument: much of the world is aging past the point of recovery, gutting consumption and labor forces.

- "The Accidental Superpower: Ten Years On" (2024 updated edition; original 2014), https://zeihan.com/tas/
  Lays out his geographic case that the US, with navigable rivers, arable land, ocean buffers, and favorable demographics, is uniquely positioned to thrive even as the global order frays. The tenth-anniversary edition revisits his original predictions about American advantage and global disorder.

- "Disunited Nations: The Scramble for Power in an Ungoverned World" (2020), https://zeihan.com/disunited-nations/
  Forecasts a world without a global policeman, where regional powers compete and former great powers (China, Germany, Russia, Saudi Arabia) face structural decline. It maps which countries are positioned to win or lose as the international system fragments.

- "Zeihan on Geopolitics" newsletter and daily video analysis (ongoing), https://zeihan.com/newsletter/
  His running commentary applies the geography-demographics-energy framework to live events such as the Ukraine war, shipping disruptions, and energy markets. This is where he updates and stress-tests his deglobalization thesis in near real time, including his framing of the mid-2020s as the final phase of globalization.
