## Who they are
Philosopher, founder and principal researcher of the nonprofit Macrostrategy Research Initiative. Previously professor and founding director of Oxford's Future of Humanity Institute (2005 to 2024, now closed). The thinker who put AI existential risk and the simulation argument on the academic map.

## Their future thesis (one line)
Machine superintelligence is the pivotal event of this century: getting the control and alignment problem right is the central challenge, but if we succeed, the harder question becomes how humans find meaning in a "solved world" of radical abundance (deep utopia).

## Key future-relevant pieces
- "Superintelligence: Paths, Dangers, Strategies" (2014), https://www.amazon.com/gp/product/0198739834
  The book that mainstreamed AI existential risk: it analyzes how an AI could undergo an "intelligence explosion," why a superintelligence might be hard to control, and why a misaligned goal (the paperclip problem) could be catastrophic. It frames alignment and control as humanity's most important unsolved problem. Hugely influential on the entire AI-safety field and on figures like Musk and Tegmark.
- "Deep Utopia: Life and Meaning in a Solved World" (2024), https://nickbostrom.com/deep-utopia/
  The optimistic counterpart to Superintelligence: it asks what human life is for if AI solves work, scarcity, and disease, so that almost nothing is left for us to do out of necessity. He explores boredom, purpose, and value in a "post-instrumental" world. A rare serious philosophical treatment of success rather than catastrophe.
- "Optimal Timing for Superintelligence: Mundane Considerations for Existing People" (working paper, v1.0, 2026), https://nickbostrom.com/optimal.pdf
  Argues that even with a fairly high probability of doom, currently living people might have a higher life expectancy if AGI arrives sooner, because near-term AI could cure aging and disease for people alive now. It reframes the timing debate around the self-interest of the present generation, not just abstract risk. A provocative, very current intervention. (PDF working paper.)

## Hooks for the brain
- The paperclip maximizer and "intelligence explosion" are the canonical AI-risk metaphors; Bostrom is their source.
- "Deep Utopia" flips the usual doom frame: the underexplored question of what we do with ourselves if everything goes right.
- His 2026 timing paper is genuinely contrarian within his own camp: a risk theorist arguing faster AGI could be in living people's self-interest.
- Hidden player turned ghost: FHI is now closed, so a foundational risk institution dissolved even as its ideas went mainstream, a story in itself.
