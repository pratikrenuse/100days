## Who they are
Co-founder of Y Combinator and one of the most influential essayists in startups and technology. Now writes full-time on paulgraham.com about ambition, writing, ideas, and how technology reshapes work and society.

## Their future thesis (one line)
Returns to talent and effort are becoming superlinear in a technology-driven world, so the future rewards a small number of exceptionally ambitious, independent-minded builders, while AI threatens to split society into those who still write (and therefore think) and those who do not.

## Key future-relevant pieces
- "Writes and Write-Nots" (Oct 2024), https://www.paulgraham.com/writes.html
  Argues that because AI can now write competently, most people will stop writing entirely, and since writing is thinking, society will divide into a small group who can still think clearly and a large group who cannot. A pointed, contrarian warning about a cognitive side effect of AI adoption. Short and widely debated.

- "Superlinear Returns" (Oct 2023), https://www.paulgraham.com/superlinear.html
  His core thesis for the modern era: outcomes increasingly scale superlinearly with performance (winner-take-most dynamics from thresholds and exponential growth). Argues this changes optimal life strategy toward seeking work with uncapped upside. A key lens for understanding inequality, ambition, and tech-driven concentration over the next decades.

- "How to Do Great Work" (Jul 2023), https://www.paulgraham.com/greatwork.html
  A long synthesis of how breakthrough work actually happens: curiosity, choosing the right problems, and consistency at the frontier of a field. Future-relevant as a manual for the small number of people who will drive outsized progress. His most comprehensive single essay.

- "Founder Mode" (Sep 2024), https://www.paulgraham.com/foundermode.html
  Argues that founders should run their companies differently from professional managers, staying deeply involved rather than fully delegating, and that conventional "manager mode" advice can wreck great companies. Sparked a major debate in tech. Relevant to how the most consequential companies will be led.

- "How People Get Rich Now" (Apr 2021), https://www.paulgraham.com/richnow.html
  Documents the shift from inherited and corporate wealth to wealth created by founding fast-growing tech companies, and argues this trend is accelerating. A data-grounded read on where economic power is concentrating. Pairs naturally with "Superlinear Returns."

## Hooks for the brain
- "Writes and Write-Nots" is a sharp, slightly alarming AI take that travels well: the warning that outsourcing writing to AI quietly erodes the ability to think. Strong contrarian angle for a content or thinking-tools product.
- "Superlinear Returns" is the intellectual backbone for almost every "winner-take-all future" argument. Cite it whenever explaining tech-driven concentration of wealth and talent.
- His essays are timeless and load-bearing in startup culture, so they are reliable "canon" to anchor futures arguments that need credibility with a founder audience.
- Hidden tension worth exploring: PG's superlinear, individual-genius optimism sits in interesting contrast with macro thinkers (Lyn Alden, Noah Smith) who focus on systemic and policy forces. Good for a "great individuals vs big systems" piece.
- The essays index is plain HTML and stable: an easy, high-signal corpus to mine for a thinking or writing product.
