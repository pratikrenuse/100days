## Who they are
AI grantmaker and forecaster at Open Philanthropy, where she funds technical AI alignment and safety research, and author of the influential "biological anchors" report estimating when transformative AI will arrive. She writes the newsletter Planned Obsolescence about a future where AI runs most decisions.

## Their future thesis (one line)
Within a couple of decades, AI systems will become capable enough to handle most R&D, strategy, and even self-replication, ushering in an "obsolescence regime" where competition no longer runs on human timescales and AI takeover becomes the central risk to manage.

## Key future-relevant pieces
- "Self-sufficient AI" (6 January 2026), https://www.planned-obsolescence.org/p/self-sufficient-ai
  Proposes a sharper forecasting milestone than the fuzzy term AGI: a self-sufficient AI population that could keep building and replicating itself even if every human died. She argues this target is mechanically linked to the most extreme takeover risk, since misaligned AI would need self-sufficiency before it could safely wipe out humanity. She estimates such a population is more likely than not within ten years.

- "What we're doing here" (founding essay), https://www.planned-obsolescence.org/p/what-were-doing-here
  Lays out the premise of the newsletter and the concept of the "obsolescence regime," a near-future world where AI conducts most research, CEOs defer to AI consultants, commanders defer to AI strategists, and leaders lean on AI advisors. It frames the alignment problem and possible AI takeover as the most important and least understood challenge ahead. This is the conceptual anchor for her public writing.

- "Draft report on AI timelines" (September 2020, foundational), https://www.alignmentforum.org/posts/KrJfoZzpSDpnrv9va/draft-report-on-ai-timelines
  Her landmark "biological anchors" analysis, which estimates the compute needed to train transformative AI by anchoring to estimates of the computation used by the human brain and evolution. It became one of the most cited quantitative frameworks for AI timelines. Her own takeaways pointed to a meaningful chance of transformative AI within decades and a median around mid-century, since revised earlier as capabilities advanced.

- "I underestimated AI capabilities (again)" (Planned Obsolescence), https://www.planned-obsolescence.org/p/i-underestimated-ai-capabilities
  A reflective update in which she acknowledges that real-world AI progress has repeatedly outpaced her earlier forecasts. It argues for shortening timelines and taking near-term transformative scenarios more seriously. The piece models the kind of public forecast-updating she advocates.
