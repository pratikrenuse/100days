## Who they are
Writer and economist; Mercers' School Memorial Professor of Business at Gresham College, Research Professor at King's College London, Digital Fellow at the Stanford Digital Economy Lab, and Senior Research Associate at Oxford's Institute for Ethics in AI. A member of the UK Government's Expert Panel on AI and the Future of Work, he is one of the most widely read public thinkers on technology and the future of jobs.

## Their future thesis (one line)
Over the long run, advancing AI will steadily encroach on tasks once thought uniquely human, so societies must prepare for a world with far less paid work, rethinking how people earn income, find meaning, and distribute the wealth that machines create.

## Key future-relevant pieces
- "A World Without Work" (2020), https://www.danielsusskind.com/books/
  Argues that technological unemployment, long dismissed by economists, is becoming a real long-term prospect as machines take on ever more tasks. The book reframes the central challenges of a low-work future as three problems: inequality (how to share prosperity), political power (concentrated in those who own the technology), and meaning (purpose beyond a job).

- "Growth: A Reckoning" (2024), https://www.danielsusskind.com/growth-a-reckoning
  Examines how two centuries of economic growth lifted billions out of poverty but also drove inequality, instability, and environmental harm, and argues we must redirect growth rather than abandon it. An Obama "Favourite Books of 2024" pick and FT Business Book of the Year runner-up, it reframes what progress should mean as AI accelerates output.

- "What Should My Children Do? How to Flourish in the Age of AI" (forthcoming 2026), https://www.danielsusskind.com/books/
  A forthcoming book turning his future-of-work analysis toward education and human development: what skills, dispositions, and choices help people thrive when AI is pervasive. It signals his shift from diagnosing disruption to advising how individuals and families should adapt.

- "The Future of the Professions" (2015, with Richard Susskind), https://www.danielsusskind.com/books/
  Argues that AI and automation will dismantle the traditional gatekeeping model of doctors, lawyers, teachers, and other professionals, redistributing their expertise more widely. An early and influential map of how knowledge work itself gets transformed, prefiguring today's generative-AI debates.

- TED Talk: "3 myths about the future of work (and why they're not true)" (2017/2020), https://www.danielsusskind.com/video
  A widely viewed talk arguing that past reassurances about technology always creating enough new jobs may not hold this time. It is his most accessible statement of why the future-of-work problem could be different in degree and kind.
