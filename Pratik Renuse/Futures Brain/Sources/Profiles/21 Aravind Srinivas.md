## Who they are
Co-founder and CEO of Perplexity AI, the "answer engine" (valued around $20B, backed by Bezos, NVIDIA, and Databricks). PhD from UC Berkeley; previously at Google Brain, DeepMind, and OpenAI. Became India's youngest billionaire in 2025. Building Comet (an AI-first browser) and Computer (a cloud agent product).

## Their future thesis (one line)
The internet will be re-architected around answers instead of links, search becomes multi-agent (you give a goal and a swarm of agents executes it), and in an AI-flooded web trustworthy, accurate sources become the scarce, decisive asset.

## Key future-relevant pieces
- "Perplexity's Aravind Srinivas on the Infinite Value of Knowledge" (Stanford GSB), https://www.gsb.stanford.edu/insights/perplexitys-aravind-srinivas-infinite-value-knowledge
  Srinivas argues for a "fundamental re-architecture of the internet around giving people answers instead of links," with trust and source accuracy as the core differentiator in a world of cheap fake content. He frames knowledge access as nearly infinitely valuable. The clearest statement of his future-of-search thesis.

- Comet AI browser launch and free global release (July and October 2025), https://techcrunch.com/2025/07/09/perplexity-launches-comet-an-ai-powered-web-browser/
  Comet, a Chromium-based AI-first browser that can search, manage tabs, draft emails, and automate tasks, launched in mid-2025 and went free worldwide that October. It embodies his bet that the browser itself becomes an agent, not just a window. Key artifact for the "agentic browser replaces search" direction.

- "Perplexity CEO explains Computer, its agent tool for non-experts" (Fortune, February 2026), https://fortune.com/2026/02/26/perplexity-ceo-aravind-srinivas-computer-openclaw-ai-agent/
  Launch of Computer, a cloud-based agent meant to make powerful but intimidating agent tools usable by non-experts, running long-horizon tasks (research, writing, coding) without taking control of a personal device. It reflects his view that agents become a shared digital workspace. Useful for the mass-adoption-of-agents angle.

- "One metric will determine who wins the AI race" (CNBC, June 2026), https://www.cnbc.com/2026/06/03/perplexity-ceo-ai-valuations-computer-agentic.html
  Srinivas argues a single usage/engagement metric, not valuation, will decide the agentic-AI race, and frames multi-agent search (e.g. "Plan my trip to Tokyo" handled by cooperating flight and hotel agents) as the near-term product future. A concise read on how he thinks competition resolves.

## Hooks for the brain
- "Answers, not links" is a clean thesis for the disruption-of-search angle and the future of how people access knowledge.
- Multi-agent search (swarms of cooperating agents per query) is a concrete near-term mechanism, vivid for a post.
- Hidden-player angle: trust and source provenance as the scarce asset in an AI-generated web, which flips the usual "more content" narrative.
- The browser-as-agent move (Comet) is an early front in the platform war against Google/Chrome, worth tracking over 10 years.
