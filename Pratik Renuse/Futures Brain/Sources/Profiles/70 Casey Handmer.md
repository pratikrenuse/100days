## Who they are
Founder and CEO of Terraform Industries, a startup doing gigascale atmospheric hydrocarbon synthesis (making cheap natural gas and fuels from sunlight, air, and water). PhD in theoretical physics from Caltech, former software and systems engineer at NASA JPL. One of the loudest technically literate voices on the solar plus batteries plus synthetics future.

## Their future thesis (one line)
Solar is getting so cheap so fast that within a couple of decades humanity reaches effectively unlimited cheap energy (Kardashev Level 1), and the winning move is to use that superabundant power to synthesize hydrocarbons and fuels rather than mine them.

## Key future-relevant pieces
- "Future of Energy Reading List" (Oct 2023, updated Jan 2026), https://caseyhandmer.wordpress.com/2023/10/19/future-of-energy-reading-list/
  His pinned thesis post and best entry point. The TL;DR is blunt: the future of energy is solar plus batteries plus synthetics, solar is deploying at roughly a megawatt per minute globally, and most of the transition is locked in. It links out to his deeper essays by topic.

- "Energy Predictions 2025" (Dec 2025), https://caseyhandmer.wordpress.com/2025/12/08/energy-predictions-2025/
  A broad state-of-play update on where energy is heading, written as a single readable reference with a printable PDF. It lays out his near-term forecasts for solar, storage, and synthetic fuels and where he thinks builders should focus.

- "Direct Current Data Centers" (Jan 2026), https://caseyhandmer.wordpress.com/2026/01/30/direct-current-data-centers/
  Co-written with Matt Weickert, originally at the Terraform blog. Argues that pure solar plus batteries is the cheapest path to powering the AI data center buildout, and connects that to his larger claim about reaching Kardashev Level 1 by harvesting a planet's worth of solar energy.

- "The enormous size of the oil and gas market drives adoption of synthetic fuel production" (Jun 2026), https://caseyhandmer.wordpress.com/2026/06/18/the-enormous-size-of-the-oil-and-gas-market-drives-adoption-of-synthetic-fuel-production/
  His market thesis for Terraform. Because the hydrocarbon market is so vast, every 1 percent cut in at-scale synthetic production cost opens up multi-billion-dollar slices of the methane and methanol markets, which he uses to argue synthetic fuels scale on economics alone, not subsidies.

## Hooks for the brain
- Handmer is the builder-optimist archetype: not just forecasting cheap solar but betting a company on what to do with it. Good source for "what does superabundant energy actually get used for" questions.
- He overlaps heavily with Ramez Naam (they did a joint talk, "How AI Changes Energy") and with Tony Seba's SWB thesis. The three of them form a cluster; Smil is the foil.
- Hidden player: Terraform Industries itself, plus the broader synthetic-fuels and direct-air-capture-for-fuel space, as a wedge on oil demand.
- Post idea: "If energy becomes nearly free, the bottleneck moves to molecules." Synthetic hydrocarbons, not batteries, may be the real story of the 2030s.
- Note: his blog is wide-ranging (space, Mars, antimatter, parenting). Filter for the energy and Terraform posts when mining for futures content.
