## Who they are
John Schulman is co-founder and Chief Scientist of Thinking Machines Lab, Mira Murati's AI startup, a role he has held since early 2025 and still holds in 2026. He co-founded OpenAI, co-invented PPO, led the reinforcement learning and post-training work behind ChatGPT, and is often called a primary architect of RLHF as applied to large language models. After a brief 2024 to 2025 stint at Anthropic, he joined Thinking Machines, where he has remained as Chief Scientist even as several co-founders departed in late 2025 and January 2026.

## Their future thesis (one line)
Capable AI will arrive through steady, incremental scaling of post-training and long-horizon reinforcement learning rather than one sudden jump, so the priority is to keep humans as the drivers and to slow down if any release looks unsafe.

## Key future-relevant pieces
- "John Schulman on dead ends, scaling RL, and building research institutions" (Dec 17, 2025), https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=29BYxvvF1iM
  In this Cursor podcast, his most recent long-form appearance, Schulman lays out where reinforcement learning goes next: the open problems of continual learning, brittle generalization, the absence of good value functions, and co-training generators with verifiers. He also reflects on how to build durable research institutions, drawing on the Bell Labs comparison and OpenAI's early culture, and gives his read on AGI forecasting and whether frontier labs would coordinate if the world needed them to. It is the clearest current window into how he thinks the next phase of capability will actually be earned.

- "John Schulman (OpenAI Cofounder), Reasoning, RLHF, and plan for 2027 AGI" (May 15, 2024), https://www.dwarkesh.com/p/john-schulman
  In this Dwarkesh Patel interview, Schulman walks through pre-training, post-training, and the path to models that can carry out whole multi-step projects rather than single suggestions. He argues progress on long-horizon tasks could go "quite far" but resists a single-unlock narrative, gives a roughly two-to-five year window for transformative capability, and stresses that if AGI arrived early, labs should be cautious and ready to slow training and deployment. His preferred future is continuous, safety-checked releases that keep people in control rather than a discontinuous leap.

- "Proximal Policy Optimization Algorithms" (Jul 20, 2017), https://arxiv.org/abs/1707.06347
  This is the foundational paper, with Schulman as lead author, that introduced PPO, a policy gradient method that is simpler and more sample-efficient than its predecessor TRPO. PPO became the default optimization workhorse for RLHF and therefore underpins the alignment fine-tuning behind ChatGPT and most modern instruction-tuned models. It is essential context for why Schulman is treated as a central figure in how today's frontier systems are actually trained.

- "TalkRL: John Schulman" (podcast interview), https://www.talkrl.com/episodes/john-schulman
  A reinforcement learning focused conversation in which Schulman discusses his research philosophy, RL fundamentals, and how the field was developing. It is useful as a technical-grounding companion to the higher-level future pieces above, showing the worldview that later shaped his views on scaling RL for language models. ## Hooks for the brain
- Feeds the "incremental vs discontinuous takeoff" thesis: Schulman is a credible insider voice arguing for gradual, scaling-driven progress with human override, useful as a counterweight to hard-takeoff and fast-singularity narratives.
- Feeds the "RL and post-training are the next frontier" thesis: his 2025 Cursor talk maps the specific unsolved problems (continual learning, brittle generalization, verifiers) that gate the next 10 to 20 years of capability, good for forecasting where compute and research effort go next.
- Feeds the "who builds frontier AI and how labs behave" thesis: as Chief Scientist at a new well-funded lab spun out of OpenAI, plus his comments on lab coordination and institution-building, he is a data point on the fragmentation and governance dynamics of the frontier AI industry.
- Use his timelines cautiously: his "two to five years" framing dates from May 2024, so treat it as a 2024 anchor and update against his more recent 2025 statements.
