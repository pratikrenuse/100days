## Who they are
Economist at George Mason University and former Future of Humanity Institute associate, known for prediction markets, the "Age of Em" forecast, and contrarian takes on hidden human motives. As of 2026 he writes Overcoming Bias on Substack and is the leading skeptic of fast-takeoff AI doom.

## Their future thesis (one line)
The big future shift is an economic growth explosion most likely driven by AI or brain emulations, but it will probably arrive as a broad, decade-long, competitive transition rather than a single rogue system suddenly "fooming" to take over.

## Key future-relevant pieces
- "The Age of Em: Work, Love, and Life When Robots Rule the Earth" (2016 book), https://ageofem.com/
  His detailed forecast of a future where human brains are scanned and emulated as software ("ems") before de novo AI is solved, producing trillions of copyable digital minds that quickly outnumber humans and run civilization at vastly faster subjective speeds. He reasons from standard economics, physics, and social science to describe their labor markets, cities, and culture. It is the most rigorous single attempt to picture a post-human-labor economy in concrete detail.

- "AI Risk, Again" (Mar 3, 2023), https://www.overcomingbias.com/p/ai-risk-again
  Written right after ChatGPT's surge, this restates his core disagreement with doom worriers: history shows innovations stay small relative to the world economy, so a single lab suddenly exploding to dominate everything is far less likely than a gradual, multi-actor transition. He argues control problems are real but best solved with concrete fielded systems, not pre-emptive regulation that could freeze progress. The clearest short statement of his anti-FOOM position.

- "Most AI Fear Is Future Fear" (2023), https://www.overcomingbias.com/p/ai-fear-is-mostly-fear-of-future
  Argues that much current AI anxiety is really displaced unease about rapid change in general, recurring roughly every three decades since the 1930s whenever automation demos impress people. He uses the historical pattern of false automation panics to discount the latest wave of extinction worry. A useful counterweight framing for any "is this time different" debate.

- "Grabby Aliens" model and updates (ongoing; e.g. "Kipping on Grabby Aliens", Sep 2024), https://www.overcomingbias.com/p/kipping-on-grabby-aliens
  His three-parameter cosmological model explains why we see no alien civilizations, why humanity appears early in cosmic history, and what that implies about expansionist ("grabby") civilizations spreading at near-light speed. The framework bears on humanity's long-run trajectory and whether we are destined to expand or be filtered. The Kipping post defends the model against a notable astrophysics critique.

## Hooks for the brain
- Hanson is the designated skeptic: pairing his anti-FOOM, gradual-transition view against Alexander, Ord, and AI 2027 gives any piece a real intellectual tension instead of one-sided doom.
- "Age of Em" is the hidden player in futures discourse: a fully worked-out post-labor economy that almost nobody references when debating UBI and job loss, despite being the most concrete model of it.
- His "future fear" lens (panics recur every ~30 years) is a sharp, contrarian hook for marketers tired of hype cycles.
