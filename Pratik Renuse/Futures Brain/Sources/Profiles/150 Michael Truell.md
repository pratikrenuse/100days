## Who they are
Co-founder and CEO of Anysphere, the company behind Cursor, the AI code editor that became one of the fastest growing software startups ever (past 1 billion dollars in annualized revenue by late 2025). An MIT computer science dropout, he is in his mid twenties; as of June 2026 he still runs Cursor while SpaceX has agreed to acquire Anysphere for 60 billion dollars in an all stock deal announced June 16, 2026 and expected to close in Q3 2026.

## Their future thesis (one line)
Software is moving past hand written code: engineers become directors of fleets of autonomous AI agents, and human value shifts from typing syntax to taste, intent, and judgment about what to build.

## Key future-relevant pieces
- "The third era of AI software development" (Feb 26, 2026), https://cursor.com/blog/third-era
  Truell frames AI coding in three eras: Tab autocomplete first, then synchronous prompt and response agents, and now a third era of cloud agents that run on their own machines and tackle large tasks over hours with little supervision. He argues Cursor is no longer mainly about writing code but about helping developers build a "factory" of agent teammates, citing that over a third of Cursor's own merged pull requests are already produced by autonomous agents, and predicts most development work will be done this way within a year.

- "The rise of Cursor: The $300M ARR AI tool that engineers can't stop using" (May 1, 2025), https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/p/the-rise-of-cursor-michael-truell
  In this Lenny's Podcast interview Truell lays out his "what comes after code" vision, where programming shifts from rigid syntax toward natural language and pseudocode while keeping precision for professionals. He argues that taste and logic design will matter more than raw coding ability, that engineers increasingly act like managers specifying intent, and counterintuitively that demand for engineers grows rather than shrinks as AI lowers the cost of building software.

- "Cursor Team: Future of Programming with AI" Lex Fridman Podcast #447 (Oct 6, 2024), https://lexfridman.com/cursor-team-transcript/
  Truell and his three Cursor co-founders give a long form account of how AI is reshaping the programming experience, describing a hybrid where the human and the model collaborate fluidly and the editor anticipates whole coherent changes rather than single characters. They sketch a near future where the bottleneck moves from writing code to specifying and verifying intent, and discuss the engineering and research bets behind making AI assisted coding fast and trustworthy.

- "Why Cursor's CEO believes OpenAI, Anthropic competition won't crush his startup" (Dec 9, 2025), https://techcrunch.com/2025/12/09/why-cursors-ceo-believes-openai-anthropic-competition-wont-crush-his-startup/
  Reporting on Truell's remarks at Fortune's AI Brainstorm, this piece captures his view that the model labs ship "concept cars" while Cursor ships the finished production vehicle by integrating the best models with the best end to end product. He signals where he thinks the work is heading: agents that handle end to end tasks (like a bug fix that would take a human weeks), pricing that shifts toward consumption as AI does hours of work, and serving whole engineering teams rather than individual coders.
