## Who they are
Australian moral philosopher and senior researcher at Oxford (associated with the former Future of Humanity Institute and AI governance work), author of "The Precipice." As of 2025-2026 his focus has shifted toward how AI scaling trends reshape governance and existential risk.

## Their future thesis (one line)
Humanity faces a roughly one-in-six chance of existential catastrophe this century, with unaligned AI the single largest contributor, so safeguarding our long-term potential is the defining moral task of our era.

## Key future-relevant pieces
- "The Precipice: Existential Risk and the Future of Humanity" (2020 book), https://theprecipice.com/
  His landmark book argues humanity has the power to destroy itself but not yet the wisdom to ensure it won't, estimating about a 1-in-6 chance of existential catastrophe within the next century. He puts unaligned artificial general intelligence at roughly 1-in-10, higher than all other risks combined, ahead of pandemics, nuclear war, and climate. The book frames protecting future generations as a moral priority comparable to abolishing slavery.

- "The Precipice Revisited" (Jun 2025), https://www.tobyord.com/writing/the-precipice-revisited
  Updates the risk picture roughly five years on, walking through how climate, nuclear, pandemic, and AI risk have each shifted. He highlights that AI went from a race between a few labs to a race between the largest companies on Earth, with nonprofit safety framing giving way to compute-hungry productization, while also noting genuine policy wins (national AI safety institutes, the Bletchley Declaration). A concise "what changed" companion to the book.

- "Inference Scaling Reshapes AI Governance" (Mar 2025), https://www.tobyord.com/writing/inference-scaling-reshapes-ai-governance
  Argues the frontier is shifting from ever-bigger pre-training runs to spending more compute at inference (test-time reasoning), which quietly breaks governance regimes keyed to training-compute thresholds like the EU AI Act and California SB 53. Consequences include fewer concurrent copies of top models, weights mattering less than compute access (reducing leak risk but also the leverage of open-sourcing), and an industry shifting toward higher marginal costs. A sharp, technical, and underappreciated governance argument.

- "The Scaling Series" / 80,000 Hours #219 (Oct 2025), https://80000hours.org/podcast/episodes/toby-ord-inference-scaling-ai-governance/
  In this interview and companion essays he unpacks "the graphs AI companies would prefer you didn't fully understand," showing how scaling curves and diminishing returns reframe both capability forecasts and policy. He connects the technical shape of progress to who holds power and how regulation should adapt. Useful as an accessible audio entry point to his 2025 thinking.

## Hooks for the brain
- The "1-in-6 this century" number is the most quotable existential-risk statistic in circulation, and the AI share (1-in-10) is the load-bearing piece for any AI-stakes post.
- His inference-scaling argument is a genuinely contrarian, underused angle: governance frameworks built around training compute may already be aimed at the wrong target.
- Ord bridges philosophy and policy: he is the rare ethicist quoted in actual government AI summits, making him a credibility anchor for "this is serious, not sci-fi" framing.
