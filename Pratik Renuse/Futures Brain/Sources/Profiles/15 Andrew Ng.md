## Who they are
Founder of DeepLearning.AI, co-founder of Coursera, founder of the AI Fund, and former head of Google Brain and Baidu AI Group. Through his weekly letters in The Batch he is one of the most widely read interpreters of where applied AI is heading.

## Their future thesis (one line)
The near future is agentic: AI agents and agentic workflows, not single prompts, are where the value is, and AI mostly raises demand for people who can use AI tools rather than causing broad direct job loss.

## Key future-relevant pieces
- "Letters from Andrew Ng" (weekly, ongoing), https://www.deeplearning.ai/the-batch/tag/letters/
  His standing column in The Batch, the single best running record of his views. Recent 2026 letters argue that prompting practice has changed dramatically since 2022, that coding agents accelerate different kinds of software work unevenly, and that readers should move from chatting with AI to having agents do real desktop work. Read chronologically, this is a live forecast of applied-AI direction.

- Agentic AI course announcement (October 2025), https://x.com/AndrewYNg/status/1975614372799283423
  Ng announces a course built around four agentic design patterns (reflection, tool use, planning, and multi-agent collaboration), framing agent-building as one of the most in-demand job-market skills. It crystallizes his claim that agentic workflows, not bigger base models, are the practical frontier. Useful as his canonical taxonomy of how agents are built.

- The Batch coverage on AI and the job market (2025-2026), https://www.deeplearning.ai/the-batch
  Across multiple issues Ng argues AI increases demand for workers who can use AI tools rather than causing widespread direct displacement, and tracks the rise of "AI Forward Deployed Engineers" embedded in client orgs. This is his measured counter to both doom and hype on labor. A grounded data-point lens for the work-of-the-future debate.

- Recurring "build an agentic workflow" recipe (ongoing), https://www.deeplearning.ai/the-batch/tag/letters/
  Ng repeatedly publishes a simple, hands-on recipe encouraging readers to build their own agentic workflows. It reflects his thesis that practitioners learn the future by building, not predicting. Good for the "how the next wave actually gets adopted" angle.

## Hooks for the brain
- Ng is the pragmatist anchor: useful as the "calm adoption curve" voice against both accelerationists and doomers.
- His "AI raises demand for AI-literate workers" claim is a concrete, testable labor thesis to track over 10-20 years.
- Hidden-player angle: the "Forward Deployed Engineer" role he highlights is an early signal of how AI actually enters enterprises.
- The Batch read longitudinally is a low-noise barometer of which applied-AI ideas are crossing from research into practice.
