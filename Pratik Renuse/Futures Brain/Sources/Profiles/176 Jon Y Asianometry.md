## Who they are
Jon Y is the independent creator behind Asianometry, a YouTube channel and weekly Substack newsletter widely regarded as one of the best resources on the business, history, and engineering of the semiconductor industry. He is based in Taipei and built the channel after a decade studying business in San Francisco.

## Their future thesis (one line)
The future of computing is gated by the physics and economics of chip manufacturing, so understanding fabs, materials, lithography, and the deep supply chain, especially across Asia, is essential to predicting where technology and geopolitics go next.

## Key future-relevant pieces
- "@Asianometry & Dylan Patel: How the semiconductor industry actually works" (2024), https://www.dwarkesh.com/p/dylan-jon
  In this long conversation with Dylan Patel, Jon Y walks through how the modern chip supply chain is structured and why a handful of firms dominate each layer. They discuss bottlenecks in lithography, packaging, and manufacturing know-how that will shape AI's hardware future. It is one of the most accessible deep dives into how the industry really functions.

- "An Interview with Jon Yu About YouTube and Making Semiconductors" (2025), https://stratechery.com/2025/an-interview-with-jon-yu-about-youtube-and-making-semiconductors/
  Ben Thompson interviews Jon Y about his approach to explaining semiconductors and his read on the industry's trajectory. The conversation covers why fab-level detail matters for understanding AI compute and geopolitics. It doubles as a window into his method of turning dense engineering into clear narratives.

- The Asianometry Newsletter and YouTube channel (ongoing, weekly), https://www.asianometry.com/archive
  Jon Y publishes weekly video essays and written pieces dissecting specific technologies, companies, and historical episodes in chips and Asian industry. Recurring themes include lithography, advanced packaging, materials science, and the rise of fabs across Asia. The archive is a continuously updated map of where semiconductor capability is concentrated and constrained.
