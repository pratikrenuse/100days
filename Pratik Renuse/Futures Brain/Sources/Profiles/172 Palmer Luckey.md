## Who they are
Founder of Oculus VR who sold it to Facebook, then in 2017 co-founded Anduril Industries, a defense technology company building autonomous drones, sensors, and software, valued around $60 billion in early 2026. He is the most visible advocate for replacing legacy defense contracting with mass-produced, software-defined autonomous weapons.

## Their future thesis (one line)
The United States must rebuild its "arsenal of democracy" by hyperscaling cheap, autonomous, software-defined weapons in commercial-style factories, because deterrence in a great-power conflict (especially with China) now depends on manufacturing volume, not exquisite one-off systems.

## Key future-relevant pieces
- "Rebuild the Arsenal" manifesto (Aug 2024), https://www.rebuildthearsenal.com/
  Anduril's ~30-page sequel to its earlier "Rebooting the Arsenal of Democracy," laying out a "manufacturing doctrine" for mass-producing autonomous defense systems. It argues the Ukraine war exposed a critical inability of the U.S. to surge production, and that the fix is hyperscale factories like Arsenal-1 making tens of thousands of systems a year. This is the clearest statement of Luckey's industrial vision for warfare.

- Arsenal-1 factory going live in Ohio (Mar 18, 2026), https://www.axios.com/2026/03/18/palmer-luckey-anduril-arsenal-drones
  Reports that Anduril's 5-million-square-foot Arsenal-1 plant is beginning production of drones and other autonomous systems. It is the physical proof-of-concept for the "rebuild the arsenal" thesis, applying consumer-electronics-style mass manufacturing to weapons. Marks the shift from manifesto to operating factory.

- "AI will make hardware so cheap you'll be able to buy a 'Ford F-150 for $1,000'" (Fortune, Feb 4, 2026), https://fortune.com/2026/02/04/who-is-palmer-luckey-anduril-founder-ai-hardware-cheap-manufacturing/
  Luckey argues that AI-driven design and automation will collapse the cost of complex hardware, a logic he applies to both consumer goods and weapons. It reveals his broader belief that software and AI will radically cheapen physical manufacturing. Useful for understanding why he thinks volume production of autonomous systems is about to become feasible.

- "Anduril's Palmer Luckey thinks the future of tech is in the past" (TechCrunch, Jan 8, 2026), https://techcrunch.com/2026/01/08/andurils-palmer-luckey-thinks-the-future-of-tech-is-in-the-past/
  A CES panel where Luckey argues vintage hardware aesthetics and intentional design are "objectively better," and bluntly frames U.S.-China relations as an irreversible "messy divorce." It captures both his cultural instincts and his hardening geopolitical worldview. The China framing directly motivates Anduril's industrial-base argument.
