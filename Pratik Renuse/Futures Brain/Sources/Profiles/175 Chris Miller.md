## Who they are
Chris Miller is a professor at the Fletcher School of Law and Diplomacy at Tufts University and a nonresident senior fellow at AEI, trained as an economic historian. His 2022 book "Chip War" became the definitive popular account of the geopolitics of semiconductors.

## Their future thesis (one line)
Computing power is the decisive resource of the 21st century, so control over the global semiconductor supply chain, especially advanced chip manufacturing and the chokepoints around Taiwan and export controls, will shape the balance of power between the US and China for decades.

## Key future-relevant pieces
- "Chip War: The Fight for the World's Most Critical Technology" (October 2022), https://www.simonandschuster.com/books/Chip-War/Chris-Miller/9781982172008
  Miller traces how semiconductors became the foundation of modern economic and military power, and how a handful of firms and nations came to control their production. He shows why Taiwan's TSMC sits at the center of global risk and why chips are now central to US-China rivalry. The book reframes the future of geopolitics around who can design and fabricate the most advanced chips.

- "How US Export Controls Have (and Haven't) Curbed Chinese AI" (July 2025), https://ai-frontiers.org/articles/us-chip-export-controls-china-ai
  Miller assesses six years of US restrictions and argues they have kept China a marginal producer of leading-edge AI chips while giving America a real lead. He cautions that the advantage is not guaranteed to last and that controls should aim to keep total US compute ahead rather than just keep China a generation behind. The piece is a clear-eyed read on the near-future trajectory of the chip competition.

- "Chips and Chokepoints: Chris Miller on the Geopolitics of the AI Supply Chain" (March 2026), https://www.cmu.edu/cmist/news-archive/news/2026/march/chips-and-chokepoints-chris-miller-on-the-geopolitics-of-the-ai-supply-chain.html
  In this Carnegie Mellon discussion, Miller maps the chokepoints in the AI hardware supply chain, from lithography to packaging, and how states are trying to control them. He weighs how export controls, reshoring, and Chinese localization will play out over the next decade. It is a useful snapshot of his 2026 thinking on where the chip-and-AI contest is heading.

- "Three Questions to Chris Miller: Navigating the Geopolitics of Semiconductors in a New Trump Era" (2025), https://www.institutmontaigne.org/en/expressions/three-questions-chris-miller-navigating-geopolitics-semiconductors-new-trump-era
  Miller explains how a second Trump administration's tariff and export posture reshapes the semiconductor landscape for allies and rivals alike. He discusses pressure on Taiwan, the durability of controls, and Europe's position. The interview captures how policy swings will steer chip supply chains in the years ahead.
