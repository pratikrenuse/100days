## Who they are
Partner at Radical Ventures (a venture firm focused on AI) and a longtime AI columnist for Forbes. Best known for deeply argued, forward-looking essays and an annual end-of-year prediction series for the AI industry.

## Their future thesis (one line)
AI's frontier is moving fast on concrete, near-term axes, capital structure, custom silicon, energy, biology, brain-computer interfaces, so the useful work is specific, falsifiable forecasting rather than abstract AGI speculation, and by 2026 the AGI/superintelligence discourse itself cools as value shifts to deployment.

## Key future-relevant pieces
- "10 AI Predictions For 2026" (Forbes, December 22, 2025; reposted January 5, 2026), https://www.forbes.com/sites/robtoews/2025/12/22/10-ai-predictions-for-2026/ (excerpts: https://radical.vc/10-ai-predictions-for-2026/)
  His flagship annual forecast. Notable calls: Anthropic goes public while OpenAI does not; China's domestic AI chip industry makes real progress toward eroding Nvidia's dominance; AGI/superintelligence discourse becomes less fashionable as value shifts to enterprise deployment; chip depreciation schedules become a critical (and risky) financial story; many more companies build custom chips; Sam Altman steps aside as OpenAI CEO; AI becomes central to the 2026 US midterms via job-loss politics; a major pharma company acquires a leading protein-AI startup; brain-computer interfaces go mainstream. The Radical repost is free and detailed.
- "10 AI Predictions For 2025" (Forbes, December 22, 2024), https://www.forbes.com/sites/robtoews/2024/12/22/10-ai-predictions-for-2025/
  The prior year's predictions, of which roughly five came true (including scaling laws extending into biology and robotics and AI data-centers-in-space announcements). Valuable for a futures brain as a track-record check on his forecasting calibration. Partial paywall.
- "AI That Can Invent AI Is Coming. Buckle Up." (2024), https://www.forbes.com/sites/robtoews/2024/11/03/ai-that-can-invent-ai-is-coming-buckle-up/
  Essay on recursive self-improvement: AI systems that design stronger AI systems, and why that feedback loop is one of the most consequential frontier directions. Directly relevant to the long-horizon question of takeoff dynamics. Partial paywall.
- "The Gaping Hole in Today's AI Capabilities" (March 23, 2025), https://www.forbes.com/sites/robtoews/2025/03/23/the-gaping-hole-in-todays-ai-capabilities-1/
  Argues continual learning (models that keep learning as they interact with the world) is a fundamental missing capability and a likely next frontier. Good for understanding the technical gap between current systems and anything resembling general intelligence. Partial paywall.
