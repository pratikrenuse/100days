## Who they are
Historian and philosopher, lecturer in history at the Hebrew University of Jerusalem and a distinguished research fellow at Cambridge's Centre for the Study of Existential Risk. Author of the "Sapiens" trilogy and co-founder of the social-impact company Sapienship. One of the most-read public intellectuals on AI and society.

## Their future thesis (one line)
Humanity's power has always come from shared stories and information networks; AI is the first technology that can create stories and make decisions by itself, so it threatens to become an unaccountable new kind of agent that could hijack the networks democracy and human meaning depend on.

## Key future-relevant pieces
- "Nexus: A Brief History of Information Networks from the Stone Age to AI" (September 2024), https://www.ynharari.com/book/nexus/
  Traces how information networks (from myth and scripture to bureaucracies and modern media) built and controlled human societies, for good and ill. It argues AI is a fundamentally new participant in these networks, an agent that can generate ideas and act, not just a tool, and warns this could erode democracy and truth if left unaccountable. His central statement on the AI era and the through-line for most of his recent talks.
- "We must not grant AI agents legal personhood" (June 8, 2026, Financial Times), https://www.ynharari.com/category/articles/
  A recent op-ed arguing that giving AI systems legal personhood would be a historic mistake, letting non-human agents own property, hold rights, and accumulate power beyond human control. He frames legal personhood as the threshold that turns AI from tool into autonomous actor inside our institutions. A concrete, current policy intervention. (FT original is paywalled; listed in his articles archive.)
- "Homo Deus: A Brief History of Tomorrow" (2015), https://www.ynharari.com/book/homo-deus/
  His earlier forecast that, having largely beaten famine, plague, and war, humanity will next pursue immortality, bliss, and divinity through biotech and AI. It introduces "dataism" and warns of a future where algorithms know us better than we know ourselves, potentially splitting society into a useless majority and an upgraded elite. The conceptual bridge from his history work to his AI warnings.

## Hooks for the brain
- "Shared fictions" (money, nations, religions, companies) as the operating system of humanity is his signature idea, and a marketer-friendly lens on brand and belief.
- His "useless class" warning about AI-displaced humans is provocative and quotable, a darker counterweight to the abundance camp.
- The legal-personhood op-ed is a sharp, specific 2026 flashpoint: a single legal threshold that decides whether AI becomes a citizen-like actor.
- Direct intellectual opponent of Andreessen and the accelerationists, making him the go-to voice for the "slow down and govern" side.
