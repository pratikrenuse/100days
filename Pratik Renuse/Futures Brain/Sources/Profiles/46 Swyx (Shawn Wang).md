## Who they are
Singapore-born developer, writer, and conference organizer who coined the term "AI Engineer." Runs Latent Space (newsletter and top-ranked AI engineering podcast), the AI Engineer conference series, and the AINews daily brief. Full name Shawn Wang; writes as swyx.

## Their future thesis (one line)
A new discipline, the "AI Engineer," is the practical center of gravity for the next era of software, and 2026 is the year coding agents "break containment" to automate everything else, so the hard problem becomes scaling output without scaling slop.

## Key future-relevant pieces
- "The Rise of the AI Engineer" (June 2023), https://www.latent.space/p/ai-engineer
  The foundational essay that named a new job category between ML researcher and traditional software engineer, arguing tasks that once took a research team and five years now take API docs and an afternoon. It predicted, accurately, that "AI Engineer" would become one of the fastest-growing titles in tech. Essential context for the future-of-software-labor question.
- "Scaling without Slop" (State of Latent Space 2026) (January 2026), https://www.latent.space/p/2026
  His 2026 outlook essay. Frames "slop" (low-effort AI-generated output flooding media, code, and the internet) as the central problem of the era and argues the answer is not cutting human output but "changing the slope of slop," scaling quantity and quality together through curation. Doubles as a state-of-the-field map of his expanding media network. Relevant to the 10-year question of information quality and trust.
- Agent Labs Thesis / AIE Europe debrief (2026), https://www.latent.space/p/unsupervised-learning-2026
  A crossover discussion laying out his "agent labs" thesis: coding agents generate software, software eats the world, therefore coding agents eat the world. Captures his view that 2025's coding agents become 2026's general-purpose automators. Audio plus notes.
- Latent Space AI Engineer Reading List / 2025 papers, https://www.latent.space/p/2025-papers
  A curated list of the papers he considers load-bearing for understanding frontier AI. Useful as a primary-source index for anyone trying to track where capability is actually heading. Updated periodically.
