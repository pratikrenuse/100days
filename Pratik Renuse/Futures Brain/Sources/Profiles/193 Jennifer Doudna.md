## Who they are
Nobel laureate biochemist who shared the 2020 Chemistry prize for co-developing CRISPR-Cas9 genome editing, and founder and Director of the Innovative Genomics Institute, a Berkeley and UCSF collaboration applying genome editing to health, agriculture, and climate. She will receive the 2026 Priestley Medal, the American Chemical Society's highest honor.

## Their future thesis (one line)
Over the next decade CRISPR will move from rare, costly bespoke treatments to scalable, affordable, personalized gene-editing therapies, and the same toolkit will help tackle disease, agriculture, and climate at population scale.

## Key future-relevant pieces
- "Gene editing has struggled to go commercial. This Nobel laureate has a $1 billion plan to fix that" (Forbes, 17 February 2026), https://www.forbes.com/sites/amyfeldman/2026/02/17/gene-editing-has-struggled-to-go-commercial-this-nobel-laureate-has-a-1-billion-plan-to-fix-that/
  Doudna lays out a plan to raise $1 billion to fund roughly $100 million a year over a decade for the Innovative Genomics Institute. The goal is to make personalized CRISPR treatments affordable and widely available and to build the next generation of CRISPR scientists. It points to a recent case where a bespoke gene-editing therapy was designed, approved, and manufactured in about six months as a template for scale.

- "Berkeley Talks: Nobel laureate Jennifer Doudna on CRISPR and the future of gene editing" (22 August 2025), https://news.berkeley.edu/2025/08/22/berkeley-talks-jennifer-doudna-on-crispr/
  In this talk she reflects on how CRISPR has progressed from discovery to clinical use and where it goes next. She emphasizes accelerating the path from research to widely accessible therapies and applications beyond human health. It is a concise statement of her decade-ahead vision for the field.

- "A Multi-Billion Dollar Market For CRISPR Therapies Is Possible" (Forbes, 29 May 2025), https://www.forbes.com/sites/juergeneckhardt/2025/05/29/jennifer-doudna-a-multi-billion-dollar-market-for-crispr-therapies-is-possible/
  Doudna argues that despite slow commercialization, CRISPR can support a large therapeutic market as manufacturing and approval pathways mature. She frames the bottleneck as translation and infrastructure rather than underlying science. The piece captures her case that gene editing is on the cusp of broad clinical impact.

- Innovative Genomics Institute mission and programs, https://innovativegenomics.org/people/jennifer-doudna/
  The institute she founded is built to take foundational CRISPR research and apply it to health, sustainable agriculture, and climate resilience. It serves as the organizational engine for her goal of democratizing genome editing. This is the platform through which her future-facing agenda is executed.
