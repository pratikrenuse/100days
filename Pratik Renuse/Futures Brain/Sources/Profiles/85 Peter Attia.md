## Who they are
Stanford, Johns Hopkins, and NIH-trained physician, founder of the medical practice Early Medical, host of The Peter Attia Drive, and author of the number one bestseller Outlive (2023). He is the leading popularizer of preventive "Medicine 3.0."

## Their future thesis (one line)
The next era of medicine shifts from treating disease late to delaying or preventing the big four killers decades early, using better biomarkers, GLP-1 and rapamycin-class drugs, exercise as medicine, and increasingly AI-driven personalization.

## Key future-relevant pieces
- "Outlive: The Science and Art of Longevity" (March 28, 2023), https://peterattiamd.com/outlive/
  Outlive lays out Attia's Medicine 3.0 thesis: rather than waiting for heart disease, cancer, neurodegeneration, and metabolic disease to appear, medicine should target their root drivers decades in advance. The book is the clearest statement of his framework and remains the reference text for how a mainstream-credible doctor thinks about extending healthspan, not just lifespan. It treats exercise, sleep, nutrition, and emotional health as primary interventions alongside drugs.
- "A new era of longevity science" (The Drive, AMA / episode, 2026), https://peterattiamd.com/podcast/
  Recent episodes argue current biological aging clocks are not yet clinically useful and that the field needs more actionable biomarkers before anti-aging drugs can be deployed responsibly. Attia tracks rapamycin dosing trials, GLP-1 receptor agonists and their effects far beyond weight loss, SGLT2 inhibitors, and compounds like urolithin A and NAD boosters with a deliberately skeptical eye. The throughline is that AI plus rising funding could accelerate aging breakthroughs, but the data bar must stay high.
- "GLP-1 receptor agonists and muscle loss" (The Drive, AMA #86, June 29, 2026), https://peterattiamd.com/podcast/
  Attia examines new data on preserving lean muscle while using GLP-1 drugs for weight loss, a fast-emerging clinical problem as these medicines go mainstream. He frames muscle as a longevity organ and resistance training plus protein as the countermeasure. This is a concrete example of his method: take a hyped new drug class and pressure-test the second-order effects for healthspan.
