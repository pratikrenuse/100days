## Who they are
President and CEO of the Population Reference Bureau (since May 2024) and an internationally recognized expert in political demography, formerly a tenured professor at Rhodes College and a demographics consultant to the US Office of the Secretary of Defense. She specializes in translating fertility, mortality, and migration data into actionable insight for policymakers and the public.

## Their future thesis (one line)
The twenty-first century is defined not by runaway population growth but by differential growth: a widening split between aging, shrinking rich countries and young, fast-growing poor ones, and how societies respond to that divergence will shape security, economies, and well-being.

## Key future-relevant pieces
- "8 Billion and Counting: How Sex, Death, and Migration Shape Our World" (W.W. Norton, 2022), https://www.amazon.com/Billion-Counting-Death-Migration-Shape/dp/1324002700
  Argues that the dominant demographic story is divergence, not a single global trend: some countries face rapid aging and decline while others are still young and growing fast. She makes the case that fertility, mortality, and migration patterns today point to the investments needed now, and warns against panic framings of both "overpopulation" and "population collapse."

- TED Talk: "The Truth About Human Population Decline" (2023), https://www.ted.com/talks/jennifer_d_sciubba_the_truth_about_human_population_decline
  Pushes back on alarmist narratives about falling birth rates, arguing that decline is neither uniform nor necessarily catastrophic and that policy responses matter more than the raw numbers. Her most-viewed public statement of why demographic destiny is more nuanced than headlines suggest.

- "The Mistaken Assumption That Immigration Is Inevitable" (The Atlantic, March 31, 2022), https://www.theatlantic.com/ideas/archive/2022/03/global-migration-population-growth-myth/629387/
  Challenges the assumption that aging rich countries can always rely on a steady inflow of migrants to offset shrinking workforces. As source countries themselves age and develop, she argues, the future supply of migrants is far less guaranteed than policymakers assume.

- "Birthrates Are Plummeting Around the World. Why?" (The Ezra Klein Show, NYT, March 19, 2024), https://www.nytimes.com/2024/03/19/opinion/ezra-klein-podcast-jennifer-sciubba.html
  A long-form conversation unpacking the global fertility decline, its uneven distribution, and what it means for economies, pensions, and geopolitics. A clear primer on why low birth rates are reshaping the long-term outlook for both rich and developing nations.

- "Get to Know PRB CEO Jennifer D. Sciubba" (PRB, 2024), https://www.prb.org/articles/get-to-know-prb-ceo-jennifer-d-sciubba/
  PRB's introduction to her leadership and approach, emphasizing data-driven, forward-looking demography to inform policy on urgent global challenges. Useful context on how she intends to steer PRB's population-trend work over the coming years.
