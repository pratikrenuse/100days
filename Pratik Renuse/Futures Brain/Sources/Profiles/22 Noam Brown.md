## Who they are
Research scientist leading reasoning research at OpenAI, a primary architect of the o1 reasoning models (credited on the o1 launch and system card). Known for earlier game-AI breakthroughs (Libratus poker, and Cicero for Diplomacy); he won the 2025 World Diplomacy Championship himself.

## Their future thesis (one line)
Test-time compute is a new scaling axis: letting models "think" longer (seconds to hours) is roughly equivalent to a 1,000-10,000x larger model, and the frontier extends from single-model reasoning toward multi-agent systems.

## Key future-relevant pieces
- "Scaling Test Time Compute to Multi-Agent Civilizations" (Latent Space, 2025), https://www.latent.space/p/noam-brown
  Brown explains the System-1/System-2 framing for AI: one unit of test-time (inference) compute can be worth orders of magnitude more parameters, so reasoning, not just bigger pretraining, becomes the growth axis. He extends this toward multi-agent "civilizations" of cooperating models. His most complete public articulation of the reasoning-scaling thesis.

- "OpenAI research lead Noam Brown thinks certain AI 'reasoning' models could've arrived decades ago" (TechCrunch, March 2025), https://techcrunch.com/2025/03/19/openai-research-lead-noam-brown-thinks-ai-reasoning-models-couldve-arrived-decades-ago/
  Brown argues the core idea behind reasoning models (spending more compute at inference to think before answering) was technically reachable far earlier and was a missing paradigm rather than a missing capability. It reframes recent progress as an unlock, not just scale. Useful for understanding why he sees large untapped headroom.

- OpenAI IMO gold-medal result (July 2025), https://sequoiacap.com/podcast/training-data-openai-imo/
  Brown and the team discuss an experimental model that scored at gold-medal level on the 2025 International Mathematical Olympiad by writing natural-language proofs, with no tools and within standard time limits. It is a concrete demonstration that test-time reasoning can reach elite human problem-solving. Strong evidence point for his thesis.

- SPIRAL: self-play reasoning research (2025-2026), https://arxiv.org/pdf/2506.24119
  Work on self-play in zero-sum games to incentivize general reasoning via multi-agent, multi-turn reinforcement learning. It connects his game-AI background to the broader project of teaching models to reason. Signals where the research is heading: reasoning learned through interaction and competition.
