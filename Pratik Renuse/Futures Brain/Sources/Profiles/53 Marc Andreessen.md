## Who they are
Co-founder and general partner of venture firm Andreessen Horowitz (a16z), and co-creator of the Mosaic/Netscape browser. The leading financial and rhetorical champion of aggressive technology acceleration ("techno-optimism").

## Their future thesis (one line)
Technology, markets, and intelligence are the engines of human progress and abundance, so the moral imperative is to build and to deploy AI as fast and aggressively as possible, treating stagnation and excessive caution as the real dangers.

## Key future-relevant pieces
- "The Techno-Optimist Manifesto" (October 16, 2023), https://a16z.com/the-techno-optimist-manifesto/
  A roughly 5,200-word credo declaring technology and free markets the source of growth, vitality, and rising well-being, and casting "de-growth," safetyism, and what he calls the enemy ideas of sustainability and risk-management as obstacles to human flourishing. It frames intelligence and energy as the master resources and argues for unconstrained building. The closest thing the accelerationist movement has to a founding document.
- "Why AI Will Save the World" (June 2023), https://a16z.com/ai-will-save-the-world/
  Argues AI is a universal tool that augments human intelligence rather than an extinction risk, and systematically rebuts the main doom arguments as a "moral panic." His policy prescription is "faster, please": let both Big Tech and startups build AI as fast as they can, with open-source encouraged and heavy regulation resisted. A core text of the pro-acceleration position on AI safety.
- "It's Time to Build" (April 2020), https://a16z.com/its-time-to-build/
  Written during the early pandemic, it argues Western institutions failed because they had lost the will and capacity to build physical things: housing, hospitals, factories, schools. It is a call to treat building as a moral and civilizational project, not just a business one. This essay seeded the broader "build" and "abundance" movements that now shape US tech politics.

## Hooks for the brain
- The manifesto literally names its "enemies" (sustainability, risk management, precautionary principle), which makes it the cleanest articulation of the accelerationist worldview to quote against.
- "It's Time to Build" is the upstream source of the entire Abundance Agenda; tracing that lineage is a strong explainer post.
- Andreessen is both investor and ideologue, so his essays double as a portfolio thesis: follow the money to see which futures a16z is funding.
- Direct foil to Harari, Bostrom, and Tegmark: same AI facts, opposite conclusion about how fast to move.
