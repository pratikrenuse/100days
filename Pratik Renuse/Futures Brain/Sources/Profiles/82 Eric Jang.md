## Who they are
Robot learning researcher and writer. Former VP of AI at 1X Technologies (humanoid robots) from 2022 to early 2026, and before that a research scientist at Google Brain. As of early 2026 he left 1X and is taking time to study and recalibrate before his next role.

## Their future thesis (one line)
Disembodied AI and robotic AI will arrive at roughly the same time because the hard engineering problems are the same, so "all roads lead to robotics" and the whole economy is reorganizing around scaling machine intelligence into the physical world.

## Key future-relevant pieces
- "All Roads Lead to Robotics" (Mar 3, 2024), https://evjang.com/2024/03/03/all-roads-robots.html
  His signature thesis. Jang argues that the engineering and infrastructure problems in large language models are essentially the same as those in robotics, so disembodied AGI and robotic AGI should happen at roughly the same time, not decades apart. As software agents interact with the messy real world (support, logistics, ride-sharing), they hit the same entropy and reliability problems robots already face. His conclusion: robotics is not a niche, it is where most software is heading.

- "As Rocks May Think" (Feb 4, 2026), https://evjang.com/2026/02/04/rocks.html
  A riff on Vannevar Bush's 1945 "As We May Think." Core idea: we have taught rocks (silicon) to think and they are getting very smart, which will create astronomical demand for inference compute, much like air conditioning unlocked productivity once it was cheap. He pictures a near future of ambient, always-on machine "thinkers" constantly re-planning deadlines, paying down tech debt, and guiding business decisions in real time. It is a strong "what does abundant cheap cognition do to the economy" piece.

- "Leaving 1X" (Jan 21, 2026), https://evjang.com/2026/01/21/leaving-1x.html
  His reflection on four years building robot learning at a leading humanoid company. He notes the Overton window on general-purpose robotics has shifted, meaning ideas once seen as fringe are now mainstream. He describes stepping back to "empty his cup of priors," re-implement papers, and catch up on the frontier. Useful as an honest insider snapshot of where humanoid robot learning actually stands in 2026.

- "Science and Engineering for Learned Robots" (Mar 14, 2021), https://evjang.com/2021/03/14/learning-robots.html
  An earlier but still-cited framing of how to actually make progress in robot learning. He distinguishes the science (understanding generalization) from the engineering (building reliable data and infrastructure pipelines) and argues the field is bottlenecked more by engineering discipline than by clever ideas. Foundational for understanding his "data and scale beat cleverness" worldview, which echoes the LLM scaling story.

## Hooks for the brain
- The "all roads lead to robotics" thesis is a clean bridge between the AI software story and the physical robot story. It lets the Futures Brain connect a marketer-facing AI tool argument to the bigger embodiment trend.
- "As Rocks May Think" gives a vivid, ownable metaphor (ambient always-on machine thinkers) for cheap cognition. Excellent raw material for a forward-looking essay about how work changes when thinking is nearly free.
- Jang is a rare practitioner-writer: he was inside a top humanoid company and writes plainly. He is a credible middle voice between Brooks (bear) and Adcock or Musk (bull).
- Watch his next move after 1X. Where a respected robot-learning lead goes next is itself a signal about which approach (humanoids, special-purpose, foundation models for action) is winning.
- His book "AI is Good for You" is an optimistic counter-narrative to AI doom. Worth mining for a balanced "is this good for us" angle.
