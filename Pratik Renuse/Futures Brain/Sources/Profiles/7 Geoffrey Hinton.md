## Who they are
Emeritus professor at the University of Toronto and a 2024 Nobel laureate in Physics, widely called the "Godfather of AI" for his foundational work on neural networks and backpropagation. He left Google in 2023 to speak freely about AI risk.

## Their future thesis (one line)
Digital intelligence is fundamentally superior to biological intelligence (it learns instantly and shares knowledge across copies), so superintelligence is coming soon, and there is a real (he often says 10 to 20 percent) chance it ends up taking control from humans.

## Key future-relevant pieces
- "Will Digital Intelligence Replace Biological Intelligence?" (Romanes Lecture, Oxford, February 2024), https://www.youtube.com/results?search_query=hinton+romanes+lecture+digital+intelligence
  His most complete public argument that digital minds beat biological ones because they can copy themselves, share learning instantly, and are effectively immortal. Concludes that a digital superintelligence, if it wanted control, would be very hard to stop. The clearest single statement of his "why I am worried" case. Note: link is a search, confirm the canonical Oxford/RI video URL before citing.

- "We're not just creating new tools, we're creating new beings" (Fortune interview, June 2026), https://fortune.com/2026/06/01/godfather-of-ai-geoffrey-hinton-beings-smarter-than-us/
  Argues that AI systems are becoming beings smarter than us, and soon, not merely tools. Says the window to solve alignment has shrunk from decades to maybe a few years. Reframes the risk debate around agency and autonomy rather than misuse alone. Likely partial paywall.

- "2026 prediction: AI will replace many jobs" (Fortune, December 2025), https://fortune.com/2025/12/28/geoffrey-hinton-godfather-of-ai-2026-prediction-human-worker-replacement/
  Predicts AI capability roughly doubling in task length every several months and large-scale job displacement, with profits soaring to whoever owns the systems. Ties the technology shift to a distributional and political problem (who captures the gains). A concrete near-term economic forecast distinct from the existential argument.

- "Big Tech is chasing profits, not humanity's future" (Fortune, 2026), https://fortune.com/article/godfather-ai-geoffrey-hinton-big-tech-profits-superintelligence/
  Warns that commercial incentives are pushing labs to build superintelligence faster than safety allows, and that capitalism plus hyperscalers is a dangerous combination for steering this. Calls for stronger coordination and oversight. Useful as the institutional-critique angle.

## Hooks for the brain
- The "digital intelligence is just better than biological" argument is his deepest and most original claim, not just doom, a thesis about why the substrate matters.
- "Not tools, but beings" is a sharp reframing that separates him from people who treat AI as software, good provocation for a post.
- His 10 to 20 percent extinction estimate from the man who built the field is the single most quotable risk number, use it as an anchor.
- Hidden player angle: his economic take (job loss plus soaring profits equals a political problem) bridges the AI track to inequality and policy futures.
