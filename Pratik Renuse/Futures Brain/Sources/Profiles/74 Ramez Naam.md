## Who they are
Technologist, futurist, and author; former Microsoft engineer and writer of the award-winning Nexus science-fiction trilogy. One of the earliest voices (from around 2011) to forecast the exponential cost declines of solar, batteries, and EVs and to predict clean energy would undercut fossil fuels. Founded the climate venture firm PlanetaryVC in 2021. Co-chair of Energy and Environment at Singularity University.

## Their future thesis (one line)
Clean energy and storage ride steep, predictable experience curves, so solar, wind, and batteries become the cheapest energy in history and disrupt fossil fuels on pure economics, with the next phase (cheap enough to undercut existing coal and gas, then to power AI) being the most consequential yet.

## Key future-relevant pieces
- "The Third Phase of Clean Energy Will Be the Most Disruptive Yet" (2 Apr 2019), https://rameznaam.com/2019/04/02/the-third-phase-of-clean-energy-will-be-the-most-disruptive-yet/
  His signature framing. Phase one was clean energy reaching price parity for new build; phase two, beating new fossil build; phase three, when new solar, wind, and storage become cheaper than just operating existing coal and gas plants. He argues phase three is when fossil assets start getting stranded and the transition becomes self-reinforcing.

- "Solar's Future is Insanely Cheap" (14 May 2020), https://rameznaam.com/2020/05/14/solars-future-is-insanely-cheap/
  His landmark update on solar learning curves, revising his own earlier (already bullish) forecasts further downward. Argues solar prices keep beating every official projection and will reach levels once considered impossible, reshaping where and how energy is produced.

- "How Cheap Can Energy Storage Get? Pretty Darn Cheap" (legacy series), https://rameznaam.com/2015/10/14/how-cheap-can-energy-storage-get-pretty-darn-cheap/
  Part of his foundational multi-part series modeling solar, wind, storage, and EV cost declines using experience curves. Storage is the linchpin of his "renewables can go very far" argument, and this piece is the analytical core that later events largely validated.

- "How AI Changes Energy" (2026, joint talk with Casey Handmer), https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Tw-LO33woPo
  Recent conversation on how surging AI power demand is accelerating solar buildout and reshaping land use and grid planning. Marks his current focus: the collision of AI energy hunger with cheap solar as the defining energy story of the late 2020s.
