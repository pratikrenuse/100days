## Key future-relevant pieces
- "SpaceX shifts focus from Mars to a self-growing city on the Moon" (Feb 2026), https://time.com/7373155/elon-musk-mars-moon-city/
  A major 2026 reversal. Musk announced SpaceX would prioritize building a self-sustaining city on the Moon, achievable in under 10 years in his framing, and pushed the realistic horizon for humans on Mars out by roughly five to seven years (now mid-to-late 2030s). His stated logic: the Moon is reachable every 10 days with a 2-day trip versus Mars every 26 months with a 6-month trip, so the Moon is the faster path to securing civilization. This deprioritized the previously planned uncrewed Starship Mars flights in the late-2026 window.

- "SpaceX Mars plan: Starships carrying Optimus robots" (2025, pre-pivot roadmap), https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SpaceX_Mars_colonization_program
  The roadmap that defined his stated ambition before the 2026 Moon pivot: uncrewed Starships landing on Mars carrying Tesla Optimus robots to set up power, infrastructure, and scout for water ice before any humans arrive. Crewed landings were floated for as early as 2029 with a self-sustaining colony by roughly 2050. Even with the timeline pushed back, this Optimus-first colonization concept remains his core long-term vision and a useful reference point.

- "Tesla repurposes Fremont lines to build Optimus humanoid robots" (Feb 2026), https://www.teslarati.com/elon-musk-spacex-moon-city-before-mars/
  Signals how load-bearing Optimus has become to the whole thesis. Tesla is ending Model S and Model X production and repurposing those Fremont lines for Optimus. In Musk's framing Optimus, FSD, Dojo, and xAI are not parallel bets, they are the capital and technology engine on Earth that makes an interplanetary civilization financially viable.

- "Elon Musk says Mars in 2026 is 50/50" (mid-2025), https://www.space.com/space-exploration/private-spaceflight/elon-musk-says-spacex-will-launch-its-biggest-starship-yet-this-year-but-mars-in-2026-is-50-50
  A characteristic Musk timeline statement showing how he sets aggressive public targets and then revises them. He gave the 2026 uncrewed Mars window a 50/50 chance before later pivoting to the Moon. Useful as a calibration note: his stated dates are direction and ambition, not commitments, which is exactly why pairing him with a skeptic like Rodney Brooks is valuable.
