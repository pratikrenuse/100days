## Who they are
Senior space editor at Ars Technica and a meteorologist by training. The most-read independent reporter on the commercial launch industry, best known for the weekly Rocket Report and for deeply sourced books on SpaceX's early history.

## Their future thesis (one line)
Cut through the hype and vaporware to show what the launch and spaceflight industry can actually do, tracking the real shift from government programs toward commercial, reusable, high-cadence space access.

## Key future-relevant pieces
- "Rocket Report" weekly newsletter (ongoing, every Friday), https://arstechnica.com/tag/rocket-report/ (URL unverified)
  His flagship running feature, tagged "Amid the hype and vaporware, we're bringing clarity to the launch industry." Each issue rounds up developments across small and large launch, from SpaceX to startups to national programs, with skeptical, sourced commentary. It is the single best ongoing pulse-check on where launch is actually heading, as opposed to where founders say it is heading. For the Futures Brain it is a recurring fact-check layer on space hype.

- "Reentry: SpaceX, Elon Musk, and the Reusable Rockets that Launched a Second Space Age" (2024 book), https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eric_Berger_(journalist)
  His deeply reported history of the Falcon 9 and Dragon era, the period that made reusable orbital rockets normal. It documents how reusability went from industry punchline to the foundation of modern spaceflight economics. Essential background for understanding why launch costs are falling and what that unlocks for the next 10 to 20 years.

- "Liftoff: Elon Musk and the Desperate Early Days that Launched SpaceX" (2021 book), https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eric_Berger_(journalist)
  The prequel, on SpaceX's near-death early years developing Falcon 1. It is the origin story of the company now reshaping space, and a case study in how a commercial newcomer overturned a government-dominated industry. Useful for grounding any forward-looking take on private space in how fragile and contingent the breakthrough actually was.

- Ongoing SpaceX, NASA, and Starship coverage at Ars Technica (continuous), https://muckrack.com/sciguyspace/articles
  His running reporting on Starship test campaigns, NASA Artemis and policy, and the commercial space race. He is often first and most accurate on schedule slips and technical setbacks, which makes him the corrective to founder timelines (including Musk's). His Muck Rack archive is the most reliable index of his latest pieces given the main site's access limits.
