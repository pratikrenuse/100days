## Who they are
Award-winning AI reporter and bestselling author, now based in London, working independently across The Atlantic, More Perfect Union, and the BBC. She was the first journalist to profile OpenAI, wrote "Empire of AI," and in 2026 co-founded The AI Resist List to track global pushback against the AI industry.

## Their future thesis (one line)
The AI industry, led by companies like OpenAI, is building a new kind of empire that concentrates wealth and power in a handful of billionaires while extracting cheap labor, data, land, water, and energy from the rest of the world, and the next decade will be decided by whether ordinary people can resist and reshape that trajectory.

## Key future-relevant pieces
- "The messy, secretive reality behind OpenAI's bid to save the world" (February 17, 2020), https://www.technologyreview.com/2020/02/17/844721/ai-openai-moonshot-elon-musk-sam-altman-greg-brockman-messy-secretive-reality/
  The first in-depth profile of OpenAI, built from office access, roughly three dozen interviews, and internal documents. Hao argued there was a gap between OpenAI's public mission of open, transparent, beneficial AI and how it actually operated, as competitive pressure and the hunt for funding eroded its founding ideals. The piece set the template for years of scrutiny of the company and reportedly led OpenAI to stop speaking with her for three years.

- "Inside the story that enraged OpenAI" (May 19, 2025), https://www.technologyreview.com/2025/05/19/1116614/hao-empire-ai-openai/
  An excerpt from "Empire of AI" recounting Hao's 2019 embed inside OpenAI and the fallout from her 2020 profile. It captures leadership defending the pursuit of AGI in circular terms while dodging questions about environmental cost and worker impact, and shows how the belief that OpenAI must be first or perish justified its huge consumption of compute, data, and resources. It is a useful primer on the company's psychology and the inevitability narrative driving the AI race.

- "Empire of AI: Dreams and Nightmares in Sam Altman's OpenAI" (book, published May 20, 2025; now in paperback), https://www.penguinrandomhouse.com/books/743569/empire-of-ai-by-karen-hao/
  An instant New York Times bestseller and Financial Times Book of the Year that frames OpenAI and the broader AI industry as a modern colonial empire. Hao documents the hidden human and environmental costs of the AI boom, from underpaid data workers in Kenya and the Philippines to freshwater and energy strains on communities in Chile and Uruguay. The book is her central statement that the future of AI is really a question of power, extraction, and who pays the price for the technology.

- "AI Resist List: Karen Hao on Data Center Resistance, Tech Billionaires, Empire of AI and More" (May 22, 2026), https://www.democracynow.org/2026/5/22/data_centers_and_ai
  A 2026 interview marking the launch of The AI Resist List, a crowdsourced database of global resistance to the AI industry. Hao argues that tech billionaires have fused with the state to override public concern about AI, and that grassroots opposition stalled over 100 billion dollars of data center projects in 2025 by mobilizing across political lines over energy use, water, and rising utility bills. She reframes the wave of AI-justified layoffs as a future bet on automation rather than proof that AI can already replace those workers.

## Hooks for the brain
- Feeds the "AI as concentration of power" and "new colonialism / extraction economy" theses. Use her as the counterweight to techno-optimist AGI narratives: she supplies the supply-chain and geopolitical underside (cheap labor, water, energy, land) that most frontier-AI commentary omits.
- Strong source for the data center and energy strain storyline of the next 10 to 20 years, and for the emerging politics of AI resistance (The AI Resist List) as a force that could shape, slow, or redirect deployment.
- Pairs well against insider or accelerationist voices in the brain: she is the most cited journalistic critic of OpenAI specifically, useful when stress testing any claim that scaling AI is inevitable or net beneficial by default.
