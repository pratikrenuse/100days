## Who they are
Professor of geophysical sciences at the University of Chicago and founding faculty director of its Climate Systems Engineering initiative, who built and previously led Harvard's Solar Geoengineering Research Program. He is one of the longest-standing serious scientists studying whether and how reflecting sunlight could reduce climate risk.

## Their future thesis (one line)
Solar geoengineering (now called sunlight reflection methods) is technically feasible and cheap and could meaningfully reduce climate risk, but it must be researched transparently and governed through multilateral public decision-making, not driven by for-profit startups or near-term unilateral deployment.

## Key future-relevant pieces
- "Why the for-profit race into solar geoengineering is bad for science and public trust" (MIT Technology Review, Nov 4, 2025), https://www.technologyreview.com/2025/11/04/1127532/why-the-for-profit-race-into-solar-geoengineering-is-bad-for-science-and-public-trust/
  Co-authored op-ed arguing that venture-backed geoengineering startups (Stardust, Make Sunsets, Sunscreen) will erode the public trust the field depends on. Keith contends that because deployment is already cheap, the hard questions are about risk balancing, equity, and legitimacy, which markets cannot resolve. He calls for publicly coordinated, transparently funded research and multilateral decisions.

- "Halving warming with idealized solar geoengineering moderates key climate hazards" (Nature Climate Change, 2019), https://davidkeith.earth/publication/halving-warming-with-idealized-solar-geoengineering-moderates-key-climate-hazards/
  A widely cited modeling study showing that using solar geoengineering to offset only half of warming reduces major climate hazards in nearly all regions, countering fears that it would create losers everywhere. It reframed the debate from all-or-nothing to moderate, targeted use. Central to the scientific case that SRM could reduce, not just shift, climate risk.

- "What's the Least Bad Way to Cool the Planet?" (New York Times, 2021), https://davidkeith.earth/the-new-york-times-whats-the-least-bad-way-to-cool-the-planet/
  An op-ed making the public case for taking solar geoengineering research seriously while being honest about its risks and limits. It positions SRM as a supplement to, not a substitute for, cutting emissions. A good entry point to Keith's careful, non-hyped framing for general readers.

- "A Case for Climate Engineering" (book, 2013), https://davidkeith.earth/book-a-case-for-climate-engineering/
  Keith's accessible book laying out the argument for researching stratospheric aerosol injection and the governance challenges it raises. It remains a foundational text for the field and his long-held position. Useful background for why he has spent more than a decade pushing for research rather than deployment.
