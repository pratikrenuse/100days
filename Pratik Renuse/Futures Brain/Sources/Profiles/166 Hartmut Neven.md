## Who they are
Founder and Lead of Google Quantum AI and a Vice President of Engineering at Google, where he started the Quantum Artificial Intelligence Lab in 2012. He leads the team behind the Willow quantum processor and Google's roadmap toward a large-scale, error-corrected quantum computer. Named to TIME's 100 most influential people in AI in 2025.

## Their future thesis (one line)
A useful, error-corrected quantum computer is achievable this decade (Google targets roughly 2029-2030), and it will unlock real-world advantage in materials science, chemistry, medicine, and energy that no classical machine can match.

## Key future-relevant pieces
- "Meet Willow, our state-of-the-art quantum chip" (December 9, 2024), https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/technology/research/google-willow-quantum-chip/
  Announces the 105-qubit Willow chip and its central claim: errors can be reduced exponentially as more qubits are added, achieving below-threshold quantum error correction, a milestone the field pursued for almost 30 years. It also reports a random-circuit-sampling benchmark Willow ran in minutes that would take classical supercomputers an astronomically long time. This is the foundational result for Neven's argument that scalable, error-corrected machines are now a matter of engineering.

- "Our Quantum Echoes algorithm is a big step toward real-world applications for quantum computing" (October 2025), https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/technology/research/quantum-echoes-willow-verifiable-quantum-advantage/
  Reports the first verifiable quantum advantage, with the Quantum Echoes algorithm on Willow running a task roughly 13,000 times faster than the best known classical algorithm on the Frontier supercomputer. The "verifiable" framing matters because it points toward results other quantum computers can reproduce, a step toward practically useful output rather than pure benchmarks.

- "Google Quantum AI roadmap" (ongoing, five-stage framework detailed November 2025), https://quantumai.google/roadmap
  Lays out Google's staged path from current processors to a large-scale, error-corrected machine, with public milestones toward 100 to 1,000 logical qubits and an eventual target near 2029-2030. A November 2025 five-stage framework extends this from algorithm discovery to deploying quantum solutions in production workflows. This is the canonical document for tracking whether Google hits its own timeline.

- "Google Quantum AI Head Sees Commercial Quantum Within Five Years" (February 5, 2025), https://thequantuminsider.com/2025/02/05/google-quantum-ai-head-sees-commercial-quantum-within-five-years/
  Reports Neven's prediction that commercially useful quantum applications arrive within about five years, naming materials science, medicine, and energy as first domains. Useful as a dated, on-the-record timeline claim to hold against actual progress.
