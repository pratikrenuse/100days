## Who they are
Doomberg is a pseudonymous team (the green chicken mascot) of entrepreneurs with backgrounds in heavy industry, private equity, and the hard sciences. They run what they describe as the most widely read finance newsletter on Substack, publishing 6 to 8 articles a month with a lateral-thinking take on energy, finance, and geopolitics. Identity is deliberately hidden so the analysis stands on its own.

## Their future thesis (one line)
The energy transition as commonly sold is naive about physics, scale, and base-load reliability, so fossil fuels, nuclear, and industrial reality will dominate far longer than activists assume, and energy security (not just decarbonization) is the variable that will decide geopolitics.

## Key future-relevant pieces
- "About" / publication thesis, https://newsletter.doomberg.com/about
  States the worldview directly: trillions of taxpayer dollars and mandates have been thrown at the energy transition, and they write the "practical voice" they felt was missing. Useful as the framing document for everything else they publish.
- "The Year of the Chicken" (origin story), https://doomberg.substack.com/p/the-year-of-the-chicken
  Explains why they went pseudonymous and how the publication is built around contrarian, lateral-thinking energy and macro analysis rather than personality.
- Energy and Renewables topic streams, https://newsletter.doomberg.com/t/renewables and https://newsletter.doomberg.com/t/investments
  Their running coverage where the forward-looking arguments live: power-grid fragility, the limits of intermittent renewables without firm capacity, natural gas and nuclear as the realistic backbone, and commodity and geopolitical knock-on effects. Most deep pieces sit behind the paywall.

(Note: most individual flagship essays are paywalled and were not fully readable. Titles and topic hubs captured above; the free About and origin posts give the clearest statement of their thesis.)

## Hooks for the brain
- Doomberg is the markets-and-skeptic voice on energy. They sit on the Smil side of the optimist-vs-realist axis but come at it from finance and commodities rather than academia.
- Strong counterweight to Seba, Handmer, Naam, and Liebreich on transition speed. Pair them to capture the "grid reliability and base load" critique of an all-renewables future.
- Hidden angle: their focus on energy security and geopolitics (not just cost curves) is a distinct lens. Useful for "what could slow or break the transition" scenarios.
- Caveat for the brain: they have a clear editorial slant and a paid model, so treat as a sharp-but-positioned source, not neutral data. Cross-check claims against Our World in Data and BNEF.
- Post idea: "The reliability tax: what the cheap-solar story leaves out about keeping the lights on."
