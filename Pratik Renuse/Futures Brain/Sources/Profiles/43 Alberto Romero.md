## Who they are
Spain-based independent AI writer behind The Algorithmic Bridge (45,000+ subscribers), framed as "a blog about AI that's actually about people." Also works as an analyst at CambrianAI focused on large language models.

## Their future thesis (one line)
The interesting future of AI lives in the wide, under-discussed middle ground between "AI sucks" and "unfathomable superintelligence," and it should be read through a human lens, not a hype cycle.

## Key future-relevant pieces
- "The Shape of Artificial Intelligence" (2024), https://www.thealgorithmicbridge.com/p/the-shape-of-artificial-intelligence
  A conceptual essay mapping how AI capability might actually unfold rather than assuming a straight line to superintelligence. Romero argues the trajectory has a "shape" with plateaus, jagged edges, and uneven domains, which matters for anyone forecasting where the technology lands in a decade. Useful as a counter to both doom and utopia framings.
- "AGI: (M)ending the World" (Algorithmic Bridge), https://www.thealgorithmicbridge.com/p/mending-the-world
  Examines how AI optimists and doomers both present superintelligence in absolute terms, as either humanity's salvation or its end. Romero pushes back on that binary and questions whether AGI is even the right frame for what AI is becoming. A good entry point for his skeptical-but-engaged stance on long-horizon AI.
- "You Have No Idea How Screwed OpenAI Is" (2024/2025), https://www.thealgorithmicbridge.com/p/you-have-no-idea-how-screwed-openai
  A contrarian read on the competitive and financial fragility of the lab everyone assumes will dominate. Relevant for the futures question of which players actually control the next decade of AI, versus which are over-leveraged. Note: high-engagement opinion piece, read critically.
- Note on scenarios between extremes (2026), https://substack.com/@thealgorithmicbridge/note/c-252473413
  A short public note arguing that scenarios like "AI as normal technology," "what if AI stopped right now," or "what if it mastered code and nothing else" get almost no attention despite being plausible. Encapsulates his core futures angle: the neglected middle is where the real forecasting work is. Quick to cite, not a long essay.
