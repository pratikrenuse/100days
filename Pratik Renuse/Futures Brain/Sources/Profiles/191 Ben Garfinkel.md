## Who they are
Director (Executive Director) of the Centre for the Governance of AI (GovAI), a non-profit research and field-building organization on the opportunities and risks of AI, and a researcher at the University of Oxford. His work covers the security implications of AI, the causes of war, and the methodology of forecasting technology's impacts.

## Their future thesis (one line)
The classic arguments that advanced AI will seize power deserve far more scrutiny than they have received; AI's transformative effects are more likely to come through gradual economic and security disruption, which makes careful, empirically grounded governance more useful than dramatic doom narratives.

## Key future-relevant pieces
- "Ben Garfinkel on scrutinising classic AI risk arguments" (80,000 Hours podcast, 2020; re-released as a classic episode), https://80000hours.org/podcast/episodes/ben-garfinkel-classic-ai-risk-arguments/
  Argues that the standard existential-accident case, as laid out in Bostrom's Superintelligence and Yudkowsky's essays, has been under-scrutinized relative to the resources now flowing into the field. He contends that many arguments lean on fuzzy abstractions like optimization power and on toy thought experiments. The episode remains a touchstone for the more skeptical, slow-and-structural wing of AI risk thinking.

- "GovAI has Relaunched" (GovAI Blog), https://www.governance.ai/post/the-centre-for-the-governance-of-ai-has-relaunched
  Sets out GovAI's mission and structure as it spun out into an independent non-profit, building a field of rigorous AI governance research and talent. It frames governance as a discipline that must mature alongside AI capabilities. This piece defines the institutional vehicle through which his agenda operates.

- "Ben Garfinkel on AI Governance" (Hear This Idea podcast), https://hearthisidea.com/episodes/garfinkel/
  A wide-ranging conversation on how to think about AI's long-run effects on security, war, and power, and where governance research can actually add value. He emphasizes forecasting as a hard methodological problem and cautions against overconfidence in any single scenario. It captures his measured, analysis-first approach to the future.

- DPhil thesis on economic growth and international security (University of Oxford), https://www.benmgarfinkel.com/
  His doctoral research examines how economic growth underpins international security, the analytical lens he carries into AI. It informs his view that transformative technologies reshape geopolitics through growth and power balances, not only through sudden capability jumps. This grounds his governance work in international-relations theory.
