## Who they are
Distinguished Professor Emeritus in the Faculty of Environment at the University of Manitoba, Fellow of the Royal Society of Canada, and Member of the Order of Canada. By January 2026 he had published 51 books and more than 500 papers on energy, environment, food, and the history of technical advance. Bill Gates has said he reads everything Smil writes.

## Their future thesis (one line)
Energy transitions are inherently slow, grand, material processes, so rapid full decarbonization by 2050 is not realistic, and any honest forecast must start from the physical scale of the systems that feed, house, and move 8 billion people.

## Key future-relevant pieces
- "How the World Really Works" (2022), https://vaclavsmil.com/book/how-the-world-really-works/
  His most accessible statement of the realist case. He argues the modern world rests on four material pillars (ammonia, steel, concrete, plastics) that are still overwhelmingly made with fossil carbon, and that the inertia of this infrastructure means transitions take decades, not years. He calls complete decarbonization by 2050 conceivable only at the cost of an unacceptable global economic retreat.

- "Energy Transitions: Global and National Perspectives" (2017, expanded eds.), https://vaclavsmil.com/book/energy-transitions-global-and-national-perspectives/
  The scholarly backbone of his slow-transition argument. He shows historically that every prior shift (wood to coal, coal to oil) took 50 to 60 years to reach majority share, and warns that wind and solar face the same scaling and substitution frictions. This is the data source most often cited against techno-optimist timelines.

- "How to Feed the World" (2025), https://vaclavsmil.com/book/how-to-feed-the-world/
  Extends his realism to the food system, which he ties tightly to energy through fertilizer, mechanization, and transport. The core claim is that feeding humanity sustainably is a logistics and efficiency problem, not a slogan, and that synthetic nitrogen remains non-negotiable for now. French and other translations rolled out through 2026.

- "Speed: How It Explains the World" (2026), https://vaclavsmil.com/book/speed-how-it-explains-the-world/
  His newest book (US edition published by Penguin, June 2026). It challenges the assumption that faster is always better across production, travel, and communication, a frame that pushes back on accelerationist framings of technological change.

## Hooks for the brain
- Smil is the designated counterweight in any energy debate. Pair him directly against Tony Seba, Casey Handmer, and Ramez Naam to stress-test optimist timelines (the "Smil vs Seba" tension is a whole post).
- He is the original "energy realism" source. Useful when the brain needs the case that physical infrastructure, not policy or price curves, sets the speed limit.
- Hidden player worth tracking: Michael Cembalest of J.P. Morgan, who collaborates with Smil on the annual Energy Paper, a quietly influential document among finance and energy decision-makers.
- Post idea: "What Smil gets right that the optimists ignore, and what the optimists see that Smil misses." The disagreement is mostly about rate, not direction.
