## Who they are
**Role change, August 5, 2026:** co-founder of **Discovery Loop**, a public benefit company automating the scientific research loop. He is leaving Google after 27 years, where he was Chief Scientist of Google DeepMind and Google Research. Google's 30th employee, co-founder of Google Brain, co-creator of TensorFlow, and a primary technical architect behind the Gemini program. Also one of the most active angel investors in AI startups.

## Their future thesis (one line)
Within roughly a year we will have AI systems operating at the level of a junior ("virtual") engineer, and the next decade is about making extremely capable models cheap enough to deploy to billions of people. **Extended August 2026:** the highest-leverage use of that capability is to automate research itself, running the propose-run-evaluate-iterate loop thousands of times over, starting with machine learning and then pointing it at engineering, medicine, materials design and clean energy.

## Key future-relevant pieces
- "Google's Jeff Dean on the Coming Era of Virtual Engineers" (2025), https://sequoiacap.com/podcast/training-data-jeff-dean/
  Dean lays out his prediction that AI will reach junior-engineer competence within a year: not just writing code but running tests, debugging performance, reading documentation, and gaining "wisdom" through iterative experimentation in virtual environments. He frames specialized hardware, multimodality, and autonomous agents as the levers that get there. A useful primary articulation of his "virtual engineer" framing.

- "Owning the AI Pareto Frontier" (Latent Space interview, 2025), https://www.latent.space/p/jeffdean
  A long technical conversation on where frontier capability and cost efficiency meet. Dean argues the key strategic frontier is pushing capability up while driving inference cost down, so the same model can serve billions. Covers model architecture, hardware co-design, and the path past current scaling limits.

- "2024: A year of extraordinary progress and advancement in AI" (January 2025), https://research.google/blog/
  Dean's annual review-and-outlook letter on the Google Research blog. Surveys the year's advances across multimodal models, agents, science applications, and efficiency, and signals where he expects the research frontier to move next. His clearest written (rather than spoken) statement of direction.

- Stanford talk "Important Trends in AI: How Did We Get Here and What Can We Do Now?" (November 2025), referenced via https://research.google/people/jeff/
  A recurring lecture (also given at ETH Zurich and Khipu 2025) tracing the arc from early neural nets to today and forecasting near-term capability. Strong for understanding how he reasons about trajectory rather than single results.

## Recent (dated)
- **Departs Google to co-found Discovery Loop (August 5, 2026).** After 27 years he leaves with Sanjay Ghemawat (Google senior fellow, his collaborator of more than two decades), Oriol Vinyals (Google DeepMind VP of research) and Quoc Le (Google Brain co-founder). Discovery Loop is a public benefit company built to automate the research loop: propose an experiment, run it, evaluate it, iterate thousands of times. It starts by automating machine learning research with itself as first customer, then aims the same system at engineering, medicine, materials design and clean energy. Radical Ventures and Khosla Ventures are co-leading the seed round; the round is not closed and no valuation was disclosed. Google is a founding investor and cloud partner. (GeekWire / TechTimes / Radical Ventures, August 5, 2026)

## Hooks for the brain
- Discovery Loop is the cleanest live test of [[Biology Becomes a Search Problem]] outside biology: if the research loop itself becomes a product, discovery stops being a bottleneck of human attention.
- "Virtual engineer within a year" is a concrete, falsifiable near-term claim worth tracking against reality.
- Hidden-player angle: Dean as one of AI's most prolific angel investors (reportedly ~37 startups including Perplexity, Sakana AI, World Labs). His portfolio is a map of where an insider bets the frontier is going.
- Cost-efficiency-as-frontier thesis cuts against the "just scale bigger" narrative and is a distinct lens for a post.
- His hardware co-design view (TPUs, model-hardware co-evolution) is an underrated determinant of the 10-20 year compute landscape.
