## Who they are
Venture capitalist and contrarian thinker. Co-founder of PayPal, Palantir, and Founders Fund, and an early Facebook investor. Influential as a political donor and as a philosopher of stagnation, monopoly, and (recently) apocalyptic theology.

## Their future thesis (one line)
The West has been in technological and institutional stagnation since roughly the 1970s ("we wanted flying cars, instead we got 140 characters"), and the deepest risk now is a one-world regime of safety and control that he frames, in explicitly theological terms, as the Antichrist.

## Key future-relevant pieces
- "The End of the Future" (October 3, 2011), https://www.nationalreview.com/2011/10/end-future-peter-thiel/
  His foundational stagnation essay: outside of computing, real technological progress (energy, transport, biotech) has slowed dramatically since about 1970, and our self-image as a fast-moving society is mostly an illusion. He ties this to the collapse of middle-class expectations of generational improvement. This is the intellectual seed of the entire "Great Stagnation" debate and his later pro-growth politics.
- "Voyages to the End of the World" (November 2025, First Things, with Sam Wolfe), https://firstthings.com/voyages-to-the-end-of-the-world/
  A theological-literary essay asking whether science will summon or suppress the Antichrist, tracing a centuries-long debate through figures like Francis Bacon and Thomas Hobbes. It argues Bacon "enthroned the Antichrist" in his scientific utopia. It is the written companion to Thiel's private Antichrist lecture series.
- The Antichrist lectures (private series, 2025 to 2026; reported by AP, CNN, Fortune), https://www.cnn.com/2026/03/16/europe/peter-thiel-antichrist-lectures-rome-intl
  In closed talks in San Francisco, Paris, and (March 2026) Rome, Thiel argues the Antichrist is not an obvious villain but a comforting administrator who promises to halt risky innovation (especially AI) in the name of safety, while actually consolidating control. He cites figures like Greta Thunberg as examples of the "end of modernity" mood. The lectures are closed to media, so coverage is secondhand via journalists.

## Hooks for the brain
- "Flying cars vs 140 characters" is one of the most repeatable lines about stagnation; Thiel is its origin.
- His Antichrist framing reframes the entire AI-safety debate as a theological power struggle, a genuinely unusual angle few futurists touch.
- Hidden player: through Founders Fund and Palantir, Thiel is materially shaping defense, surveillance, and AI infrastructure, so his cultural essays and his capital point the same direction.
- The lectures being secret and only reported secondhand is itself a story: a billionaire shaping discourse through unpublished talks.
