## Who they are
Partner at FirstMark Capital investing in early-stage AI, ML, and data startups. Best known for the annual MAD (Machine Learning, AI and Data) Landscape market map and the MAD Podcast of long-form interviews with AI and data leaders.

## Their future thesis (one line)
AI in the mid-2020s is "bubbling and building" at once: real deployment of agentic, reasoning-driven systems is happening underneath the froth, and the constraints that will shape the next decade are data, energy, and economics as much as model capability.

## Key future-relevant pieces
- "Bubble and Build: The 2025 MAD (Machine Learning, AI and Data) Landscape" (October 2025), https://www.mattturck.com/mad2025
  The eleventh edition of his annual industry map, plus a long essay reading the state of the field. Argues 2025 is the year AI shifted from chatbots to systems that do work: agents with tools and memory wired into governed data and powered by reasoning models. He deliberately cut the map from 2,000+ logos to ~1,150 and flags energy, not just GPUs, as the pacing constraint. The single best annual snapshot of where the AI stack is consolidating.
- MAD Landscape archive and interactive map, https://mad.firstmark.com/
  The full searchable database of companies across the AI/ML/data stack, updated yearly. Valuable as a structural view of who occupies which layer, which matters for forecasting consolidation, commoditization, and which categories survive. Interactive, JavaScript-based.
- "Intelligence Isn't Enough: Why Energy and Compute Decide the AGI Race" (Eiso Kant), MAD Podcast (2025/2026), https://open.spotify.com/episode/3gPhaNzX6jigYOPQBasTF0
  A podcast conversation centering the argument that the AGI race is gated by energy and compute, not algorithms alone. Directly relevant to the 10-to-20-year question of physical and infrastructural limits on AI. Audio.
- MAD Podcast episode with Mostafa Dehghani (Google DeepMind) on "thinking in loops" and recursive self-improvement (2026), https://www.mattturck.com/podcast
  Explores the bottlenecks holding back true AGI, from evaluation limits and formal verification to the math of long-horizon reliability. Useful for understanding the technical ceilings researchers actually worry about. Audio; find on the podcast page.
