## Who they are
Founder and managing partner of a16z crypto (Andreessen Horowitz's crypto arm, the largest dedicated crypto venture franchise). Author of "Read Write Own" and one of the most influential proponents of the thesis that blockchains will rebuild the internet's economic layer.

## Their future thesis (one line)
The internet is entering a third era ("Read Write Own") where blockchains let users, creators, and developers own the networks they use, providing the only credible counterweight to a future where a handful of big tech companies (now supercharged by AI) control the entire digital economy.

## Key future-relevant pieces
- "Read Write Own: Building the Next Era of the Internet" (book, Jan 2024), https://readwriteown.com/
  His definitive statement: the internet went from "read" (early web) to "read-write" (social platforms) and should now move to "read-write-own" via blockchains. Argues corporate networks inevitably extract from their users once growth slows, while blockchain networks can keep commitments via code. The core framework behind everything he writes.

- "Why decentralization matters" (Feb 2018, foundational), https://cdixon.org/2018/02/18/why-decentralization-matters/
  The essay that crystallized the case for crypto as an antidote to platform power: centralized platforms court users then turn against them, whereas decentralized networks align incentives long-term. Predates the book but remains his clearest short argument. Essential background reading.

- "Blockchain innovation will put an AI-powered internet back into users' hands" (2025), https://a16zcrypto.com/posts/article/blockchain-ai-internet/
  His direct attempt to fuse the crypto and AI futures: as AI concentrates power in big model owners, blockchains can return ownership and economic upside to users and creators. Frames crypto plus AI as complementary rather than competing. The most future-forward piece tying his thesis to the AI era.

- "The World in 2025" (Wired UK op-ed, Dec 11, 2024)
  A near-term forecast (drawn from Read Write Own ideas) on how blockchains will decentralize an increasingly AI-driven internet. Compact statement of his expectations for the immediate years ahead. (Published at Wired UK; may be behind Wired's metered paywall.)

- "The next big thing will start out looking like a toy" (Jan 2010, evergreen), https://cdixon.org/2010/01/03/the-next-big-thing-will-start-out-looking-like-a-toy/
  His most-cited heuristic for spotting disruptive technology: the important new thing is usually dismissed as a toy because it underperforms incumbents on existing metrics while being better on a new dimension. A durable mental model for evaluating any emerging tech (crypto, AI agents, robotics). Timeless lens for a futures brain.

## Hooks for the brain
- "Read Write Own" is a clean three-act story of the internet that any audience can grasp: useful scaffolding for explaining why ownership/crypto matters in plain terms.
- His "toy" heuristic and "come for the tool, stay for the network" are evergreen frameworks for evaluating which 2026 toys become 2035 infrastructure. Strong for "what looks like a toy now" posts.
- Tension to mine: a16z is fundraising hard for crypto in 2026 while critics say "read write own" stalled into a "financial era." His rebuttal (the thesis is early, not wrong) is a live debate worth tracking.
