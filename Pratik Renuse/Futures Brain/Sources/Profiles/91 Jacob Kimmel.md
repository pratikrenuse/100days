## Who they are
Computational biologist, co-founder and CEO of NewLimit, the cellular reprogramming company he started in 2021 with Coinbase CEO Brian Armstrong and bioengineer Blake Byers. NewLimit pairs machine learning with genomics to design medicines that reverse the age of cells.

## Their future thesis (one line)
Aging is plastic at the cellular level, so reprogramming old cells back to a youthful state could both treat diseases of aging and preserve health in nearly everyone, an opportunity orders of magnitude larger than treating one disease at a time.

## Key future-relevant pieces
- "NewLimit raises $435M led by Founders Fund to bring longevity medicines to human trials" (June 2, 2026), https://blog.newlimit.com/p/newlimit-raises-435m-led-by-founders
  NewLimit closed a $435M Series C (reported valuation around $3.1B) led by Founders Fund, with Thrive, Greenoaks, Eli Lilly Ventures, and others. The money funds a first human trial, planned for next year, of a liver reprogramming therapy after a prototype reversed the age of old human liver cells. Kimmel says the company originally expected reprogramming to take more than a decade to reach humans but pulled the timeline forward on the strength of recent results. He frames aging medicines as potentially one of the few projects that benefit humanity for centuries.
- "How NewLimit discovers reprogramming medicines with AI and genomics" (NewLimit blog / technical posts, 2024 to 2026), https://blog.newlimit.com
  NewLimit's core method is to screen combinations of transcription factors at scale, read the effect on cells with single-cell genomics, and use machine learning to find factor sets that make old cells behave young without erasing cell identity. The same platform is being aimed across metabolic, vascular, and immune programs, with liver first. This is the clearest example of the "AI plus wet lab in a loop" model applied specifically to rejuvenation, distinct from a single hero molecule.
- Kimmel's prior academic work on partial reprogramming and cell-state machine learning (Calico and earlier, ongoing reference)
  Before NewLimit, Kimmel worked on quantitative models of cell state and aging, including time at Calico. That background frames NewLimit's bet: treat reprogramming as a controllable, measurable engineering problem rather than a vague rejuvenation promise. It is why the company leans so heavily on genomics readouts and ML rather than supplement-style claims.

## Hooks for the brain
- NewLimit is the most credible, best-funded pure-play on epigenetic reprogramming as medicine, and the liver-first strategy (a contained, regenerating organ with clear disease endpoints) is a smart way to get a real trial. Strong anchor for a "reprogramming race" thesis alongside Sinclair's Life Biosciences (eye) and chemical cocktail (whole body).
- The cap table is a who's-who of tech-into-bio capital: Founders Fund (Thiel), Thrive, Nat Friedman and Daniel Gross, plus Eli Lilly Ventures as a pharma signal. A post could track how Silicon Valley money is reshaping longevity timelines and risk appetite.
- Brian Armstrong (Coinbase) as co-founder ties crypto wealth directly to longevity, a recurring 2020s pattern (vitalism, the "death is wrong" crowd from the MIT Tech Review Vitalists piece). Worth noting the funder culture, not just the science.
- Concrete tripwire: NewLimit's first human liver trial is slated to start in 2027. That date, plus Life Biosciences' eye trial readout, makes 2026 to 2028 the window where reprogramming either delivers human signal or stalls. Track both.
