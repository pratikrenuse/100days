## Who they are
Benedict Evans is an independent technology analyst (formerly a partner at Andreessen Horowitz) who writes a widely read weekly newsletter and produces a twice-yearly "macro and strategic trends" presentation watched across tech, media, and finance. He is known for cool-headed, skeptical framing of hype cycles.

## Their future thesis (one line)
AI is a real platform shift, but for most general and consumer use models are converging and have no network effects, so "models will just be infra" and innovation (and value capture) moves up the stack, while the impact on jobs and which firms win is genuinely hard to predict in advance.

## Key future-relevant pieces
- "AI eats the world" (Spring 2026 presentation, May 2026), https://www.ben-evans.com/presentations
  His flagship twice-yearly deck on where AI is actually going. Key points: the big four hyperscalers plan to spend roughly $700B on AI infrastructure in 2026 (more than twice global telecom capex, approaching global oil and gas), models are converging with no network effects so they become commodity infrastructure, and value likely accrues up the stack (as it did to Meta, Apple, Google, Amazon during the mobile boom while telcos stayed flat). The single best snapshot of his current thinking.

- "Predicting AI job exposure" (May 24, 2026), https://www.ben-evans.com/benedictevans/2026/5/24/ai-job-exposure
  Argues that ranking which jobs, companies, and industries are most exposed to AI is mostly impossible: you cannot know how jobs will change, what else will change around them, or measure knowledge work cleanly enough. A bracing corrective to confident job-loss forecasts and a case for epistemic humility about the future of work.

- "The next 50 years of computing" (B2BMX keynote, 2026), https://www.ben-evans.com/presentations
  Evans situates AI within the long arc of computing platform shifts (mainframe, PC, web, mobile, AI), arguing each shift redistributes power and that the current one is early and unevenly understood. Useful for placing the 10-to-20-year horizon in historical context.

- "Essays" hub (ongoing), https://www.ben-evans.com/essays
  His running collection of strategic essays on AI, platform shifts, and value capture. Treat as a continuously updated tracker of his framing rather than a single dated piece (note: no RSS, so check the site directly).

## Hooks for the brain
- "Models will just be infra, innovation moves up the stack": Evans's sharpest claim, and it overlaps with Azeem Azhar. A cross-source consensus worth foregrounding.
- The mobile-capex analogy: huge infrastructure spend, value captured by platforms not infrastructure owners. A historical template for asking who actually wins from AI capex.
- "AI job exposure is unpredictable": a deliberate anti-forecast that sharpens how the brain should treat confident future-of-work claims.
- Deliberately disabled RSS to force site visits: a small but telling signal about his views on attention and distribution in the AI era.
