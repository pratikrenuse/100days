## Who they are
Co-founder and CEO of OpenAI, the lab behind ChatGPT and the GPT model line. Former president of Y Combinator. Among the most influential and most scrutinized figures shaping how AI reaches the mass market.

## Their future thesis (one line)
We have passed the event horizon into a "gentle singularity" where intelligence and energy become wildly abundant in the 2030s, transforming work and the economy gradually rather than in a single dramatic moment.

## Key future-relevant pieces
- "The Gentle Singularity" (June 2025), https://blog.samaltman.com/the-gentle-singularity
  Argues we are already past the takeoff point but experiencing it gradually, so wonders quickly become routine and then table stakes. Predicts systems that produce novel insights around 2026 and useful real-world robots around 2027, with the cost of intelligence converging toward the cost of electricity. Names "larval recursive self-improvement" (AI accelerating AI research) and concedes whole classes of jobs will disappear while the world gets richer fast enough to fund new policy.

- "Abundant Intelligence" (September 2025), https://blog.samaltman.com/abundant-intelligence
  Calls for making AI as universal as electricity and frames the bottleneck as physical infrastructure, not ideas. States the goal of a "factory" that can produce one gigawatt of new AI infrastructure every week, pitched as the Apollo program of this generation. Uses the line that with 10 gigawatts of compute AI might cure cancer or tutor every student, arguing compute scarcity forces brutal prioritization.

- "Three Observations" (February 2025), https://blog.samaltman.com/three-observations
  A short framework piece: the intelligence of an AI model roughly equals the log of resources used to train and run it, the cost to use a given level of AI falls about 10x every 12 months, and the value of linearly more intelligence is super-exponential. Used to justify aggressive, sustained investment and to argue progress will feel relentless.

- "Reflections" (January 2025), https://blog.samaltman.com/reflections
  Looks back on the ChatGPT era and forward to AGI, stating OpenAI is now confident it knows how to build AGI as traditionally understood and is beginning to turn toward superintelligence. Frames the next few years as the moment the bet either pays off or does not, and acknowledges the leadership turbulence of late 2023.
