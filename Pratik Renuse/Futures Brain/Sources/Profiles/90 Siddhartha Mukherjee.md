## Who they are
Pulitzer-winning physician, oncologist, and author at Columbia University, known for The Emperor of All Maladies (2010), The Gene (2016), and The Song of the Cell (2022). In 2025 he co-founded the AI drug-discovery startup Manas AI with Reid Hoffman, and he was named to the 2026 TIME100 Health list.

## Their future thesis (one line)
Medicine is entering a "new human" era built on cells and genes as therapy, with AI compressing drug discovery and continuous data turning each patient into a monitored, predictable system, while the same tools raise hard questions about overdiagnosis and what counts as disease.

## Key future-relevant pieces
- "The Song of the Cell: An Exploration of Medicine and the New Human" (2022, ongoing reference), https://www.simonandschuster.com/books/The-Song-of-the-Cell/Siddhartha-Mukherjee/9781982117368
  Mukherjee traces medicine's shift from treating organs to engineering cells, covering CAR-T cancer therapies, stem cells, and the prospect of cellular and genetic engineering that could make hereditary disease a thing of the past. He frames the coming decades as the age of the "new human," where we build and deploy living cells as medicine. This is the canonical text for his view that cell and gene therapy, not just small molecules, define the future of treatment.
- "Manas AI launches to transform discovery of novel cancer medicines" (announced January to February 2025), https://manasai.co/news/manas-ai-launches-to-transform-discovery-of-novel-cancer-medicines
  Mukherjee and Reid Hoffman launched Manas AI with about $24.6M in seed funding to build a full-stack AI pipeline from target identification through clinical trials, starting with aggressive cancers like prostate, lymphoma, and triple-negative breast cancer. Mukherjee says the platform has virtually screened hundreds of thousands of candidate molecules, some chemically unimaginable before, with plans to reach millions. It is his concrete bet that generative chemistry plus biology will reshape how cancer drugs are found.
- "Early Warnings: New technologies promise to catch more cancers sooner, but such screening can pose hidden hazards" (The New Yorker, June 23, 2025), https://www.newyorker.com/contributors/siddhartha-mukherjee
  Mukherjee examines multi-cancer early-detection blood tests, which could revolutionize prevention, against their real risks: false positives, overdiagnosis, and unequal access. He argues that catching more cancers sooner is not automatically better and that the technology needs careful evidence before mass deployment. The essay is a model of how he holds promise and peril together, relevant to the whole "screen everything" future of medicine.

- Mukherjee is the rare figure who is both a frontline cancer doctor and a builder (Manas AI, Immuneel), which makes him a credible bridge between "AI will transform drug discovery" optimism and clinical reality. Strong anchor for an AI-plus-cancer node.

- His New Yorker essays are the best place to watch him reason about the downside of new biotech (overdiagnosis, screening harms, equity). Good counterbalance to pure techno-optimism, and a recurring source for nuance.
