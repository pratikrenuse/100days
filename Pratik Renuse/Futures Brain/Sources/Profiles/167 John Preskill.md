## Who they are
The Richard P. Feynman Professor of Theoretical Physics at Caltech and director of its quantum information institute (IQIM). He coined the term "NISQ" for today's noisy intermediate-scale quantum machines and is one of the most cited voices framing where quantum computing is headed.

## Their future thesis (one line)
Quantum computing is moving beyond the noisy NISQ era toward error-corrected "megaquop" machines that run millions of reliable operations, with broad practical value still years out but approaching.

## Key future-relevant pieces
- "Beyond NISQ: The Megaquop Machine" (February 2025, preprint; published April 2025), https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.17368
  Preskill's essay defining the "megaquop" machine, one capable of roughly a million reliable quantum operations, as the next milestone past NISQ. He argues such a system, running on the order of 100 logical qubits at circuit depth near 10,000, could do useful things no classical or NISQ device can, while cautioning that truly transformative applications need far more.

- "Quantum Computing in the NISQ era and beyond" (2018), https://arxiv.org/abs/1801.00862
  The foundational paper that coined "NISQ" and set expectations for an era of imperfect, error-prone quantum machines. It remains the reference point for understanding why quantum error correction, not just more qubits, is the real barrier to practical quantum advantage.

- "Our Quantum Future" (APS Global Physics Summit, March 2025), https://preskill.caltech.edu/talks/MAR-EV40-Preskill-2025.pdf
  A keynote slide deck laying out his long-range view of where quantum information science is heading. It connects near-term error-correction progress to the longer arc of fault-tolerant computing and the scientific payoffs Preskill expects over the next decade-plus.

- "Move Aside NISQ: John Preskill Sets New Quantum Target, the Megaquop Machine" (December 2024), https://www.hpcwire.com/2024/12/16/move-aside-nisq-john-preskill-sets-new-quantum-target-the-megaquop-machine/
  HPCwire's accessible writeup of Preskill's Q2B keynote introducing the megaquop target. It is a useful plain-language entry point for the shift in how the field frames its next goalpost, away from raw qubit counts toward reliable logical operations.
