## Who they are
Alexander C. Karp is co-founder and CEO of Palantir Technologies, the data and AI software company widely used by Western defense and intelligence agencies. He holds a doctorate in social theory from Frankfurt and co-wrote "The Technological Republic" with Nicholas Zamiska.

## Their future thesis (one line)
The West must rebuild the partnership between Silicon Valley and the national security state, and embrace AI-enabled weapons, or it will lose its hard-power and technological supremacy to authoritarian rivals over the coming decades.

## Key future-relevant pieces
- "The Technological Republic: Hard Power, Soft Belief, and the Future of the West" (February 18, 2025), https://techrepublicbook.com/
  Karp and Zamiska argue that American technologists abandoned national purpose in favor of consumer apps, leaving a dangerous gap as rivals militarize AI. They call for engineers and companies to recommit to defense and to a shared civic mission. The book is Karp's manifesto for how Western technology and statecraft should fuse in the AI era.

- "Our Oppenheimer Moment: The Creation of A.I. Weapons" (July 25, 2023), https://www.nytimes.com/2023/07/25/opinion/karp-palantir-artificial-intelligence.html
  Karp argues the West cannot afford to pause or cede development of AI-enabled weapons, comparing the moment to the dawn of nuclear arms. He contends that responsible deployment, paired with regulation, is safer than abstention that hands the advantage to adversaries. It crystallizes his case that AI militarization is inevitable and must be led by democracies.

- Palantir shareholder letters (ongoing, quarterly), https://www.palantir.com/
  In his quarterly letters Karp frames Palantir's commercial momentum as inseparable from a civilizational argument about Western strength, AI adoption, and software eating defense. The letters double as essays on geopolitics, culture, and the future role of AI in institutions. They are the most regular window into his evolving worldview.
