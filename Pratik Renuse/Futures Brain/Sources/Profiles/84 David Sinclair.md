## Who they are
Tenured professor of genetics at Harvard Medical School and co-director of the Paul F. Glenn Center for Biology of Aging Research. He is the field's highest-profile and most polarizing voice, author of Lifespan (2019), and co-founder of Life Biosciences.

## Their future thesis (one line)
Aging is a loss of epigenetic information that can be reversed, and within a decade a doctor could prescribe a reprogramming drug that makes the whole body measurably years younger.

## Key future-relevant pieces
- "David Sinclair plans to test whole-body rejuvenation drugs in the XPrize competition" (June 9, 2026), https://www.technologyreview.com/2026/06/09/1138545/david-sinclair-plans-to-test-whole-body-rejuvenation-drugs-in-the-xprize-competition/
  Sinclair confirmed plans to give volunteers an oral "chemical reprogramming" cocktail, code-named SL-100, as part of the $101M XPRIZE Healthspan competition, which rewards teams that can restore a person to a 10-years-younger state across immune, cognitive, and muscle measures. Chemical reprogramming uses small molecules to mimic the embryonic reprogramming genes, with the advantage that drugs travel the bloodstream and can reach the whole body, unlike gene therapy. Other scientists are skeptical: rival labs report whole-animal chemical rejuvenation has so far been toxic or ineffective, and the data behind SL-100 is unpublished. The piece doubles as a map of the field, naming XPRIZE finalists due August 2026 and the standardization-of-aging-measurement problem as the real bottleneck.
- "The first human test of a rejuvenation method will begin shortly" (Life Biosciences trial, January 27, 2026), https://www.technologyreview.com/2026/01/27/1131796/the-first-human-test-of-a-rejuvenation-method-will-begin-shortly/
  Life Biosciences, co-founded by Sinclair, won approval for a first-in-human Phase 1 trial of partial epigenetic reprogramming using three Yamanaka factors delivered to the eye, aiming to restore vision in glaucoma and NAION. This is the first time partial reprogramming has moved from animals into a regulated human trial. The eye is the proving ground because it is contained and observable; success would be the strongest signal yet that resetting epigenetic marks can rejuvenate human tissue. The company announced it treated its first patient in June 2026.
- "Lifespan: Why We Age and Why We Don't Have To" (2019, ongoing reference), https://www.lifespanpodcast.com
  Sinclair's book set out his Information Theory of Aging: aging is driven by the cell losing track of which genes to express (epigenetic noise) rather than by DNA damage itself. It popularized partial cellular reprogramming and reframed aging as a treatable condition rather than an inevitability. The thesis remains the intellectual backbone of his current drug and gene-therapy bets, so it is the canonical starting text for his worldview.
