## Who they are
Associate Professor at UC Berkeley (deep RL and robotic learning) and co-founder and a lead scientist of Physical Intelligence, a robotics-foundation-model startup that has raised over $1.1B at roughly a $5.6B valuation. His lab pioneered deep reinforcement learning for robotic manipulation.

## Their future thesis (one line)
General-purpose robot foundation models, trained like LLMs but on diverse embodied data, will trigger a "self-improvement flywheel," with fully autonomous household robots plausibly arriving around 2030.

## Key future-relevant pieces
- "Fully autonomous robots are much closer than you think" (Dwarkesh Podcast, September 2025), https://www.dwarkesh.com/p/sergey-levine
  Levine argues robotics is approaching a self-improvement flywheel where better models collect better data which trains still-better models. His median estimate for robots that can run a household fully autonomously is around 2030. He details why scaling both data and hardware is the gating problem. His most quotable timeline statement.

- "pi-zero: A Foundation Model for Robotics" (with TWIML, late 2025), https://open.spotify.com/episode/6YgfanrTvBKpHVrAEueBnN
  Discussion of pi-zero, a general robot model pairing a vision-language model with a diffusion-based "action expert," trained with broad real-world data across pre-training and post-training. The thesis is that the foundation-model recipe transfers from text to physical action. Core technical basis for his broader claims.

- pi-0.7 "general-purpose robot brain" coverage (April 2026), https://techcrunch.com/2026/04/16/physical-intelligence-a-hot-robotics-startup-says-its-new-robot-brain-can-figure-out-tasks-it-was-never-taught/
  Physical Intelligence's newer model is described as an early but meaningful step toward a robot brain that can be pointed at an unfamiliar task, coached in plain language, and actually perform it. It demonstrates generalization to tasks the robot was never explicitly trained on. Strong evidence for the "one brain, any robot" direction.

- Physical Intelligence blog and research (ongoing), https://www.physicalintelligence.company/blog
  The company's running technical posts on building general robot policies. They lay out the data-mixture and scaling strategy behind each model release. Best primary source for tracking the flywheel claim against actual capability.
