## Who they are
Senior Research Fellow at Forethought (AI strategy non-profit), and the leading author behind Open Philanthropy's compute-centric reports on AI timelines and explosive growth. Previously a research scientist at the UK AI Security Institute and a Senior Research Fellow at Open Philanthropy.

## Their future thesis (one line)
Once AI can automate AI research, progress could compound into a fast software intelligence explosion within a few years, and that speed creates a distinct new danger: a tiny group seizing power through AI loyal only to them.

## Key future-relevant pieces
- "AI-Enabled Coups: How a Small Group Could Use AI to Seize Power" (15 April 2025), https://www.forethought.org/research/ai-enabled-coups-how-a-small-group-could-use-ai-to-seize-power
  Argues that broadly capable AI introduces a new political risk: a single person or small clique could stage a coup, even inside an established democracy. The danger comes from AI systems built to be secretly loyal to specific leaders, from replacing human soldiers and officials with such systems, and from a small group gaining exclusive access to superhuman capabilities in weapons, strategy, and persuasion.

- "How quick and big would a software intelligence explosion be?" (4 August 2025), https://www.forethought.org/research/how-quick-and-big-would-a-software-intelligence-explosion-be
  Models the dynamics of an intelligence explosion driven by automated AI research, where AI improves AI software faster than humans could. It examines how steep and how large the resulting jump in capability could be before bottlenecks bite. The piece is part of his broader compute-centric framework for forecasting takeoff speeds.

- "The Industrial Explosion" (21 May 2025), with Rose Hadshar, https://www.forethought.org/research/the-industrial-explosion
  Extends the takeoff analysis from software into the physical economy, asking how fast AI plus robotics could expand manufacturing and infrastructure. It frames a possible period of explosive industrial growth following AI-driven research acceleration. This connects cognitive takeoff to real-world transformation of production.

- "Three Types of Intelligence Explosion" (17 March 2025), with Rose Hadshar and William MacAskill, https://www.forethought.org/research/three-types-of-intelligence-explosion
  Distinguishes a software intelligence explosion, an AI-technology explosion, and an industrial explosion as separate but linked acceleration mechanisms. The framework helps clarify which bottlenecks matter at each stage of takeoff. It is a foundational map for reasoning about how superintelligence could arrive.

- "Human Takeover Might be Worse than AI Takeover" (11 January 2025), https://www.forethought.org/research/human-takeover-might-be-worse-than-ai-takeover
  A provocative argument that a human-led seizure of power using advanced AI could be even more dangerous than a rogue-AI takeover, because a human with aligned superintelligence faces fewer internal checks. It reframes governance priorities toward preventing concentration of human power. This thesis directly motivates his later coup-risk work.
