## Who they are
Economist and senior fellow / chief economist at the Foundation for American Innovation (formerly Director of Social Policy at the Niskanen Center), writing the Substack Second Best. As of 2026 he is a leading voice on how advanced AI reshapes the state, institutions, and political economy, bridging AI policy and conservative-adjacent governance circles.

## Their future thesis (one line)
AI is a regime-changing technology that could break the institutional foundations of the modern liberal-democratic state, so the central question is whether governments can modernize and adapt fast enough to avoid a drift toward "techno-feudalism" or centralized control.

## Key future-relevant pieces
- "Ninety-five theses on AI" (May 7, 2024), https://www.secondbest.ca/p/ninety-five-theses-on-ai
  A sweeping, numbered manifesto of his core claims about where AI is heading, deliberately echoing Luther's theses to signal an institutional reformation. It spans timelines, economics, governance, and the philosophical stakes, arguing that AI forces a rethink of nearly every institution we take for granted. The single best overview of his worldview in one place.

- "AI and Leviathan: Part I" (Aug 23, 2023), https://www.secondbest.ca/p/ai-and-leviathan-part-i
  Opens his signature trilogy on the institutional economics of an intelligence explosion, asking what AI does to the bargain between states and citizens. He argues that the competitive advantage democracies held in the industrial era may have been an artifact of that technology, and AI could shift the balance toward more centralized regimes. Foundational reading for the "AI versus the state" thesis. (Followed by Part II, "Preparing for regime change," Aug 28 2023, and Part III, "A timeline of our techno-feudalist future," Sep 11 2023.)

- "Why AGI is closer than you think" (Sep 22, 2023), https://www.secondbest.ca/p/why-agi-is-closer-than-you-think
  Makes the case for relatively short AGI timelines and argues policymakers and institutions are nowhere near ready for the disruption. He ties near-term capability gains to concrete governance and labor-market shocks. Useful as his explicit timelines statement, on the faster end of the spectrum.

- "Time to take AI consciousness seriously" (May 27, 2025), https://www.secondbest.ca/p/time-to-take-ai-consciousness-seriously
  Argues that frontier models like Claude and ChatGPT may already have something like subjective experience, and that we should start treating AI moral status as a live question rather than science fiction. He connects this to the political and legal frameworks that would have to follow. A frontier-of-the-debate piece on a question that becomes load-bearing in any future with billions of AI agents.

## Hooks for the brain
- The "AI versus Leviathan" thesis is genuinely distinctive: most futurists focus on jobs or doom, Hammond focuses on what AI does to the nation-state itself, which is underexplored territory.
- His claim that democratic advantage was an "artifact of industrial technology" is a provocative, contrarian frame for a post on whether AI favors open or authoritarian systems.
- Hammond is a connector between AI safety thinking and Washington policy / right-of-center circles (FAI, formerly Niskanen), making him a bridge node for the politics-of-AI angle.
