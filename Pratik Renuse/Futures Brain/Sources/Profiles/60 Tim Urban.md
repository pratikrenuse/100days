## Who they are
Writer and illustrator behind Wait But Why, the long-form stick-figure blog that popularized deep explainers on AI, space, and the human condition. As of 2026 he posts infrequently, mixing personal essays with big-picture pieces, and his 2015 AI series remains a canonical on-ramp for thinking about superintelligence.

## Their future thesis (one line)
Artificial superintelligence is plausibly coming this century and could mean either humanity's immortality or its extinction, so the stakes of how we build it dwarf almost everything else.

## Key future-relevant pieces
- "The AI Revolution: The Road to Superintelligence" (Jan 2015), https://waitbutwhy.com/2015/01/artificial-intelligence-revolution-1.html
  Part 1 of his two-part series walks a general reader from narrow AI to artificial general intelligence and explains why recursive self-improvement could make progress feel impossibly fast once it starts. He uses the staircase-of-progress and exponential-growth framing to argue that human intuition badly underrates how quickly an intelligence explosion could arrive. It is the single most-shared introduction to the topic for non-specialists.

- "The AI Revolution: Our Immortality or Extinction" (Jan 2015), https://waitbutwhy.com/2015/01/artificial-intelligence-revolution-2.html
  Part 2 lays out the two-sided bet: a superintelligence aligned with human goals could end disease and death, while a misaligned one could wipe us out almost as a side effect. Urban frames this as the most important fork in human history and stresses that small early choices in how we build AI compound into radically different endpoints. The piece remains his clearest statement of the high-variance future.

- "Neuralink and the Brain's Magical Future" (Apr 2017), https://waitbutwhy.com/2017/04/neuralink.html
  A very long explainer on brain-computer interfaces, built around Elon Musk's Neuralink, arguing that direct neural links may be humanity's way to stay relevant alongside advancing AI. He frames merging with machines as a possible answer to the alignment and obsolescence problem. Useful as a future-of-human-augmentation reference even though the company specifics have aged.

- "What's Our Problem?" (2023), https://waitbutwhy.com/whatsourproblem
  His self-published book reframes societal dysfunction as a contest between "high-rung" (independent, truth-seeking) and "low-rung" (tribal) thinking, arguing that our ability to navigate powerful future technologies depends on the quality of our collective reasoning. The thesis is less about a specific technology and more about whether our information ecosystem can stay sane enough to make good choices. Relevant to any "can we govern the future" angle.

## Hooks for the brain
- The "immortality or extinction" binary is a clean, high-variance framing for any post on AI stakes, and it predates the current frontier-lab race by nearly a decade.
- Urban is a translator, not an original researcher: his value is making expert futures legible to mass audiences, so he is a bridge node between thinkers like Bostrom and the general public.
- His shift since 2023 toward personal/travel essays (Bhutan, parenting) over tech is itself a signal: even the great AI explainers have stepped back from the frontier commentary, leaving the field to Substack specialists.
- Post idea: revisit his 2015 timeline predictions against 2026 reality (the affordable brain-scale computer call) as a case study in how futurist forecasts age.
