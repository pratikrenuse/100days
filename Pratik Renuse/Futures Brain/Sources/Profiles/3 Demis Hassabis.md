## Who they are
**Role change, August 5, 2026:** now **Chair of Google DeepMind and Chief Scientist of Alphabet**, stepping back from the CEO seat he founded. Koray Kavukcuoglu becomes SVP of Google DeepMind, reporting to Sundar Pichai, and now owns Gemini model development, frontier research, and the Gemini app and developer teams. Hassabis remains a 2024 Nobel laureate in Chemistry for AlphaFold, a neuroscientist and former game designer who frames AI as a tool to accelerate science itself. The move puts him closer to the research agenda and further from the product one, which is worth noting given his AI-for-science thesis.

## Their future thesis (one line)
AGI is roughly three to four years away (around 2030) and represents a "species-level transition" that could deliver something like ten times the impact of the Industrial Revolution at ten times the speed, demanding extreme care.

## Key future-relevant pieces
- Google I/O 2026 remarks and interviews on AGI timelines (May 2026), https://www.axios.com/2026/05/26/deepmind-ceo-demis-hassabis
  States he still broadly expects AGI around 2030 but now sees 2029 as possible, reflecting growing confidence the field has found the right technical path. Stresses that agents are visibly improving year over year and will become far more useful. Pairs the optimism with a warning that this power demands rigorous oversight.

- "AI is a species-level transition" interview (May 2026), https://stanforddaily.com/2026/05/29/deepmind-ceo-warns-about-ai/
  Frames the next decade as a moment that leaves humanity little margin for error and calls for careful navigation. Notable as one of his more cautionary public framings, distinct from pure capability hype. Useful as the "scientist who is bullish but worried" voice.

- "AGI 10x the Industrial Revolution" remarks (2026), https://finance.yahoo.com/news/demis-hassabis-predicts-agi-10x-143113425.html
  The signature soundbite: AGI as roughly ten times the impact of the Industrial Revolution but unfolding over a decade rather than a century. Used to convey both the scale and the compressed timeline he expects. Good for sizing the magnitude of change against historical baselines.

- "The skills humans need in the AGI era" interview (2026), https://www.fastcompany.com/91563254/google-deepmind-ceo-says-these-are-the-skills-that-will-set-humans-apart-from-ai
  Discusses what will distinguish humans once AGI is widespread, emphasizing meta-skills like learning to learn, adaptability, and creativity. Directly relevant to the "what should people do in 10 to 20 years" question. Practical and human-facing rather than purely technical.

## Recent (dated)
- **Moves to Chair of Google DeepMind and Chief Scientist of Alphabet (August 5 to 6, 2026).** Koray Kavukcuoglu takes the operating role as SVP of Google DeepMind. Announced alongside Jeff Dean's departure to found Discovery Loop. Alphabet shares fell roughly 4% to 5% on the news. Read together, the two moves say the same thing: the people who built the research engine are separating from the people who ship the product. (Bloomberg / Axios, August 5, 2026; Time, August 6, 2026; Google blog "The next chapter of our AI momentum")
- John Jumper, who led AlphaFold under Hassabis and shared the 2024 Nobel in Chemistry with him, announced he is leaving Google DeepMind for Anthropic after nearly nine years; reports followed that Gemini researchers Jonas Adler and Alexander Pritzel are also moving to Anthropic. (June 19 to 24, 2026, TechCrunch / Bloomberg) A notable science-talent loss for DeepMind and a sign AI-for-science is the next contested front.

## Hooks for the brain
- His Nobel for AlphaFold makes him the strongest "AI for science" case, distinct from chatbot-centric narratives; angle: the futures where AI's biggest impact is in the lab, not the office.
- The "species-level transition, little margin for error" line is a rare cautionary note from a lab CEO, pairs well against Altman's gentle framing.
- Hidden player angle: Isomorphic Labs (his drug-discovery spinout) is the concrete bet on AI compressing medicine, underexplored versus the model releases.
- "Learning to learn" as the durable human skill is a usable thesis for a post on careers and education in the AGI era.
