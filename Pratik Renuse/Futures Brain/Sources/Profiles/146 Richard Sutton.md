## Who they are
Pioneer of reinforcement learning, co-author of the standard RL textbook, and 2024 ACM Turing Award winner (with Andrew Barto) for the foundations of RL. A vocal critic of the large language model paradigm, he argues true intelligence must come from agents learning continually through their own experience.

## Their future thesis (one line)
General methods that scale with computation (search and learning from experience) always beat human-knowledge-engineered shortcuts, so the next era of AI will be built on agents that learn continually from interaction with the world, not from static internet data and next-token prediction.

## Key future-relevant pieces
- "The Bitter Lesson" (March 13, 2019), http://www.incompleteideas.net/IncIdeas/BitterLesson.html
  A short, hugely influential essay arguing that across 70 years of AI, methods that leverage massive computation (search and learning) consistently outperform approaches that hand-code human knowledge. The lesson is bitter because researchers keep trying to bake in human priors and keep getting outscaled. It is the intellectual license for the entire scale-everything era, even as Sutton now disputes how the industry applies it.
- "Welcome to the Era of Experience" (April 2025), https://storage.googleapis.com/deepmind-media/Era-of-Experience%20/The%20Era%20of%20Experience%20Paper.pdf
  Co-authored with David Silver, this paper argues progress from learning on human-generated data is plateauing and that the next generation of agents will gain superhuman ability by learning predominantly from their own experience. It points to systems like AlphaProof that generate their own training data through interaction. A preprint chapter for the MIT Press book "Designing an Intelligence", it is the manifesto for experience-driven AI.
- Dwarkesh Podcast: "Father of RL thinks LLMs are a dead end" (2025), https://www.dwarkesh.com/p/richard-sutton
  In this interview Sutton argues that LLMs are a sophisticated dead end for building genuinely intelligent systems because they imitate human text rather than learn from grounded experience and rewards. He calls for agents that learn on the job, continually, from interaction with an environment. The sharpest available statement of the experience-versus-imitation split.
- Oak Lab launch (announced ~July 15, 2026), https://the-decoder.com/turing-award-winner-rich-sutton-founds-oak-lab-to-build-ai-agents-that-learn-on-their-own/
  He put the thesis into a company. Oak Lab is built on the OaK (Options and Knowledge) architecture from the Alberta Plan for AI Research (Sutton, Bowling, Pilarski), targeting agents that learn in real time from experience without storing or replaying data. Stated ambition: a one-trillion-parameter agent, "Oak," making decisions on roughly 20 watts. In the launch coverage he calls current deep learning "weak and inefficient" and says it needs "not more tweaks, but fundamentally new ideas and a thorough reworking." This converts him from a critic with an essay into a competitor with a lab, which raises the stakes on the experience-versus-imitation split. (TechTimes, The Decoder, BetaKit, heise, July 15, 2026)
- 2024 Turing Award citation and remarks (announced March 2025), https://x.com/RichardSSutton/status/1897356290474832042
  The ACM honored Sutton and Barto for the conceptual and algorithmic foundations of reinforcement learning. In his reaction he ties the award to reviving Turing's own idea of machines that learn from experience, an idea he calls essential but still nascent. Establishes his authority and frames experiential learning as unfinished business, not a solved approach.
