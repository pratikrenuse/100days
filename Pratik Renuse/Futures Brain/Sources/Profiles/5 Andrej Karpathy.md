## Their future thesis (one line)
This is the "decade of agents," not the year of agents: AGI is still roughly ten years away, and software is entering a "Software 3.0" era where you program models in English and orchestrate fallible agents rather than write all the code yourself.

## Key future-relevant pieces
- Dwarkesh Patel interview, "AGI is still a decade away" (October 2025), https://www.dwarkesh.com/p/andrej-karpathy
  A 2 hour 25 minute conversation arguing the industry chronically over-predicts, and that capable agents will take a decade to mature, hence "decade of agents" as a deliberate correction to "year of agents." Explains why today's models have "jagged intelligence" (superhuman in spots, brittle in others) and what still has to be solved. High-signal counterweight to aggressive 2027-ish AGI timelines.

- "Software 3.0" (Sequoia AI Ascent talk and blog summary) (June 2026), https://karpathy.bearblog.dev/sequoia-ascent-2026/
  Lays out three eras: Software 1.0 (explicit code), Software 2.0 (training neural nets on datasets), Software 3.0 (programming models with natural-language prompts). Argues LLMs are a new kind of computer and operating system, and that the durable skill is orchestrating agents around things humans can verify (tests, proofs, scores). Frames a long transition rather than an overnight replacement of programmers.

- "2025 LLM Year in Review" (late 2025 / early 2026), https://karpathy.bearblog.dev/year-in-review-2025/
  His annual synthesis of where models actually stand versus the narrative, separating real capability gains from hype. Useful as a calibrated baseline for what changed in a year and what is still unsolved. Reflects his "agentic engineering" framing of coordinating AI coding agents at professional quality.

- "Software 2.0" (2017), https://karpathy.medium.com/software-2-0-a64152b37c35
  The original essay that reframed neural networks as a new way of writing software, where you curate data and let optimization write the program. The foundational idea that his 2026 "Software 3.0" extends. Worth citing as the origin point of the whole framework.
