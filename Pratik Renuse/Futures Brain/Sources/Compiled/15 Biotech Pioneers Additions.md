## 192. George Church

#source
Domain: Biotech and longevity
Verified: June 2026

### Read them
- Homepage: https://wyss.harvard.edu/team/core-faculty/george-church/
- Blog / where published / org: Wyss Institute at Harvard (Founding Core Faculty, Lead for Synthetic Biology); Harvard Medical School (Robert Winthrop Professor of Genetics); Church Lab
- X / social: LinkedIn https://www.linkedin.com/in/george-church-2b86301
- Podcast / other: Co-founder and Lead Genetics Advisor at Colossal Biosciences (https://colossal.com); frequent podcast guest (FoundMyFitness, Wyss Ignition Sequence)
- Paywall: no

### Who they are
Pioneering geneticist at Harvard and the Wyss Institute who helped launch the Human Genome Project, developed early genome sequencing methods, and has co-founded around 50 biotech companies. He co-founded Colossal Biosciences with Ben Lamm to pursue de-extinction of the woolly mammoth and other species.

### Their future thesis (one line)
Reading and writing genomes at scale will let us reverse aging, eradicate genetic disease, and even revive extinct species, with longevity escape velocity plausibly reachable around 2035 if therapies are fast-tracked.

### Key future-relevant pieces
- "The End of Aging and Extinction: George Church on Longevity, Dire Wolves, and Woolly Mammoths" (22 July 2025), https://wyss.harvard.edu/media-post/the-end-of-aging-and-extinction-george-church-ph-d-on-longevity-dire-wolves-and-woolly-mammoths/
  In this Wyss Ignition Sequence interview, Church explores gene therapy, de-extinction, and the future of human health. He argues we cannot rule out reaching longevity escape velocity by 2035, the point where new therapies extend life faster than time passes. He links the same genome-engineering toolkit to reviving species like the dire wolf and the woolly mammoth.

- "George Church: Learn from COVID and fast-track therapies that reverse aging" (Longevity.Technology), https://longevity.technology/news/george-church-learn-from-covid-and-fast-track-therapies-that-reverse-aging/
  Church argues that the rapid COVID vaccine effort shows how aging therapies could be accelerated if regulators and funders treated aging as urgent. He advocates combination gene therapies that target multiple age-related pathways at once. The piece captures his conviction that aging is an engineering problem we can fast-track.

- Colossal Biosciences dire wolf announcement (April 2025), https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colossal_Biosciences
  Colossal, which Church co-founded, announced gene-edited wolf pups carrying dire-wolf traits, reconstructed using ancient DNA from a 13,000-year-old tooth and a 72,000-year-old skull. The company frames de-extinction as a tool for conservation and ecosystem restoration. It is the first high-profile demonstration of Church's de-extinction vision.

- Colossal woolly mammoth program, on track for 2028, https://www.denofgeek.com/culture/colossal-woolly-mammoth-on-track-2028-de-extinction-evolves/
  Colossal aims to produce woolly mammoth hybrid calves by 2028 by editing mammoth-like traits into Asian elephants, with reintroduction to Arctic tundra as a goal. A 2025 milestone produced gene-edited "woolly mice" carrying cold-adaptation traits. This is the flagship long-horizon project of Church's de-extinction agenda.

### Hooks for the brain
- Feeds the "longevity and aging reversal" thesis: gene therapy and the possibility of longevity escape velocity by ~2035.
- Feeds the "synthetic biology platform" thesis: cheap genome reading and writing as a general-purpose technology for medicine and conservation.
- Feeds the "de-extinction and ecosystem engineering" thesis: reviving lost species and reshaping ecosystems.

## 193. Jennifer Doudna

#source
Domain: Biotech and longevity
Verified: June 2026

### Read them
- Homepage: https://innovativegenomics.org/people/jennifer-doudna/
- Blog / where published / org: Innovative Genomics Institute (IGI), which she founded and directs; UC Berkeley (https://vcresearch.berkeley.edu/faculty/jennifer-doudna)
- X / social: IGI channels (https://innovativegenomics.org)
- Podcast / other: Berkeley Talks; Aspen Ideas (https://www.aspenideas.org/speakers/jennifer-doudna)
- Paywall: no (IGI and Berkeley content free; some press coverage such as Forbes is partial)

### Who they are
Nobel laureate biochemist who shared the 2020 Chemistry prize for co-developing CRISPR-Cas9 genome editing, and founder and Director of the Innovative Genomics Institute, a Berkeley and UCSF collaboration applying genome editing to health, agriculture, and climate. She will receive the 2026 Priestley Medal, the American Chemical Society's highest honor.

### Their future thesis (one line)
Over the next decade CRISPR will move from rare, costly bespoke treatments to scalable, affordable, personalized gene-editing therapies, and the same toolkit will help tackle disease, agriculture, and climate at population scale.

### Key future-relevant pieces
- "Gene editing has struggled to go commercial. This Nobel laureate has a $1 billion plan to fix that" (Forbes, 17 February 2026), https://www.forbes.com/sites/amyfeldman/2026/02/17/gene-editing-has-struggled-to-go-commercial-this-nobel-laureate-has-a-1-billion-plan-to-fix-that/
  Doudna lays out a plan to raise $1 billion to fund roughly $100 million a year over a decade for the Innovative Genomics Institute. The goal is to make personalized CRISPR treatments affordable and widely available and to build the next generation of CRISPR scientists. It points to a recent case where a bespoke gene-editing therapy was designed, approved, and manufactured in about six months as a template for scale.

- "Berkeley Talks: Nobel laureate Jennifer Doudna on CRISPR and the future of gene editing" (22 August 2025), https://news.berkeley.edu/2025/08/22/berkeley-talks-jennifer-doudna-on-crispr/
  In this talk she reflects on how CRISPR has progressed from discovery to clinical use and where it goes next. She emphasizes accelerating the path from research to widely accessible therapies and applications beyond human health. It is a concise statement of her decade-ahead vision for the field.

- "A Multi-Billion Dollar Market For CRISPR Therapies Is Possible" (Forbes, 29 May 2025), https://www.forbes.com/sites/juergeneckhardt/2025/05/29/jennifer-doudna-a-multi-billion-dollar-market-for-crispr-therapies-is-possible/
  Doudna argues that despite slow commercialization, CRISPR can support a large therapeutic market as manufacturing and approval pathways mature. She frames the bottleneck as translation and infrastructure rather than underlying science. The piece captures her case that gene editing is on the cusp of broad clinical impact.

- Innovative Genomics Institute mission and programs, https://innovativegenomics.org/people/jennifer-doudna/
  The institute she founded is built to take foundational CRISPR research and apply it to health, sustainable agriculture, and climate resilience. It serves as the organizational engine for her goal of democratizing genome editing. This is the platform through which her future-facing agenda is executed.

### Hooks for the brain
- Feeds the "genome editing at scale" thesis: CRISPR moving from bespoke to affordable, mainstream therapy within a decade.
- Feeds the "engineering biology for global problems" thesis: gene editing applied to agriculture and climate, not only disease.
- Feeds the "speed of biomedical translation" thesis: therapies designed and approved in months rather than years.

## 194. David Baker

#source
Domain: Biotech and longevity
Verified: June 2026

### Read them
- Homepage: https://www.ipd.uw.edu/ (Institute for Protein Design, which he directs)
- Blog / where published / org: Baker Lab (https://www.bakerlab.org/); University of Washington School of Medicine; Howard Hughes Medical Institute investigator
- X / social: IPD and Baker Lab channels (https://www.ipd.uw.edu/)
- Podcast / other: Coverage and interviews via GEN Biotechnology, GeekWire, Nature; tools released open-source on GitHub
- Paywall: partial (research and IPD news free; some journal Q&As such as GEN Biotechnology are partial)

### Who they are
Biochemist who won the 2024 Nobel Prize in Chemistry for computational protein design, and Director of the Institute for Protein Design at the University of Washington. He pioneered tools like RoseTTAFold and RFdiffusion that let scientists design entirely new proteins, and has co-founded more than 20 biotech companies.

### Their future thesis (one line)
AI-driven de novo protein design is becoming a general-purpose technology for building made-to-order medicines, vaccines, antibodies, enzymes, and materials, shifting biology from discovering natural proteins to engineering new ones on demand.

### Key future-relevant pieces
- "The Promise of Protein Design: A Q&A with Nobel Laureate David Baker" (GEN Biotechnology, 17 February 2025), https://www.liebertpub.com/doi/10.1089/genbio.2025.0004
  In this Q&A, Baker explains how AI methods now let researchers design protein binders directly from a target's sequence, without needing its solved structure. He discusses applications spanning vaccines, therapeutics, and company formation, and reflects on mentorship and translation. It is a clear statement of where he sees protein design heading.

- "Teaching AI to build antibodies from scratch" / "Atomically accurate de novo design of antibodies with RFdiffusion" (Institute for Protein Design, Nature, November 2025), https://www.ipd.uw.edu/2025/11/rfantibody-in-nature/
  Baker's team published a method to design antibodies de novo that bind user-specified targets with atomic-level precision, with the RFantibody software released freely. Baker argues AI antibody design will transform biotech and pharma by enabling precise targeting and simpler drug development. This is a direct line into the roughly $100 billion antibody drug market.

- "A diffusion model for protein design" / RFdiffusion (Institute for Protein Design, 2022), https://www.ipd.uw.edu/2022/12/a-diffusion-model-for-protein-design/
  Introduces RFdiffusion, the generative model that lets scientists design novel proteins not found in nature for applications including pharmaceuticals, vaccines, nanomaterials, and biosensors. It marked a step change from designing variants toward generating proteins from scratch. The tool underpins much of the lab's later work.

- "Seven technologies to watch in 2024: protein design" (Nature, featured), https://www.ipd.uw.edu/2024/01/nature-seven-technologies-to-watch-in-2024/
  Nature highlighted de novo protein design, much of it from Baker's lab, as a technology maturing into a practical tool for custom enzymes, biomaterials, and therapeutics. The piece frames protein design as moving from research curiosity to broad utility. It signals mainstream scientific recognition of the field's trajectory.

### Hooks for the brain
- Feeds the "AI for science" thesis: machine learning turning protein design into a fast, general-purpose engineering discipline.
- Feeds the "programmable medicine" thesis: custom antibodies, vaccines, and therapeutics designed on demand rather than discovered.
- Feeds the "engineered biology and new materials" thesis: novel proteins as building blocks for biosensors, enzymes, and nanomaterials.

## 195. Feng Zhang

#source
Domain: Biotech and longevity
Verified: June 2026

### Read them
- Homepage / blog / X / podcast / RSS:
  - Broad Institute profile: https://www.broadinstitute.org/bios/feng-zhang
  - Zhang Lab (publications, tools): https://www.zlab.bio/ and https://www.zlab.bio/publications
  - MIT McGovern Institute profile: https://mcgovern.mit.edu/profile/feng-zhang/
  - No personal newsletter or podcast; follow his work through Broad and McGovern news feeds and his lab's publication list. Broad news index: https://www.broadinstitute.org/news
- Paywall: no (institutional news and lab pages are free; underlying journal papers are sometimes paywalled, though Broad summaries are open)

### Who they are
A core institute member at the Broad Institute of MIT and Harvard and the James and Patricia Poitras Professor of Neuroscience at MIT, Zhang is one of the architects of mammalian CRISPR genome editing and a serial discoverer of new molecular tools. His lab hunts the microbial and animal world for programmable proteins that can read and rewrite DNA and RNA, then re-engineers them for therapy and diagnostics.

### Their future thesis (one line)
Nature has already invented a vast, mostly undiscovered toolkit of programmable molecular machines, and systematically mining and re-engineering them will make precise genome and cell editing safe, deliverable, and routine medicine within a generation.

### Key future-relevant pieces
- "Scientists engineer CRISPR enzymes that evade the immune system" (January 9, 2025), https://www.broadinstitute.org/news/scientists-engineer-crispr-enzymes-evade-immune-system
  Zhang's lab, with Cyrus Biotechnology, mapped the specific protein fragments of Cas9 and Cas12 that the human immune system recognizes, then used computational design to build versions that dodge that recognition while still cutting DNA efficiently. Because roughly 80 percent of people carry pre-existing immunity to these bacterial proteins, stealth nucleases matter for making in-body gene therapies both safer and more durable. It is a concrete step toward CRISPR treatments that the body does not reject before they work.
- "An ancient RNA-guided system could simplify delivery of gene editing therapies (TIGR-Tas)" (February 27, 2025), https://www.broadinstitute.org/news/ancient-rna-guided-system-could-simplify-delivery-gene-editing-therapies
  Reporting in Science, the lab described TIGR-Tas, a family of compact RNA-guided DNA-targeting systems that can be reprogrammed to hit essentially any sequence and, unlike CRISPR, carry no PAM site requirement. Their small size is a major advantage for squeezing editors into delivery vehicles like viral vectors. This points toward a future where the binding part of gene editing is far more flexible and far easier to ship into cells.
- "Scientists develop technology that brings new precision to genome editing" (August 6, 2025), https://www.broadinstitute.org/news/technology-brings-new-precision-to-genome-editing
  This work advances control over genome editing using anti-CRISPR proteins delivered to cells, tightening when and where editing happens. Better off-switches and spatial control reduce unintended edits, a key safety frontier as editing moves into more clinical trials. It reflects the field's shift from "can we cut" to "can we cut exactly here and nowhere else."
- "Feng Zhang awarded the National Medal of Technology and Innovation" (2025), https://www.broadinstitute.org/news/feng-zhang-awarded-national-medal-technology-and-innovation
  Zhang received the United States' highest honor for technological achievement, recognizing his role in turning CRISPR into a usable platform across biology and medicine. The award is a useful marker of how central his toolmaking has become to the trajectory of biotech. It also signals continued national investment in genome editing as strategic infrastructure.

### Hooks for the brain
- The "tool discovery" engine: Zhang treats microbial and animal genomes as a library of undiscovered programmable machines (CRISPR variants, Fanzor, TIGR-Tas), so the future of editing is gated less by invention and more by search and re-engineering.
- Delivery and immune evasion are the real 10 to 20 year bottlenecks, not the cut itself; watch compactness and stealth as the metrics that decide which therapies reach patients.
- A bellwether for longevity and disease: his 2025 immune-rejuvenation and precision-editing work hints at editing being applied to aging biology, not just rare monogenic disease.
- Track via primary sources (zlab.bio publications, Broad and McGovern news) rather than secondary hype; the lab's paper cadence is the leading indicator.

## 196. Drew Endy

#source
Domain: Biotech and longevity
Verified: June 2026

### Read them
- Homepage / blog / X / podcast / RSS:
  - Stanford Bioengineering profile: https://bioengineering.stanford.edu/people/drew-endy
  - Stanford Profiles (publications): https://profiles.stanford.edu/drew-endy
  - Stanford Emerging Technology Review author page: https://setr.stanford.edu/profiles/drew-endy
  - Hoover Institution profile: https://www.hoover.org/profiles/drew-endy
  - X: @drewendy (commentary and links; no dedicated newsletter)
  - Talks and podcast appearances: Stanford eCorner / Entrepreneurial Thought Leaders, e.g. https://stvp.stanford.edu/videos/drew-endy-stanford-university-a-synthetic-biology-dream-entire-talk/
- Paywall: no (Stanford, SETR, Hoover, and STVP content are free)

### Who they are
An associate professor of bioengineering at Stanford and a founder of the modern synthetic biology field, Endy helped create undergraduate bioengineering majors at MIT and Stanford and co-launched the iGEM genetic engineering competition. He also serves on biosecurity bodies and is a Hoover fellow, sitting at the intersection of building biology, open technical standards, and national policy.

### Their future thesis (one line)
Biology is becoming an engineering substrate, and whether that leads to civilization-scale flourishing or catastrophe depends on building open standards, broad access, and serious biosecurity now, not after the technology arrives.

### Key future-relevant pieces
- "Made in China 2025: Who Is Winning?" testimony before the US-China Economic and Security Review Commission (February 6, 2025), https://setr.stanford.edu/news/drew-endy-made-china-2025-who-winning
  Endy testified to the congressional USCC on where the United States and China stand in biotechnology and advanced manufacturing, framing biomanufacturing as a contested strategic capability. He argues American leadership in engineering biology cannot be assumed and depends on sustained investment and policy attention. The written statement (linked from the page) is a compact read on bio as geopolitics. Video: https://www.uscc.gov/hearings/made-china-2025-who-winning
- "Biotechnology and Synthetic Biology" chapter, Stanford Emerging Technology Review 2025 (2025), https://setr.stanford.edu/technology/biotechnology-synthetic-biology/2025
  As part of the SETR team, Endy helps frame synthetic biology for policymakers and executives: it is already roughly 5 percent of US GDP and poised to grow, while carrying real misuse risk as pathogens become synthesizable from scratch. The chapter lays out the dual-use tension at the core of the field's next decade. It is one of the cleanest primers connecting the science to security and economic strategy.
- "Drew Endy: A Synthetic Biology Dream" (Entire Talk), Stanford eCorner / Entrepreneurial Thought Leaders (October 2025), https://stvp.stanford.edu/videos/drew-endy-stanford-university-a-synthetic-biology-dream-entire-talk/
  In this talk Endy gives a primer on synthetic biology, the obstacles it has hit in the US, and where he hopes the field goes, then offers a framework for imagining and building futures. It captures his "civilization-scale flourishing" framing in his own voice. Useful as an accessible, forward-looking statement of his worldview. Also on YouTube: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=PROX8gg7pSc
- "The Future of Synthetic Biology" (2025), https://setr.stanford.edu/news/future-synthetic-biology
  A Stanford Emerging Technology Review piece situating where synthetic biology is heading, including the institutions and standards needed for it to scale responsibly. It reinforces Endy's recurring theme that infrastructure and governance, not just lab breakthroughs, determine the trajectory. A good companion to the technology chapter.

### Hooks for the brain
- Dual-use is the defining tension: Endy is simultaneously an accelerationist (open standards, iGEM, BioBricks, democratized tools) and a biosecurity hawk (NSABB, WHO variola committee, CISAC), so he is a sharp guide to both the upside and the catastrophic tail of engineered biology.
- "Bio as geopolitics": his 2025 USCC testimony reframes synthetic biology as a US-China strategic competition, a lens likely to dominate the next 10 to 20 years of bio policy.
- Standards and access as leverage: watch BioBricks, iGEM, and the BIOFAB / "expression operating systems" idea as the plumbing that decides who gets to engineer life.
- Credible bridge between Silicon Valley, academia, and Washington, which makes his framing unusually influential on actual policy.

## 197. Isomorphic Labs

#source
Domain: Biotech and longevity
Verified: June 2026

### What it is
Isomorphic Labs is a Google DeepMind spinout building an AI drug-design engine on top of AlphaFold, aiming to reimagine the entire drug discovery process and, in its own framing, eventually "solve all diseases." It both partners with large pharma and develops its own internal pipeline.

### Access
- URL: https://www.isomorphiclabs.com/
- RSS / feeds / newsletter / API: News and announcements at https://www.isomorphiclabs.com/articles ; no public API (proprietary platform), no public newsletter. Follow via LinkedIn and press coverage.
- Paywall: no (corporate site and press releases are free; the platform itself is not public)

### What it does for the future
Isomorphic is the clearest test of whether AI moves from predicting protein structure to actually designing approved medicines. If its in silico design engine produces molecules that clear human trials faster and cheaper than traditional discovery, it reshapes the cost curve and timeline of pharma over the next 10 to 20 years, with knock-on effects for longevity, oncology, and immunology.

### Notable recent work (2025 to 2026)
- "Isomorphic Labs raises $600M to advance AI drug design" (March 2025), https://www.isomorphiclabs.com/articles
  In its first external raise, Isomorphic took on roughly $600 million led by Thrive Capital with Alphabet and GV participating, capital earmarked to push programs toward the clinic. The round signaled that investors were willing to fund AI-native discovery as a standalone bet rather than a DeepMind side project.
- "Isomorphic Labs gears up for first human trials of AI-designed drugs" (July 6, 2025), https://fortune.com/2025/07/06/deepmind-isomorphic-labs-cure-all-diseases-ai-now-first-human-trials/
  Fortune reported the company was staffing up and positioning oncology candidates to be the first AI-designed drugs to enter first-in-human testing. Leadership described trials as "very close," though the timeline later slipped toward end of 2026.
- "Isomorphic Labs enters cross-modality research collaboration with Johnson & Johnson" (January 20, 2026), https://www.isomorphiclabs.com/articles/isomorphic-labs-enters-into-a-research-collaboration-with-johnson-johnson
  J&J became Isomorphic's third major pharma partner after Eli Lilly and Novartis, but this deal notably extended beyond small molecules to large molecules and biologics. Isomorphic supplies in silico predictions while J&J runs the wet-lab assays and advances selected programs.
- "Isomorphic Labs secures $2.1 billion Series B to scale its AI drug design engine" (May 12, 2026), https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/isomorphic-labs-secures-2-1-billion-funding-to-scale-its-ai-drug-design-engine-302769674.html
  Led by Thrive Capital with Alphabet, GV, MGX, Temasek, CapitalG, and the UK Sovereign AI Fund participating, the raise dwarfed the 2025 round and signaled confidence ahead of the clinic. The company reiterated it expects to enter human trials by the end of 2026, with internal programs concentrated in oncology and immunology.

### Hooks for the brain
- Watch the first-in-human trial readout (targeted end of 2026): the single biggest data point on whether AI-designed drugs translate to real clinical benefit.
- Partnership breadth (small molecules to biologics) is a tell for how general the design engine is, not just how good it is at one modality.
- A leading indicator for "AI compresses the drug discovery timeline," a core futures thesis for biotech and longevity.
