## 69. Vaclav Smil

# Vaclav Smil

#source
Domain: Energy
Verified: June 2026

## Read them
- Homepage: https://vaclavsmil.com/
- Blog: none (he does not blog; output is books and a News page at https://vaclavsmil.com/news/)
- RSS: https://vaclavsmil.com/feed/ (WordPress default, unverified but standard for this site)

- Podcast / other: frequent guest (not a host). Notable long interviews: Man Group RI podcast, The Walrus, plus his recurring contribution to J.P. Morgan's annual Energy Paper by Michael Cembalest.
- Paywall: no (his site is free; the books themselves are paid)

## Who they are
Distinguished Professor Emeritus in the Faculty of Environment at the University of Manitoba, Fellow of the Royal Society of Canada, and Member of the Order of Canada. By January 2026 he had published 51 books and more than 500 papers on energy, environment, food, and the history of technical advance. Bill Gates has said he reads everything Smil writes.

## Their future thesis (one line)
Energy transitions are inherently slow, grand, material processes, so rapid full decarbonization by 2050 is not realistic, and any honest forecast must start from the physical scale of the systems that feed, house, and move 8 billion people.

## Key future-relevant pieces
- "How the World Really Works" (2022), https://vaclavsmil.com/book/how-the-world-really-works/
  His most accessible statement of the realist case. He argues the modern world rests on four material pillars (ammonia, steel, concrete, plastics) that are still overwhelmingly made with fossil carbon, and that the inertia of this infrastructure means transitions take decades, not years. He calls complete decarbonization by 2050 conceivable only at the cost of an unacceptable global economic retreat.

- "Energy Transitions: Global and National Perspectives" (2017, expanded eds.), https://vaclavsmil.com/book/energy-transitions-global-and-national-perspectives/
  The scholarly backbone of his slow-transition argument. He shows historically that every prior shift (wood to coal, coal to oil) took 50 to 60 years to reach majority share, and warns that wind and solar face the same scaling and substitution frictions. This is the data source most often cited against techno-optimist timelines.

- "How to Feed the World" (2025), https://vaclavsmil.com/book/how-to-feed-the-world/
  Extends his realism to the food system, which he ties tightly to energy through fertilizer, mechanization, and transport. The core claim is that feeding humanity sustainably is a logistics and efficiency problem, not a slogan, and that synthetic nitrogen remains non-negotiable for now. French and other translations rolled out through 2026.

- "Speed: How It Explains the World" (2026), https://vaclavsmil.com/book/speed-how-it-explains-the-world/
  His newest book (US edition published by Penguin, June 2026). It challenges the assumption that faster is always better across production, travel, and communication, a frame that pushes back on accelerationist framings of technological change.

## Hooks for the brain
- Smil is the designated counterweight in any energy debate. Pair him directly against Tony Seba, Casey Handmer, and Ramez Naam to stress-test optimist timelines (the "Smil vs Seba" tension is a whole post).
- He is the original "energy realism" source. Useful when the brain needs the case that physical infrastructure, not policy or price curves, sets the speed limit.
- Hidden player worth tracking: Michael Cembalest of J.P. Morgan, who collaborates with Smil on the annual Energy Paper, a quietly influential document among finance and energy decision-makers.
- Post idea: "What Smil gets right that the optimists ignore, and what the optimists see that Smil misses." The disagreement is mostly about rate, not direction.

## 70. Casey Handmer

# Casey Handmer

#source
Domain: Energy
Verified: June 2026

## Read them
- Homepage: https://www.caseyhandmer.com/ (personal landing page, mostly bio and links)

- X: https://twitter.com/cjhandmer
- Podcast / other: frequent guest. Some posts ship with audio versions. Company writing also appears on the Terraform Industries blog.
- Paywall: no

## Who they are
Founder and CEO of Terraform Industries, a startup doing gigascale atmospheric hydrocarbon synthesis (making cheap natural gas and fuels from sunlight, air, and water). PhD in theoretical physics from Caltech, former software and systems engineer at NASA JPL. One of the loudest technically literate voices on the solar plus batteries plus synthetics future.

## Their future thesis (one line)
Solar is getting so cheap so fast that within a couple of decades humanity reaches effectively unlimited cheap energy (Kardashev Level 1), and the winning move is to use that superabundant power to synthesize hydrocarbons and fuels rather than mine them.

## Key future-relevant pieces
- "Future of Energy Reading List" (Oct 2023, updated Jan 2026), https://caseyhandmer.wordpress.com/2023/10/19/future-of-energy-reading-list/
  His pinned thesis post and best entry point. The TL;DR is blunt: the future of energy is solar plus batteries plus synthetics, solar is deploying at roughly a megawatt per minute globally, and most of the transition is locked in. It links out to his deeper essays by topic.

- "Energy Predictions 2025" (Dec 2025), https://caseyhandmer.wordpress.com/2025/12/08/energy-predictions-2025/
  A broad state-of-play update on where energy is heading, written as a single readable reference with a printable PDF. It lays out his near-term forecasts for solar, storage, and synthetic fuels and where he thinks builders should focus.

- "Direct Current Data Centers" (Jan 2026), https://caseyhandmer.wordpress.com/2026/01/30/direct-current-data-centers/
  Co-written with Matt Weickert, originally at the Terraform blog. Argues that pure solar plus batteries is the cheapest path to powering the AI data center buildout, and connects that to his larger claim about reaching Kardashev Level 1 by harvesting a planet's worth of solar energy.

- "The enormous size of the oil and gas market drives adoption of synthetic fuel production" (Jun 2026), https://caseyhandmer.wordpress.com/2026/06/18/the-enormous-size-of-the-oil-and-gas-market-drives-adoption-of-synthetic-fuel-production/
  His market thesis for Terraform. Because the hydrocarbon market is so vast, every 1 percent cut in at-scale synthetic production cost opens up multi-billion-dollar slices of the methane and methanol markets, which he uses to argue synthetic fuels scale on economics alone, not subsidies.

## Hooks for the brain
- Handmer is the builder-optimist archetype: not just forecasting cheap solar but betting a company on what to do with it. Good source for "what does superabundant energy actually get used for" questions.
- He overlaps heavily with Ramez Naam (they did a joint talk, "How AI Changes Energy") and with Tony Seba's SWB thesis. The three of them form a cluster; Smil is the foil.
- Hidden player: Terraform Industries itself, plus the broader synthetic-fuels and direct-air-capture-for-fuel space, as a wedge on oil demand.
- Post idea: "If energy becomes nearly free, the bottleneck moves to molecules." Synthetic hydrocarbons, not batteries, may be the real story of the 2030s.
- Note: his blog is wide-ranging (space, Mars, antimatter, parenting). Filter for the energy and Terraform posts when mining for futures content.

## Who they are
Doomberg is a pseudonymous team (the green chicken mascot) of entrepreneurs with backgrounds in heavy industry, private equity, and the hard sciences. They run what they describe as the most widely read finance newsletter on Substack, publishing 6 to 8 articles a month with a lateral-thinking take on energy, finance, and geopolitics. Identity is deliberately hidden so the analysis stands on its own.

## Their future thesis (one line)
The energy transition as commonly sold is naive about physics, scale, and base-load reliability, so fossil fuels, nuclear, and industrial reality will dominate far longer than activists assume, and energy security (not just decarbonization) is the variable that will decide geopolitics.

## Key future-relevant pieces
- "About" / publication thesis, https://newsletter.doomberg.com/about
  States the worldview directly: trillions of taxpayer dollars and mandates have been thrown at the energy transition, and they write the "practical voice" they felt was missing. Useful as the framing document for everything else they publish.
- "The Year of the Chicken" (origin story), https://doomberg.substack.com/p/the-year-of-the-chicken
  Explains why they went pseudonymous and how the publication is built around contrarian, lateral-thinking energy and macro analysis rather than personality.
- Energy and Renewables topic streams, https://newsletter.doomberg.com/t/renewables and https://newsletter.doomberg.com/t/investments
  Their running coverage where the forward-looking arguments live: power-grid fragility, the limits of intermittent renewables without firm capacity, natural gas and nuclear as the realistic backbone, and commodity and geopolitical knock-on effects. Most deep pieces sit behind the paywall.

(Note: most individual flagship essays are paywalled and were not fully readable. Titles and topic hubs captured above; the free About and origin posts give the clearest statement of their thesis.)

## Hooks for the brain
- Doomberg is the markets-and-skeptic voice on energy. They sit on the Smil side of the optimist-vs-realist axis but come at it from finance and commodities rather than academia.
- Strong counterweight to Seba, Handmer, Naam, and Liebreich on transition speed. Pair them to capture the "grid reliability and base load" critique of an all-renewables future.
- Hidden angle: their focus on energy security and geopolitics (not just cost curves) is a distinct lens. Useful for "what could slow or break the transition" scenarios.
- Caveat for the brain: they have a clear editorial slant and a paid model, so treat as a sharp-but-positioned source, not neutral data. Cross-check claims against Our World in Data and BNEF.
- Post idea: "The reliability tax: what the cheap-solar story leaves out about keeping the lights on."

## Who they are
Independent energy and climate analyst, writer, and adviser. Full name Nathaniel Bullard. Former Chief Content Officer and lead analyst at BloombergNEF, now writes independently and advises climate-tech ventures (including Halcyon). Best known for his sweeping annual decarbonization presentation, a 200-plus-slide read on the state of the energy transition.

## Their future thesis (one line)
Decarbonization is now a deep, measurable, mostly economic phenomenon driven by exponential technology cost declines, and the job is to track the real-world stocks, flows, and growth curves carefully rather than to forecast with false precision.

## Key future-relevant pieces
- "Decarbonization: Parameters, Dollars and Sense, Electrons Photons Molecules" (15 Jan 2026), https://www.nathanielbullard.com/presentations
  His latest annual deck, the flagship artifact. It is a once-a-year synthesis of how we got to now, where energy and climate are going, and what is new, organized around measurement, economics, and the shift from electrons to molecules. The single best snapshot of his current worldview.

- "Why everyone missed solar's exponential growth" / The Forecaster's Gap (20 Nov 2024), https://www.exponentialview.co/p/the-forecasters-gap
  An essay for Exponential View arguing that mainstream forecasters systematically underestimated solar because linear and even exponential models fail on real technology adoption. Central to his case that the future arrives faster than institutional projections expect.

- "Sun Machines" (contributor, The Economist cover essay, 20 Jun 2024), https://www.economist.com/interactive/essay/2024/06/20/solar-power-is-going-to-be-huge
  He was quoted in and helped shape The Economist cover story on solar's coming dominance. Captures his view that solar is on track to become the largest source of electricity, with profound system-wide consequences.

- "Growth Measurements" (Decarbonization no. 16, Jun 2025), https://www.nathanielbullard.com/blog/decarbonization-no-16-growth-measurements
  A representative newsletter issue showing his method: reading present-day growth signals (Waymo ride growth, global solar generation, European gas import shifts) to infer where energy transitions are heading. Good example of his data-first, chart-led style.

## Hooks for the brain
- Bullard is the measurement-and-charts anchor for the optimist-realist middle. Less ideological than Seba, more current than Smil. His annual deck is a yearly tentpole the brain can revisit.
- Hidden players surfaced in his work: Waymo and autonomous transport as energy-relevant indicators, Halcyon (climate-tech), and the BNEF lineage (he and Michael Liebreich both come from there).
- Strong on the electrons-to-molecules framing: the next phase is not just power but fuels, chemicals, and industrial heat. Overlaps with Handmer's synthetics thesis.
- Note: tentative entry listed "Substack" as a venue. He publishes primarily on his own Squarespace site at nathanielbullard.com, not Substack. Corrected here.
- Post idea: "The forecaster's gap: why every official energy outlook keeps lowballing solar, and what that implies for 2035."

## 73. Tony Seba

# Tony Seba

#source
Domain: Energy
Verified: June 2026

## Read them
- Homepage: https://tonyseba.com/ (personal) and https://www.rethinkx.com/ (his think tank, RethinkX)
- Blog: https://www.rethinkx.com/blog (RethinkX "Insights"); reports at https://www.rethinkx.com/publications
- RSS: unverified (RethinkX runs on HubSpot; no public feed link surfaced. Use the newsletter signup at https://www.rethinkx.com/newsletter-sign-up)

- Podcast / other: prolific keynote speaker; talks on the RethinkX YouTube channel. Not a podcast host.
- Paywall: no (reports are free downloads)

## Who they are
Author, educator, and Silicon Valley entrepreneur; co-founder (with James Arbib) of RethinkX, an independent think tank that forecasts technology disruptions. Wrote the Amazon number-one bestseller "Clean Disruption of Energy and Transportation." Famous for early, aggressive, and often vindicated predictions that solar, wind, batteries, EVs, and autonomy would disrupt incumbents far faster than mainstream forecasters believed.

## Their future thesis (one line)
Technology disruptions are fast and non-linear, not gradual, so the convergence of solar, wind, and batteries (SWB) will deliver superabundant near-zero-marginal-cost "Stellar Energy" in the 2020s and 2030s, collapsing the economics of fossil fuels, legacy utilities, and conventional transport.

## Key future-relevant pieces
- "Stellar: A world beyond limits and how to get there" (2025), https://www.rethinkx.com/publications/stellar
  The flagship book (with James Arbib) laying out the superabundance thesis. The core idea: once SWB reaches a critical mass, the system hits an "ignition point" and clean energy output becomes self-sustaining and effectively unlimited, the way a star ignites. This is the fullest statement of where he thinks the 2030s and 2040s lead.

- "Understanding Stellar Energy" (May 2025), https://www.rethinkx.com/publications/understandingstellarenergy2025.en
  The technical companion report (with Adam Dorr and James Arbib). It introduces analytical tools (the Clean Energy U-Curve, SWB Superpower Curve, SWB Coverage Curve) to show how solar, wind, and batteries deliver superabundant power at near-zero marginal cost, aimed at investors and policymakers.

- "Rethinking Transportation 2020-2030" (2017), https://www.rethinkx.com/publications/rethinkingtransportation2017.en
  His landmark transport forecast. Predicts Transport-as-a-Service (autonomous electric fleets) collapses car ownership and oil demand, saving the average US family thousands per year and cutting road transport energy demand by around 80 percent. The reference point for judging how his disruption framework has aged.

- "Clean Disruption: 10 Years Later" (30 May 2025), https://www.rethinkx.com/publications/clean-disruption-2025
  A retrospective by the RethinkX team revisiting his 2014 predictions from the vantage of 2025: solar and battery costs down roughly 66 and 78 percent, EVs at price parity, autonomy deployed in multiple cities. Useful for scoring the framework's hit rate.

## Hooks for the brain
- Seba is the canonical disruption-optimist. His Seba Technology Disruption Framework (cost curves predict timing) is a reusable mental model for the whole brain, not just energy.
- The "ignition point / superabundance" idea is a strong, quotable futures frame. Pair against Smil and Doomberg for the central energy debate.
- Hidden players: James Arbib (co-founder, less visible) and Adam Dorr (RethinkX research director, increasingly the public-facing analyst). Worth tracking Dorr separately.
- RethinkX also forecasts food (precision fermentation), labor (humanoid robots), and transport, so Seba is a cross-domain disruption source, not energy-only.
- Post idea: "Seba's scoreboard: which of his wild 2014 predictions came true, which slipped, and what that says about his 2030s call."

## 74. Ramez Naam

# Ramez Naam

#source
Domain: Energy
Verified: June 2026

## Read them
- Homepage: https://www.rameznaam.com/ (now hosted as a Substack: "A Brighter Future for Humanity: AI, Energy, Climate, and Technology")
- Blog: older long-form essays live on the legacy WordPress archive at https://rameznaam.com/blog/ (frozen, newest posts around 2020); current writing and notes are on the Substack at https://www.rameznaam.com/
- RSS: https://www.rameznaam.com/feed (Substack default, standard for this site)
- X: https://twitter.com/ramez
- Podcast / other: frequent guest (AEI, Canary Media with David Roberts, TED). Recent joint talk with Casey Handmer, "How AI Changes Energy." Not a regular host.
- Paywall: no (Substack is free)

## Who they are
Technologist, futurist, and author; former Microsoft engineer and writer of the award-winning Nexus science-fiction trilogy. One of the earliest voices (from around 2011) to forecast the exponential cost declines of solar, batteries, and EVs and to predict clean energy would undercut fossil fuels. Founded the climate venture firm PlanetaryVC in 2021. Co-chair of Energy and Environment at Singularity University.

## Their future thesis (one line)
Clean energy and storage ride steep, predictable experience curves, so solar, wind, and batteries become the cheapest energy in history and disrupt fossil fuels on pure economics, with the next phase (cheap enough to undercut existing coal and gas, then to power AI) being the most consequential yet.

## Key future-relevant pieces
- "The Third Phase of Clean Energy Will Be the Most Disruptive Yet" (2 Apr 2019), https://rameznaam.com/2019/04/02/the-third-phase-of-clean-energy-will-be-the-most-disruptive-yet/
  His signature framing. Phase one was clean energy reaching price parity for new build; phase two, beating new fossil build; phase three, when new solar, wind, and storage become cheaper than just operating existing coal and gas plants. He argues phase three is when fossil assets start getting stranded and the transition becomes self-reinforcing.

- "Solar's Future is Insanely Cheap" (14 May 2020), https://rameznaam.com/2020/05/14/solars-future-is-insanely-cheap/
  His landmark update on solar learning curves, revising his own earlier (already bullish) forecasts further downward. Argues solar prices keep beating every official projection and will reach levels once considered impossible, reshaping where and how energy is produced.

- "How Cheap Can Energy Storage Get? Pretty Darn Cheap" (legacy series), https://rameznaam.com/2015/10/14/how-cheap-can-energy-storage-get-pretty-darn-cheap/
  Part of his foundational multi-part series modeling solar, wind, storage, and EV cost declines using experience curves. Storage is the linchpin of his "renewables can go very far" argument, and this piece is the analytical core that later events largely validated.

- "How AI Changes Energy" (2026, joint talk with Casey Handmer), https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Tw-LO33woPo
  Recent conversation on how surging AI power demand is accelerating solar buildout and reshaping land use and grid planning. Marks his current focus: the collision of AI energy hunger with cheap solar as the defining energy story of the late 2020s.

## Hooks for the brain
- Naam is the experience-curve evangelist who was early and largely right. He bridges the optimist cluster (Seba, Handmer) and connects energy to AI and to broader human-progress themes.
- His "three phases" model is a clean, citable structure for narrating the transition. The brain can use it to place any given year on the curve.
- Hidden player: PlanetaryVC and the climate-startup pipeline he funds (energy, ag-tech, carbon). Tracks where optimist theses turn into companies.
- Caveat: his most-cited analytical essays predate 2021; current output is shorter Substack notes plus talks. For deep argument, mine the legacy archive; for current views, follow the Substack and his AI-energy talks.
- Post idea: "AI is the new demand shock that finishes what cheap solar started." The data-center buildout as the forcing function for the grid of the 2030s.

## Who they are
Data scientist and researcher. Head of Research at Our World in Data and a Senior Researcher at the University of Oxford. She specializes in turning large datasets on energy, climate, food, and global development into clear, myth-busting charts and explainers for non-experts. Author of "Not the End of the World," a data-driven case for cautious environmental optimism.

## Their future thesis (one line)
We are the first generation that can plausibly build a sustainable world, and the data shows real, fast progress (especially in clean energy and electrification), so the right posture is urgent, evidence-based optimism rather than doom or complacency.

## Key future-relevant pieces
- "Will Africa still be energy-poor in 2050?" (17 Mar 2026), https://hannahritchie.substack.com/p/africa-energy-poverty-projections
  Uses long-range projections to warn that on current trajectories, electricity consumption across much of Africa will remain extremely low for decades. A sobering counter to blanket optimism: the transition is uneven, and energy poverty is the development challenge that cheap solar has not yet solved.

- "How much electricity does AI consume? [2025 summary]" (5 May 2026), https://hannahritchie.substack.com/p/ai-electricity-2025
  A data-grounded reckoning with the AI-energy panic. She quantifies the share of electricity used by data centers and the real footprint of chatbots, cutting through hype in both directions. Important for any 10-to-20-year view of how AI demand reshapes the grid.

- "How quickly do electric car sales translate into cars on the road?" (12 Jun 2026), https://hannahritchie.substack.com/p/ev-fleet-transition
  Explores the lag between EV sales share and EV fleet share, a crucial and underappreciated dynamic. Sales can hit majority well before the cars on the road do, which reframes how fast transport emissions actually fall.

- "Not the End of the World" (2024), https://hannahritchie.com/not-the-end-of-the-world/
  Her flagship book and clearest thesis statement. It walks through seven big environmental problems (climate, air pollution, deforestation, food, biodiversity, ocean plastics, overfishing) and argues each is solvable with current trajectories and tools, making the case for "the first generation that can build a sustainable planet."

## Hooks for the brain
- Ritchie is the data-discipline anchor for the whole energy and climate section. When other sources make a sweeping claim, her charts are the place to check it.
- She is an optimist but an empirical one, and she publishes the caveats (Africa energy poverty, EV fleet lag, AI demand). Useful for nuancing the Seba and Naam optimism without flipping to Doomberg pessimism.
- Hidden player: Our World in Data itself (and its data pipelines) as critical infrastructure that the whole futures conversation quietly depends on.
- Note: her tentative entry name "Sustainability by Numbers" is now outdated. The Substack rebranded to "By the Numbers" in 2026 and she broadened scope beyond energy and climate to global health, demographics, and development. Corrected here.
- Post idea: "The optimist's caveats: three places where the clean-energy success story still breaks down (Africa, fleets, and AI demand)."

## Who they are
Founder of Bloomberg New Energy Finance (BNEF), which Bloomberg acquired in 2009, and still a Senior Contributor there. Co-Managing Partner of EcoPragma Capital and CEO of Liebreich Associates. A leading "pragmatic optimist" on the energy transition: bullish on electrification and clean power, skeptical of hype around hydrogen and some other silver bullets. Former adviser to the UN on Sustainable Energy for All and former Transport for London board member.

## Their future thesis (one line)
The clean energy transition is real, accelerating, and ultimately unstoppable on economics, but it must be navigated pragmatically: electrify everything you can, use clean electrons directly, and be ruthless about which technologies (and which hydrogen dreams) actually pencil out.

## Key future-relevant pieces
- "The Great Clean Energy Acceleration 2.0" (27 May 2026), https://about.bnef.com/insights/clean-energy/liebreich-the-great-clean-energy-acceleration-2-0/
  His latest big BNEF essay arguing the transition has re-accelerated despite political headwinds, revisiting the post-2022 energy shock and where momentum stands now. A current state-of-the-transition read from one of its most data-literate observers.

- "The Pragmatic Climate Reset" (Dec 2025), https://about.bnef.com/insights/clean-energy/liebreich-the-pragmatic-climate-reset-part-i/
  A multi-part essay resetting climate strategy around what is actually working: cheap clean electricity and electrification, with a hard look at where targets and narratives have drifted from reality. Captures his "pragma" brand and his critique of both doomers and over-promisers.

- "Make Electricity Cheap Again" (21 Jun 2026), https://mliebreich.substack.com/p/make-electricity-cheap-again
  His Substack 8-point plan arguing electricity must be cheap, clean, abundant, and resilient, and that cheap clean power is the master key to the whole transition. A concise, prescriptive statement of his policy worldview.

- The Clean Energy Ladder / hydrogen "ladder" framework (Cleaning Up and BNEF essays), https://www.cleaningup.live/
  His widely cited "hydrogen ladder" ranks end uses by how sensible hydrogen is versus direct electrification. It has become a standard reference for separating credible decarbonization from hype, and underpins much of his forward analysis.

## Hooks for the brain
- Liebreich is the pragmatic-optimist anchor: more bullish than Smil and Doomberg, more disciplined and skeptical than Seba and Naam. He occupies the credible center of the energy debate.
- His "Cleaning Up" podcast is a long-running, high-signal interview library with transition leaders, a rich secondary source for surfacing other players.
- Hidden players: BNEF as an institution, plus the guests on Cleaning Up. His hydrogen-skeptic stance is a useful corrective to hydrogen hype cycles.
- The "electrify everything, electricity is the master key" thesis overlaps directly with Saul Griffith, but Liebreich brings the finance and policy lens.
- Post idea: "Liebreich's hydrogen ladder, five years on: which rungs survived contact with reality."

## Who they are
Australian-American inventor, engineer, and electrification advocate. Founder of Rewiring America, Rewiring Australia, and Rewiring Aotearoa (nonprofits pushing to decarbonize by electrifying everything), founder and chief scientist at Otherlab, and co-founder of Makani Power and Instructables. MacArthur "genius" Fellow. The leading popularizer of the "electrify everything" household-and-grid thesis.

## Their future thesis (one line)
The fastest, cheapest route to decarbonization is to electrify every machine in daily life (cars, heating, cooking, water heating) and power them with cheap rooftop and grid solar plus batteries, which makes household energy dramatically cheaper and electricity eventually almost free.

## Key future-relevant pieces
- "Pax Electricana" (19 Apr 2026), https://energyandstuff.substack.com/p/pax-electricana
  His most ambitious recent essay, subtitled a middle-power strategy for a decentralized electro-industrial order. He argues that cheap distributed electricity reorders geopolitics, letting middle powers and households escape dependence on imported fossil fuels and build a more decentralized industrial base. A genuine 10-to-20-year worldview piece.

- "Electricity 'too cheap to meter' is so... 1975. FREE ELECTRICITY is 2025!" (7 Nov 2025), https://energyandstuff.substack.com/p/electricity-too-cheap-to-meter-is
  Uses the Australian electricity market as a preview of where the world is heading: so much cheap rooftop solar that electricity trends toward free at times. Argues the US could get there too if it makes rooftop solar cheaper, and that this changes the economics of everything downstream.

- "The Iran war exposes our stupidity. Australia should stop buying oil and electrify everything." (4 May 2026), https://energyandstuff.substack.com/p/the-iran-war-exposes-our-stupidity
  An energy-security argument: Australia exports most of its crude and buys back refined fuel, and electrifying end uses would save vast sums daily while removing geopolitical exposure. A concrete national case study of his electrify-everything thesis.

- "Electrify: An Optimist's Playbook for Our Clean Energy Future" (2021, MIT Press), https://www.rewiringamerica.org/research/electrify-the-book-saul-griffith
  His foundational book and the manifesto behind Rewiring America. Lays out a detailed, machine-by-machine blueprint for decarbonizing the US by electrifying homes, vehicles, and industry, framed as a jobs-and-prosperity program, not a sacrifice. The reference text for the whole movement.

## Hooks for the brain
- Griffith is the electrify-everything anchor, the household-and-demand-side complement to the supply-side optimists (Seba, Naam, Handmer). His unit of analysis is the machine in your home, which makes the transition tangible.
