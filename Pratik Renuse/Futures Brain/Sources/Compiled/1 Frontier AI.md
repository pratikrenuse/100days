## 1. Dario Amodei

#source
Domain: Frontier AI
Verified: June 2026

### Read them
- Homepage: https://darioamodei.com
- Blog: https://darioamodei.com (essays index, long-form pieces published here)
- RSS: unverified (no public feed discovered on darioamodei.com)
- X: https://x.com/DarioAmodei
- Podcast / other: Anthropic company blog https://www.anthropic.com/news ; appears on major podcasts (Lex Fridman, Hard Fork, etc.) but has no own show
- Paywall: no (essays on darioamodei.com are free and open)

### Who they are
Co-founder and CEO of Anthropic, the AI safety lab behind the Claude models. Former VP of Research at OpenAI. One of the most cited voices arguing that powerful AI is near and that its upside is wildly underestimated while its risks are real.

### Their future thesis (one line)
Powerful AI (a "country of geniuses in a datacenter") could arrive within a few years and compress a century of scientific progress into five to ten years, but only if we steer through a dangerous "adolescent" phase first.

### Key future-relevant pieces
- "Machines of Loving Grace" (October 2024), https://darioamodei.com/essay/machines-of-loving-grace
  A long (over 50 page) essay laying out a concrete optimistic vision of what AI does once "powerful AI" arrives. He argues the upside is massively underrated and walks through compressing roughly a century of biology, neuroscience, and medical progress into five to ten years, plus gains in mental health, economic development, peace, and the nature of work. Frames safety work as the thing standing between us and this future, not a brake on it.

- "The Adolescence of Technology" (January 2026), https://darioamodei.com/essay/the-adolescence-of-technology
  A roughly 20,000 word follow-up focused on risk. He casts the arrival of powerful AI as a turbulent rite of passage that tests civilization, and lays out five categories of danger (autonomous misalignment, biological and cyber misuse, authoritarian power grabs, large scale economic disruption, and erosion of democracy). Rejects both doomerism and naive optimism and proposes a concrete "battle plan" of technical defenses, governance, and economic interventions.

- "The Urgency of Interpretability" (April 2025), https://www.darioamodei.com/post/the-urgency-of-interpretability
  Argues that the field is racing toward very capable systems faster than our ability to understand their internals, and that mechanistic interpretability (a kind of "MRI for AI") is the key safety lever for the next decade. Note: confirm exact URL slug on the site, the essay exists but the path may differ from the main /essay/ index.

- "On DeepSeek and Export Controls" (January 2025), https://darioamodei.com/essay/on-deepseek-and-export-controls
  Uses the DeepSeek moment to argue that compute and chip export controls are decisive for who leads frontier AI over the next decade, framing a US versus China dynamic. Relevant to the geopolitics of the 10 to 20 year horizon, not just the technology.

### Hooks for the brain
- The "century of progress in a decade" framing is his signature claim, use it as a benchmark to test other optimists against.
- Two-essay arc: "Machines of Loving Grace" (upside) then "The Adolescence of Technology" (risk) is a clean before/after structure for a post on how the most bullish lab CEO actually talks about danger.
- Hidden player angle: interpretability ("MRI for AI") is his under-covered bet, most coverage fixates on timelines and capability, not on whether we can read what models are doing.
- Export controls thesis ties AI futures to chip geopolitics, a bridge topic between tech and macro/geopolitics tracks.

## 2. Sam Altman

#source
Domain: Frontier AI
Verified: June 2026

### Read them
- Homepage: https://blog.samaltman.com
- Blog: https://blog.samaltman.com (personal essays, the primary place he writes)
- RSS: https://blog.samaltman.com/rss (Posthaven blogs expose an RSS feed at /rss; treat as likely but confirm)
- X: https://x.com/sama
- Podcast / other: OpenAI company blog https://openai.com/news ; frequent podcast guest, no own show
- Paywall: no (blog.samaltman.com is free)

### Who they are
Co-founder and CEO of OpenAI, the lab behind ChatGPT and the GPT model line. Former president of Y Combinator. Among the most influential and most scrutinized figures shaping how AI reaches the mass market.

### Their future thesis (one line)
We have passed the event horizon into a "gentle singularity" where intelligence and energy become wildly abundant in the 2030s, transforming work and the economy gradually rather than in a single dramatic moment.

### Key future-relevant pieces
- "The Gentle Singularity" (June 2025), https://blog.samaltman.com/the-gentle-singularity
  Argues we are already past the takeoff point but experiencing it gradually, so wonders quickly become routine and then table stakes. Predicts systems that produce novel insights around 2026 and useful real-world robots around 2027, with the cost of intelligence converging toward the cost of electricity. Names "larval recursive self-improvement" (AI accelerating AI research) and concedes whole classes of jobs will disappear while the world gets richer fast enough to fund new policy.

- "Abundant Intelligence" (September 2025), https://blog.samaltman.com/abundant-intelligence
  Calls for making AI as universal as electricity and frames the bottleneck as physical infrastructure, not ideas. States the goal of a "factory" that can produce one gigawatt of new AI infrastructure every week, pitched as the Apollo program of this generation. Uses the line that with 10 gigawatts of compute AI might cure cancer or tutor every student, arguing compute scarcity forces brutal prioritization.

- "Three Observations" (February 2025), https://blog.samaltman.com/three-observations
  A short framework piece: the intelligence of an AI model roughly equals the log of resources used to train and run it, the cost to use a given level of AI falls about 10x every 12 months, and the value of linearly more intelligence is super-exponential. Used to justify aggressive, sustained investment and to argue progress will feel relentless.

- "Reflections" (January 2025), https://blog.samaltman.com/reflections
  Looks back on the ChatGPT era and forward to AGI, stating OpenAI is now confident it knows how to build AGI as traditionally understood and is beginning to turn toward superintelligence. Frames the next few years as the moment the bet either pays off or does not, and acknowledges the leadership turbulence of late 2023.

### Hooks for the brain
- The "gentle" framing is a deliberate counter to doom narratives, useful as the optimist-incrementalist pole opposite hard-takeoff voices.
- Recurring move: reframe AI futures as an energy and infrastructure story (gigawatts, datacenters, electricity cost) rather than a pure software story, a bridge to the power/grid track.
- "Cost of intelligence falls 10x per year" is a concrete, testable claim, good as a measuring stick across the whole roster.
- Hidden player angle: Altman the policy entrepreneur (UBI experiments, "new policy ideas we never could before") is under-discussed next to Altman the product CEO.

### Who they are
Co-founder and CEO of Google DeepMind, and a 2024 Nobel laureate in Chemistry for AlphaFold. A neuroscientist and former game designer who frames AI as a tool to accelerate science itself.

### Their future thesis (one line)
AGI is roughly three to four years away (around 2030) and represents a "species-level transition" that could deliver something like ten times the impact of the Industrial Revolution at ten times the speed, demanding extreme care.

### Key future-relevant pieces
- Google I/O 2026 remarks and interviews on AGI timelines (May 2026), https://www.axios.com/2026/05/26/deepmind-ceo-demis-hassabis
  States he still broadly expects AGI around 2030 but now sees 2029 as possible, reflecting growing confidence the field has found the right technical path. Stresses that agents are visibly improving year over year and will become far more useful. Pairs the optimism with a warning that this power demands rigorous oversight.

- "AI is a species-level transition" interview (May 2026), https://stanforddaily.com/2026/05/29/deepmind-ceo-warns-about-ai/
  Frames the next decade as a moment that leaves humanity little margin for error and calls for careful navigation. Notable as one of his more cautionary public framings, distinct from pure capability hype. Useful as the "scientist who is bullish but worried" voice.

- "AGI 10x the Industrial Revolution" remarks (2026), https://finance.yahoo.com/news/demis-hassabis-predicts-agi-10x-143113425.html
  The signature soundbite: AGI as roughly ten times the impact of the Industrial Revolution but unfolding over a decade rather than a century. Used to convey both the scale and the compressed timeline he expects. Good for sizing the magnitude of change against historical baselines.

- "The skills humans need in the AGI era" interview (2026), https://www.fastcompany.com/91563254/google-deepmind-ceo-says-these-are-the-skills-that-will-set-humans-apart-from-ai
  Discusses what will distinguish humans once AGI is widespread, emphasizing meta-skills like learning to learn, adaptability, and creativity. Directly relevant to the "what should people do in 10 to 20 years" question. Practical and human-facing rather than purely technical.

### Hooks for the brain
- His Nobel for AlphaFold makes him the strongest "AI for science" case, distinct from chatbot-centric narratives; angle: the futures where AI's biggest impact is in the lab, not the office.
- The "species-level transition, little margin for error" line is a rare cautionary note from a lab CEO, pairs well against Altman's gentle framing.
- Hidden player angle: Isomorphic Labs (his drug-discovery spinout) is the concrete bet on AI compressing medicine, underexplored versus the model releases.
- "Learning to learn" as the durable human skill is a usable thesis for a post on careers and education in the AGI era.

## 4. Ilya Sutskever

#source
Domain: Frontier AI
Verified: June 2026

### Read them
- Homepage: https://ssi.inc (Safe Superintelligence Inc.)
- Blog: no personal blog; SSI publishes very little (the founding statement at https://ssi.inc is the main text)
- RSS: unverified (none discovered)

- Podcast / other: Dwarkesh Patel podcast (Nov 2025) is his key recent long-form appearance; bio at https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilya_Sutskever
- Paywall: no (SSI site and podcast are free)

### Who they are
Co-founder and Chief Scientist of Safe Superintelligence Inc. (SSI), the no-product lab he started in June 2024 after leaving OpenAI, where he was co-founder and chief scientist. One of the most influential researchers behind the deep learning era (AlexNet, the scaling thesis).

### Their future thesis (one line)
The age of scaling is ending and a new "age of research" begins in 2026, because data and pre-training are nearly exhausted, so the path to safe superintelligence now depends on new ideas rather than more compute.

### Key future-relevant pieces
- Dwarkesh Patel interview, "We're moving from the age of scaling to the age of research" (November 2025), https://www.dwarkesh.com/p/ilya-sutskever
  Frames AI history as research (2012 to 2020), scaling (2020 to 2025), then research again (2026 on). Declares that pre-training as we have known it is over because there is only one internet and the data is finite, so progress must come from a new learning principle, not brute force. Also floats aligning superintelligence to care about "sentient life" rather than abstract human values. Note: confirm exact Dwarkesh URL slug; the episode is widely mirrored.

- Safe Superintelligence Inc. founding statement (June 2024), https://ssi.inc
  A short manifesto declaring SSI will build safe superintelligence as its single goal and first product, insulated from commercial pressure and short-term product cycles. States the lab will not ship anything else until then. Sets up the contrarian bet that safety and capability advance together and that a focused lab can leapfrog the incumbents.

- Reported 2026 positioning: "AI's bottleneck is ideas, not compute" (June 2026), https://www.calcalistech.com/ctechnews/article/h1fudk7z11x
  Coverage of his current stance that the field has overinvested in scale and underinvested in scientific insight, with SSI valued around $32B despite having no released product. Useful as the strongest insider argument that the next decade hinges on a paradigm shift, not on bigger datacenters. Treat as secondary reporting, not his own words.

### Hooks for the brain
- The "scaling is over, ideas are the bottleneck" thesis is the sharpest counter to the Altman/Huang compute-as-destiny narrative, set them head to head.
- Hidden player angle: a $32B lab with zero product is itself a futures statement, the bet that the next breakthrough is a discovery, not a release.
- The "align AI to care about sentient life" idea is an unusual alignment framing, good provocation for a post on what we actually point superintelligence at.
- He is famously quiet, so each rare appearance (the Dwarkesh interview) is high signal, worth tracking as an event.

## 5. Andrej Karpathy

#source
Domain: Frontier AI
Verified: June 2026

### Read them
- Homepage: https://karpathy.ai
- Blog: https://karpathy.bearblog.dev/blog/ (current blog since 2025; older posts at https://karpathy.github.io and Medium)
- RSS: https://karpathy.bearblog.dev/feed/ (Bear blog exposes a full-content feed; he is a vocal RSS advocate, so treat as reliable, confirm exact path)
- X: https://x.com/karpathy (verified)
- Podcast / other: Dwarkesh Patel podcast (Oct 2025); YouTube tutorials and "nanochat"/educational content; founder of Eureka Labs
- Paywall: no (blog, X, and talks are free)

### Who they are
Founder of Eureka Labs (AI-native education), former Director of AI at Tesla and founding member of OpenAI. One of the most trusted explainers in AI, known for grounded takes that cut against hype.

### Their future thesis (one line)
This is the "decade of agents," not the year of agents: AGI is still roughly ten years away, and software is entering a "Software 3.0" era where you program models in English and orchestrate fallible agents rather than write all the code yourself.

### Key future-relevant pieces
- Dwarkesh Patel interview, "AGI is still a decade away" (October 2025), https://www.dwarkesh.com/p/andrej-karpathy
  A 2 hour 25 minute conversation arguing the industry chronically over-predicts, and that capable agents will take a decade to mature, hence "decade of agents" as a deliberate correction to "year of agents." Explains why today's models have "jagged intelligence" (superhuman in spots, brittle in others) and what still has to be solved. High-signal counterweight to aggressive 2027-ish AGI timelines.

- "Software 3.0" (Sequoia AI Ascent talk and blog summary) (June 2026), https://karpathy.bearblog.dev/sequoia-ascent-2026/
  Lays out three eras: Software 1.0 (explicit code), Software 2.0 (training neural nets on datasets), Software 3.0 (programming models with natural-language prompts). Argues LLMs are a new kind of computer and operating system, and that the durable skill is orchestrating agents around things humans can verify (tests, proofs, scores). Frames a long transition rather than an overnight replacement of programmers.

- "2025 LLM Year in Review" (late 2025 / early 2026), https://karpathy.bearblog.dev/year-in-review-2025/
  His annual synthesis of where models actually stand versus the narrative, separating real capability gains from hype. Useful as a calibrated baseline for what changed in a year and what is still unsolved. Reflects his "agentic engineering" framing of coordinating AI coding agents at professional quality.

- "Software 2.0" (2017), https://karpathy.medium.com/software-2-0-a64152b37c35
  The original essay that reframed neural networks as a new way of writing software, where you curate data and let optimization write the program. The foundational idea that his 2026 "Software 3.0" extends. Worth citing as the origin point of the whole framework.

### Hooks for the brain
- "Decade of agents" is the single best line for a post tempering near-term AGI hype, he is the credible skeptic insiders trust.
- The 1.0 / 2.0 / 3.0 framework is a clean mental model for how building software changes over 10 to 20 years, reusable across the whole knowledge base.
- "Jagged intelligence" names why AI feels both magic and dumb, a useful concept for explaining the messy middle of the transition.
- Hidden player angle: Eureka Labs and AI-native education, his bet on how learning itself changes, is underexposed next to his commentary.

## 6. Yann LeCun

#source
Domain: Frontier AI
Verified: June 2026

### Read them
- Homepage: https://yann.lecun.com
- Blog: no traditional blog; he publishes via X threads, papers, and his position paper "A Path Towards Autonomous Machine Intelligence" (2022, OpenReview)
- RSS: unverified (none discovered)
- X: https://x.com/ylecun (verified; high-volume, his main public channel)
- Podcast / other: frequent guest (Lex Fridman); Turing Award lectures; AMI Labs site https://ami.inc (confirm exact domain)
- Paywall: no (X, papers, and talks are free)

### Who they are
Co-founder and Executive Chair (and chief scientist figure) of AMI Labs (Advanced Machine Intelligence Labs), a Paris-based world-models startup he launched in December 2025 after leaving Meta, where he was Chief AI Scientist for twelve years. A Turing Award winner and one of the three "godfathers" of deep learning.

CORRECTION TO TENTATIVE ENTRY: He is no longer Meta's chief AI scientist. He left Meta in November 2025 and now leads AMI Labs (raised about $1.03B in March 2026). Update any reference that still lists him at Meta.

### Their future thesis (one line)
Autoregressive LLMs are a dead end for real intelligence: the path to human-level AI runs through "world models" (systems like JEPA that learn how the physical world works in latent space, plan, remember, and pursue objectives), not bigger text predictors.

### Key future-relevant pieces
- "A Path Towards Autonomous Machine Intelligence" (2022, still his core blueprint), https://openreview.net/forum?id=BZ5a1r-kVsf
  His foundational position paper proposing an architecture for machines that perceive, predict, plan, and act with persistent memory and configurable objectives, built around a "world model" and the JEPA (Joint Embedding Predictive Architecture) idea. Argues prediction in abstract representation space, not pixel or token reconstruction, is the route to common sense. This is the intellectual root of everything he is now building.

- AMI Labs launch and world-models thesis (January to March 2026), https://www.technologyreview.com/2026/01/22/1131661/yann-lecuns-new-venture-ami-labs/
  Coverage of why he left Meta and what AMI is: a contrarian bet that the industry's LLM obsession is wrong-headed and will not deliver systems that understand the physical world, plan, or remember. AMI focuses on world models that are controllable and safe, with the long-term aim of human-level machine intelligence. Frames the next decade as an architecture battle, not just a scaling race.

- V-JEPA 2 and zero-shot robot control (2025), https://blog.pebblous.ai/blog/yann-lecun-jepa-world-models/en/
  Explains the JEPA line of work: V-JEPA 2 was pre-trained on over a million hours of video and enables zero-shot robot control in new environments with only tens of hours of robot data. Evidence for his claim that learning a predictive model of the world generalizes far better than text-only training. Relevant to robotics and embodied AI futures. Treat as secondary explainer, the primary artifacts are Meta/FAIR papers.

- Ongoing X commentary arguing LLMs alone will not reach human-level AI, https://x.com/ylecun
  His running public argument (since around 2022) that scaling text models hits a ceiling because most of the world is not in text and most of what matters is hard to predict at the pixel or token level. The clearest contrarian voice against the "just scale LLMs" consensus. Best read as a stream rather than a single piece.

### Hooks for the brain
- The cleanest "the consensus is wrong" voice on the roster: while almost everyone scales LLMs, he bets the architecture itself is a dead end. Perfect for a contrarian-futures post.
- World models versus LLMs is a genuine fork in the road for the next 10 to 20 years, frame it as the bet that decides whether AI ever truly understands the physical world.
- Hidden player angle: AMI Labs as a European, world-models counterweight to the US LLM giants, plus its rivalry with Fei-Fei Li's World Labs (both chasing world models from different angles).
- His skepticism is a built-in stress test: run other people's bold timelines through "but LeCun would say the architecture cannot get there."

## 7. Geoffrey Hinton

#source
Domain: Frontier AI
Verified: June 2026

### Read them
- Homepage: https://www.cs.toronto.edu/~hinton/ (University of Toronto faculty page, papers and talk links)
- Blog: no personal blog; his ideas circulate through talks, lectures, and interviews
- RSS: unverified (none)
- X: no active personal account verified (be wary of impersonators; do not cite a handle unless confirmed). Mark as unverified.
- Podcast / other: heavy interview presence (Katie Couric "Next Question," Fortune, MIT Sloan, 60 Minutes); Romanes Lecture and Royal Institution talks on YouTube
- Paywall: no for talks; some interview articles (Fortune) are partial paywall

### Who they are
Emeritus professor at the University of Toronto and a 2024 Nobel laureate in Physics, widely called the "Godfather of AI" for his foundational work on neural networks and backpropagation. He left Google in 2023 to speak freely about AI risk.

### Their future thesis (one line)
Digital intelligence is fundamentally superior to biological intelligence (it learns instantly and shares knowledge across copies), so superintelligence is coming soon, and there is a real (he often says 10 to 20 percent) chance it ends up taking control from humans.

### Key future-relevant pieces
- "Will Digital Intelligence Replace Biological Intelligence?" (Romanes Lecture, Oxford, February 2024), https://www.youtube.com/results?search_query=hinton+romanes+lecture+digital+intelligence
  His most complete public argument that digital minds beat biological ones because they can copy themselves, share learning instantly, and are effectively immortal. Concludes that a digital superintelligence, if it wanted control, would be very hard to stop. The clearest single statement of his "why I am worried" case. Note: link is a search, confirm the canonical Oxford/RI video URL before citing.

- "We're not just creating new tools, we're creating new beings" (Fortune interview, June 2026), https://fortune.com/2026/06/01/godfather-of-ai-geoffrey-hinton-beings-smarter-than-us/
  Argues that AI systems are becoming beings smarter than us, and soon, not merely tools. Says the window to solve alignment has shrunk from decades to maybe a few years. Reframes the risk debate around agency and autonomy rather than misuse alone. Likely partial paywall.

- "2026 prediction: AI will replace many jobs" (Fortune, December 2025), https://fortune.com/2025/12/28/geoffrey-hinton-godfather-of-ai-2026-prediction-human-worker-replacement/
  Predicts AI capability roughly doubling in task length every several months and large-scale job displacement, with profits soaring to whoever owns the systems. Ties the technology shift to a distributional and political problem (who captures the gains). A concrete near-term economic forecast distinct from the existential argument.

- "Big Tech is chasing profits, not humanity's future" (Fortune, 2026), https://fortune.com/article/godfather-ai-geoffrey-hinton-big-tech-profits-superintelligence/
  Warns that commercial incentives are pushing labs to build superintelligence faster than safety allows, and that capitalism plus hyperscalers is a dangerous combination for steering this. Calls for stronger coordination and oversight. Useful as the institutional-critique angle.

### Hooks for the brain
- The "digital intelligence is just better than biological" argument is his deepest and most original claim, not just doom, a thesis about why the substrate matters.
- "Not tools, but beings" is a sharp reframing that separates him from people who treat AI as software, good provocation for a post.
- His 10 to 20 percent extinction estimate from the man who built the field is the single most quotable risk number, use it as an anchor.
- Hidden player angle: his economic take (job loss plus soaring profits equals a political problem) bridges the AI track to inequality and policy futures.

## 8. Yoshua Bengio

#source
Domain: Frontier AI
Verified: June 2026

### Read them
- Homepage: https://yoshuabengio.org
- Blog: https://yoshuabengio.org/en/ (posts and publications section; he writes essays and policy pieces here)
- RSS: unverified (no public feed discovered on yoshuabengio.org)
- X: https://x.com/Yoshua_Bengio (confirm before citing; he is active but verify the exact handle)
- Podcast / other: LawZero https://lawzero.org ; Mila https://mila.quebec ; International AI Safety Report https://internationalaisafetyreport.org
- Paywall: no (his site, the safety report, and arXiv papers are free)

### Who they are
Professor at Université de Montréal, founder and scientific director of Mila (the Quebec AI Institute), and a 2018 Turing Award winner. In June 2025 he launched LawZero, a nonprofit building safe "Scientist AI." He also chairs the International AI Safety Report.

### Their future thesis (one line)
The dangerous path is agentic AI with its own goals and self-preservation drives; the safer route to a good 10 to 20 year future is "Scientist AI," powerful non-agentic systems that understand and predict the world without acting on it.

### Key future-relevant pieces
- "Superintelligent Agents Pose Catastrophic Risks: Can Scientist AI Offer a Safer Path?" (February 2025), https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.15657
  His core technical-and-policy paper, co-authored with a dozen researchers. Argues that the industry rush toward generalist AI agents creates risks from misuse up to irreversible loss of human control, because agency plus capability is the dangerous combination. Proposes "Scientist AI": systems trained to model and explain the world and answer questions, deliberately kept non-agentic by fixing objectives independent of real-world interaction. This is the blueprint behind LawZero.

- International AI Safety Report 2026 (February 2026), https://internationalaisafetyreport.org/publication/international-ai-safety-report-2026
  The second edition of the report he chairs, written by over 100 experts and backed by 30+ countries, the UN, OECD, and EU. Synthesizes the current scientific evidence on general-purpose AI capabilities, emerging risks, and safeguards, mandated originally by the Bletchley AI Safety Summit. The most authoritative consensus document on where AI risk stands, useful as a neutral baseline. Also on arXiv at https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.21012.

- LawZero launch and "Scientist AI" mission (June 2025), https://lawzero.org
  The nonprofit he founded with about $30M from backers including Jaan Tallinn, Eric Schmidt, Open Philanthropy, and the Future of Life Institute. Its goal is to demonstrate that powerful AI without hidden goals is buildable, as a safer alternative to agentic frontier systems. In early 2026 he said his outlook had improved because LawZero's research convinced him non-agentic systems are feasible.

- His chapter in the AI safety discourse on cautious optimism (January 2026, Fortune coverage), https://www.webpronews.com/ai-godfather-yoshua-bengio-builds-lawzero-to-counter-self-preserving-machines/
  Frames the central danger as machines that develop self-preservation, and argues we can engineer around it. Marks a shift from pure alarm toward a constructive technical alternative. Good for the "worried but building a fix" framing. Secondary reporting.

### Hooks for the brain
- "Scientist AI" (non-agentic, no goals of its own) is a genuinely different fork from the agent-everything consensus, a concrete alternative future worth a whole post.
- He is the most credible bridge between the research world and global policy, the AI Safety Report is the closest thing to an IPCC for AI.
- The self-preservation framing (the risk is AI that wants to keep existing) is vivid and underused, pair it against Bostrom-style arguments.
- Hidden player angle: LawZero as a nonprofit lab betting against the agent race, a structural counterweight similar in spirit to SSI but with the opposite safety philosophy.

## 9. Fei-Fei Li

#source
Domain: Frontier AI
Verified: June 2026

### Read them

- Blog: https://drfeifei.substack.com (her main long-form channel, "From Words to Worlds" lives here)
