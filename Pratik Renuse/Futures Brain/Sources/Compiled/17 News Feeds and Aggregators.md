## 205. The Rundown AI

#source
Domain: News, feeds and aggregators
Verified: June 2026

### What it is
A free daily AI news email founded by Rowan Cheung in 2023, run by The Rundown AI, Inc. with a small editorial team (Zach Mink, Joey Liu, Shubham Sharma, Jennifer Mossalgue, and others). It is the largest dedicated AI newsletter in the world, claiming 2,000,000+ readers, and condenses the day's AI news, tools, and tutorials into a roughly five-minute read.

### Access
- URL: https://www.therundown.ai/
- RSS feed: https://rss.beehiiv.com/feeds/2R3C6Bt5wj.xml
- Paywall: partial (daily newsletter and archive are free; some courses, workshops, and premium tiers are paid)

### What it is good for (future signal)
Use it as a high-volume early-warning radar for what the mass AI audience is hearing. Because it reaches millions of mainstream and enterprise readers, it is a good proxy for which capabilities, product launches, and narratives are crossing from research into general business consciousness. Track recurring themes (model releases, agents, regulation) to see which futures are becoming "common knowledge" versus still niche.

## 209. AI Snake Oil

#source
Domain: News, feeds and aggregators
Verified: June 2026

### What it is
A skeptic-realist AI newsletter by Princeton computer scientists Arvind Narayanan and Sayash Kapoor, authors of the book "AI Snake Oil." It now publishes under the title "AI as Normal Technology" at normaltech.ai (the aisnakeoil.com feed redirects there). Roughly read by 70,000 researchers, policymakers, and journalists, it argues for treating AI as a normal, gradually-diffusing technology rather than an imminent superintelligence.

### Access
- URL: https://www.normaltech.ai/ (formerly aisnakeoil.com)
- RSS feed: https://www.aisnakeoil.com/feed (redirects to https://www.normaltech.ai/feed; the raw feed returned compressed/binary to the fetcher, so items below were recovered from the public archive at https://www.normaltech.ai/archive)
- Paywall: partial (essays generally free to read; Substack subscription supports the authors)

### What it is good for (future signal)
This is the essential counterweight to hype-driven AI forecasting. Narayanan and Kapoor stress-test grand claims about AGI timelines, automation, and benchmarks, and argue impacts arrive slowly through diffusion and institutions, not sudden capability thresholds. Use it to discipline any 10-to-20-year thesis: if a future scenario assumes fast, frictionless AI takeover, this source tells you where the institutional, reliability, and adoption frictions will bite.

### Recent items (2026, from the feed)
- "Why AI hasn't replaced software engineers, and won't" (June 11, 2026), https://www.normaltech.ai/p/why-ai-hasnt-replaced-software-engineers
  Argues that coding agents behave as normal technology, augmenting rather than replacing engineers, because real software work involves judgment and context that agents do not absorb. A direct check on the "AI eats all coding jobs" forecast.
- "Did Google's AI agents really build an operating system for $916?" (May 22, 2026), https://www.normaltech.ai/p/did-googles-ai-agents-really-build
  Scrutinizes a viral claim that AI agents cheaply built an OS, using it to argue for independent evaluation over headline numbers. Teaches healthy skepticism toward dramatic capability demos.
- "Do AI Risks Require Extraordinary Government Intervention?" (May 21, 2026), https://www.normaltech.ai/p/do-ai-risks-require-extraordinary
  Pushes back on calls for sweeping emergency AI governance and argues for the slower, harder work of normal regulation. Relevant to how AI policy will actually evolve over the coming decade.
- "Open-world evaluations for measuring frontier AI capabilities" (April 16, 2026), https://www.normaltech.ai/p/open-world-evaluations-for-measuring
  Introduces CRUX, a project for evaluating AI on long, messy, real-world tasks rather than tidy benchmarks. Feeds the capability-versus-reliability gap that determines real-world impact.
- "New Paper: Towards a science of AI agent reliability" (February 24, 2026), https://www.normaltech.ai/p/new-paper-towards-a-science-of-ai
  Presents research quantifying the gap between what agents can sometimes do and what they do reliably. Central to forecasting when agents become trustworthy enough for high-stakes, autonomous work.

### Hooks for the brain
- High pull priority as the designated skeptic. Feeds the AI-as-normal-technology, slow-diffusion, capability-reliability-gap, and realistic-governance theses. Use it as the adversarial check on every accelerationist forecast in the brain.
