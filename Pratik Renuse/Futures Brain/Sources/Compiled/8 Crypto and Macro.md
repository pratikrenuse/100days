## 92. Vitalik Buterin

#source
Domain: Crypto, networks and macro-tech
Verified: June 2026

### Read them
- Homepage: https://vitalik.eth.limo/
- Blog: https://vitalik.eth.limo/ (mirror of vitalik.ca, same content)
- RSS: https://vitalik.eth.limo/feed.xml (full-archive RSS 2.0, verified working)
- X: https://x.com/VitalikButerin
- Podcast / other: No own podcast. Frequent guest (Bankless, etc.). Research also posts to ethresear.ch and Farcaster.
- Paywall: no (fully free, static HTML, plain text, easy to fetch)

### Who they are
Co-founder of Ethereum and its most influential thinker. As of 2026 he has stepped back from day-to-day leadership but still drives protocol direction, research, and the broader d/acc (decentralized, defensive acceleration) philosophy.

### Their future thesis (one line)
Technology should be steered, not just accelerated: build open, verifiable, privacy-preserving, decentralized systems (crypto plus defensive tech) so that the AI era keeps power balanced among many people rather than concentrating it.

### Key future-relevant pieces
- "Balance of power" (Dec 30, 2025), https://vitalik.eth.limo/general/2025/12/30/balance_of_power.html
  Argues that the central political and technical question of the coming decades is how to keep power distributed as AI and other powerful technologies scale. Frames crypto, open source, and verifiability as tools to prevent any single actor (a state, a company, or an AI lab) from gaining decisive control. A clear statement of his governing worldview heading into the AI age.

- "My response to AI 2027" (Jul 10, 2025), https://vitalik.eth.limo/general/2025/07/10/2027.html
  His rebuttal to the influential "AI 2027" superintelligence-by-2027 scenario. He accepts fast AI progress is plausible but argues the doom timeline understates how much defensive technology (cyber defense, bio defense, verification, decentralized infrastructure) can shift the balance toward safety. Lays out his d/acc alternative to both pure acceleration and pure pause.

- "AI as the engine, humans as the steering wheel" (Feb 28, 2025), https://vitalik.eth.limo/general/2025/02/28/aihumans.html
  Proposes a design pattern for the AI era where AI provides raw capability but humans retain directional control, applied to governance, prediction markets, and funding mechanisms. Connects crypto primitives (info finance, voting, allocation) to keeping humans meaningfully in the loop. Useful for anyone thinking about human-AI institutional design.

- "d/acc: one year later" (Jan 5, 2025), https://vitalik.eth.limo/general/2025/01/05/dacc2.html
  A one-year update on his d/acc framework (decentralized, defensive, differential acceleration). Reviews progress across defensive tech categories (bio, cyber, info, physical) and argues for accelerating defense-favoring technologies specifically rather than tech in general. The canonical articulation of his preferred path through the next decade.

- "Possible futures of the Ethereum protocol" series, parts 1 to 6 (Oct 2024), https://vitalik.eth.limo/general/2024/10/14/futures1.html
  A six-part roadmap (The Merge, Surge, Scourge, Verge, Purge, Splurge) laying out where Ethereum's protocol is headed over the coming years on scaling, decentralization, security, and simplification. The definitive technical future-map for the network. Dense but the reference for Ethereum's multi-year direction.

- "The promise and challenges of crypto + AI applications" (Jan 30, 2024), https://vitalik.eth.limo/general/2024/01/30/cryptoai.html
  Categorizes how crypto and AI intersect: AI as a player, as an interface, as the rules, and as the objective of crypto mechanisms. Flags which combinations are genuinely useful versus hype, and where the real long-term value sits. A grounding map for the crypto-x-AI narrative that will define much of the decade.

### Hooks for the brain
- d/acc (defensive acceleration) is an underused frame for marketers and builders: position products as defense-favoring, verifiable, or privacy-preserving rather than just "AI-powered."
- Vitalik is one of the few crypto figures actively writing AGI-safety arguments. Cross-reference his "AI 2027" response with the doomer and accelerationist camps for a balanced map.
- "Balance of power" as a thesis lens: who controls compute, models, and infrastructure in 10 to 20 years is the meta-question under most of his recent essays.
- Hidden angle: his interest in info finance and prediction markets (separate Nov 2024 essay) ties crypto directly to better collective forecasting, relevant to any "futures" knowledge base.
- His blog is fully open, plain HTML, RSS-complete back to 2016: an ideal primary source to mine programmatically without paywalls.

## 93. Chris Dixon

#source
Domain: Crypto, networks and macro-tech
Verified: June 2026

### Read them
- Homepage: https://cdixon.org/ (personal site: "Selected" essays plus archive. NOTE: largely an archive now, newest post is Jun 2023, the Read Write Own announcement.)
- Blog: https://cdixon.substack.com/ (his ACTIVE newsletter as of 2026. Major new essays also publish at https://a16zcrypto.com/ under his author page.)
- RSS: https://cdixon.org/feed/ (personal-site feed; for current writing use the Substack feed https://cdixon.substack.com/feed)
- X: https://x.com/cdixon
- Podcast / other: a16z crypto author page https://a16zcrypto.com/team/chris-dixon/. Book site https://readwriteown.com/. Frequent podcast guest (a16z, Conversations with Tyler).
- Paywall: partial (cdixon.org and a16zcrypto.com essays are free; some Substack posts may be subscriber-only. Substack is JS-heavy but server-renders article text for fetching.)

### Who they are
Founder and managing partner of a16z crypto (Andreessen Horowitz's crypto arm, the largest dedicated crypto venture franchise). Author of "Read Write Own" and one of the most influential proponents of the thesis that blockchains will rebuild the internet's economic layer.

### VENUE CORRECTION
The dossier listed "cdixon.org" as the place to read him. That site is real and useful for his canonical older essays, but it is now mostly an archive (last post June 2023). His current 2024-2026 writing lives on his Substack (cdixon.substack.com) and at a16zcrypto.com. Use those for anything recent.

### Their future thesis (one line)
The internet is entering a third era ("Read Write Own") where blockchains let users, creators, and developers own the networks they use, providing the only credible counterweight to a future where a handful of big tech companies (now supercharged by AI) control the entire digital economy.

### Key future-relevant pieces
- "Read Write Own: Building the Next Era of the Internet" (book, Jan 2024), https://readwriteown.com/
  His definitive statement: the internet went from "read" (early web) to "read-write" (social platforms) and should now move to "read-write-own" via blockchains. Argues corporate networks inevitably extract from their users once growth slows, while blockchain networks can keep commitments via code. The core framework behind everything he writes.

- "Why decentralization matters" (Feb 2018, foundational), https://cdixon.org/2018/02/18/why-decentralization-matters/
  The essay that crystallized the case for crypto as an antidote to platform power: centralized platforms court users then turn against them, whereas decentralized networks align incentives long-term. Predates the book but remains his clearest short argument. Essential background reading.

- "Blockchain innovation will put an AI-powered internet back into users' hands" (2025), https://a16zcrypto.com/posts/article/blockchain-ai-internet/
  His direct attempt to fuse the crypto and AI futures: as AI concentrates power in big model owners, blockchains can return ownership and economic upside to users and creators. Frames crypto plus AI as complementary rather than competing. The most future-forward piece tying his thesis to the AI era.

- "The World in 2025" (Wired UK op-ed, Dec 11, 2024)
  A near-term forecast (drawn from Read Write Own ideas) on how blockchains will decentralize an increasingly AI-driven internet. Compact statement of his expectations for the immediate years ahead. (Published at Wired UK; may be behind Wired's metered paywall.)

- "The next big thing will start out looking like a toy" (Jan 2010, evergreen), https://cdixon.org/2010/01/03/the-next-big-thing-will-start-out-looking-like-a-toy/
  His most-cited heuristic for spotting disruptive technology: the important new thing is usually dismissed as a toy because it underperforms incumbents on existing metrics while being better on a new dimension. A durable mental model for evaluating any emerging tech (crypto, AI agents, robotics). Timeless lens for a futures brain.

### Hooks for the brain
- "Read Write Own" is a clean three-act story of the internet that any audience can grasp: useful scaffolding for explaining why ownership/crypto matters in plain terms.
- His "toy" heuristic and "come for the tool, stay for the network" are evergreen frameworks for evaluating which 2026 toys become 2035 infrastructure. Strong for "what looks like a toy now" posts.
- Tension to mine: a16z is fundraising hard for crypto in 2026 while critics say "read write own" stalled into a "financial era." His rebuttal (the thesis is early, not wrong) is a live debate worth tracking.
- Cross-link his crypto-plus-AI essay with Vitalik (balance of power) and Tunguz (stablecoins, agentic payments) for an "ownership layer of the AI internet" cluster.
- Hidden player angle: a16z crypto itself is a quiet kingmaker shaping which protocols get capital. Worth a "who funds the decentralized future" piece.

## 94. Naval Ravikant

#source
Domain: Crypto, networks and macro-tech
Verified: June 2026

### Read them
- Homepage: https://nav.al/

- RSS: https://nav.al/feed (WordPress feed; unverified exact path, standard WP default, treat as likely working)
- X: https://x.com/naval (personal). Podcast account: https://x.com/navalpodcast
- Podcast / other: Naval Podcast (https://nav.al/podcast), also AngelList founder, Airchat co-founder. Instagram @naval.
- Paywall: no (essays are free, clean HTML, easy to fetch)

### Who they are
Angel investor and AngelList founder, one of the most quoted voices on wealth creation, leverage, and judgment. As of 2026 he is heavily focused on AI as the next industrial revolution and on "vibe coding," plus running his podcast and writing short philosophical essays.

### Their future thesis (one line)
AI is the new industrial revolution, a fifth form of leverage that lets a single person with judgment and specific knowledge build and ship at superhuman scale, so the future belongs to small, code-and-media-leveraged individuals rather than large organizations.

### Key future-relevant pieces
- "The AI Industrial Revolution" (2026), https://nav.al/industrial
  Frames AI as a general-purpose technology on par with steam and electricity, reshaping who can create economic value. Argues the advantage shifts to people who can direct AI with taste and judgment rather than those who own labor or capital. His central 2026 statement on where the economy is heading. (Likely interview/transcript format.)

- "A Motorcycle for the Mind" (2026), https://nav.al/ai
  Riffs on the old "computer as a bicycle for the mind" line, arguing AI is a far more powerful amplifier of human capability. Positions AI as a tool that extends individual reach rather than replacing the human in the loop. A concise articulation of his human-plus-AI optimism.

- "A Return to Code" (2026), https://nav.al/code
  On "vibe coding": the idea that natural-language AI tools return software creation to anyone who can describe what they want. Argues the ability to build is being democratized, collapsing the gap between idea and product. Relevant to any thesis about who builds software in the next decade.

- "Waste Tokens, Save Time" (2026), https://nav.al/tokens
  A practical mindset piece arguing that compute (AI tokens) is cheap and human time is the scarce resource, so you should spend AI generously. Captures the economic logic of the AI era at the individual level. Short and actionable.

- "Sell the Truth" (2026), https://nav.al/sell
  Argues that in an AI-saturated, low-trust information environment, honesty and authenticity become the durable competitive edge. Connects to the broader theme that distribution and trust matter more as content becomes infinite. Useful counterpoint to pure AI-volume strategies.

- "How to Get Rich (without getting lucky)" (2019, evergreen), https://nav.al/rich
  His foundational framework on wealth: seek equity not wages, build specific knowledge, and use the four leverages (labor, capital, code, media), with code and media as the new permissionless ones. Not future-dated but the lens through which all his AI-era writing should be read. The anchor text for his worldview.

### Hooks for the brain
- Naval's "four leverages, now five with AI" framework is a clean way to explain to marketers why solo and tiny teams will out-compete bloated ones in the next decade.
- "Waste tokens, save time" inverts the usual cost-anxiety around AI: a strong, contrarian message for a productivity or AI-tooling product.
- Hidden angle: his "Sell the Truth" thesis (trust as moat in an AI-fake world) directly rhymes with Lyn Alden's "Wild West" information-disorder argument. Cross-link these two for a "trust economy" post.
- He rarely writes long-form now; most content is short essays and podcast transcripts. Mine the podcast for richer future predictions.
- Watch Airchat (voice plus AI social) as his bet on what post-text social media looks like.

### Read them

- RSS: https://www.noahpinion.blog/feed (Substack RSS; standard /feed path)

- Paywall: partial (about 3 of 5 weekly posts are free; deeper analysis often paywalled. Substack is JS-heavy but article text server-renders for fetching.)

### Who they are
Economist and writer (PhD economics, University of Michigan; former Stony Brook finance professor; ex-Bloomberg columnist). Runs Noahpinion full-time, one of the most-read economics newsletters, covering macro, technology, geopolitics, and industrial policy from an explicitly techno-optimist, pro-abundance stance.

### Their future thesis (one line)
The 2020s are a hinge decade defined by two general-purpose technologies, AI and the "electric tech stack" (batteries, motors, power electronics), and the countries (especially the US versus China) that master manufacturing, energy, and the new drone-and-AI mode of warfare will set the balance of power, so the West must choose building and adaptation over nostalgia and scarcity.

### Key future-relevant pieces
- "The future of war is the future of society" (Nov 20, 2025), https://www.noahpinion.blog/p/the-future-of-war-is-the-future-of
  Argues we are mid-revolution in military affairs as cheap autonomous drones (soon AI-piloted) make human infantry and expensive manned platforms obsolete, as already visible in Ukraine. Draws on history (Mongols, gunpowder, industrial war) to argue each new weapons package forces deep social and institutional change, and that China is currently adapting fastest. A sweeping, vivid thesis linking technology, war, and how societies must reorganize.

- "Thoughts on Sinofuturism" (Apr 2026), https://www.noahpinion.blog/p/thoughts-on-sinofuturism
  Examines China's tech trajectory (delivery drones, robots, air taxis, EVs, humanoid robots) and argues its mastery of the battery-and-motor stack is producing a visibly futuristic society. A counterweight to declinist takes on China and a prompt to ask which country actually builds the future. Pairs with his US-falling-behind warnings.

- "Updated thoughts on AI risk" / "My thoughts on AI safety" (2025-2026), https://www.noahpinion.blog/p/updated-thoughts-on-ai-risk
  His evolving, deliberately moderate position on AI: skeptical of both doomer extinction narratives and naive utopianism, focused on concrete economic and security disruptions. Useful as a calibrated middle-ground reference between the AI-safety camps. (Companion piece: "My thoughts on AI safety".)

- "The future isn't what it used to be" (Mar 2026), https://www.noahpinion.blog/p/the-future-isnt-what-it-used-to-be
  Reflects on how AI has reopened radical uncertainty about the future, which could deliver wonders (ending aging and disease) or catastrophes (bioweapons, drone war). Argues the future has become genuinely unpredictable again after decades of feeling stagnant. A good framing essay for any "where is the world heading" project.

- "Why every country needs to master the electric tech stack" (2025), https://www.noahpinion.blog/p/why-every-country-needs-to-master
  Introduces his "electric tech stack" concept (batteries, electric motors, power electronics) as the foundational industrial base of the coming era, underpinning EVs, robots, drones, and grids. Argues America is dangerously behind China here. Central to his industrial-policy and national-power arguments.

### Hooks for the brain
- The "electric tech stack" is a sharp, underused framing: batteries plus motors plus power electronics as the new oil. Strong organizing concept for a hardware/energy futures piece.
- His drone-warfare thesis (cheap autonomous drones obsolete the soldier, forcing societal reorganization) is dramatic and well-evidenced from Ukraine. High-engagement angle, handle the subject matter soberly.
- Recurring tension worth mining: US scarcity/nostalgia versus China's build-everything pace. Good for "who actually builds the future" content aimed at a Western audience.
- He is a techno-optimist with credibility because he criticizes both doomers and hype: a balanced anchor source when other voices are polarized.
- Cross-link his AI-risk centrism with Vitalik (d/acc) and Noah's own abundance agenda for a "calibrated optimism" cluster, contrast with pure accelerationists.

## 96. Byrne Hobart

#source
Domain: Crypto, networks and macro-tech
Verified: June 2026

### Read them

- RSS: https://diff.substack.com/feed (Substack RSS; standard /feed path)
- X: https://x.com/ByrneHobart
- Podcast / other: "The Riff" (podcast companion to The Diff). Author of "Boom: Bubbles and the End of Stagnation" (Stripe Press, 2024, with Tobias Huber). Investor/consultant.
- Paywall: partial (The Diff is a paid daily; some free posts plus paywalled deep dives. Substack server-renders text but most premium content requires a subscription.)

### Who they are
Writer, investor, and analyst behind The Diff, a widely read newsletter covering "inflections in finance and tech." Known for dense, first-principles essays connecting markets, technology, and history. As of 2026 he also runs the more accessible "Capital Gains" and is co-author of the book "Boom."

### Their future thesis (one line)
Escaping the post-1970s "Great Stagnation" requires financial bubbles: by lowering collective risk aversion and over-funding ambitious, mission-driven groups, bubbles are the mechanism that funds civilization-scale breakthroughs (from Apollo to fracking to crypto to AI), so the right response to a bubble is often to lean in, not flee.

### Key future-relevant pieces
- "Boom: Bubbles and the End of Stagnation" (book, 2024, with Tobias Huber), https://press.stripe.com/boom
  Argues that since the 1970s innovation shifted from atoms to bits and the West entered an age of stagnation (slow wage growth, expensive incremental science). The counterintuitive thesis: financial bubbles, by overfunding experimental work and organizing high-agency people around a transcendent mission, are the engine of transformative progress, not a bug. Frames AI as a potentially productive bubble. His definitive long-form future argument.

- The Diff (newsletter, ongoing), https://www.thediff.co/
  His daily vehicle, built around the question of what future historians will say mattered today. Recurring future-relevant themes: AI's effect on labor and capital, the economics of compute and data centers, US-China competition, energy, and how capital markets fund (or starve) innovation. The single best ongoing source for his thinking, though most depth sits behind the paywall.

- "Capital Gains" (companion publication, ongoing), https://www.thediff.co/ (linked from The Diff)
  His more accessible explainer newsletter covering finance and economics fundamentals. Useful for grounding his bubble-and-progress arguments in teachable concepts. Lower-jargon entry point to his worldview.

- "The Riff" (podcast, ongoing)
  Conversation companion to The Diff where he and guests work through inflection points in tech and markets in real time. Good for hearing his live takes on AI capex, crypto, and macro. Audio, so not text-fetchable.

### Hooks for the brain
- The "bubbles are good actually" thesis is a genuinely contrarian, memorable angle: the AI capex boom may be a productive bubble that funds the future even if many investors lose money. Strong, counterintuitive post material.
- His framing question ("what will historians say mattered about today?") is a perfect organizing prompt for an entire futures knowledge base.
- Hidden-player lens: he is unusually good at surfacing the financial plumbing behind tech stories (who funds it, how the capital cycle works). Mine The Diff for the money mechanics other futurists skip.
- Tension to explore: Hobart's pro-bubble optimism versus Lyn Alden's caution about debt-fueled fragility. Both watch capital cycles closely but draw opposite lessons. Great for a debate-style piece.
- Caveat for the brain: most of his best work is paywalled, so plan to subscribe or rely on the book, free posts, and podcast for sourcing.

We are entering "The Great Acceleration," a period where converging exponential technologies (AI, robotics, energy storage, blockchain, multiomics) compound on each other to add multiple percentage points to global GDP growth and create tens of trillions in new value by 2030, with Bitcoin maturing into a major store-of-value asset.

  ARK's 10th annual flagship report and the single best summary of her worldview.   ARK projects Bitcoin reaching roughly a $16 trillion market cap by 2030 (about $750,000 per coin base case, up to ~$1.25M bull case), with the broader crypto market near $28 trillion. Notably, ARK raised its "digital gold" estimate after gold's 2025 run but cut Bitcoin's emerging-market safe-haven role due to fast stablecoin adoption. A concrete, falsifiable long-range crypto forecast. (Reported via CoinDesk; primary numbers are from the ARK report.)

- "Cathie Wood's 2026 Outlook: The US Economy Is A Coiled Spring" (early 2026), https://www.ark-invest.com/articles/market-commentary/cathie-woods-2026-outlook
  Her macro framing for 2026: deflationary pressure from AI and innovation, a US economy poised to accelerate as rates ease and productivity gains land. Argues innovation-driven deflation is the dominant force, not the inflation most investors fear. Useful for understanding her contrarian read on the macro setup.

- "In The Know with Cathie Wood" (monthly video series, ongoing), https://www.ark-invest.com/videos/market-commentary/may-2026-in-the-know-cathie-wood
  Her recurring monthly commentary connecting fiscal/monetary policy, market signals, and ARK's innovation theses. The best running source for how her views update month to month. Video format, so not text-fetchable, but ARK often posts summaries.

### Hooks for the brain
- "The Great Acceleration" (convergence of five exponential platforms compounding together) is a strong, citable macro-optimist frame. Useful as the bull-case bookend against more cautious voices.
- ARK's Bitcoin-to-$16T-by-2030 and crypto-to-$28T calls are the most aggressive mainstream targets out there: ideal as the high anchor in any crypto-future range, with appropriate skepticism noted.

## 98. Tomasz Tunguz

#source

### Read them

- RSS: https://tomtunguz.com/index.xml (Hugo default feed; unverified exact path but standard for this static site)
- X: https://x.com/ttunguz
- Podcast / other: Founder and GP at Theory Ventures (theoryvc.com). Newsletter via Kit (ttunguz.kit.com), "read by 150k+ founders & operators." Former Redpoint partner, former Google PM.
- Paywall: no (posts are free, clean static HTML, very easy to fetch)

### Who they are
Founder and General Partner of Theory Ventures, an early-stage fund focused on data, AI, and infrastructure. A prolific daily blogger on SaaS metrics and venture theory who, as of 2026, writes almost entirely about the agentic-AI transformation of software.

### Their future thesis (one line)
Software is being rebuilt around autonomous AI agents: the value moves from UI and data-management to the "harness" (reliability, orchestration, observability) around models, and agents will soon out-earn humans for many business tasks while breaking the current database and web architecture.

### Key future-relevant pieces
- "12 Predictions for 2026" (Dec 22, 2025), https://tomtunguz.com/2026-predictions/
  His annual scored prediction list, and his single best snapshot of where he thinks tech is heading. Calls for businesses paying more for AI agents than people for the first time, AI executing 8-plus-hour autonomous workstreams by late 2026, the web flipping to "agent-first" design, stablecoins reaching 30% of international payments, and the data-center buildout hitting 3.5% of US GDP. Concrete, falsifiable, and wide-ranging.

- "Software After AI" / "Harnessing AI" (2025-2026), https://tomtunguz.com/harnessing-ai/
  Argues software engineering has shifted from building UI and managing data to building the "harness": the runtime, constraints, and reliability layer that turns a raw LLM into a dependable agent. Defines a seven-part new stack (context/memory, tools/action, orchestration, state, sandbox/compute, observability/governance, cost optimization). A foundational map of the emerging AI-native software stack.

- "AI Managing AI" / "The Scaffolding Shift" (2025-2026), https://tomtunguz.com/the-scaffolding-shift/
  Explores a near future where AI systems supervise other AI systems and humans become "agent managers." Raises the question of how many agents one person can realistically manage and what org charts look like when most "workers" are software. Useful for thinking about labor and team structure in the late 2020s.

- "The Golden Age of AI Applications" (2025-2026), https://tomtunguz.com/golden-age-of-applications/
  Makes the case that value is shifting up the stack from foundation models to applications, as model capability becomes commoditized and the winners are those who wrap it into reliable workflows. Counter-narrative to "models eat everything." Investment-thesis relevant.

### Hooks for the brain
- "Agent managers" is a sharp, sticky concept for content: the job of the near future is managing a fleet of AI agents, not doing the task yourself.
- His "agent-first web" prediction (sites and docs designed for robots first, humans second) is a concrete, near-term shift marketers should plan for. Strong post angle on SEO becoming "AEO" (agent experience optimization).

### Their future thesis (one line)
Returns to talent and effort are becoming superlinear in a technology-driven world, so the future rewards a small number of exceptionally ambitious, independent-minded builders, while AI threatens to split society into those who still write (and therefore think) and those who do not.

### Key future-relevant pieces
- "Writes and Write-Nots" (Oct 2024), https://www.paulgraham.com/writes.html
  Argues that because AI can now write competently, most people will stop writing entirely, and since writing is thinking, society will divide into a small group who can still think clearly and a large group who cannot.
