## 60. Tim Urban

#source
Domain: Futurists and big-picture
Verified: June 2026

### Read them
- Homepage: https://waitbutwhy.com/
- Blog: https://waitbutwhy.com/ (long-form posts archive at https://waitbutwhy.com/archive)
- RSS: https://waitbutwhy.com/feed
- X: https://twitter.com/waitbutwhy (@waitbutwhy)
- Podcast / other: Book "What's Our Problem?" (2023), self-published at https://waitbutwhy.com/whatsourproblem ; TED talk "Inside the Mind of a Master Procrastinator"
- Paywall: no (free site, optional Patreon support)

### Who they are
Writer and illustrator behind Wait But Why, the long-form stick-figure blog that popularized deep explainers on AI, space, and the human condition. As of 2026 he posts infrequently, mixing personal essays with big-picture pieces, and his 2015 AI series remains a canonical on-ramp for thinking about superintelligence.

### Their future thesis (one line)
Artificial superintelligence is plausibly coming this century and could mean either humanity's immortality or its extinction, so the stakes of how we build it dwarf almost everything else.

### Key future-relevant pieces
- "The AI Revolution: The Road to Superintelligence" (Jan 2015), https://waitbutwhy.com/2015/01/artificial-intelligence-revolution-1.html
  Part 1 of his two-part series walks a general reader from narrow AI to artificial general intelligence and explains why recursive self-improvement could make progress feel impossibly fast once it starts. He uses the staircase-of-progress and exponential-growth framing to argue that human intuition badly underrates how quickly an intelligence explosion could arrive. It is the single most-shared introduction to the topic for non-specialists.

- "The AI Revolution: Our Immortality or Extinction" (Jan 2015), https://waitbutwhy.com/2015/01/artificial-intelligence-revolution-2.html
  Part 2 lays out the two-sided bet: a superintelligence aligned with human goals could end disease and death, while a misaligned one could wipe us out almost as a side effect. Urban frames this as the most important fork in human history and stresses that small early choices in how we build AI compound into radically different endpoints. The piece remains his clearest statement of the high-variance future.

- "Neuralink and the Brain's Magical Future" (Apr 2017), https://waitbutwhy.com/2017/04/neuralink.html
  A very long explainer on brain-computer interfaces, built around Elon Musk's Neuralink, arguing that direct neural links may be humanity's way to stay relevant alongside advancing AI. He frames merging with machines as a possible answer to the alignment and obsolescence problem. Useful as a future-of-human-augmentation reference even though the company specifics have aged.

- "What's Our Problem?" (2023), https://waitbutwhy.com/whatsourproblem
  His self-published book reframes societal dysfunction as a contest between "high-rung" (independent, truth-seeking) and "low-rung" (tribal) thinking, arguing that our ability to navigate powerful future technologies depends on the quality of our collective reasoning. The thesis is less about a specific technology and more about whether our information ecosystem can stay sane enough to make good choices. Relevant to any "can we govern the future" angle.

### Hooks for the brain
- The "immortality or extinction" binary is a clean, high-variance framing for any post on AI stakes, and it predates the current frontier-lab race by nearly a decade.
- Urban is a translator, not an original researcher: his value is making expert futures legible to mass audiences, so he is a bridge node between thinkers like Bostrom and the general public.
- His shift since 2023 toward personal/travel essays (Bhutan, parenting) over tech is itself a signal: even the great AI explainers have stepped back from the frontier commentary, leaving the field to Substack specialists.
- Post idea: revisit his 2015 timeline predictions against 2026 reality (the affordable brain-scale computer call) as a case study in how futurist forecasts age.

## 61. Scott Alexander

#source
Domain: Futurists and big-picture
Verified: June 2026

### Read them
- Homepage: https://www.astralcodexten.com/
- Blog: https://www.astralcodexten.com/ (Astral Codex Ten, on Substack; successor to Slate Star Codex)
- RSS: https://www.astralcodexten.com/feed
- X: https://substack.com/@astralcodexten (primary presence is Substack Notes; pen name, no traditional personal X handle)
- Podcast / other: AI Futures Project / "AI 2027" scenario (https://ai-2027.com); archived Slate Star Codex at https://slatestarcodex.com
- Paywall: partial (most posts free, some subscriber-only and open threads)

### Who they are
Pseudonymous psychiatrist and one of the most-read rationalist bloggers, writing Astral Codex Ten on Substack as of 2026. He is a central voice translating AI safety, forecasting, and timelines debates for a wide intellectual audience, and co-authored the influential "AI 2027" scenario.

### Their future thesis (one line)
Transformative AGI is probably arriving within decades with serious risk of catastrophe, but the outcome is genuinely uncertain and improvable, so careful probabilistic thinking plus alignment and coordination work matter enormously.

### Key future-relevant pieces
- "My AI Opinions" (Jun 11, 2026), https://www.astralcodexten.com/p/my-ai-opinions
  His single best one-stop summary of current beliefs, giving explicit probabilities across timelines, safety, geopolitics, and outcomes. He estimates a 25% chance of AGI (90% of knowledge work) by 2027, 50% by 2034, 75% by 2045, and roughly a 20% p(doom) given current corporate safety effort. He also breaks down a "diffusion gap," a "superhuman gap," and a "point of no return," and lays out where he agrees and disagrees with shorter-timeline forecasters.

- "Introducing AI 2027" (Apr 2025), https://www.astralcodexten.com/p/introducing-ai-2027
  Introduces the AI Futures Project's detailed month-by-month scenario in which coding agents improve through 2025-2026, then trigger an R&D feedback loop and intelligence explosion around 2027. The scenario is a concrete, falsifiable forecast meant to make abstract timeline debates legible, and Alexander notes his own median is somewhat later than the headline 2027 date. It became one of the most-discussed AI futures artifacts of the period.

- "Updated Look At Long-Term AI Risks" (2025), https://www.astralcodexten.com/p/updated-look-at-long-term-ai-risks
  Revisits the landscape of catastrophic and existential AI risks with current evidence, weighing alignment-by-default optimism against structural pressures that could still produce disaster. He stresses how much of the outcome hinges on the quality and resourcing of alignment teams versus rushed corporate incentives. A useful map of the risk arguments without doom-certainty.

- "The New AI Consciousness Paper" (2026), https://www.astralcodexten.com/p/the-new-ai-consciousness-paper
  Reviews philosophers-of-mind work assessing whether current AI systems are conscious and why they concluded it probably is not, while flagging how fast that could change. The piece sits at the frontier of the moral-status-of-AI question that becomes load-bearing in any future with vast numbers of AI agents. Speculative but a leading edge of the debate.

### Hooks for the brain
- "My AI Opinions" is a ready-made probability table: a single post that quantifies AGI timelines, doom, pause odds, and even simulation arguments, ideal for a "what do the serious forecasters actually believe" piece.
- The diffusion gap (AI that could do jobs vs AI that does do jobs) is an underused framing for marketers and operators: capability arriving years before adoption is the practical reality, not instant disruption.
- He is a hub connecting the AI Futures Project (Daniel Kokotajlo, Eli Lifland) and the wider rationalist forecasting world: trace the network through his citations.
- Post idea: contrast his ~2034 AGI median against Khosla's 5-year "80% of jobs" and Hammond's near-term timelines to show the real spread among smart people.

## 62. Robin Hanson

#source
Domain: Futurists and big-picture
Verified: June 2026

### Read them
- Homepage: https://mason.gmu.edu/~rhanson/home.html
- Blog: https://www.overcomingbias.com/ (Overcoming Bias, moved to Substack Feb 2023)
- RSS: https://www.overcomingbias.com/feed
- X: https://x.com/robinhanson (@robinhanson)
- Podcast / other: Substack handle @wildideaman ; books "The Age of Em" (2016, Oxford UP) and "The Elephant in the Brain" (2017, with Kevin Simler)
- Paywall: partial (most posts free on Substack, some subscriber-only)

### Who they are
Economist at George Mason University and former Future of Humanity Institute associate, known for prediction markets, the "Age of Em" forecast, and contrarian takes on hidden human motives. As of 2026 he writes Overcoming Bias on Substack and is the leading skeptic of fast-takeoff AI doom.

### Their future thesis (one line)
The big future shift is an economic growth explosion most likely driven by AI or brain emulations, but it will probably arrive as a broad, decade-long, competitive transition rather than a single rogue system suddenly "fooming" to take over.

### Key future-relevant pieces
- "The Age of Em: Work, Love, and Life When Robots Rule the Earth" (2016 book), https://ageofem.com/
  His detailed forecast of a future where human brains are scanned and emulated as software ("ems") before de novo AI is solved, producing trillions of copyable digital minds that quickly outnumber humans and run civilization at vastly faster subjective speeds. He reasons from standard economics, physics, and social science to describe their labor markets, cities, and culture. It is the most rigorous single attempt to picture a post-human-labor economy in concrete detail.

- "AI Risk, Again" (Mar 3, 2023), https://www.overcomingbias.com/p/ai-risk-again
  Written right after ChatGPT's surge, this restates his core disagreement with doom worriers: history shows innovations stay small relative to the world economy, so a single lab suddenly exploding to dominate everything is far less likely than a gradual, multi-actor transition. He argues control problems are real but best solved with concrete fielded systems, not pre-emptive regulation that could freeze progress. The clearest short statement of his anti-FOOM position.

- "Most AI Fear Is Future Fear" (2023), https://www.overcomingbias.com/p/ai-fear-is-mostly-fear-of-future
  Argues that much current AI anxiety is really displaced unease about rapid change in general, recurring roughly every three decades since the 1930s whenever automation demos impress people. He uses the historical pattern of false automation panics to discount the latest wave of extinction worry. A useful counterweight framing for any "is this time different" debate.

- "Grabby Aliens" model and updates (ongoing; e.g. "Kipping on Grabby Aliens", Sep 2024), https://www.overcomingbias.com/p/kipping-on-grabby-aliens
  His three-parameter cosmological model explains why we see no alien civilizations, why humanity appears early in cosmic history, and what that implies about expansionist ("grabby") civilizations spreading at near-light speed. The framework bears on humanity's long-run trajectory and whether we are destined to expand or be filtered. The Kipping post defends the model against a notable astrophysics critique.

### Hooks for the brain
- Hanson is the designated skeptic: pairing his anti-FOOM, gradual-transition view against Alexander, Ord, and AI 2027 gives any piece a real intellectual tension instead of one-sided doom.
- "Age of Em" is the hidden player in futures discourse: a fully worked-out post-labor economy that almost nobody references when debating UBI and job loss, despite being the most concrete model of it.
- His "future fear" lens (panics recur every ~30 years) is a sharp, contrarian hook for marketers tired of hype cycles.
- Post idea: use his prediction-market expertise to frame "why we should bet on the future, not just argue about it," tying futures thinking to decision markets.

## 63. Holden Karnofsky

#source
Domain: Futurists and big-picture
Verified: June 2026

### Read them
- Homepage: https://www.cold-takes.com/about/
- Blog: https://www.cold-takes.com/ (Cold Takes; largely dormant since he joined Anthropic in 2025, but remains his canonical futures archive). AI highlights index: https://www.cold-takes.com/cold-takes-on-ai/
- RSS: https://www.cold-takes.com/rss/
- X: https://x.com/coldtakes12 (@coldtakes12)
- Podcast / other: "Most Important Century" series available as Cold Takes Audio podcast and as a free PDF/ePub; long Dwarkesh Patel and 80,000 Hours interviews
- Paywall: no (free blog)

### Who they are
Co-founder of GiveWell and Open Philanthropy who, as of 2025, joined Anthropic as a member of technical staff working on its Responsible Scaling Policy and AI safety planning. His blog Cold Takes hosts the "most important century" thesis, one of the most cited frameworks for why advanced AI makes this century pivotal.

### Their future thesis (one line)
This could be the most important century in history because AI capable of automating science and technology (PASTA) would trigger explosive growth and lock in a galaxy-scale future, so we are dangerously unprepared and should respond with vigilance rather than hype or denial.

### Key future-relevant pieces
- "The Most Important Century" series (2021, summary updated 2022), https://www.cold-takes.com/most-important-century/
  The flagship series argues that if AI can fully automate innovation, it would restart an old economic feedback loop and drive productivity toward a singularity this century, getting us to a radically unfamiliar future faster than intuition allows. He introduces PASTA (Process for Automating Scientific and Technological Advancement) as the pivotal technology and contends it is more likely than not this century. The throughline is that all plausible long-run views are "wild," so we should take the stakes seriously now.

- "This Can't Go On" (2021), https://www.cold-takes.com/this-cant-go-on/
  Shows that the few-percent annual growth we treat as normal is historically bizarre and mathematically unsustainable, since at current rates the economy would soon exceed the atoms available in the galaxy. He uses this to argue we live in a uniquely special, accelerating sliver of time rather than a stable steady state. A compact, persuasive piece for resetting intuitions about "business as usual."

- "All Possible Views About Humanity's Future Are Wild" (2021), https://www.cold-takes.com/all-possible-views-about-humanitys-future-are-wild/
  Argues that whether transformative change comes this century or in 100,000 years, we still occupy an extraordinary position as among the first beings in a galaxy about to be populated. The point is to disarm the "this is too sci-fi to take seriously" reflex by showing the boring alternative is also wild. Good framing for getting skeptics to engage.

- "Forecasting Transformative AI: What's the Burden of Proof?" (2021), https://www.cold-takes.com/forecasting-transformative-ai-whats-the-burden-of-proof/
  Tackles the meta-question of how confident we should be about transformative AI given there is no settled "field of AI forecasting" or expert consensus. He argues the available estimates (including biological-anchors methods) make something like PASTA more likely than not this century, and that absence of consensus is not a reason to dismiss it. Sets up how to reason under deep uncertainty.

### Hooks for the brain
- Note the career signal: the author of the "most important century" thesis stopped blogging and went inside a frontier lab to work on safety policy. That move is itself a data point about where serious futurists think leverage now lies.
- "This Can't Go On" is the single most-quotable intuition-reset in the whole futures canon: one chart that reframes normal growth as a historical anomaly.
- PASTA (AI that automates R&D) is a cleaner, more economic framing than "AGI" and maps directly to the recursive-self-improvement debates Alexander and the AI 2027 team obsess over.
- Post idea: "Call to Vigilance" versus "call to action" is a distinctive emotional stance (intensity and humility, not excitement or fear) that contrasts well with both doomers and accelerationists.

## 64. Toby Ord

#source
Domain: Futurists and big-picture
Verified: June 2026

### Read them
- Homepage: https://www.tobyord.com/
- Blog: https://www.tobyord.com/writing (essays page; not a frequent blog, more a paper-and-essay archive)
- RSS: unverified (no clear public feed found on tobyord.com as of June 2026)
- X: https://x.com/tobyordoxford (@tobyordoxford)
- Podcast / other: book "The Precipice" (2020, theprecipice.com); recurring guest on the 80,000 Hours podcast (episodes on The Precipice and on inference scaling, #219)
- Paywall: no (essays free; book sold commercially)

### Who they are
Australian moral philosopher and senior researcher at Oxford (associated with the former Future of Humanity Institute and AI governance work), author of "The Precipice." As of 2025-2026 his focus has shifted toward how AI scaling trends reshape governance and existential risk.

### Their future thesis (one line)
Humanity faces a roughly one-in-six chance of existential catastrophe this century, with unaligned AI the single largest contributor, so safeguarding our long-term potential is the defining moral task of our era.

### Key future-relevant pieces
- "The Precipice: Existential Risk and the Future of Humanity" (2020 book), https://theprecipice.com/
  His landmark book argues humanity has the power to destroy itself but not yet the wisdom to ensure it won't, estimating about a 1-in-6 chance of existential catastrophe within the next century. He puts unaligned artificial general intelligence at roughly 1-in-10, higher than all other risks combined, ahead of pandemics, nuclear war, and climate. The book frames protecting future generations as a moral priority comparable to abolishing slavery.

- "The Precipice Revisited" (Jun 2025), https://www.tobyord.com/writing/the-precipice-revisited
  Updates the risk picture roughly five years on, walking through how climate, nuclear, pandemic, and AI risk have each shifted. He highlights that AI went from a race between a few labs to a race between the largest companies on Earth, with nonprofit safety framing giving way to compute-hungry productization, while also noting genuine policy wins (national AI safety institutes, the Bletchley Declaration). A concise "what changed" companion to the book.

- "Inference Scaling Reshapes AI Governance" (Mar 2025), https://www.tobyord.com/writing/inference-scaling-reshapes-ai-governance
  Argues the frontier is shifting from ever-bigger pre-training runs to spending more compute at inference (test-time reasoning), which quietly breaks governance regimes keyed to training-compute thresholds like the EU AI Act and California SB 53. Consequences include fewer concurrent copies of top models, weights mattering less than compute access (reducing leak risk but also the leverage of open-sourcing), and an industry shifting toward higher marginal costs. A sharp, technical, and underappreciated governance argument.

- "The Scaling Series" / 80,000 Hours #219 (Oct 2025), https://80000hours.org/podcast/episodes/toby-ord-inference-scaling-ai-governance/
  In this interview and companion essays he unpacks "the graphs AI companies would prefer you didn't fully understand," showing how scaling curves and diminishing returns reframe both capability forecasts and policy. He connects the technical shape of progress to who holds power and how regulation should adapt. Useful as an accessible audio entry point to his 2025 thinking.

### Hooks for the brain
- The "1-in-6 this century" number is the most quotable existential-risk statistic in circulation, and the AI share (1-in-10) is the load-bearing piece for any AI-stakes post.
- His inference-scaling argument is a genuinely contrarian, underused angle: governance frameworks built around training compute may already be aimed at the wrong target.
- Ord bridges philosophy and policy: he is the rare ethicist quoted in actual government AI summits, making him a credibility anchor for "this is serious, not sci-fi" framing.
- Post idea: contrast Ord's precautionary 1-in-10 doom estimate with Hanson's dismissal of FOOM and Khosla's optimism to map the full risk-temperament spectrum among elite thinkers.

## 65. Reid Hoffman

#source
Domain: Futurists and big-picture
Verified: June 2026

### Read them
- Homepage: https://www.reidhoffman.org/
- Blog: https://www.reidhoffman.org/writing (writing hub; also https://www.reidhoffman.org/books and https://www.reidhoffman.org/podcasts). Greylock profile: https://greylock.com/team/reid-hoffman/
- RSS: unverified (no clear public feed for reidhoffman.org as of June 2026)
- X: https://x.com/reidhoffman (@reidhoffman)
- Podcast / other: hosts "Possible" (with Aria Finger, since 2023) and "Masters of Scale"; YouTube https://www.youtube.com/@reidhoffman ; books "Superagency" (2025), "Impromptu" (2023, co-written with GPT-4), "The Startup of You", "Blitzscaling"
- Paywall: no (essays, podcasts, and YouTube free; books sold commercially)

### Who they are
Co-founder of LinkedIn, early PayPal team member, partner at Greylock, and co-founder of Inflection AI and Manas AI. As of 2026 he is among the most prominent optimistic-but-pragmatic investor-authors on the AI future, hosting the "Possible" podcast and writing books that argue AI broadly empowers ordinary people.

### Their future thesis (one line)
Widely accessible AI creates "superagency," a state where millions of individuals gain new superpowers at once and benefit from everyone else's too, so the realistic path is to steer AI inclusively toward expanded human agency rather than fear or ban it.

### Key future-relevant pieces
- "Superagency: What Could Possibly Go Right with Our AI Future" (Jan 2025 book, with Greg Beato), https://www.superagency.ai/
  His central argument is that breakthrough technologies historically expand individual agency at scale, and AI is the next such wave if deployed broadly and adaptively. He coins "superagency" for the compounding effect when everyone, not just elites, gets AI superpowers, and counters doom narratives by drawing on the printing press, the car, and the internet. He acknowledges risks like disinformation and job disruption but frames them as steerable, not destiny.

- "Impromptu: Amplifying Our Humanity Through AI" (2023 book, co-written with GPT-4), https://www.reidhoffman.org/books/
  Written in dialogue with GPT-4 itself, the book tours how large language models could augment education, work, creativity, and decision-making, using the model's own outputs as live demonstrations. The format is the argument: AI as a collaborative amplifier rather than a replacement. An early, optimistic field guide to generative AI's everyday uses.

- "Possible" podcast (ongoing, since 2023), https://www.possible.fm/
  His interview show explores optimistic but concrete futures with technologists, scientists, and builders, asking what a genuinely good AI-shaped world would look like and how to build it. Episodes function as a rolling map of upside scenarios across health, climate, governance, and work. A useful counterweight to risk-dominated futures media.

- Greylock / Manas AI and Inflection AI work (ongoing), https://greylock.com/team/reid-hoffman/
  Beyond writing, Hoffman backs and co-founds AI companies (Inflection AI's personal-assistant push, Manas AI in drug discovery), so his future thesis is also expressed as capital allocation. His bets cluster around AI as a personal amplifier and as an accelerant for science. Watching where he invests is a leading indicator of his "what could go right" stance in practice.

### Hooks for the brain
- "Superagency" is a marketer-friendly upside frame: AI as mass individual empowerment, not just enterprise efficiency, which reframes adoption as giving customers superpowers.
- Hoffman is the optimist anchor of the panel: useful tension against Ord's risk focus and Hanson's skepticism, and more grounded than pure accelerationism because he concedes real harms.
- The "Impromptu" gimmick (a book co-authored with the AI) is a concrete artifact of human-AI collaboration worth citing whenever the topic is augmentation versus replacement.
- Post idea: track Hoffman's company bets (Inflection, Manas) as a "follow the money" read on which optimistic futures a top investor actually backs with capital.

## 66. Vinod Khosla

#source
Domain: Futurists and big-picture
Verified: June 2026

### Read them
- Homepage: https://www.khoslaventures.com/
- Blog: https://www.khoslaventures.com/entrepreneurs/thought-provoking (his "Thought Provoking" essays index at Khosla Ventures)
- RSS: unverified (no clear public feed found for the Khosla Ventures essays as of June 2026)
- X: https://x.com/vkhosla (@vkhosla)
- Podcast / other: frequent long-form video and podcast interviews (e.g. with Rajan Anandan); not a podcast host himself
- Paywall: no (essays free on khoslaventures.com)

### Who they are
Billionaire venture capitalist, co-founder of Sun Microsystems, and founder of Khosla Ventures (an early OpenAI investor). As of 2026 he is one of the most aggressive techno-optimist forecasters, publishing long essays predicting that AI and robotics will automate most work and usher in an era of radical abundance.

### Their future thesis (one line)
AI is the steam engine of the mind: within 10 to 20 years it will do roughly 80% of the work in 80% of jobs, drive expertise toward free, and create deflationary abundance, with the main risks being geopolitical and distributional rather than rogue AI.

### Key future-relevant pieces
- "AI: Dystopia or Utopia?" (Sep 20, 2024), https://www.khoslaventures.com/posts/ai-dystopia-or-utopia
  His flagship 13,150-word essay (with a 3,150-word summary) frames AI as the largest and fastest change humanity has ever seen, comparing it to the steam engine amplifying muscle power. He predicts AI will handle "80% of 80% of all jobs," making doctors, tutors, and engineers near-free, with severe labor disruption concentrated in years 10 to 20 and broad abundance and GDP growth thereafter. He calls for reducing income disparity and exploring UBI funded by AI-driven productivity, and argues the real danger is hostile nation-states, not sentient machines.

- "Vinod Khosla's 2050: 14 predictions for an age of intelligent machines" (Jan 5, 2026), https://www.khoslaventures.com/entrepreneurs/thought-provoking
  Returning to forecasts he first made two years earlier, he lays out 14 technology-driven visions for 2050 spanning AI, robotics, energy, medicine, transportation, manufacturing, and climate. The throughline is that intelligent machines plus cheap energy collapse the cost of most goods and services toward zero. It is his most explicit long-horizon "blueprint for abundance." (Indexed under "Thought Provoking"; confirm exact post link on that page.)

- "Reasonable AI predictions" / public forecasts (2024-2026, ongoing), https://x.com/vkhosla/status/1847701748213035070
  Across talks and posts he repeats concrete claims: AI doing 80% of all economically valuable work within five years, a faster die-off of Fortune 500 incumbents in the 2030s than ever before, free education, and AI outperforming doctors on diagnosis and empathy. These are aggressive, near-term, and falsifiable, which makes him a useful high-confidence pole in any forecast comparison. Many are delivered via interviews rather than written essays.

### Hooks for the brain
- The "80% of 80% of jobs" line is the most aggressive mainstream-investor labor forecast on record, a perfect provocation for any future-of-work piece aimed at marketers and operators.
- Khosla is the abundance-optimist pole: expertise becoming free and a deflationary economy is the upside mirror image of doom, and he funds it (early OpenAI bet) rather than just predicting it.
- His framing that the real risk is geopolitical (hostile states weaponizing AI) sidesteps the alignment debate entirely, a distinctive angle versus Ord and Alexander.
- Post idea: stress-test his "within five years" job-automation claim against Alexander's ~2034 AGI median and the slow "diffusion gap," surfacing the capability-vs-adoption gap marketers actually face.

## 67. Sam Hammond

#source
Domain: Futurists and big-picture
Verified: June 2026

### Read them
- Homepage: https://www.secondbest.ca/about
- Blog: https://www.secondbest.ca/ (Second Best, on Substack; archive at https://www.secondbest.ca/archive)
- RSS: https://www.secondbest.ca/feed
- X: https://x.com/hamandcheese (@hamandcheese); Substack handle @secondbest
- Podcast / other: frequent guest on Mercatus "Macro Musings" and AI policy podcasts; affiliated with the Foundation for American Innovation (FAI)
- Paywall: partial (most essays free on Substack, some subscriber-only)

### Who they are
Economist and senior fellow / chief economist at the Foundation for American Innovation (formerly Director of Social Policy at the Niskanen Center), writing the Substack Second Best. As of 2026 he is a leading voice on how advanced AI reshapes the state, institutions, and political economy, bridging AI policy and conservative-adjacent governance circles.

### Their future thesis (one line)
AI is a regime-changing technology that could break the institutional foundations of the modern liberal-democratic state, so the central question is whether governments can modernize and adapt fast enough to avoid a drift toward "techno-feudalism" or centralized control.

### Key future-relevant pieces
- "Ninety-five theses on AI" (May 7, 2024), https://www.secondbest.ca/p/ninety-five-theses-on-ai
  A sweeping, numbered manifesto of his core claims about where AI is heading, deliberately echoing Luther's theses to signal an institutional reformation. It spans timelines, economics, governance, and the philosophical stakes, arguing that AI forces a rethink of nearly every institution we take for granted. The single best overview of his worldview in one place.

- "AI and Leviathan: Part I" (Aug 23, 2023), https://www.secondbest.ca/p/ai-and-leviathan-part-i
  Opens his signature trilogy on the institutional economics of an intelligence explosion, asking what AI does to the bargain between states and citizens. He argues that the competitive advantage democracies held in the industrial era may have been an artifact of that technology, and AI could shift the balance toward more centralized regimes. Foundational reading for the "AI versus the state" thesis. (Followed by Part II, "Preparing for regime change," Aug 28 2023, and Part III, "A timeline of our techno-feudalist future," Sep 11 2023.)

- "Why AGI is closer than you think" (Sep 22, 2023), https://www.secondbest.ca/p/why-agi-is-closer-than-you-think
  Makes the case for relatively short AGI timelines and argues policymakers and institutions are nowhere near ready for the disruption. He ties near-term capability gains to concrete governance and labor-market shocks. Useful as his explicit timelines statement, on the faster end of the spectrum.

### Who they are
Writer and venture investor behind Not Boring, a widely read Substack (around 264,000 subscribers in 2026) that blends technology strategy, optimism, and storytelling. As of 2026 he is a leading popularizer of the "technology will eat every industry" thesis and runs an associated early-stage fund.

### Their future thesis (one line)
Over the next 20 years startups will displace incumbents across energy, healthcare, defense, manufacturing, education, and more by building products so much better that the shift is inevitable, and the next leap in AI comes less from language models than from "world models" that understand physical reality.

### Key future-relevant pieces
- "Everything is Technology" (May 13, 2025), https://www.notboring.co/p/everything-is-technology
  His most succinct manifesto: a wave of billion-dollar exits and multi-billion-dollar raises is just the appetizer, and the next two decades will see trillions in new value as technology companies displace incumbents across energy, healthcare, defense, manufacturing, education, housing, finance, and aerospace. He uses numbers and history to argue the displacement is inevitable once products get decisively better, and that even fragmented sectors like law, accounting, and consulting become addressable markets. A core optimism-with-receipts piece.

- "World Models: Computing the Uncomputable" (Mar 2026, with Pim de Witte), https://www.notboring.co/p/world-models
  A roughly 19,000-word deep dive arguing that "world models," AI systems that internally simulate physical reality, are a more promising path to general intelligence than language models alone. It covers the philosophy, history, key papers, progress, competing approaches, and what controllable world models would mean for robotics and machines acting on our behalf. His clearest statement of where he thinks the real AI frontier is heading.

- "Us Against Spacetime" (2025), https://www.notboring.co/p/us-against-spacetime
  Frames the grand human project as a long fight to overcome the constraints of physics, energy, and distance, casting technological progress as humanity's tool against spacetime itself. It is his most philosophical statement of why building matters and where the species is headed. Useful as a high-altitude "why the future is worth building" frame.

- "Welcome to Not Boring World" (Jan 2026), https://www.notboring.co/p/welcome-to-not-boring-world
  Announces a new paid section where outside founders, researchers, and investors publish their best long-form ideas, positioning Not Boring as a platform rather than one writer's blog. It signals a bet that distributed expert writing about the future scales better than a single voice. Relevant as a media-of-the-future move and a directory of emerging thinkers.
