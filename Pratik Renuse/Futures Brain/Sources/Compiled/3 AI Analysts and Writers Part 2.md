- Podcast / other: No own podcast. Also a tech analyst at CambrianAI; has spoken at IAI (iai.tv).

### Who they are
Spain-based independent AI writer behind The Algorithmic Bridge (45,000+ subscribers), framed as "a blog about AI that's actually about people." Also works as an analyst at CambrianAI focused on large language models.

### Their future thesis (one line)
The interesting future of AI lives in the wide, under-discussed middle ground between "AI sucks" and "unfathomable superintelligence," and it should be read through a human lens, not a hype cycle.

### Key future-relevant pieces
- "The Shape of Artificial Intelligence" (2024), https://www.thealgorithmicbridge.com/p/the-shape-of-artificial-intelligence
  A conceptual essay mapping how AI capability might actually unfold rather than assuming a straight line to superintelligence. Romero argues the trajectory has a "shape" with plateaus, jagged edges, and uneven domains, which matters for anyone forecasting where the technology lands in a decade. Useful as a counter to both doom and utopia framings.
- "AGI: (M)ending the World" (Algorithmic Bridge), https://www.thealgorithmicbridge.com/p/mending-the-world
  Examines how AI optimists and doomers both present superintelligence in absolute terms, as either humanity's salvation or its end. Romero pushes back on that binary and questions whether AGI is even the right frame for what AI is becoming. A good entry point for his skeptical-but-engaged stance on long-horizon AI.
- "You Have No Idea How Screwed OpenAI Is" (2024/2025), https://www.thealgorithmicbridge.com/p/you-have-no-idea-how-screwed-openai
  A contrarian read on the competitive and financial fragility of the lab everyone assumes will dominate. Relevant for the futures question of which players actually control the next decade of AI, versus which are over-leveraged. Note: high-engagement opinion piece, read critically.
- Note on scenarios between extremes (2026), https://substack.com/@thealgorithmicbridge/note/c-252473413
  A short public note arguing that scenarios like "AI as normal technology," "what if AI stopped right now," or "what if it mastered code and nothing else" get almost no attention despite being plausible. Encapsulates his core futures angle: the neglected middle is where the real forecasting work is. Quick to cite, not a long essay.

### Hooks for the brain
- Strong "neglected middle" framing: a built-in antidote to binary AGI takes, good for a futures product that wants nuance over hype.
- Pairs well as a foil to accelerationist voices (Toews, Roose) and to the harder doom camp; he is the "human lens" pole.
- Post idea: "The shapes AI could take" using his plateau/jagged-edge model as a scenario-planning scaffold.
- Watch his OpenAI-skeptic thread for the "which labs survive the decade" question.

Domain: AI analysts and writers

### Who they are
Partner at FirstMark Capital investing in early-stage AI, ML, and data startups. Best known for the annual MAD (Machine Learning, AI and Data) Landscape market map and the MAD Podcast of long-form interviews with AI and data leaders.

### Their future thesis (one line)
AI in the mid-2020s is "bubbling and building" at once: real deployment of agentic, reasoning-driven systems is happening underneath the froth, and the constraints that will shape the next decade are data, energy, and economics as much as model capability.

### Key future-relevant pieces
- "Bubble and Build: The 2025 MAD (Machine Learning, AI and Data) Landscape" (October 2025), https://www.mattturck.com/mad2025
  The eleventh edition of his annual industry map, plus a long essay reading the state of the field. Argues 2025 is the year AI shifted from chatbots to systems that do work: agents with tools and memory wired into governed data and powered by reasoning models. He deliberately cut the map from 2,000+ logos to ~1,150 and flags energy, not just GPUs, as the pacing constraint. The single best annual snapshot of where the AI stack is consolidating.
- MAD Landscape archive and interactive map, https://mad.firstmark.com/
  The full searchable database of companies across the AI/ML/data stack, updated yearly. Valuable as a structural view of who occupies which layer, which matters for forecasting consolidation, commoditization, and which categories survive. Interactive, JavaScript-based.
- "Intelligence Isn't Enough: Why Energy and Compute Decide the AGI Race" (Eiso Kant), MAD Podcast (2025/2026), https://open.spotify.com/episode/3gPhaNzX6jigYOPQBasTF0
  A podcast conversation centering the argument that the AGI race is gated by energy and compute, not algorithms alone. Directly relevant to the 10-to-20-year question of physical and infrastructural limits on AI. Audio.
- MAD Podcast episode with Mostafa Dehghani (Google DeepMind) on "thinking in loops" and recursive self-improvement (2026), https://www.mattturck.com/podcast
  Explores the bottlenecks holding back true AGI, from evaluation limits and formal verification to the math of long-horizon reliability. Useful for understanding the technical ceilings researchers actually worry about. Audio; find on the podcast page.

### Hooks for the brain
- The MAD Landscape is a ready-made "map of the territory" asset: use its layer structure to frame which parts of the AI economy are commoditizing versus defensible.
- His "bubble and build" framing is a useful neutral lens between hype and doom, good for credibility.
- Energy-as-constraint thread (via his guests) is a strong hidden-player angle: data centers, grid, and compute supply as the real 10-year story.
- Post idea: "What got deleted from the AI map" using his logo-count cuts as a signal of which startup categories are dying.

### Who they are
Founder and managing partner of Conviction, a venture firm she launched in 2022 (after nearly a decade at Greylock) purpose-built to back AI-native "Software 3.0" companies. Co-host of No Priors, a widely followed podcast interviewing leading AI founders, researchers, and engineers.

### Their future thesis (one line)
AI is creating "Software 3.0," a wave of net-new intelligent applications, and durable value will concentrate in the "untrainable" work that lives in a company's private reality, while anything benchmarkable rapidly commoditizes.

### Key future-relevant pieces
- "The Untrainable" (2025), https://saranormous.substack.com/p/the-untrainable
  Her sharpest thesis essay: argues that anything that can be put on a benchmark is already commoditizing, so the valuable, defensible work is "illegible by construction." An application earns its place in the "untrainable corner" by arranging a company's private reality so a model can act on it, work won by teams that put domain-specialized engineers next to the customer. A strong frame for predicting where economic value in AI actually accrues over the next decade.
- No Priors with Jensen Huang on the "superintelligence century" (early 2026), https://www.youtube.com/@NoPriorsPodcast
  Guo and Elad Gil sit down with NVIDIA's CEO to take stock of AI at the start of 2026 and discuss what a "superintelligence century" could look like. High-signal for the long-horizon compute and infrastructure view from the company powering most frontier AI. Audio/video.
- No Priors on agents replacing tools (Notion, Simon Last) (2026), https://www.youtube.com/@NoPriorsPodcast
  A conversation on the shift in productivity software from "humans do the work" to "humans manage a swarm of agents." Concrete illustration of how the agentic transition reshapes everyday knowledge work. Audio/video.
- No Priors / Latent Space crossover at Microsoft Build on autonomous agents and software engineering (2026), https://www.youtube.com/@NoPriorsPodcast
  A crossover episode (with swyx) on how autonomous coding agents are reshaping the software engineer's role. Useful for the future-of-work and future-of-software-labor thread. Audio/video.

### Hooks for the brain
- "The Untrainable" is a portable mental model: legible/benchmarkable equals commoditizing, illegible/private equals defensible. Great spine for a "where does the value go" futures piece.
- Investor vantage point: she sees deal flow before it is public, so her bets hint at which categories she expects to matter in 5 to 10 years.
- No Priors guest list is a who's-who; mine specific episodes for first-party founder predictions (hidden players, timelines).
- Post idea: contrast Guo's "Software 3.0 / untrainable" optimism with skeptic voices on AI's economic ceiling.

### Who they are
Singapore-born developer, writer, and conference organizer who coined the term "AI Engineer." Runs Latent Space (newsletter and top-ranked AI engineering podcast), the AI Engineer conference series, and the AINews daily brief. Full name Shawn Wang; writes as swyx.

### Their future thesis (one line)
A new discipline, the "AI Engineer," is the practical center of gravity for the next era of software, and 2026 is the year coding agents "break containment" to automate everything else, so the hard problem becomes scaling output without scaling slop.

### Key future-relevant pieces
- "The Rise of the AI Engineer" (June 2023), https://www.latent.space/p/ai-engineer
  The foundational essay that named a new job category between ML researcher and traditional software engineer, arguing tasks that once took a research team and five years now take API docs and an afternoon. It predicted, accurately, that "AI Engineer" would become one of the fastest-growing titles in tech. Essential context for the future-of-software-labor question.
- "Scaling without Slop" (State of Latent Space 2026) (January 2026), https://www.latent.space/p/2026
  His 2026 outlook essay. Frames "slop" (low-effort AI-generated output flooding media, code, and the internet) as the central problem of the era and argues the answer is not cutting human output but "changing the slope of slop," scaling quantity and quality together through curation. Doubles as a state-of-the-field map of his expanding media network. Relevant to the 10-year question of information quality and trust.
- Agent Labs Thesis / AIE Europe debrief (2026), https://www.latent.space/p/unsupervised-learning-2026
  A crossover discussion laying out his "agent labs" thesis: coding agents generate software, software eats the world, therefore coding agents eat the world. Captures his view that 2025's coding agents become 2026's general-purpose automators. Audio plus notes.
- Latent Space AI Engineer Reading List / 2025 papers, https://www.latent.space/p/2025-papers
  A curated list of the papers he considers load-bearing for understanding frontier AI. Useful as a primary-source index for anyone trying to track where capability is actually heading. Updated periodically.

### Hooks for the brain
- "Coding agents eat the world" is a crisp, quotable thesis on AI automation, good as a provocation to test against.
- The "slop" framing is a strong futures angle on information environment decay (Merriam-Webster word of the year 2025), pairs with the dead-internet thread.
- He is a hub node: AI Engineer conferences and AINews put him close to practitioners, so he surfaces hidden builders before they are famous.
- Post idea: "The AI Engineer" as the canonical new job, then extrapolate which human roles get created versus erased by 2035.
