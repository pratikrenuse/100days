## 166. Hartmut Neven

#source
Domain: Quantum computing; Founder/lead, Google Quantum AI; Willow chip
Verified: June 2026

### Read them
- Homepage: https://research.google/people/hartmutneven/
- Blog / where published: Author page on the Google blog at https://blog.google/authors/hartmut-neven/ ; Google Quantum AI roadmap at https://quantumai.google/roadmap
- X / social: Posts primarily through Google Quantum AI channels rather than a prominent personal account
- Podcast / other: Interviews with Physics World and The Quantum Insider; named to TIME100 AI 2025
- Paywall: no (Google blog and roadmap are free)

### Who they are
Founder and Lead of Google Quantum AI and a Vice President of Engineering at Google, where he started the Quantum Artificial Intelligence Lab in 2012. He leads the team behind the Willow quantum processor and Google's roadmap toward a large-scale, error-corrected quantum computer. Named to TIME's 100 most influential people in AI in 2025.

### Their future thesis (one line)
A useful, error-corrected quantum computer is achievable this decade (Google targets roughly 2029-2030), and it will unlock real-world advantage in materials science, chemistry, medicine, and energy that no classical machine can match.

### Key future-relevant pieces
- "Meet Willow, our state-of-the-art quantum chip" (December 9, 2024), https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/technology/research/google-willow-quantum-chip/
  Announces the 105-qubit Willow chip and its central claim: errors can be reduced exponentially as more qubits are added, achieving below-threshold quantum error correction, a milestone the field pursued for almost 30 years. It also reports a random-circuit-sampling benchmark Willow ran in minutes that would take classical supercomputers an astronomically long time. This is the foundational result for Neven's argument that scalable, error-corrected machines are now a matter of engineering.

- "Our Quantum Echoes algorithm is a big step toward real-world applications for quantum computing" (October 2025), https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/technology/research/quantum-echoes-willow-verifiable-quantum-advantage/
  Reports the first verifiable quantum advantage, with the Quantum Echoes algorithm on Willow running a task roughly 13,000 times faster than the best known classical algorithm on the Frontier supercomputer. The "verifiable" framing matters because it points toward results other quantum computers can reproduce, a step toward practically useful output rather than pure benchmarks.

- "Google Quantum AI roadmap" (ongoing, five-stage framework detailed November 2025), https://quantumai.google/roadmap
  Lays out Google's staged path from current processors to a large-scale, error-corrected machine, with public milestones toward 100 to 1,000 logical qubits and an eventual target near 2029-2030. A November 2025 five-stage framework extends this from algorithm discovery to deploying quantum solutions in production workflows. This is the canonical document for tracking whether Google hits its own timeline.

- "Google Quantum AI Head Sees Commercial Quantum Within Five Years" (February 5, 2025), https://thequantuminsider.com/2025/02/05/google-quantum-ai-head-sees-commercial-quantum-within-five-years/
  Reports Neven's prediction that commercially useful quantum applications arrive within about five years, naming materials science, medicine, and energy as first domains. Useful as a dated, on-the-record timeline claim to hold against actual progress.

### Hooks for the brain
- Feeds the quantum-computing-arrives-this-decade thesis and any forecast touching drug discovery, materials, batteries, and cryptography. Use Neven as the optimistic-but-credible incumbent timeline anchor (2029-2030), to be triangulated against Preskill's more cautious framing and O'Brien's photonic bet. His roadmap is the single best artifact for checking whether the field is on or off schedule.

## 167. John Preskill

#source
Domain: Quantum computing
Verified: June 2026

### Read them
- Homepage: https://www.preskill.caltech.edu (personal site, including talks and publications)
- Blog / where published: Quantum Frontiers blog (quantumfrontiers.com/author/preskill/); papers on arXiv; talks posted as PDFs on his Caltech page
- X / social: @preskill on X, where he comments actively on quantum computing progress
- Best English source / podcast: Native English. His Q2B and APS keynote slide decks (preskill.caltech.edu/talks), the "Beyond NISQ: The Megaquop Machine" paper, and HPCwire's coverage are the best entry points.
- Paywall: no (arXiv, his site, and Quantum Frontiers are all free; the journal version is paywalled but the preprint is open)

### Who they are
The Richard P. Feynman Professor of Theoretical Physics at Caltech and director of its quantum information institute (IQIM). He coined the term "NISQ" for today's noisy intermediate-scale quantum machines and is one of the most cited voices framing where quantum computing is headed.

### Their future thesis (one line)
Quantum computing is moving beyond the noisy NISQ era toward error-corrected "megaquop" machines that run millions of reliable operations, with broad practical value still years out but approaching.

### Key future-relevant pieces
- "Beyond NISQ: The Megaquop Machine" (February 2025, preprint; published April 2025), https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.17368
  Preskill's essay defining the "megaquop" machine, one capable of roughly a million reliable quantum operations, as the next milestone past NISQ. He argues such a system, running on the order of 100 logical qubits at circuit depth near 10,000, could do useful things no classical or NISQ device can, while cautioning that truly transformative applications need far more.

- "Quantum Computing in the NISQ era and beyond" (2018), https://arxiv.org/abs/1801.00862
  The foundational paper that coined "NISQ" and set expectations for an era of imperfect, error-prone quantum machines. It remains the reference point for understanding why quantum error correction, not just more qubits, is the real barrier to practical quantum advantage.

- "Our Quantum Future" (APS Global Physics Summit, March 2025), https://preskill.caltech.edu/talks/MAR-EV40-Preskill-2025.pdf
  A keynote slide deck laying out his long-range view of where quantum information science is heading. It connects near-term error-correction progress to the longer arc of fault-tolerant computing and the scientific payoffs Preskill expects over the next decade-plus.

- "Move Aside NISQ: John Preskill Sets New Quantum Target, the Megaquop Machine" (December 2024), https://www.hpcwire.com/2024/12/16/move-aside-nisq-john-preskill-sets-new-quantum-target-the-megaquop-machine/
  HPCwire's accessible writeup of Preskill's Q2B keynote introducing the megaquop target. It is a useful plain-language entry point for the shift in how the field frames its next goalpost, away from raw qubit counts toward reliable logical operations.

### Hooks for the brain
- Feeds the realistic-timeline thesis for quantum computing: error correction is the gating step, not qubit count.
- Provides the vocabulary (NISQ, megaquop) the whole field uses to mark progress through 2035.
- Anchors the "quantum advantage arrives in stages" scenario against both hype and dismissal.

## 168. Jeremy O'Brien

#source
Domain: Quantum computing
Verified: June 2026

### Read them
- Homepage: https://www.psiquantum.com (and the Omega chipset page, psiquantum.com/omega)
- Blog / where published: PsiQuantum newsroom (psiquantum.com/news); peer-reviewed results in Nature; technical materials via the company site
- X / social: PsiQuantum posts via @PsiQuantum and LinkedIn; O'Brien speaks mainly through company announcements, conference talks, and interviews rather than a personal feed
- Best English source / podcast: Native English. Best entry points are the BusinessWire releases on Omega and funding, The Quantum Insider, and Quantum Zeitgeist coverage.
- Paywall: partial (Nature paper is paywalled; PsiQuantum releases and most trade coverage are free)

### Who they are
Co-founder of PsiQuantum and, as of February 2026, its Executive Chairman after stepping back from the CEO role (Victor Peng became interim CEO). PsiQuantum is building a fault-tolerant, photonic, million-qubit quantum computer using silicon photonic chips made in a commercial semiconductor fab.

### Their future thesis (one line)
Useful quantum computing requires going straight to error-corrected, million-qubit machines built on manufacturable silicon photonics, skipping the small NISQ devices most rivals chase.

### Key future-relevant pieces
- "PsiQuantum Announces Omega, a Manufacturable Chipset for Photonic Quantum Computing" (February 2025), https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20250226714082/en/PsiQuantum-Announces-Omega-a-Manufacturable-Chipset-for-Photonic-Quantum-Computing
  Announces Omega, a silicon photonic chipset built at GlobalFoundries and detailed in a Nature paper, with every component designed for a fault-tolerant, million-qubit system. It is the core evidence for the company's bet that quantum computing scales through standard chip manufacturing, not lab-bench hardware.

- "PsiQuantum Raises 1 Billion Dollars to Build Million-Qubit Scale, Fault-Tolerant Quantum Computers" (September 2025), https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20250910135739/en/PsiQuantum-Raises-$1-Billion-to-Build-Million-Qubit-Scale-Fault-Tolerant-Quantum-Computers
  Reports a Series E of over 1 billion dollars to fund utility-scale sites and large prototype systems. The scale of capital underlines O'Brien's thesis that the path to useful quantum computing is a heavy-industrial buildout, not incremental qubit demos.

- "PsiQuantum Breaks Ground in Australia on Site of World's First Utility-Scale Quantum Computer" (June 2026), https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20260617018403/en/PsiQuantum-Breaks-Ground-in-Australia-on-Site-of-Worlds-First-Utility-Scale-Quantum-Computer
  Marks construction starting in Brisbane on what the company calls the first utility-scale, fault-tolerant quantum computer, with O'Brien present as Executive Chairman alongside the new CEO. It turns the million-qubit ambition into physical infrastructure with a concrete site and timeline.

- "PsiQuantum Appoints Victor Peng as Interim CEO; Co-Founder Jeremy O'Brien to Become Executive Chairman" (February 2026), https://thequantuminsider.com/2026/02/10/psiquantum-appoints-victor-peng-as-interim-ceo-co-founder-jeremy-obrien-to-become-executive-chairman/
  Documents the leadership transition as PsiQuantum shifts from research toward utility-scale deployment, with O'Brien moving to Executive Chairman to guide strategy and partnerships. Useful for understanding the company's governance heading into its build-out phase.

### Hooks for the brain
- Feeds the "go straight to fault tolerance" thesis, a sharp contrast to Preskill's incremental megaquop framing.
- Anchors the photonics and manufacturability bet for quantum scaling through 2030 to 2040.
- Supports scenarios where quantum capacity becomes industrial infrastructure (data-center-scale sites) rather than lab curiosities.

## 169. Tom Oxley

#source
Domain: Neurotech and BCI
Verified: June 2026

### Read them
- Homepage: https://synchron.com (company) and https://tomoxl.com (personal bio)
- Blog / where published: Synchron newsroom (synchron.com/news); peer-reviewed clinical results (e.g. JAMA Neurology COMMAND study); company announcements
- X / social: @tomoxl on X and Thomas Oxley on LinkedIn; he speaks regularly at TED, Milken, and industry events
- Best English source / podcast: Native English. Best entry points are his TED talk, the NPR interview, and the Medical Design and Outsourcing interviews ("Opening the brain's secret back door").
- Paywall: no for the talks and Synchron releases; the JAMA clinical paper is paywalled, trade coverage is free

### Who they are
Co-founder and CEO of Synchron, the neurotech company building the Stentrode, an endovascular brain-computer interface implanted through a blood vessel rather than via open-brain surgery. An Australian-trained neurologist, he conceived the approach in 2007 and has implanted the device in early human trial patients in the US and Australia.

### Their future thesis (one line)
A brain-computer interface delivered through blood vessels, no open-skull surgery, is the safe and scalable path to restoring communication and, eventually, mainstream human-AI interaction.

### Key future-relevant pieces
- "A brain implant that turns your thoughts into text" (TED, 2022), https://www.ted.com/talks/tom_oxley_a_brain_implant_that_turns_your_thoughts_into_text
  Oxley's TED talk explaining the core insight that blood vessels are natural highways into the brain, letting Synchron place sensors near the motor cortex without cutting into brain tissue. He frames the endovascular route as the way to make BCIs safe enough for wide use, not just rare research cases.

- "Synchron Raises 200 Million Dollars Series D to Advance Brain-Computer Interface Technology" (November 2025), https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20251106150841/en/Synchron-Raises-$200-Million-Series-D-to-Advance-Brain-Computer-Interface-Technology
  Announces a 200 million dollar round to fund commercialization and a next-generation device, bringing total funding to 345 million dollars. It signals the program moving from feasibility trials toward a product, with edge-AI processing and an expanded cognitive AI effort.

- "Synchron Unveils Chiral, the World's First Cognitive AI Brain Foundation Model" (March 2025), https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20250319964709/en/Synchron-Unveils-Chiral-the-Worlds-First-Cognitive-AI-Brain-Foundation-Model
  Introduces Chiral, a foundation model trained directly on human neural activity to decode thought in real time. It marks Oxley's bet that BCIs plus AI become a new computing interface, where models learn from brain data rather than only from text and images.

- "Synchron to Advance Implantable Brain-Computer Interface Technology with NVIDIA Holoscan" (January 2025), https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20250113376337/en/Synchron-to-Advance-Implantable-Brain-Computer-Interface-Technology-with-NVIDIA-Holoscan
  Details a partnership to run on-device neural processing using NVIDIA's edge platform, cutting latency and keeping brain data local. It points toward a future where BCIs decode intent in real time at the edge, a prerequisite for everyday, responsive thought-driven control.

### Hooks for the brain
- Feeds the "brain-computer interfaces go from medical to mainstream" thesis over 10 to 20 years.
- Anchors the safer, less invasive path to BCI as a contrast to open-skull approaches like Neuralink.
- Supports the convergence-of-AI-and-neurotech scenario, where foundation models trained on brain data become a new interface layer.

## 170. Rafael Yuste

#source
Domain: Neurotech and BCI; Columbia neurobiologist; founder of the NeuroRights movement
Verified: June 2026

### Read them
- Homepage: https://ntc.columbia.edu/rafael-yuste/ (Columbia NeuroTechnology Center profile)
- Blog / where published: Peer-reviewed journals (Nature, Cell, Science); the Neurorights Foundation site at https://neurorightsfoundation.org/
- X / social: LinkedIn https://www.linkedin.com/in/rafaelyuste ; not highly active on X
- Podcast / other: Frequent interviews (UNESCO Courier, EFF "How to Fix the Internet," Columbia Magazine); author of the book "Neuroderechos / Neurorights" (2023)
- Paywall: partial (academic papers sometimes paywalled; advocacy writing and interviews are open)

### Who they are
Spanish-American neurobiologist, professor of biological sciences at Columbia University and director of its NeuroTechnology Center, who helped launch the U.S. BRAIN Initiative and now chairs the Neurorights Foundation. He is the central figure pushing for legal "neurorights" to protect the human mind from invasive neurotechnology.

### Their future thesis (one line)
As brain-reading and brain-writing technology matures, humanity must enshrine a new class of human rights protecting mental privacy, identity, agency, fair access, and freedom from bias before the technology becomes ubiquitous.

### Key future-relevant pieces
- "Four ethical priorities for neurotechnologies and AI" (Nov 8, 2017), https://www.nature.com/articles/551159a
  The founding manifesto of the neurorights movement, co-authored with the Morningside Group, arguing that AI and brain-computer interfaces must preserve privacy, identity, agency, and equality. It proposes adding new rights to protect against unauthorized brain-data access, manipulation of the sense of self, and algorithmic bias. This framework became the template for later national laws and the UNESCO standard.

- "Neuroderechos / Neurorights" (book, 2023), https://www.amazon.com/Neuroderechos-protecci%C3%B3n-humanos-Neurorights-Spanish/dp/6076391308
  Yuste's book-length argument for protecting "what makes us human" as neurotechnology advances, tracing how he persuaded Chile and other countries to write neurorights into law. It frames mental privacy and cognitive liberty as the next frontier of civil rights. The book is aimed at policymakers and a general audience, not just scientists.

- UNESCO Recommendation on the Ethics of Neurotechnology adopted (Nov 12, 2025), https://www.unesco.org/en/articles/ethics-neurotechnology-unesco-adopts-first-global-standard-cutting-edge-technology
  The first global standard on neurotechnology ethics, adopted by all 194 UNESCO member states, which operationalizes the neurorights principles Yuste has championed for nearly a decade. It sets a rights-based framework covering the full life cycle of neurotech products. This marks the movement's jump from national experiments (Chile) to an international norm.

- "Worldwide Campaign for Neurorights Notches Its First Win" (IEEE Spectrum), https://spectrum.ieee.org/neurotech-neurorights
  Profiles how Chile in 2021 became the first country to amend its constitution to protect neurorights after Yuste's lobbying, and how Brazil, Mexico, and Uruguay followed. It lays out the five neurorights and the strategy of treating brain data like a protected human right rather than ordinary consumer data. A useful primer on the policy mechanics of the movement.

### Hooks for the brain
- Feeds the "neurotech and BCI go mainstream in 10 to 20 years" thesis, supplying the governance and human-rights counterweight to the build-fast positions of Rapoport and others.
- Anchors a "mental privacy becomes a contested frontier" thesis: brain data as the next battleground after personal data and biometrics.
- Connects to broader AI-governance and bodily-autonomy themes, since his framework explicitly couples neurotech with AI manipulation risk.

## 171. Benjamin Rapoport

#source
Domain: Neurotech and BCI; Co-founder/CSO, Precision Neuroscience; Neuralink co-founder
Verified: June 2026

### Read them
- Homepage: https://precisionneuro.io/ (Precision Neuroscience); Mount Sinai profile https://profiles.mountsinai.org/benjamin-rapoport
- Blog / where published: Peer-reviewed neurosurgery and engineering journals; company press releases; interviews in trade outlets
- X / social: LinkedIn https://www.linkedin.com/in/benjamin-rapoport-7a007351/
- Podcast / other: Long-form interview in Neurotech Futures (Substack); speaker at Aspen Ideas; chairs the Clinical Practice Guidelines workgroup of the FDA implantable BCI Collaborative Community (iBCI-CC)
- Paywall: partial (some interviews paywalled; scientific papers vary)

### Who they are
Neurosurgeon and engineer (MD Harvard, PhD MIT) who was on the eight-member founding team of Neuralink, left in 2018, and co-founded Precision Neuroscience in 2021, where he is Chief Science Officer. He champions a minimally invasive, surface-electrode approach to brain-computer interfaces designed for clinical scale and safety.

### Their future thesis (one line)
The core science of BCI is effectively solved; the next decade is about turning it into safe, scalable, reversible medical devices that restore digital and physical function to paralyzed patients, implanted through low-risk surgery.

### Key future-relevant pieces
- "Building BCI For the Real World: A Chat With Dr. Benjamin Rapoport of Precision Neuroscience" (Jul 25, 2024), https://neurotechnology.substack.com/p/rapoport-precision-bci
  A detailed interview laying out his thesis that BCI must be safe, scalable, and minimally invasive rather than research-grade. He explains why Precision uses a thin surface array (Layer 7) that sits on the brain without penetrating it, enabling safe explantation and hardware upgrades over a patient's life. He frames the near-term goal as giving quadriplegic patients fluid, thought-based control of standard computers.

- Precision Neuroscience world record for electrodes placed on the human brain (May 28, 2024), https://www.globenewswire.com/en/news-release/2024/05/28/2889069/0/en/Precision-Neuroscience-Announces-World-Record-for-Number-of-Electrodes-Placed-On-Human-Brain.html
  Announces a record number of electrodes placed on a human brain at once, demonstrating the high-resolution recording his approach can achieve without penetrating tissue. It is a concrete milestone toward the data density needed to decode intention. Signals that surface arrays can compete with penetrating electrodes on signal quality.

- "Initial experience with the Precision Neuroscience Layer 7 micro-electrocorticography interface for real-time intraoperative neural decoding" (Neurosurgical Focus, 2026), https://thejns.org/focus/view/journals/neurosurg-focus/60/2/article-pE3.xml
  A peer-reviewed clinical report on using the Layer 7 micro-ECoG array for real-time neural decoding during surgery. It moves the company's claims from press release into the clinical literature. Marks the transition of surface-array BCI from prototype to documented clinical use.

- FDA implantable BCI Collaborative Community (iBCI-CC) clinical practice guidelines work, https://www.ibci-cc.org/
  Rapoport chairs the workgroup writing the first clinical practice guidelines for implantable BCI, covering the patient journey from candidacy through implantation, training, and eventual explantation. This is the regulatory and standards scaffolding that will shape how BCI scales clinically over the next 3 to 5 years. It signals that the field is professionalizing before mass deployment.

### Hooks for the brain
- Feeds the "BCI becomes a real medical category in 10 to 20 years" thesis, with the build-and-commercialize counterpart to Yuste's governance stance.
- Supports a "reversibility and upgradeability matter" thesis: implants designed to come out, not last forever, which reframes the human-implant relationship.
- Connects to the competitive landscape (Neuralink, Synchron, Precision) and to questions about who controls neural data once devices are widespread.

## 172. Palmer Luckey

#source
Domain: Defense tech; Founder, Anduril
Verified: June 2026

### Read them
- Homepage: https://www.anduril.com/ ; manifesto site https://www.rebuildthearsenal.com/
- Blog / where published: Anduril company blog and manifestos; long interviews (60 Minutes, CES panels, Tablet, Fortune)
- X / social: X https://x.com/PalmerLuckey (active)
- Podcast / other: Frequent conference talks and TV interviews; also runs ModRetro (retro gaming hardware)
- Paywall: no for manifestos and his own posts; partial for some outlet profiles

### Who they are
Founder of Oculus VR who sold it to Facebook, then in 2017 co-founded Anduril Industries, a defense technology company building autonomous drones, sensors, and software, valued around $60 billion in early 2026. He is the most visible advocate for replacing legacy defense contracting with mass-produced, software-defined autonomous weapons.

### Their future thesis (one line)
The United States must rebuild its "arsenal of democracy" by hyperscaling cheap, autonomous, software-defined weapons in commercial-style factories, because deterrence in a great-power conflict (especially with China) now depends on manufacturing volume, not exquisite one-off systems.

### Key future-relevant pieces
- "Rebuild the Arsenal" manifesto (Aug 2024), https://www.rebuildthearsenal.com/
  Anduril's ~30-page sequel to its earlier "Rebooting the Arsenal of Democracy," laying out a "manufacturing doctrine" for mass-producing autonomous defense systems. It argues the Ukraine war exposed a critical inability of the U.S. to surge production, and that the fix is hyperscale factories like Arsenal-1 making tens of thousands of systems a year. This is the clearest statement of Luckey's industrial vision for warfare.

- Arsenal-1 factory going live in Ohio (Mar 18, 2026), https://www.axios.com/2026/03/18/palmer-luckey-anduril-arsenal-drones
  Reports that Anduril's 5-million-square-foot Arsenal-1 plant is beginning production of drones and other autonomous systems. It is the physical proof-of-concept for the "rebuild the arsenal" thesis, applying consumer-electronics-style mass manufacturing to weapons. Marks the shift from manifesto to operating factory.

- "AI will make hardware so cheap you'll be able to buy a 'Ford F-150 for $1,000'" (Fortune, Feb 4, 2026), https://fortune.com/2026/02/04/who-is-palmer-luckey-anduril-founder-ai-hardware-cheap-manufacturing/
  Luckey argues that AI-driven design and automation will collapse the cost of complex hardware, a logic he applies to both consumer goods and weapons. It reveals his broader belief that software and AI will radically cheapen physical manufacturing. Useful for understanding why he thinks volume production of autonomous systems is about to become feasible.

- "Anduril's Palmer Luckey thinks the future of tech is in the past" (TechCrunch, Jan 8, 2026), https://techcrunch.com/2026/01/08/andurils-palmer-luckey-thinks-the-future-of-tech-is-in-the-past/
  A CES panel where Luckey argues vintage hardware aesthetics and intentional design are "objectively better," and bluntly frames U.S.-China relations as an irreversible "messy divorce." It captures both his cultural instincts and his hardening geopolitical worldview. The China framing directly motivates Anduril's industrial-base argument.

### Hooks for the brain
- Feeds the "autonomous weapons and software-defined warfare reshape geopolitics in 10 to 20 years" thesis.
- Anchors a "manufacturing volume becomes the decisive military variable" thesis, linking defense to the advanced-manufacturing themes covered by Brian Potter.
- Connects to U.S.-China decoupling and reshoring narratives, and to the rise of venture-backed defense primes displacing legacy contractors.

## 173. Paul Scharre

#source
Domain: Defense tech
Verified: June 2026

### Read them
- Homepage: https://www.paulscharre.com
- Blog / where published: CNAS profile and publications hub at https://www.cnas.org/people/paul-scharre ; also writes for Foreign Affairs, Foreign Policy, War on the Rocks, The New York Times, The Atlantic
- X / social: https://twitter.com/paul_scharre (@paul_scharre)
- Podcast / other: Frequent guest on national security and AI podcasts (for example 80,000 Hours); books published by W.W. Norton
- Paywall: partial (CNAS and his own site are free; some outlets like Foreign Affairs and NYT are paywalled)

### Who they are
Paul Scharre is Executive Vice President at the Center for a New American Security (CNAS), a former U.S. Army Ranger and Office of the Secretary of Defense official, and author of "Army of None" and "Four Battlegrounds." He is one of the most widely cited analysts on autonomous weapons and military AI.

### Their future thesis (one line)
AI will reshape the character of war over the next two decades, and the country that best integrates autonomy, data, talent, and compute into its military, while managing the new risks to stability, will hold the decisive advantage.

### Key future-relevant pieces
- "Losing the War of the Future: How New Technologies Threaten America's Military Advantage" (June 23, 2026), https://www.foreignaffairs.com/united-states/losing-war-future-paul-scharre
  Scharre argues that mass-produced drones and autonomous systems, proven at scale in Ukraine, are eroding the United States' traditional edge in expensive crewed platforms. He warns that the Pentagon's procurement and industrial base are too slow to field cheap autonomous weapons in the numbers a future conflict would demand. The piece is a call to restructure how America builds and buys military technology.

- "Nuclear Stability in the Age of AI" (June 24, 2026), https://www.cnas.org/publications/commentary/nuclear-stability-in-the-age-of-ai
  Scharre examines how integrating AI into nuclear command, early warning, and decision support could either strengthen or dangerously destabilize deterrence. He cautions that speed pressures and automation bias may compress decision timelines in a crisis. The analysis pushes for human control safeguards before AI is woven into the highest-stakes systems.

- "Four Battlegrounds: Power in the Age of Artificial Intelligence" (March 2023), https://wwnorton.com/books/9780393866865
  This book frames the global AI competition as fought across four arenas: data, compute, talent, and institutions. Scharre argues that national power in the coming decades will hinge on which states can convert AI advances into military and economic strength. It is his core long-form statement on where AI-driven geopolitical competition is heading.

- "Army of None: Autonomous Weapons and the Future of War" (April 2018), https://wwnorton.com/books/Army-of-None/
  Scharre lays out how increasingly autonomous weapons will change warfare and the hard questions about keeping meaningful human control over lethal force. Drawing on his own military and policy experience, he maps the technical and ethical fault lines. It remains a foundational reference for the autonomous weapons debate.

- "How the Pentagon Can Manage the Risks of AI Warfare" (April 13, 2026), https://www.cnas.org/publications/commentary/how-the-pentagon-can-manage-the-risks-of-ai-warfare
  Scharre offers concrete steps for the U.S. military to adopt AI quickly while containing the risks of accidents, escalation, and loss of control. He stresses testing, doctrine, and guardrails over a pure race for capability. The piece bridges his cautionary and pro-adoption arguments into practical guidance.

### Hooks for the brain
- Feeds theses on the future of warfare, autonomous and drone-saturated battlefields, AI arms competition between the US and China, and AI's effect on nuclear stability. Use Scharre as the measured, institution-grounded voice that pairs adoption urgency with risk management; a strong counterweight to pure accelerationist or pure ban arguments.
