## 141. Mira Murati

#source
Domain: Frontier AI
Verified: June 2026

### Read them
- Homepage: https://thinkingmachines.ai/
- Blog: https://thinkingmachines.ai/blog/ (research blog, called "Connectionism")
- X: https://x.com/miramurati (personal); company: https://x.com/thinkymachines
- Podcast / other: Bloomberg Tech 2026 appearance (June 4, 2026), video on YouTube; no regular podcast
- RSS: not advertised on the site (blog is a static Hugo index, no public feed link found, unverified)
- Paywall: no

### Who they are
Founder and CEO of Thinking Machines Lab, an AI research and product company she started in February 2025. Former Chief Technology Officer of OpenAI, where she led work across ChatGPT, DALL-E, and the GPT model line before departing in 2024.

### Their future thesis (one line)
The next leap is not more autonomy but better collaboration: AI that works alongside people in real time across voice, video, and text, with the science of how models are built shared openly so everyone can adapt them.

### Key future-relevant pieces
- "Interaction Models: A Scalable Approach to Human-AI Collaboration" (May 11, 2026), https://thinkingmachines.ai/blog/interaction-models/
  Thinking Machines argues the industry over-optimized for autonomous agents and left no room for humans to stay in the loop. Their answer is an "interaction model" that processes continuous streams of audio, video, and text in 200-millisecond micro-turns, so it can interrupt, backchannel, react to visual cues, and speak while listening. The bet is that interactivity should scale with intelligence as part of the model itself, not bolted on through external scaffolding.
- "Thinking Machines Lab" mission and founding statement (2026, undated on page), https://thinkingmachines.ai/
  Lays out three pillars: share science openly (frequent papers, code, model specs), build AI that adapts to the full spectrum of human expertise rather than only coding and math, and keep model intelligence plus infrastructure quality as the foundation. Frames safety as empirical and iterative, learned from real-world deployment rather than theory alone.
- "Defeating Nondeterminism in LLM Inference" (Sep 10, 2025), https://thinkingmachines.ai/blog/defeating-nondeterminism-in-llm-inference/
  An early technical post showing how to make large language model outputs reproducible bit-for-bit by using batch-invariant kernels. Signals the lab's "build it correctly for the long haul" stance on infrastructure, and this determinism work later underpinned their interaction model training.
- "Tinker" product and API (2025-2026), https://thinkingmachines.ai/tinker/
  Thinking Machines' first shipped product, a tool for fine-tuning and customizing open-weight models, reflecting the thesis that knowledge of how to train frontier systems should be put in more hands. Confirms the company is co-designing research and product rather than pursuing a single chatbot.

### Hooks for the brain
- Feeds the "human-AI collaboration / interface" thesis: real-time, multimodal interaction as the next product paradigm after turn-based chat. Useful counterweight to the "full autonomy" camp (Pachocki, Sutton on agents).
- Feeds the "open science vs closed labs" thesis: a frontier lab betting on publishing recipes and customizable models. Pair with Anthropic and OpenAI's more guarded postures to map the openness spectrum.

Concretizes Pachocki's stated safety approach for the coming era of long-running autonomous systems. (Title and date inferred from the MIT Technology Review piece; verify wording on the OpenAI page.)

## 143. Noam Shazeer

#source
Domain: Frontier AI
Verified: June 2026

### Read them

- Podcast / other: LinkedIn https://www.linkedin.com/in/noam-shazeer-3b27288/ ; no regular podcast of his own
- RSS: none found
- Paywall: no

### Who they are
Co-inventor of the Transformer ("Attention Is All You Need", 2017) and former co-lead of Google's Gemini. In June 2026 he left Google to join OpenAI as lead of architecture research, tasked with finding the next-generation model architecture beyond the current Transformer-based GPT line.

### Their future thesis (one line)
The path to far more capable AI runs through new model architectures, not just bigger versions of today's Transformers, so the next breakthrough is structural rather than purely scale-driven.

### Key future-relevant pieces
- "Attention Is All You Need" (June 2017), https://arxiv.org/abs/1706.03762
  The paper that introduced the Transformer, replacing recurrence and convolutions with pure attention. It became the foundation for nearly every modern large language model, so Shazeer's later thinking about "what comes after the Transformer" carries unusual weight. Co-authored with Vaswani and six others while at Google.
- "Outrageously Large Neural Networks: The Sparsely-Gated Mixture-of-Experts Layer" (January 2017), https://arxiv.org/abs/1701.06538
  Shazeer's lead-author work on Mixture-of-Experts (MoE), letting a model route each input to a small subset of specialist subnetworks. MoE is now central to scaling frontier models efficiently, a direct ancestor of the sparse architectures used in 2026 systems. Signals his long-standing bet that smarter routing, not just raw size, unlocks capability.
- OpenAI hire announcement and "next-generation architectures" mandate (June 18, 2026), https://www.cnbc.com/2026/06/18/google-gemini-co-lead-noam-shazeer-leaves-for-openai.html
  Reporting on his move to OpenAI confirms his brief is to explore architectures beyond the incremental GPT line. The signal for futures work is that one of the Transformer's own inventors thinks the dominant architecture has a ceiling worth designing past. Frames the late-2020s as a period of architectural search, not just compute accumulation.

### Hooks for the brain
- Feeds the "architecture is not finished" thesis: a counterweight to pure scaling-law narratives, useful whenever a futures claim assumes today's Transformer just keeps getting bigger.
- Use as the inside-the-lab voice on what structurally changes next; pair against Kaplan (scaling) and Sutton (experience/RL) to triangulate the three competing routes to more capable AI.

## 145. Jared Kaplan

#source
Domain: Frontier AI
Verified: June 2026

### Read them
- Homepage: https://physics-astronomy.jhu.edu/directory/jared-kaplan/ (Johns Hopkins faculty page); Anthropic team context at https://www.anthropic.com
- Blog: none personal; research published via Anthropic and arXiv (Google Scholar https://scholar.google.com/citations?user=KNr3vb4AAAAJ)
- X: no widely confirmed active personal handle found (mark unverified); commentary often surfaces via others quoting him
- Podcast / other: Y Combinator Startup Library talk; Strings 2024 physics-conference talk; periodic press interviews (Time, TechCrunch)
- RSS: none found
- Paywall: no

### Who they are
Theoretical physicist turned AI researcher, co-founder and chief science officer of Anthropic, and the company's responsible scaling officer. He is the lead author of the 2020 neural scaling laws work that gave the field a predictive framework for how model performance improves with size, data, and compute.

### Their future thesis (one line)
AI capability improves predictably with scale across both pretraining and reinforcement learning, so human-level AI is reachable this decade if you keep scaling compute while adding the missing ingredients of memory, organizational context, and long-horizon task ability.

### Key future-relevant pieces
- "Scaling Laws for Neural Language Models" (January 23, 2020), https://arxiv.org/abs/2001.08361
  The foundational paper showing language model loss falls as a smooth power law in model size, dataset size, and compute, across more than seven orders of magnitude. It turned model improvement from guesswork into something you can forecast and budget for, underpinning the entire scale-up era. The intellectual basis for believing bigger and more compute reliably means more capable.
- "Scaling and the Road to Human-Level AI" (Y Combinator talk, 2024 to 2025), https://www.ycombinator.com/library/Ml-scaling-and-the-road-to-human-level-ai-anthropic-co-founder-jared-kaplan
  Kaplan argues the missing pieces for human-level AI are organizational knowledge (models learning to operate inside companies), persistent memory for long-running tasks, and that scaling laws apply to the reinforcement learning phase, not just pretraining. He notes memory began shipping in Claude 4. A clear map of what still has to be solved beyond raw scale.
- "Human-level AI by 2030?" (Strings 2024 physics-conference talk), https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4a5lzYreMME
  A technical talk to physicists laying out the case for human-level AI within the decade, grounded in scaling trends rather than hype. Valuable because it is Kaplan reasoning quantitatively for a skeptical scientific audience. Useful as a sober, mechanism-based timeline anchor.
- Responsible Scaling Policy change interview (February 2026), https://www.cnn.com/2026/02/25/tech/anthropic-safety-policy-change
  Coverage in which Kaplan frames a 2026 revision to Anthropic's safety commitments as motivated by safety rather than competition, amid a dispute over AI red lines. Relevant to futures work because it shows how the leading safety-branded lab is adjusting its self-binding rules as capability and government pressure rise. A signal that voluntary safety commitments are mutable under real-world stakes.

### Hooks for the brain
- Feeds the "scaling works and the timeline is short" thesis: the canonical source for predictable capability gains. Use whenever a scenario depends on capability curves continuing to climb.
- Use his "missing ingredients" list (memory, organizational context, long-horizon RL) as a checklist for what has to be true before AI displaces knowledge work; pair against Sutton, who argues this whole paradigm is a dead end.
- The 2026 RSP change is a hook for governance scenarios: evidence on how durable industry self-regulation is when competition and policy collide.

## 146. Richard Sutton

#source
Domain: Frontier AI
Verified: June 2026

### Read them
- Homepage: http://www.incompleteideas.net/ (personal site, essays and the RL textbook)
- Blog: essays at incompleteideas.net (IncIdeas section)
- X: https://x.com/RichardSSutton (@RichardSSutton)
- Podcast / other: Dwarkesh Podcast (2025) https://www.dwarkesh.com/p/richard-sutton ; frequent lecture circuit
- RSS: none found
- Paywall: no

### Who they are
Pioneer of reinforcement learning, co-author of the standard RL textbook, and 2024 ACM Turing Award winner (with Andrew Barto) for the foundations of RL. A vocal critic of the large language model paradigm, he argues true intelligence must come from agents learning continually through their own experience.

### Their future thesis (one line)
General methods that scale with computation (search and learning from experience) always beat human-knowledge-engineered shortcuts, so the next era of AI will be built on agents that learn continually from interaction with the world, not from static internet data and next-token prediction.

### Key future-relevant pieces
- "The Bitter Lesson" (March 13, 2019), http://www.incompleteideas.net/IncIdeas/BitterLesson.html
  A short, hugely influential essay arguing that across 70 years of AI, methods that leverage massive computation (search and learning) consistently outperform approaches that hand-code human knowledge. The lesson is bitter because researchers keep trying to bake in human priors and keep getting outscaled. It is the intellectual license for the entire scale-everything era, even as Sutton now disputes how the industry applies it.
- "Welcome to the Era of Experience" (April 2025), https://storage.googleapis.com/deepmind-media/Era-of-Experience%20/The%20Era%20of%20Experience%20Paper.pdf
  Co-authored with David Silver, this paper argues progress from learning on human-generated data is plateauing and that the next generation of agents will gain superhuman ability by learning predominantly from their own experience. It points to systems like AlphaProof that generate their own training data through interaction. A preprint chapter for the MIT Press book "Designing an Intelligence", it is the manifesto for experience-driven AI.
- Dwarkesh Podcast: "Father of RL thinks LLMs are a dead end" (2025), https://www.dwarkesh.com/p/richard-sutton
  In this interview Sutton argues that LLMs are a sophisticated dead end for building genuinely intelligent systems because they imitate human text rather than learn from grounded experience and rewards. He calls for agents that learn on the job, continually, from interaction with an environment. The sharpest available statement of the experience-versus-imitation split.
- 2024 Turing Award citation and remarks (announced March 2025), https://x.com/RichardSSutton/status/1897356290474832042
  The ACM honored Sutton and Barto for the conceptual and algorithmic foundations of reinforcement learning. In his reaction he ties the award to reviving Turing's own idea of machines that learn from experience, an idea he calls essential but still nascent. Establishes his authority and frames experiential learning as unfinished business, not a solved approach.

### Hooks for the brain
- Feeds the "LLMs are not the road to AGI" counter-thesis: the most credible dissent against the scale-the-Transformer consensus. Use as the deliberate steelmanned opposition in any frontier-AI scenario.
- Use the experience-versus-imitation frame to interrogate timeline claims: if Sutton is right, current scaling curves mislead and the real breakthrough is continual on-the-job learning, which resets the clock.
- Pair directly against Kaplan (scaling) and Shazeer (architecture) as the three-way disagreement on what unlocks the next era; "The Bitter Lesson" is also a reusable lens for any tech-forecasting question about general methods beating handcrafted ones.

### Who they are
Co-founder, president, and chairman of OpenAI, and formerly its first CTO. In 2026 he took interim charge of OpenAI's product strategy (covering for Fidji Simo) and is the company's loudest public voice on compute as the binding constraint on AI progress.

### Their future thesis (one line)
The world is shifting to a "compute-powered economy" where intent converts directly into software, science, and companies, and the rate of progress (eventually even GDP growth) becomes bound by how much compute you can access.

### Key future-relevant pieces
- "The world is transitioning to a compute-powered economy" thread (April 2026), https://x.com/gdb/status/2043831031468568734
  Brockman's signature 2026 argument: AI has already transformed software engineering and is now collapsing the team size needed for complex work, with friction disappearing so small teams match large ones. He frames intent as convertible into "software, spreadsheets, presentations, workflows, science and companies." The clearest statement of his economy-reshaped-by-compute worldview.
- "OpenAI's Brockman says GDP growth may soon be tied to compute" (April 2026), https://techcrunch.com/snippet/3080160/openais-brockman-says-gdp-growth-may-soon-be-tied-to-compute/
  Coverage of his claim that national economic growth could become a function of compute access, with problem-solving capacity gated by available compute. This reframes AI infrastructure (chips, data centers, energy) as the central macroeconomic variable of the late 2020s. A concrete, testable macro thesis worth tracking against actual buildout.
- CES 2026 remarks: "Human intent is the most precious resource" (January 2026), https://x.com/AMD/status/2009015736044286001
  On stage with AMD's Lisa Su, Brockman argued human intent deserves "an ocean of low-latency, high-throughput agentic compute." It captures his view that as models get capable, the bottleneck becomes giving humans enough compute to act on their intentions. Useful for scenarios about agentic AI demand and the hardware that has to back it.
- "My path to OpenAI" (essay), https://blog.gregbrockman.com/my-path-to-openai
  A personal essay on how he came to co-found OpenAI, useful as background on his motivations and his all-in builder temperament. Less a forecast than a window into the founder mindset driving OpenAI's strategy. Context for reading his more recent compute-economy claims.

### Hooks for the brain
- Feeds the "compute is the bottleneck and the macro variable" thesis: the strongest articulation that infrastructure, not algorithms alone, sets the pace. Use in any scenario about data centers, energy demand, chip supply, or AI-driven GDP.
- Use as the OpenAI-leadership view on agentic AI and the "intent into output" collapse of team sizes; pair with Wang (AI does science) and Kaplan (scaling) for the bull case, and against Sutton for the skeptic case.
- His GDP-tied-to-compute claim is a falsifiable forecast: track real compute buildout and productivity data against it over the next few years.

Marks a shift from "can we build it" to "what happens after".

### Hooks for the brain

- Use his cognitive-tasks-regular-people-can-do definition as the operational test for any "we have AGI" claim; it filters hype from substance.
- The post-AGI economist hire is a hook for second-order scenarios (labor, distribution, governance after AGI); pair his alignment focus with Kaplan and Sutton on the capability side to separate "will it arrive" from "will it be safe".

## 149. John Schulman

#source
Domain: Frontier AI
Verified: June 2026

### Read them
- Homepage: http://joschu.net (bio confirms "cofounder and chief scientist at Thinking Machines")
- Blog: http://joschu.net/blog.html (largely dormant, last posts dated 2020; his current thinking lives in podcasts and papers, not the blog)
- X: https://x.com/johnschulman2 (handle @johnschulman2)
- Podcast / other: Cursor podcast, "John Schulman on dead ends, scaling RL, and building research institutions" (Dec 2025), https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=29BYxvvF1iM ; Dwarkesh Podcast (May 2024), https://www.dwarkesh.com/p/john-schulman ; publications at http://joschu.net/publications.html
- RSS: none found for the blog (static HTML index, no feed detected)
- Paywall: no (homepage, blog, arXiv papers, and YouTube/podcast appearances are all free; some secondary coverage of him sits behind The Information or NYT paywalls)

### Who they are
John Schulman is co-founder and Chief Scientist of Thinking Machines Lab, Mira Murati's AI startup, a role he has held since early 2025 and still holds in 2026. He co-founded OpenAI, co-invented PPO, led the reinforcement learning and post-training work behind ChatGPT, and is often called a primary architect of RLHF as applied to large language models. After a brief 2024 to 2025 stint at Anthropic, he joined Thinking Machines, where he has remained as Chief Scientist even as several co-founders departed in late 2025 and January 2026.

### Their future thesis (one line)
Capable AI will arrive through steady, incremental scaling of post-training and long-horizon reinforcement learning rather than one sudden jump, so the priority is to keep humans as the drivers and to slow down if any release looks unsafe.

### Key future-relevant pieces
- "John Schulman on dead ends, scaling RL, and building research institutions" (Dec 17, 2025), https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=29BYxvvF1iM
  In this Cursor podcast, his most recent long-form appearance, Schulman lays out where reinforcement learning goes next: the open problems of continual learning, brittle generalization, the absence of good value functions, and co-training generators with verifiers. He also reflects on how to build durable research institutions, drawing on the Bell Labs comparison and OpenAI's early culture, and gives his read on AGI forecasting and whether frontier labs would coordinate if the world needed them to. It is the clearest current window into how he thinks the next phase of capability will actually be earned.

- "John Schulman (OpenAI Cofounder), Reasoning, RLHF, and plan for 2027 AGI" (May 15, 2024), https://www.dwarkesh.com/p/john-schulman
  In this Dwarkesh Patel interview, Schulman walks through pre-training, post-training, and the path to models that can carry out whole multi-step projects rather than single suggestions. He argues progress on long-horizon tasks could go "quite far" but resists a single-unlock narrative, gives a roughly two-to-five year window for transformative capability, and stresses that if AGI arrived early, labs should be cautious and ready to slow training and deployment. His preferred future is continuous, safety-checked releases that keep people in control rather than a discontinuous leap.

- "Proximal Policy Optimization Algorithms" (Jul 20, 2017), https://arxiv.org/abs/1707.06347
