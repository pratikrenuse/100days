## 51. Ray Kurzweil

#source
Domain: Futurists and big-picture
Verified: June 2026

### Read them
- Homepage: https://www.thekurzweillibrary.com/ (the Kurzweil Library, full archive of his writings and commentary)
- Blog: https://www.kurzweilai.net/ (KurzweilAI news on accelerating intelligence) and essay archive at https://www.writingsbyraykurzweil.com/essays
- RSS: unverified (no canonical feed url confirmed on his current sites)
- X: https://twitter.com/raykurzweil (personal); network accounts @kurzweilsocial and @KurzweilAINews
- Podcast / other: Key books: "The Singularity Is Nearer" (2024) https://www.thesingularityisnearer.com/ ; "The Singularity Is Near" (2005); "How to Create a Mind" (2012). No personal podcast; appears widely as a guest.
- Paywall: no (own sites free; some interviews like The Economist are paywalled)

### Who they are
Inventor, author, and AI pioneer. Since 2012 a Principal Researcher and AI visionary at Google working on natural language and machine intelligence. Best known futurist for the "singularity" concept and exponential-technology forecasting.

### Their future thesis (one line)
Information technology improves exponentially, so by 2029 AI reaches human level, by 2045 we merge our biological brains with the cloud, and along the way medicine reaches longevity escape velocity.

### Key future-relevant pieces
- "The Singularity Is Nearer: When We Merge with AI" (June 25, 2024), https://www.thesingularityisnearer.com/
  A sequel to his 2005 book that updates his timeline using the last decade of AI, machine learning, and quantum progress. He restates 2029 for human-level AI and 2045 for the singularity, when nanobots in our capillaries connect our neocortex to cloud computing and multiply human intelligence. The book also argues AI-driven biotech will compress drug discovery and push lifespans toward escape velocity. An instant New York Times bestseller.
- "The Singularity Is Near: When Humans Transcend Biology" (2005), https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Singularity_Is_Near
  His landmark statement of the Law of Accelerating Returns: technological change is exponential, not linear, so the 21st century will see thousands of times more progress than the 20th. It forecasts the merger of human and machine intelligence and frames the singularity as a near-inevitable phase transition for civilization. The reference text for most modern singularity discourse.
- Longevity escape velocity argument, updated in The Economist (2024), https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Longevity_escape_velocity
  Kurzweil argues medical progress will soon add more than a year of life expectancy per year lived, so people who stay healthy long enough may keep outrunning death. He revised his timeline to roughly 2029 to 2035 and tied it to AI simulating biology to accelerate drug discovery. (The Economist piece itself is paywalled.)

### Hooks for the brain
- The original "exponential vs linear" mental model that nearly every AI-timeline argument now borrows from, named and dated.
- Longevity escape velocity is the underrated marketer angle: a date by which "anti-aging" stops being lifestyle and becomes a countdown.
- Kurzweil sits inside Google, so his forecasts double as a read on what a frontier lab quietly believes about merge timelines.
- Contrast pairing: Kurzweil (merge, optimism) vs Bostrom or Tegmark (control, caution) on the exact same 2045 horizon.

## 52. Balaji Srinivasan

#source
Domain: Futurists and big-picture
Verified: June 2026

### Read them

- Blog: https://balajis.com/ (newsletter archive) and book site https://thenetworkstate.com/
- RSS: https://balajis.com/feed (standard Substack feed path; treat as likely, lightly unverified)
- X: https://x.com/balajis (his primary channel, very high volume)
- Podcast / other: The Network State Podcast https://balajis.com/podcast ; founder of the Network School (Forest City, Malaysia). Book: "The Network State: How to Start a New Country" (2022), free online.
- Paywall: partial (newsletter and book are free; some posts and the school are gated)

### Who they are
Entrepreneur and investor, former CTO of Coinbase and former general partner at a16z. Author of "The Network State" and founder of the Network School, a residential community for builders. One of the most-followed tech-futurist voices on X.

### Their future thesis (one line)
The nation-state is being disrupted by the internet: cloud-first communities that share values, crowdfund territory, and gain diplomatic recognition (network states) will compete with legacy governments, while fiat systems risk debt crises and capital controls.

### Key future-relevant pieces
- "The Network State: How to Start a New Country" (2022), https://thenetworkstate.com/
  His core book argues you can build a new country the way you build a startup: start with an online community aligned on one moral premise, grow membership and a crypto-backed economy, crowdfund physical territory, and eventually win recognition from existing states. It reframes citizenship, governance, and even law as opt-in software-like systems. The full text is free to read online.
- The Network State Podcast (ongoing, episodes through 2026), https://balajis.com/podcast
  Long interviews with founders and policymakers about running money and people at scale and what it takes to start a new kind of state. A January 28, 2026 episode with a16z cofounder Ben Horowitz explores how internet-native institutions are starting to mirror and challenge traditional states. Functions as the running R&D log for the network-state idea.
- Sovereign-debt and capital-controls warnings (2026), https://x.com/balajis
  Across X and his newsletter in early 2026 he argued the US and UK are heading toward sovereign-debt stress and that a "Berlin Wall" of capital controls could trap wealth inside borders. He uses this to push exit, hard assets, and crypto as hedges against legacy finance. Primarily delivered as threads and posts, so best read directly on X.

### Hooks for the brain
- "Build a country like a startup" is the single most quotable governance meme of the decade: the marketer angle is opt-in communities and membership as the new nation.
- Hidden player: the Network School in Malaysia is a live, physical test of the thesis, not just an essay, worth tracking as a case study.
- His debt/capital-controls framing is a fear-driven macro narrative that pairs naturally with the optimism camp for a tension piece.
- He is a prolific X-native thinker, so the canonical source is threads, not polished essays: a reminder that some futurists live in the feed.

## 53. Marc Andreessen

#source
Domain: Futurists and big-picture
Verified: June 2026

### Read them
- Homepage: https://a16z.com/ (Andreessen Horowitz, where his major essays are published)
- Blog: https://pmarca.substack.com/ (his Substack, "The Pmarca Blog Archives" and reposts)
- RSS: https://pmarca.substack.com/feed (standard Substack feed path; lightly unverified)
- X: https://x.com/pmarca (extremely active, his main channel)
- Podcast / other: The a16z Podcast https://a16z.com/podcast/ (frequent guest). Key essays: "The Techno-Optimist Manifesto," "Why AI Will Save the World," "It's Time to Build."
- Paywall: no (a16z essays and Substack are free)

### Who they are
Co-founder and general partner of venture firm Andreessen Horowitz (a16z), and co-creator of the Mosaic/Netscape browser. The leading financial and rhetorical champion of aggressive technology acceleration ("techno-optimism").

### Their future thesis (one line)
Technology, markets, and intelligence are the engines of human progress and abundance, so the moral imperative is to build and to deploy AI as fast and aggressively as possible, treating stagnation and excessive caution as the real dangers.

### Key future-relevant pieces
- "The Techno-Optimist Manifesto" (October 16, 2023), https://a16z.com/the-techno-optimist-manifesto/
  A roughly 5,200-word credo declaring technology and free markets the source of growth, vitality, and rising well-being, and casting "de-growth," safetyism, and what he calls the enemy ideas of sustainability and risk-management as obstacles to human flourishing. It frames intelligence and energy as the master resources and argues for unconstrained building. The closest thing the accelerationist movement has to a founding document.
- "Why AI Will Save the World" (June 2023), https://a16z.com/ai-will-save-the-world/
  Argues AI is a universal tool that augments human intelligence rather than an extinction risk, and systematically rebuts the main doom arguments as a "moral panic." His policy prescription is "faster, please": let both Big Tech and startups build AI as fast as they can, with open-source encouraged and heavy regulation resisted. A core text of the pro-acceleration position on AI safety.
- "It's Time to Build" (April 2020), https://a16z.com/its-time-to-build/
  Written during the early pandemic, it argues Western institutions failed because they had lost the will and capacity to build physical things: housing, hospitals, factories, schools. It is a call to treat building as a moral and civilizational project, not just a business one. This essay seeded the broader "build" and "abundance" movements that now shape US tech politics.

### Hooks for the brain
- The manifesto literally names its "enemies" (sustainability, risk management, precautionary principle), which makes it the cleanest articulation of the accelerationist worldview to quote against.
- "It's Time to Build" is the upstream source of the entire Abundance Agenda; tracing that lineage is a strong explainer post.
- Andreessen is both investor and ideologue, so his essays double as a portfolio thesis: follow the money to see which futures a16z is funding.
- Direct foil to Harari, Bostrom, and Tegmark: same AI facts, opposite conclusion about how fast to move.

## 54. Peter Thiel

#source
Domain: Futurists and big-picture
Verified: June 2026

### Read them
- Homepage: no personal site; an unofficial curated index of his work is https://www.startuparchive.org/p/the-essays-talks-and-interviews-of-peter-thiel
- Blog: none (he writes via guest essays, lectures, and interviews, not a regular blog)
- RSS: unverified (no personal feed exists)
- X: unverified (no consistently active personal account; he is largely off social media)
- Podcast / other: frequent long-form interview guest (New York Times, First Things podcast). Book: "Zero to One" (2014). Co-founder of Founders Fund and Palantir.
- Paywall: partial (First Things and National Review pieces vary; some lectures are private/unpublished)

### Who they are
Venture capitalist and contrarian thinker. Co-founder of PayPal, Palantir, and Founders Fund, and an early Facebook investor. Influential as a political donor and as a philosopher of stagnation, monopoly, and (recently) apocalyptic theology.

### Their future thesis (one line)
The West has been in technological and institutional stagnation since roughly the 1970s ("we wanted flying cars, instead we got 140 characters"), and the deepest risk now is a one-world regime of safety and control that he frames, in explicitly theological terms, as the Antichrist.

### Key future-relevant pieces
- "The End of the Future" (October 3, 2011), https://www.nationalreview.com/2011/10/end-future-peter-thiel/
  His foundational stagnation essay: outside of computing, real technological progress (energy, transport, biotech) has slowed dramatically since about 1970, and our self-image as a fast-moving society is mostly an illusion. He ties this to the collapse of middle-class expectations of generational improvement. This is the intellectual seed of the entire "Great Stagnation" debate and his later pro-growth politics.
- "Voyages to the End of the World" (November 2025, First Things, with Sam Wolfe), https://firstthings.com/voyages-to-the-end-of-the-world/
  A theological-literary essay asking whether science will summon or suppress the Antichrist, tracing a centuries-long debate through figures like Francis Bacon and Thomas Hobbes. It argues Bacon "enthroned the Antichrist" in his scientific utopia. It is the written companion to Thiel's private Antichrist lecture series.
- The Antichrist lectures (private series, 2025 to 2026; reported by AP, CNN, Fortune), https://www.cnn.com/2026/03/16/europe/peter-thiel-antichrist-lectures-rome-intl
  In closed talks in San Francisco, Paris, and (March 2026) Rome, Thiel argues the Antichrist is not an obvious villain but a comforting administrator who promises to halt risky innovation (especially AI) in the name of safety, while actually consolidating control. He cites figures like Greta Thunberg as examples of the "end of modernity" mood. The lectures are closed to media, so coverage is secondhand via journalists.

### Hooks for the brain
- "Flying cars vs 140 characters" is one of the most repeatable lines about stagnation; Thiel is its origin.
- His Antichrist framing reframes the entire AI-safety debate as a theological power struggle, a genuinely unusual angle few futurists touch.
- Hidden player: through Founders Fund and Palantir, Thiel is materially shaping defense, surveillance, and AI infrastructure, so his cultural essays and his capital point the same direction.
- The lectures being secret and only reported secondhand is itself a story: a billionaire shaping discourse through unpublished talks.

## 55. Tyler Cowen

#source
Domain: Futurists and big-picture
Verified: June 2026

### Read them
- Homepage: https://tylercowen.com/ and his GMU page https://publicchoice.gmu.edu/tylercowen
- Blog: https://marginalrevolution.com/ (co-authored daily with Alex Tabarrok since 2003)
- RSS: https://feeds.feedblitz.com/marginalrevolution (confirmed on-site; legacy feed http://feeds.feedburner.com/marginalrevolution/feed)
- X: https://twitter.com/tylercowen
- Podcast / other: Conversations with Tyler https://conversationswithtyler.com/ ; columnist at The Free Press (and formerly Bloomberg). Founder of Emergent Ventures. Many books incl. "The Great Stagnation," "Average Is Over."
- Paywall: no (blog and podcast free; some columns paywalled)

### Who they are
Holbert L. Harris Professor of Economics at George Mason University and chairman of its Mercatus Center. One of the most widely read economics bloggers, an "infovore," and a connector of underrated talent via Emergent Ventures.

### Their future thesis (one line)
After decades of "Great Stagnation," AI is the breakthrough general-purpose technology that ends it, raising the returns to talent and mobility, reorganizing labor and firms, and rewarding those who learn to use it (while not, he argues, causing mass unemployment).

### Key future-relevant pieces
- "The Great Stagnation" (2011), https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Great_Stagnation
  Argues the US picked the "low-hanging fruit" (free land, smart uneducated kids to educate, powerful one-time technologies) and hit a productivity plateau from roughly the 1970s. It explains slow median-income growth and political dysfunction as symptoms of a technological slowdown. This is the framework he now uses to judge whether AI finally breaks the plateau.
- "The Marginal Revolution: Rise and Decline, and the Pending AI Revolution" (2026, generative book), https://tylercowen.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/TheMarginalRevolution-Tyler_Cowen.pdf
  A recent book-length argument designed to be read with an AI assistant, on long-run cycles of rise and decline and where AI fits. It treats AI as a turning point comparable to past general-purpose technologies. Notable as much for its format (AI-augmented reading) as its content.
- "My ARC talk on AI and jobs" (June 28, 2026), https://marginalrevolution.com/marginalrevolution/2026/06/my-arc-talk-on-ai-and-jobs.html
  A short post linking his 12-minute talk making the case that AI will not put everyone out of work, framed as "one stop shopping" for the anti-mass-unemployment argument. He stresses complementarity between humans and AI and rising returns to resource mobility. A compact, current statement of his labor-market optimism.

### Hooks for the brain
- "Low-hanging fruit" and "Great Stagnation" are durable mental models; Cowen is the economist who made them mainstream.
- His 2026 generative book is itself a future artifact: a book meant to be co-read with AI, a concrete example of how media changes.
- He is the highest-signal aggregator of others' futures thinking (daily links plus Conversations with Tyler), so his blog is a map to the whole field.
- Emergent Ventures is a hidden lever: he is quietly funding the people who will build the next decade, useful for spotting rising names early.

## 56. Nick Bostrom

#source
Domain: Futurists and big-picture
Verified: June 2026

### Read them
- Homepage: https://nickbostrom.com/ (full archive of papers, books, talks; on-page "Newsletter" signup for rare updates)
- Blog: none as such; the homepage "Recent additions" section serves as his update log
- RSS: unverified (no feed found; he advises checking the homepage directly)
- X: unverified (no personal handle or social links listed on his site)
- Podcast / other: Radio Bostrom https://radiobostrom.com/ (audio readings of his papers). Books: "Superintelligence" (2014), "Deep Utopia" (2024).
- Paywall: no (papers are free PDFs on his site; books are purchased)

### Who they are
Philosopher, founder and principal researcher of the nonprofit Macrostrategy Research Initiative. Previously professor and founding director of Oxford's Future of Humanity Institute (2005 to 2024, now closed). The thinker who put AI existential risk and the simulation argument on the academic map.

### Their future thesis (one line)
Machine superintelligence is the pivotal event of this century: getting the control and alignment problem right is the central challenge, but if we succeed, the harder question becomes how humans find meaning in a "solved world" of radical abundance (deep utopia).

### Key future-relevant pieces
- "Superintelligence: Paths, Dangers, Strategies" (2014), https://www.amazon.com/gp/product/0198739834
  The book that mainstreamed AI existential risk: it analyzes how an AI could undergo an "intelligence explosion," why a superintelligence might be hard to control, and why a misaligned goal (the paperclip problem) could be catastrophic. It frames alignment and control as humanity's most important unsolved problem. Hugely influential on the entire AI-safety field and on figures like Musk and Tegmark.
- "Deep Utopia: Life and Meaning in a Solved World" (2024), https://nickbostrom.com/deep-utopia/
  The optimistic counterpart to Superintelligence: it asks what human life is for if AI solves work, scarcity, and disease, so that almost nothing is left for us to do out of necessity. He explores boredom, purpose, and value in a "post-instrumental" world. A rare serious philosophical treatment of success rather than catastrophe.
- "Optimal Timing for Superintelligence: Mundane Considerations for Existing People" (working paper, v1.0, 2026), https://nickbostrom.com/optimal.pdf
  Argues that even with a fairly high probability of doom, currently living people might have a higher life expectancy if AGI arrives sooner, because near-term AI could cure aging and disease for people alive now. It reframes the timing debate around the self-interest of the present generation, not just abstract risk. A provocative, very current intervention. (PDF working paper.)

### Hooks for the brain
- The paperclip maximizer and "intelligence explosion" are the canonical AI-risk metaphors; Bostrom is their source.
- "Deep Utopia" flips the usual doom frame: the underexplored question of what we do with ourselves if everything goes right.
- His 2026 timing paper is genuinely contrarian within his own camp: a risk theorist arguing faster AGI could be in living people's self-interest.
- Hidden player turned ghost: FHI is now closed, so a foundational risk institution dissolved even as its ideas went mainstream, a story in itself.

## 57. Yuval Noah Harari

#source
Domain: Futurists and big-picture
Verified: June 2026

### Read them
- Homepage: https://www.ynharari.com/
- Blog: https://www.ynharari.com/category/articles/ (articles archive); media at https://www.ynharari.com/media/
- RSS: unverified (no canonical feed confirmed on his site)
- X: https://x.com/harari_yuval
- Podcast / other: no own podcast; frequent guest (Ezra Klein Show, etc.). Newsletter via ynharari.com (email info@ynharari.com). Books: "Sapiens," "Homo Deus," "Nexus" (2024). Co-founder of Sapienship.
- Paywall: partial (his site free; some articles like FT op-eds are paywalled)

### Who they are
Historian and philosopher, lecturer in history at the Hebrew University of Jerusalem and a distinguished research fellow at Cambridge's Centre for the Study of Existential Risk. Author of the "Sapiens" trilogy and co-founder of the social-impact company Sapienship. One of the most-read public intellectuals on AI and society.

### Their future thesis (one line)
Humanity's power has always come from shared stories and information networks; AI is the first technology that can create stories and make decisions by itself, so it threatens to become an unaccountable new kind of agent that could hijack the networks democracy and human meaning depend on.

### Key future-relevant pieces
- "Nexus: A Brief History of Information Networks from the Stone Age to AI" (September 2024), https://www.ynharari.com/book/nexus/
  Traces how information networks (from myth and scripture to bureaucracies and modern media) built and controlled human societies, for good and ill. It argues AI is a fundamentally new participant in these networks, an agent that can generate ideas and act, not just a tool, and warns this could erode democracy and truth if left unaccountable. His central statement on the AI era and the through-line for most of his recent talks.
- "We must not grant AI agents legal personhood" (June 8, 2026, Financial Times), https://www.ynharari.com/category/articles/
  A recent op-ed arguing that giving AI systems legal personhood would be a historic mistake, letting non-human agents own property, hold rights, and accumulate power beyond human control. He frames legal personhood as the threshold that turns AI from tool into autonomous actor inside our institutions. A concrete, current policy intervention. (FT original is paywalled; listed in his articles archive.)
- "Homo Deus: A Brief History of Tomorrow" (2015), https://www.ynharari.com/book/homo-deus/
  His earlier forecast that, having largely beaten famine, plague, and war, humanity will next pursue immortality, bliss, and divinity through biotech and AI. It introduces "dataism" and warns of a future where algorithms know us better than we know ourselves, potentially splitting society into a useless majority and an upgraded elite. The conceptual bridge from his history work to his AI warnings.

### Hooks for the brain
- "Shared fictions" (money, nations, religions, companies) as the operating system of humanity is his signature idea, and a marketer-friendly lens on brand and belief.
- His "useless class" warning about AI-displaced humans is provocative and quotable, a darker counterweight to the abundance camp.
- The legal-personhood op-ed is a sharp, specific 2026 flashpoint: a single legal threshold that decides whether AI becomes a citizen-like actor.
- Direct intellectual opponent of Andreessen and the accelerationists, making him the go-to voice for the "slow down and govern" side.

## 58. Max Tegmark

#source
Domain: Futurists and big-picture
Verified: June 2026

### Read them
- Homepage: https://space.mit.edu/home/tegmark/ (MIT personal page) and faculty page https://physics.mit.edu/faculty/max-tegmark/
- Blog: none personal; he publishes through the Future of Life Institute https://futureoflife.org/
- RSS: FLI feed unverified (institute publishes a blog/newsletter; no confirmed personal feed)
- X: https://x.com/tegmark
- Podcast / other: Future of Life Institute Podcast https://futureoflife.org/podcast/ (he appears and the institute produces it). Book: "Life 3.0" (2017).
- Paywall: no (FLI content and his academic pages are free)

### Who they are
Professor of physics at MIT researching the "physics of intelligence" (using physics to understand biological and artificial intelligence). Co-founder and president of the Future of Life Institute, the nonprofit behind the 2023 AI-pause letter. A leading scientific voice for AI-safety governance.

### Their future thesis (one line)
Uncontrollable, superhuman, general-purpose AI poses a real risk of human disempowerment or extinction, so civilization should deliberately steer toward powerful but controllable AI tools and away from racing to build autonomous AGI and superintelligence.

### Key future-relevant pieces
- "Life 3.0: Being Human in the Age of Artificial Intelligence" (2017), https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Life_3.0
  A wide-ranging map of possible AI futures, from utopian to catastrophic, organized around how AI could reshape work, war, law, and the cosmos. It popularized thinking in terms of scenarios and the alignment/control problem for a general audience. The book that established Tegmark as a public AI-safety thinker and helped seed the Future of Life Institute's agenda.
- "Pause Giant AI Experiments: An Open Letter" (March 2023, Future of Life Institute), https://futureoflife.org/open-letter/pause-giant-ai-experiments/
  An FLI letter, organized under Tegmark's leadership, calling on all labs to pause training of AI systems more powerful than GPT-4 for at least six months to build safety standards, or for governments to impose a moratorium. It gathered 30,000+ signatures including Bengio, Russell, Musk, Wozniak, and Harari. The single highest-profile attempt to slow the AI race and a landmark in AI governance.
- Future of Life Institute mission and AI-risk work (ongoing), https://futureoflife.org/
  Through FLI, Tegmark pushes to "steer transformative technologies away from extreme, large-scale risks and toward benefiting life," focusing on AI policy, standards, and public awareness. In 2026 he featured in TIME's "The People Vs. AI" cover story on rising public resistance to unchecked AI. FLI is the institutional engine behind much of the cautionary-governance movement.

### Hooks for the brain
- "Life 1.0 / 2.0 / 3.0" (hardware-and-software evolution of life) is a clean framework for explaining what makes AI civilizationally different.
- The 2023 pause letter is the canonical "we tried to hit the brakes" moment; its failure to actually pause anything is a story about who really controls AI.
- Hidden player: FLI quietly shapes AI policy and funds safety work, so Tegmark's influence runs through an institution, not just his own byline.
- Note for accuracy: the related 2025 essay "Keep the Future Human" is authored by FLI co-founder Anthony Aguirre, not Tegmark, though it shares FLI's worldview.

## 59. Kevin Kelly

#source
Domain: Futurists and big-picture
Verified: June 2026

### Read them
- Homepage: https://kk.org/
- Blog: The Technium https://kk.org/thetechnium/ ; Substack https://kevinkelly.substack.com/
- RSS: https://feedpress.me/thetechnium (confirmed feed link on his site)
- X: https://twitter.com/kevin2kelly
- Podcast / other: Cool Tools podcast https://kk.org/cooltools/category/podcast-2/ ; Recomendo newsletter https://www.recomendo.com/ (123,000+ subscribers). Books: "The Inevitable" (2016), "What Technology Wants" (2010), "Excellent Advice for Living" (2023).
- Paywall: no (kk.org and The Technium free; some Warp News essays are member content)

### Who they are
Founding executive editor of Wired (now "Senior Maverick" with no fixed duties) and a long-time optimist futurist. Writes The Technium blog about where technology is heading. In 2026 he is working on a "Desirable 100-year Future" (Protopia) scenario project.

### Their future thesis (one line)
Technology is a quasi-living force (the "technium") with its own tendencies, certain developments are effectively inevitable, and the right posture is "protopian" optimism: not utopia but steady, compounding incremental improvement that, over the long term, the optimists win.

### Key future-relevant pieces
- "The Inevitable: Understanding the 12 Technological Forces That Will Shape Our Future" (2016), https://kk.org/books/the-inevitable
  Identifies twelve deep, ongoing verbs (becoming, cognifying, flowing, screening, accessing, sharing, filtering, remixing, etc.) that he argues will inevitably shape the next thirty years regardless of who is in charge. "Cognifying" (adding AI to everything) is the central one and reads as prescient about the current AI wave. A practical, calm framework for anticipating where tech is heading.
- "What Technology Wants" (2010), https://kk.org/books/what-technology-wants
  Argues that technology as a whole (the "technium") behaves like a seventh kingdom of life with its own evolutionary drives and wants, much as biology does. It reframes our relationship with tools as a co-evolution rather than mere human control. This is the philosophical foundation under all his later forecasting.
- The case for optimism / Protopia (TED talk and essays, ongoing), https://www.ted.com/talks/kevin_kelly_3_reasons_for_optimism_in_difficult_times
  In his TED talk and related essays he argues the next two decades will be a global boom and that progress comes not from utopian leaps but "protopia": each year a little better than the last. In 2026 he is developing a "Desirable 100-year Future" full of AI, genetic engineering, and constant monitoring that he would actually want to live in. His attempt to give optimism a concrete, buildable shape. (Some long-form versions on Warp News are member-only.)

### Hooks for the brain
- "Protopia" (incremental better, not utopia) is a rare, usable word for realistic optimism, great framing for any "future" content.
- "Cognifying" predicted the add-AI-to-everything pattern years early, a clean example of a futurist call that landed.
- The "technium" idea (technology as a living kingdom with wants) is a genuinely different mental model from both doomers and accelerationists.
- His 2026 "Desirable 100-year Future" project is a positive, specific counter-vision worth tracking as it ships (likely first as a talk).
