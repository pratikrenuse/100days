## Headline

For a year this brain has tracked the constraint sliding down the stack and asked the same question each time: who owns the scarce thing. In one week in September, roughly **$1.65 billion** went to three companies whose entire premise is **not owning it**.

[[Positron AI]] took $875M at a $5B valuation to build an inference chip on the commodity memory that goes in phones, because high-bandwidth memory is rationed. [[Celero Communications]] took $275M at $3B-plus, having validated the first 2nm coherent DSP, to make separate data centres a hundred miles apart behave like one machine, because no single site can get the power. [[Fab2]], first disclosed round, took $500M at $3.7B to mass-produce small chip factories, because everything upstream eventually needs fab capacity and fab capacity is a queue.

The tripwire written on August 7, when [[OLIX]] became the first funded company here built to route around a chokepoint rather than supply one, fired three times in seven days at three different layers. That is no longer an experiment. It is a category, and it is the first real challenge to this brain's strongest thesis.

Alongside it, the energy ceiling produced its largest number yet and its most revealing sentence: Microsoft is going from about 12 GW to more than 38 GW by 2032, and the reported trigger is that shortages made it **turn away business**.

## Theses moved (and how)

**[[The Bottleneck Slides Down the Stack]]** (the big one, and for the first time it is being pressured rather than confirmed).
- Added all three September raises as a single connected piece of evidence rather than three separate items, because the point is the pattern: [[Positron AI]] $875M at $5B (September 10), [[Fab2]] $500M at $3.7B (September 9), [[Celero Communications]] $275M at $3B-plus (September 8).
- Recorded the [[OLIX]] and [[Positron AI]] pairing explicitly: same **around** motion, opposite routes. OLIX went up-market into on-chip SRAM and photonics on August 3; Positron went down-market into commodity LPDDR5X five weeks later. Both funded, neither shipped, and they cannot both be the right answer.
- Noted that [[Positron AI]] is the memory wall inverted. [[SK Hynix]] was this brain's first published post. This is the same problem answered by refusing to buy the scarce part.
- **Tripwire result: the August 7 "design around the chokepoint" tripwire fired three times in one week.** Restated: the thesis's core claim, that the chokepoint owner wins, is only safe while all three escapes fail. First real reads are Positron's H2 2027 production, Celero's 2nm silicon entering a named customer design, and Fab2's throughput ceiling.
- **New tripwire: the escape becomes cheaper than the chokepoint.** The old breaker assumed capital chases the scarce thing. Capital is now also chasing the way past it. If an escape route reaches cost parity before the chokepoint's new capacity arrives, pricing power collapses without a single new fab being built.
- **Sharpened: the fifth motion is BEHIND.** Fab2 does not sit *under* the semiconductor industry the way [[AXT]] does. It sits *behind* it, in the machines that make the machines. That is the ASML-shaped position, and it is the one layer that cannot be relieved by any of the other four motions, because all of them eventually need a tool. Whether Fab2's throughput limit keeps it out of that position is now the single most important thing to watch in this thesis.
