## LinkedIn post

The most powerful computers of the next century will not be built on Earth.

That is not science fiction anymore. In December 2025, a company called Starcloud put an Nvidia chip on a satellite and trained an AI model in orbit. The first time in history a machine has ever learned in space.
.
.
For seventy years, every computer we built sat on the ground. Plugged into our power grids. Fed by our rivers and our fuel.

That is about to change.

Because the AI boom is running out of the one thing Earth struggles to give it. Power. Up in orbit, the sun never sets. Solar panels there produce up to eight times more energy than they do down here, almost without pause.
.
.
The only thing that ever held us back was the price of the rocket. And that number is falling fast. Google's own engineers say that once a launch costs around 200 dollars a kilo, building data centres in space becomes cheaper than building them here. They expect that by the mid 2030s.

SpaceX has already filed to launch up to one million AI satellites.
.
.
We spent our whole lives learning to send our thoughts up into the cloud.

Our children will look up and realise the cloud was in the sky all along.

## Proof behind each claim (all traceable to the curve note)
- **Movement 1-2 (bold claim + proof):** Starcloud, Nvidia-backed, trained the first AI model in orbit on an H100, December 10, 2025. Real, dated.
- **The break (power):** in the right orbit solar panels are up to ~8x more productive and run nearly continuously (curve note).
- **Future punch (attributed + dated):** Google's Project Suncatcher pegs the tipping point at ~$200/kg launch cost, projected by the mid-2030s; SpaceX filed for up to ~1 million AI satellites (Jan 2026).
- **Honest caveat kept OUT of the post on purpose but noted here:** cooling, not radiation, is the real wall in vacuum (radiators can outweigh the compute ~10x), and only one toy model has actually run in orbit so far. The post claims "first trained in orbit" and "engineers say it pencils out," never that it is done at scale, so it stays defensible. Deliberately did not repeat the "8x solar = free cooling" misconception.
- Close is a declarative, futuristic wow (the cloud / sky pun), not a question.
