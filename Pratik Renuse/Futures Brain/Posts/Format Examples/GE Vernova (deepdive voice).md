Honestly, this one broke my brain a little.

The most back-ordered product in the entire AI boom is not a chip. It is a gas turbine, and the company that makes them barely existed two years ago. Full deepdive below.

..

First up, everyone fights over GPUs. Almost nobody asks the dumb question underneath: what actually plugs the GPU in?

A data centre is just a very hungry building. It needs power, on demand, right now. Reactors take a decade. Solar needs the sun to be up. So the electron that shows up this decade comes from burning gas.

And the machine that burns it is a heavy-duty gas turbine. There it is. The real bottleneck, hiding in plain sight.

..

Then AI happened, and the orders went vertical.

How sold out? Booked through 2029, with orders stretching to 2031. Backlog and reservations heading past 110 GW, up from 83 GW at the end of 2025.

New buyers are paying 10 to 20% more than the people who ordered just before them. Some have paid up to triple the old price just to hold a slot.

..

The state of play, dated to June 2026:

- GE Vernova turbines sold out to 2029, orders to 2031
- Backlog tracking past 110 GW by year-end
- New-order pricing up 10 to 20% over recent backlog
- And this from a business spun out of General Electric only in 2024

..

There is a quiet irony here. The hottest asset in a story about artificial intelligence is a century-old machine that just spins and burns fuel. In other words, the future of software is gated by some very old hardware.

..

Here is the part I keep turning over.

You can raise a hundred billion dollars for AI in a weekend. You cannot build a turbine factory in a weekend. Or a reactor. Or the grid to carry the power.

Money is fast. Atoms are slow. And when everyone has the money and almost nobody has the atoms, whoever owns the atoms sets the speed limit for everyone above them.

..

This is what a real bottleneck is: power, not intelligence.

Over the next 10 to 20 years, a sleepy turbine maker may have more say over how fast AI grows than any lab in San Francisco. Nobody is putting that on a magazine cover.

They probably should. No? ..
