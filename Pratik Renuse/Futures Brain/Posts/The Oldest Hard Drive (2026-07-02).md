## LinkedIn post

The best hard drive ever invented is almost four billion years old.

It is DNA. A single gram of it, a pinch you could lose between your fingers, can hold around 215,000 terabytes of data. Nature has used it to store the entire blueprint of a human being inside a cell too small to see, since life began.
.
.
For a hundred years, we have done it the opposite way. We record our lives onto spinning metal and silicon. Hard drives that die in a decade. Servers that overheat, rot, and forget.

That is about to flip.

Because scientists can now write our data straight into synthetic DNA. No power. No heat. It sits in the dark and survives for thousands of years. Microsoft, Illumina and Twist are already building it. Jeff Bezos and the CIA's own venture fund just backed a company to make it real.
.
.
It is not ready yet. Today it is painfully slow and absurdly expensive, thousands of dollars for a single megabyte.

But every technology that begins that way ends the same way. Cheap. Everywhere. Ordinary.
.
.
We spent a century teaching machines to remember for us.

Soon we will keep the whole of human memory in the same ink that wrote us.
