# 01 Human Elements Bank

Spoken-voice texture for posts so they read like a person talking, not an analyst reporting. The failure mode is a clean, correct, LIFELESS post. These are the little human beats that make a reader feel a real person is leaning across the table.

**Rule of use:** sprinkle, don't flood. Aim for 3 to 5 human beats in a ~1,200-char post, spread out (one near the hook, one or two mid, one at the turn). ROTATE. Never lean on the same two ("you know what", "my god") post after post. Before shipping, scan the last 2 to 3 posts and pick beats you have NOT used recently. Vary the category too, don't use three "interjections" in a row.

---

## 1. Interjections (the gasp / the reaction)
Use at a genuine surprise, never decoratively.

Look. / Okay. / Wait. / Wow. / Honestly? / No really. / I mean it. / My god. / You know what? / Here's the thing. / And get this. / Get this. / Picture it. / Think about that. / Let that sink in. / Sit with that for a second. / Read that again. / I'm not exaggerating. / I know how that sounds. / Sounds like sci-fi. It isn't.

## 2. Direct address (pull the reader in)
You. / Your kids. / Your father. / Someone you love. / You, right now, reading this. / Be honest. / Ask yourself. / You already know this. / You've felt it. / Think about your own parents. / Where were you when...? / Imagine your kitchen. / Picture your own hands doing it.

## 3. Conversational connective tissue (the "talking" glue)
Here's the thing. / Here's the part nobody says. / Here's what gets me. / And here's the twist. / But here's the catch. / The surprising part? / The strange part? / Now the uncomfortable bit. / So what does that mean for you? / And that's where it gets weird. / Which brings me to the real point.

## 4. Emotional confession / stance (put a human behind the words)
I'll be honest. / This one keeps me up a little. / I didn't believe it either. / It scared me a bit. / I find this beautiful and terrifying at once. / Part of me hates writing this. / I've changed my mind on this twice. / A year ago I'd have laughed at this.

## 5. Rhetorical fragments & rhythm breaks (short punches)
Think about that. / Just sit with it. / Not someday. Now. / Not a toy. A tool. / They don't trickle in. They flood in. / Cheaper than a phone. Faster than a car. / Not the smart part. The human part. / Let that number breathe. / Slow down and reread that.

## 6. Sensory / everyday anchors (make it physical, not abstract)
The dishwasher. The laundry. 3am. / A spoon. A screwdriver. / Your grandmother's hand. / The smell of the factory floor. / A used car in the driveway. / The fridge humming in the corner. / The school run. / A hospital corridor at night. Ground every big number in one small, touchable thing.

## 7. The turn (the emotional pivot line)
So the real story isn't X. It's Y. / It was never about X. It was always about Y. / We were watching the wrong thing. / The headline is X. The truth is Y. / Everyone's asking X. Nobody's asking Y.

## 8. Closers that feel human (a question TO the reader, not a stat)
Would you let one into your home? / Be honest, would you trust it with your mother? / Tell me I'm wrong. / Where do you land on this? / Does this excite you or scare you? / Would you say yes? / What would you do? / And you, honestly? Ready or not?

---

## Anti-patterns (kill on sight)
- **The #1 AI tell: staccato parallelism.** Neat, balanced, rhythmic pairs and triplets are what machine writing sounds like now. Kill: "Not a toy. Not a gadget." / "They don't trickle in. They flood in." / "It isn't brains. It's touch." (one is fine, a whole post of them is a dead giveaway) / "So the future won't arrive the day X. It arrives the day Y." / "Get this:" and "Here's the thing:" as colon setups. If three sentences in a row share the same shape, break them.
- **Real people ramble a little.** A human telling a friend this over coffee does NOT speak in perfectly balanced antithesis. Let a sentence run long, then a short one. Use "basically", "probably", "kind of", "the funny part is", real contractions, first person ("what I didn't get until recently"). Slightly loose beats suspiciously smooth.
- Don't INJECT beats mechanically. A pasted-in "Sit with this for a second" reads as fake as a pasted-in stat. The voice has to carry it, not the label.
- The same interjection twice in one post ("My god... my god...").
- "Wild, right?" (banned, overused) and "Genuinely curious" (banned).
- Interjections with nothing behind them (a "Wow." on a boring fact reads fake).
- Repeating the exact opener/closer from a recent post. Rotate.
