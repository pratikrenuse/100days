## LinkedIn post

In our one human lifetime, there will be more robots on Earth than cars.

Not tucked away in a factory in China. In kitchens. In hospitals.

 
Standing in your parents' living room at 2am, so you don't have to drive four hours through the dark to get there.
.
.
Right now there are maybe 15K humanoid robots in the entire world. 

By the time a child born today grows up, expect three billion of them. 

More walking machines than every car on the planet combined, built inside a single lifetime.
.
.
In a single lifetimeeee!!!

Let that land for a moment. We are the generation that gets to watch the world fill up with a second kind of body.

For a hundred thousand years, one thing never changed. If something physical needed doing, a human being had to do it. Lift it. Carry it. Clean it. Hold it. Heal it.

 
Your hands were the most valuable tool on the planet, and they were the one tool that could never be mass produced.
.
.
That is the law about to break.

Because a humanoid is really just a smartphone with arms.
It rides the same falling curve your phone did. One cost about 35 grand to build last year. By 2030, less than a used car. And then, like every machine before it, cheaper and better every single year.
.
.
One thing still holds it back. Touch. A machine that has read the entire internet still cannot lift a spoon. But that wall is already cracking.
.
.
And when it falls, the world tips over fast.

Morgan Stanley expects one billion of these machines at work by 2050. A five trillion dollar workforce that never sleeps, never tires, never once asks for a day off.
.
.
And the strangest part is where they end up.

By 2060, most of the world's robots will not be on factory floors at all.

 
They will be home. In the kitchen. Standing in the corner of yours.
