Wild.

China just put a company called MP Materials on an export blacklist this month, and almost nobody stopped to ask why a magnet maker is worth blacklisting. Full deepdive below.

..

First up, rare-earth magnets are the quiet muscle inside the modern world. They turn electricity into motion. Robot joints, EV motors, wind turbines, drones, guided missiles, all of it.

Who actually makes them? One country. China refines around 90% of the world's rare earths and makes most of the finished magnets.

For thirty years that was simply how it was. You bought from the cheapest supplier, the cheapest supplier sat in China, and nobody gave it a second thought.

..

Then the leverage got used. On June 22, 2026, China's commerce ministry named MP Materials, owner of the only operating rare-earth mine in America, on its export-control list. By name.

Sit with that. You do not blacklist a company unless it has become a threat.

And MP only became a threat because of one number: it expects its first magnets out of a brand-new Texas plant in the second half of 2026.

..

The picture, dated:

- China has shipped no terbium or dysprosium, two of the heavy magnet metals, to Japan since November 2025
- MP runs Mountain Pass, the only working rare-earth mine in the US
- It already supplies the US Department of Defense and Apple
- Its Texas plant is the West's first real shot at mine-to-magnet at scale

..

There is a delicious irony here. The cheap Chinese supply everyone leaned on for decades is exactly what hollowed out any Western rival. In other words, the moat was built by the buyers now trapped behind it.

..

Here is the part I keep thinking about.

A magnet was the most boring, solved purchase on earth. You ordered it like screws. That era is over. When intelligence gets cheap, the leverage slides to the physical thing intelligence has to act through.

A humanoid robot is mostly software wrapped around a few dozen magnets that someone has to be willing to sell you.

..

This is what a supply chain actually is: a map of who can say no.

Over the next 10 to 20 years, the handful of companies that can make magnets without anyone's permission quietly become some of the most strategic on the planet. In the West, right now, there is basically one.

Worth watching, no? ..
