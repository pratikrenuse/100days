This one stopped me cold.

There is a factory in California whose entire product is other workers. It does not have a famous name yet. It is called BotQ. Full deepdive below.

..

First up, this week Figure put its newest humanoid robot to work on a BMW line in South Carolina. Everyone looked at the robot.

Why did I look past it? Because behind the robot was the thing that actually matters. To put robots on real lines at scale, Figure first had to build a plant whose only job is manufacturing humanoids.

A factory that builds the things that work in factories. Sit with that for a second.

..

Then you look at the numbers they are aiming at, and it gets stranger.

The plan? Ship 100,000 humanoids over four years. The ceiling? A line designed to scale toward a million robots a year.

And on the BMW floor, the robots have already helped build more than 30,000 vehicles at 99% accuracy on their tasks.

..

The state of play, dated:

- Figure plans to ship 100,000 humanoids over four years (2025 statement)
- BotQ is designed to scale toward as many as 1 million robots a year
- At BMW, robots have helped build 30,000+ vehicles at 99% task accuracy (early 2026)
- BMW reported roughly a 40% cut in per-unit cost on those operations (June 2026)

..

There is a deep irony here. For all of human history, making more workers took twenty years and a whole generation. BotQ is an attempt to make labour something you order, ramp, and price like any other product. In other words, a factory for the workforce itself.

..

Here is the part I keep thinking about.

The robot is the part we will all stare at. The factory that stamps them out is the part that quietly rewrites what physical work costs.

If it works even halfway, the price of labour starts to behave like the price of software. It falls, then it keeps falling.

..

This is what automation actually is: not one clever robot, but the machine that makes the robots.

Over the next 10 to 20 years, the most important factories may be the ones whose product walks out of the building and goes to work. Today this one has a forgettable name and almost no audience.

Both of those are about to change, no? ..
