# 00 The Master Template -- Future Predictions

Scrapped and rebuilt. The old approach reported the present ("here's a company quietly doing X") and it did not spread. This one predicts the FUTURE, and that is what goes viral: bold, vivid, personal predictions people cannot help but argue with.

**Every post is ONE bold prediction about the world 10 to 20 years out, grounded in a real trend from the brain, written short, told like a story, ending on a wow statement (never a question).**

---

## THE PROVEN FORMAT: build every post on [[Posts/03 The Winning Format]]
The 7-movement "Overturned Law" structure (bold claim -> proof -> old law -> the break + hard number -> the turn -> future punch -> declarative close) is what finally worked. Gold-standard examples: [[Posts/The End of Discovery by Luck (2026-07-02)]] and [[Posts/The Robot in Your Kitchen (2026-07-02)]]. The 7 Laws below are the principles; that file is the assembly instructions.

## THE 7 LAWS (read these before EVERY post, do not violate one)

These are non-negotiable. Most rewrites happen because one of these was broken. Check them first.

1. **Every sentence exists to make you read the next one.** The only job of line 1 is to pull you to line 2. If a sentence doesn't earn the next, cut it or rewrite it.
2. **Write like a human speaking to another human.** First person, contractions, slightly loose. NOT robotic, NOT balanced-parallelism ("Not a toy. Not a gadget." / "They don't trickle. They flood."). That neat rhythm is the AI tell, see [[Posts/01 Human Elements Bank]].
3. **Use the human elements, as many as land naturally.** Pull from [[Posts/01 Human Elements Bank]] and rotate them so we never repeat the same two. Woven in, never pasted on.
4. **It is STORYTELLING. Everything connects.** One flowing narrative where each beat sets up the next. Not a list of facts. Not disconnected paragraphs.
5. **The takeaway is FUTURISTIC. That is the theme, always.** 10 years, 20 years, 30 years out. Every post must LAND somewhere in the future. The whole thing exists to deliver one futuristic realization.
6. **100x WOW.** The reader should nearly fall off their chair and share it everywhere, because this insight is not available anywhere else, and never packaged this simply. If it's just "information", it failed.
7. **NEVER end on a question.** End on a blank, declarative statement: crystal clear, a real takeaway, a wow, and futuristic. No "would you...?", no "what do you think?". A line that lands like a closing door.

## The rule of the format

- **Future tense. Always 10 to 20 years out. Bold, not hedged.** "In 10 years, no one will type." Never "this is happening right now."
- **Personal and vivid.** Talk to the reader and their actual life: "You'll...", "Your kids will never...".
- **One prediction per post.**
- **PACKED WITH HARD DATA. Non-negotiable.** Every post stacks several specific, dated numbers (TWh, gigawatts, dollars, percentages, growth multiples, years) with a named source or two (IEA, Exponential View, Epoch AI, Grid Strategies). It must read like a scientist or analyst wrote it. Vague futurism with no numbers ("everything will change!") is the number-one failure mode, never ship it. The FRAME is the future; the CREDIBILITY is the data.
- **Short-ish. ~1,000 to 1,300 characters.** Tight, but never cut the data to hit a character count. Density beats brevity. Staircase layout: one thought per line, blank line between, white space.
- **Ends on a question that splits the room.**

---

## The 5 beats (short)

1. **THE PREDICTION (hook, 1 line).** The bold future claim. Personal. Slightly provocative. Must stop the scroll on its own, above the "see more" fold.
2. **THE PICTURE (2 to 3 lines).** Paint the future scene, or state the shift plainly and vividly.
3. **THE PROOF (the core, 3 to 5 lines) -- LOAD IT WITH DATA.** Stack multiple hard, dated numbers from the brain's Outlook sections and signal notes, each with a source where possible. This is the whole point and the LONGEST part of the post: it should feel like a rigorous analyst wrote it, not a motivational speaker. If this beat has fewer than four real numbers, it is not finished.
4. **THE REFRAME (1 line).** The deeper meaning. What actually changes. The quotable, screenshot-able line.
5. **THE PROVOCATION (1 line).** A question or challenge that makes people argue in the comments.

## Where the ideas come from (the brain's theses ARE future bets)

Turn each thesis into a vivid, personal prediction:
- **Energy is the Ceiling** -> "In 10 years, the most valuable company on earth won't make AI. It'll make electricity."
- **Atoms over Algorithms** -> "In 10 years, being smart won't matter, because intelligence will be free."
- **Biology becomes a search problem** -> "In 15 years, your whole life will fit inside a single drop of liquid."
- **The physical world gets automated** -> "In 10 years, a robot will cost less than a used car, and never call in sick."
- **Compute and resources become geopolitics** -> "In 20 years, countries will fight wars over sand and orbit, not oil."

Read the brain's Theses and their Outlook sections for the real 2035 to 2045 forecasts that make each prediction defensible.

---

## Hard voice rules

- **Plain words. No jargon.** A normal person gets it on the first read.
- **Confident futurist.** You are not guessing, you are seeing where it goes. A little provocative is good.
- **Human and warm. Contractions.** Not an essay, not a press release.
- **No em dashes. No emojis. 0 to 2 hashtags, or none.**
- **NEVER reuse across posts.** Before writing, read the last 2 to 3 posts. Fresh opener, fresh reactions, fresh reframe, fresh closing question, every time.
  - **Banned openers, forever:** "I went digging", "I went looking", "so this week I...", "I followed the heat", "I went down a rabbit hole", "this broke my brain." Do NOT open on yourself finding the thing. Open on the PREDICTION.
  - **Crutch words, never back-to-back:** "quietly", "boring", "Wild, right?", "So tell me", "Genuinely curious".

## The example (~1,100 characters, data-packed)

### Energy is the Ceiling -> a scientist-grade future call

> By 2035, the AI race won't be won by the smartest model. It'll be won by the biggest power plant.
>
> The numbers are already brutal.
>
> The world's data centres use about 460 terawatt-hours of electricity a year today, close to the entire consumption of Germany. The IEA expects that to nearly double to ~945 TWh by 2030, and reach up to 1,750 TWh by 2035, roughly what all of India uses right now.
>
> And AI's slice is exploding: from around 10% of that power today to as much as half by 2030.
>
> The US grid shows the strain. The extra power America needs by 2030 has jumped 7x since 2022, from 24 to 166 gigawatts. Data centres are 55% of it.
>
> But you can't print electricity. The gas turbines these plants run on are already sold out to 2029, and power alone eats ~66% of a data centre's running cost.
>
> So the most valuable company of 2035 may not be an AI lab in California. It'll be whoever can generate the most electricity.
>
> We spent 20 years fighting over who owns the data. The next war is over who owns the watts.
>
> Can we build power fast enough? Or does AI hit a hard wall by 2030?

Notice the DENSITY of hard, dated numbers in the middle (460 / 945 / 1,750 TWh, Germany, India, 10% to 50%, 7x, 24 to 166 GW, 55%, 2029, 66%), with a named source (IEA). THAT is what makes a future prediction read as credible instead of as a horoscope. If a post can't stack this much real data, it is not ready. Then it lands the future frame and a debate-starting question. (The failed early drafts, "in 10 years no one will type", had zero numbers. That is exactly the trap: never ship a data-free future post.)

- [ ] It is a bold FUTURE prediction (10 to 20 years), not a "this is happening now" report
- [ ] ~1,000 to 1,300 characters (data density beats brevity)
- [ ] First line stops the scroll and is personal or provocative
- [ ] STACKS at least 4 hard, dated numbers with a named source (scientist-grade credibility). No numbers = do not ship
- [ ] One quotable reframe line
- [ ] Ends on a question that starts a debate
- [ ] Fresh wording: nothing reused from the last few posts, no banned openers
