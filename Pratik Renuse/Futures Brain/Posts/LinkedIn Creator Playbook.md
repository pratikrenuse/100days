# LinkedIn Creator Playbook

What the top LinkedIn creators in tech, business, and finance actually do, pulled from studying their work and analyses of large samples of viral posts (June 2026). This is the evidence base behind [[Post Format (LinkedIn)]].

## Who we studied
- **Jayant Mundhra** (~93k followers): one data-rich deep dive a day on startups and finance. Crisp tone, scannable bullets, an explicit "why this matters" turn at the end.
- **Sahil Bloom**: short, sharp, simple sentences a 5th-grader could read. Heavy use of dot points to signal "this is easy." Teaches one concept per post using the Feynman technique (explain it simply). Spends 4 to 8 hours researching a single piece.
- **Ruben Hassid** (100M+ views): direct, anti-fluff, contrarian hooks paired with fact-dense payloads. His own data: posts that win are either 350+ words (deep dives) or 80 to 180 words (snacks). The 200 to 350 word "middle" underperforms. Deep dives average the highest engagement.
- General analyses of 1,000 to 34,000 viral posts (Mallah, Ciela, others).

## The five questions every strong post answers (Mallah, 1,247 posts)
The single most useful finding. Before writing, answer:
1. Who is the one person this is for?
2. What do they currently believe that is wrong or incomplete?
3. What is the key insight that will shift their thinking?
4. What concrete example or story proves that insight?
5. What emotion should they feel by the end?

Question 3 is the takeaway. Question 4 is what makes it earned instead of hollow. This is exactly what our earlier drafts missed: a takeaway that is a real shift, proven by the facts, not a metaphor.

## The hook (this is 90% of reach)
- The first 1 to 2 lines decide everything, because they are all that shows above the "see more" fold.
- Best performers: curiosity-gap (~6.8% engagement) and contrarian (~6.2%).
- Keep it under ~10 words. 5 to 10 word hooks beat longer ones by ~40% on "see more" clicks.
- Be specific, not generic. "The most back-ordered product in the AI boom is not a chip" beats "AI has supply problems."
- Hint, do not spoil. Create the gap, then make them open the post to close it.

## Structure that holds attention (dwell time is the algorithm)
1. **Hook**: the surprising, specific claim.
2. **Rehook**: one short line that re-engages and raises the stakes ("Here is the part almost nobody talks about").
3. **Body**: the concrete, dated facts told as a clear cause-and-effect story. Short paragraphs, lots of white space, a scannable bullet list of the punchy facts. Teach one mechanism. Simple words.
4. **The shift**: state the key insight plainly. This is the payoff. It must follow logically from the facts above.
5. **Close**: reinforce the insight, then a simple, easy-to-answer question (posts ending in an easy question perform meaningfully better and drive comments).

## Formatting and length
- Substantial wins for a deep dive: aim ~300 to 450 words, roughly 1,500 to 2,500 characters.
- 14+ short paragraphs. No walls of text. Generous white space.
- One clean bullet list of the hard facts. Creators often use emoji bullets; our brand uses plain dashes and no emojis.
- Scannable beats dense. A reader should be able to skim the bullets and still get value.

## What makes a reader feel smart (and share)
- Teach something concrete and non-obvious they did not know, then hand them the one-line insight to repeat.
- Myth-buster and contrarian framings ("everyone watches X, the real story is Y") invite the reader to feel ahead of the crowd, and drive comments.
- The brag is the shift line plus one stat. "AI's real bottleneck isn't chips, it's electricity, and turbines are sold out to 2029."

## How this maps to our brand
- We already had the right idea (hidden player, dated facts). What we were missing: a substantial length, and a strong earned takeaway that states the shift plainly and is proven by the facts.
- Our addition the creators do not have: a researched 10 and 20 year outlook that interlinks our own notes (other players and theses) into a logical, factual projection no single article offers. Label the speculative parts as bets. This is the edge of having a brain instead of a feed.
- Humanize: contractions, varied rhythm, one genuine first-person beat. It should read like a sharp friend, not a brief.
- Keep our hard rules: no em dashes, no emojis, every number dated and sourced in the Player or Thesis note, the hidden player as the hook, never reuse a player as a lead twice.
