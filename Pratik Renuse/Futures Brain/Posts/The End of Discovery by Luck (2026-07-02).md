## LinkedIn post

Every medicine you have ever taken was found by luck.

Penicillin, the drug that went on to save hundreds of millions of lives, was found because Alexander Fleming left a dish out, went on holiday, and came back to mold that had killed the bacteria by accident.

That is how we fought disease for the whole of human history. Stumble onto a mold, a plant, a stray molecule. Trial, error, and a lot of praying.
.
.
That era just ended.

An AI called AlphaFold has now mapped the shape of almost every protein known to science. 200 million of them. Humans, working since the 1960s, had cracked about 180,000.

The machine did a thousand times more in two years.
.
.
And it no longer just reads biology. It writes it. Last year, the first medicine designed by generative AI passed a real human trial.

We used to wait for cures to be discovered. Soon we will design them, the way we design software.
.
.
Demis Hassabis won a Nobel Prize for this. He believes AI could help end most disease within ten to twenty years.

Your grandchildren may never hear the words there is nothing more we can do.

## Proof behind each claim (all traceable)
- "Found by luck" + penicillin: Fleming's 1928 accidental discovery (contaminated culture dish after a holiday); penicillin credited with saving hundreds of millions of lives. Sources below.
- AlphaFold "200 million proteins vs ~180,000 in 60 years, ~1000x": [[Signals/Biology - The Long-Horizon Curve (2026-07-02)]] (AlphaFold DB 200M+ structures, ~1000x the ~180,000 experimentally solved since the 1960s).
- "First medicine designed by generative AI passed a real human trial": Insilico Medicine's rentosertib, first generative-AI drug to post Phase 2a clinical proof, Nature Medicine, June 2025 (curve note).
- Hassabis Nobel + "end most disease in 10 to 20 years": his stated view, flagged as his prediction, not fact (curve note). Honest counterweight in the note: ~90% clinical failure still persists and no AI-designed drug is FDA-approved yet, so the post claims "passed a trial," not "cured."
