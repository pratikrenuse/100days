Everyone thinks the AI race is about chips.

So this week I went digging into what's really holding it back.

Turns out, it's not a chip at all.

It's a gas turbine. And right now? You basically can't buy one.

Wild, right? I had to know why.

..

It comes down to one thing: power.

Every AI data center needs a massive, steady flow of it, the second you switch it on. And solar can't do that. It stops at night. Nuclear can't either. It takes ten years to build.

So for now, that power comes from burning gas. And the machine that burns gas into electricity is a giant turbine. Only a handful of companies on Earth can build one.

..

The biggest is GE Vernova. Barely anyone knows the name, and it spun out of General Electric just two years ago.

But get this:

- Its turbines are sold out until 2029. Some buyers pay triple just to hold a spot in line
- Its stock went from 115 dollars to nearly 1,100 in two years. Almost 10x, now worth 300 billion dollars

And the tech giants? They'll spend 725 billion dollars on AI this year. Not a dollar of it can buy them a turbine any faster.

..

So the AI race isn't really about the smartest model. It's about electricity.

And it only gets tighter. By 2030, the world's data centers will use as much power as all of Japan. Solar, wind, new nuclear, all of it helps. None of it is fast enough.

..

So here's my bet.

The company deciding how fast AI grows today? It's GE Vernova, and it makes gas turbines. Tomorrow, it'll be someone else, whoever cracks cheap nuclear, or even fusion.

Because the real race was never about the smartest AI. It was about the power to run it.
