# 02 Robin Sharma Voice (the target voice for posts)

This is the voice we write in. Built from Robin Sharma's actual blog posts, book excerpts and quote corpus (The Greatness Guide, The 5AM Club, The Monk Who Sold His Ferrari, Who Will Cry When You Die?, The Everyday Hero Manifesto). Read this before writing. The point is not to sound like a self-help guru about tech; it is to steal his RHYTHM and his intimacy so the writing sounds like a person who has thought deeply talking straight to one reader.

---

## The rhythm (this is the whole game)
- **The wave.** Stack 2 to 4 short, hard declaratives, then let ONE long, comma-and-"and"-spliced sentence flood in and build, then snap back to a tiny 2 to 4 word punch. Ratio roughly 3 short : 1 long. The long sentence exists to be relieved by the short one after it.
- **Fragments are sentences.** One word can be a whole line. "Touch." "But not yet." "Isolation." Used as a verdict.
- **Start sentences with And, But, So.** Constantly. This is core, not sloppy.
- **White space.** One thought per line, blank line between. Short paragraphs. It should look like a poem, not an essay.

## The structure of a piece
1. **Cold open: a blunt, contrarian claim** ("Most people think X. They're wrong.") or a tiny concrete scene.
2. **Zoom from the small thing to a universal law** within a few lines.
3. **Turn to the reader as "you"** and hand them the realization directly.
4. **Build a repetition ladder to a peak** (anaphora).
5. **Land on a short declarative hammer line. Never a question.**

## The named devices (use several every post)
- **Anaphora:** repeat the opening words of consecutive lines. "Somebody still has to... Somebody has to... Somebody has to..." / "Every single time you..." / "You've got to..."
- **Rule of three, climax-weighted:** three items, the third longest and heaviest. "hard at first, messy in the middle, gorgeous at the end."
- **Antithesis / reversal:** two halves that flip. "Victims have big TVs. Leaders own large libraries." "It doesn't arrive slowly. It arrives all at once."
- **Direct address + imperative:** talk to one person, give gentle commands. "Look at Japan." "Remember this decade."
- **The everyman roll-call:** name real people so everyone is included (the nurse, the father, the two-year-old).
- **Aphorism close:** the last line is screenshot-bait, flat and certain.

## The tone
Warm authority. A mentor across the table, not a lecturer. Intimate spoken tics are welcome ("Here's the part that stops you cold"). Confident, urgent, a little tender. He commands, but it reads as care.

## Signature words (use SPARINGLY, 1 to 2 a post, or it turns cheesy)
legendary, world-class, iconic, gorgeous, momentum, mastery, greatest life, rare-air, gloriously, epic, "the work", history-maker. For our futuristic tech posts, lean on the rhythm and intimacy far more than these words, which can tip into motivational-poster territory on the wrong topic.

## How he opens (steal these shapes)
- "Most people think [X]. They're wrong."
- "Look at [thing]."
- A flat mortality line: "Life goes by in a blink."
- A tiny confession: "I love skiing." / "I'm a big fan of Thomas Edison."

## How he closes (ALWAYS declarative, never a question)
- Punchy verdict aphorism: "Your phone is costing you your fortune."
- Reversal that reframes everything above it.
- A single certain line the reader wants to screenshot.

## How this fuses with our 7 Laws (see [[Posts/00 The Master Template]])
Sharma's voice IS Laws 1 to 4 (pull, human, human-elements, storytelling). We ADD our Laws 5 and 6: the story must land in the FUTURE (10/20/30 yrs) and carry one 100x-wow insight found nowhere else. So: Sharma cadence, futuristic payload, declarative wow close.
