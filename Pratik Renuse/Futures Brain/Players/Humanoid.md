# Humanoid

**Proves:** [[The Physical World Gets Automated]]
**Awareness:** Obscure (two-year-old London company, one week of trade coverage, no retail awareness; the generic company name actively hides it)

## One line
The London robot company whose investors are also its only real customer and its factory, and which won them by taking the legs off.

## The chokepoint it owns
Not the robot. The contract. Most humanoid startups have a demo and are hunting for a buyer and a manufacturer. Humanoid raised from its buyer and its manufacturer at the same time: Schaeffler both deploys the robots and supplies the joints that move them, and Bosch both invests and builds the machine. Actuators are an estimated 40% to 60% of a humanoid's bill of materials, so locking more than half of that supply into the same deal that commits the customer removes the two largest risks in the business at once. The structural cost is obvious: partners who are also customers and suppliers can redirect the roadmap.

## Key facts (dated)
- $152M Series A at $1.35B post-money announced July 21, 2026, led by Prime Movers Lab, with Schaeffler, Bosch, Fubon Financial Holding Venture Capital and Aglaé Ventures (the Arnault family vehicle). Total raised to date $270M. (Humanoid/BusinessWire, Forbes, Tech.eu, July 21, 2026)
- Incorporated as SKL Robotics Ltd. in 2024. Founder and CEO Artem Sokolov. Over 250 staff across London, Boston, Vancouver and San Diego. (TechTimes, July 24, 2026)
- Schaeffler agreement signed May 13, 2026: binding, phased, targeting a four-digit fleet of wheeled robots across Schaeffler's global plants by 2032. First phase December 2026 to June 2027 at Herzogenaurach (box handling in live production) and Schweinfurt (six-month continuous-operation validation). (Humanoid, RoboticsTomorrow, May 13, 2026)
- Schaeffler is also preferred actuator supplier, covering more than 50% of Humanoid's joint-actuator demand for wheeled platforms through 2031, described by both parties as a seven-digit number of actuators. (May 13, 2026)
- Bosch, via Robert Bosch Robotics GmbH, is contract manufacturer with capacity earmarked for up to 100,000 units over five years, after a completed proof of concept at a Bosch intralogistics site in Bühl, Germany. (Forbes, July 21, 2026)
- Product: HMND 01 Alpha, a wheeled dual-armed mobile manipulator. 220 cm tall, 7.2 km/h top speed, 15 kg payload, ~29 degrees of freedom, Nvidia Jetson Thor compute. Wheels chosen deliberately: bipedal balance consumes compute and complicates CE certification, which Humanoid targets for 2027. (July 2026)
- Control stack: KinetIQ, four layers at four timescales, from fleet-level task assignment down to 50 Hz whole-body control; the locomotion layer is trained in simulation (~15,000 hours) and transfers across wheeled and bipedal bodies. (July 2026, company description)
- Context: IDC projects wheeled humanoids growing at roughly 120% CAGR versus roughly 95% for bipeds. Global robotics companies raised $55.8B in 2026 through July, nearly double all of 2025. (IDC, Forbes, July 21, 2026)
- Unverified company claims: 34,000 pre-orders worth ~$2.4B and nine completed Fortune 500 proofs of concept. Independently confirmed proofs of concept: Bosch Bühl (box transfers across five footprints) and Siemens Erlangen (tote logistics). (July 2026)
- Peer context: NEURA Robotics closed up to $1.4B at ~$7B on June 10, 2026, making it Europe's most-funded humanoid maker; Humanoid's "first" claim rests on the "pure-play" qualifier.

## Why it matters for the future
The humanoid story has been told as a race between American demos and Chinese volume. Europe's entry is a third model: the robot as an industrial component, ordered by the same firms that supply its parts, certified before it is charismatic. And the form factor matters more than the funding. If wheels really do reach factories years before legs do, then the first million working robots will not look human at all, they will look like a torso on a base, and the "humanoid" branding will turn out to have been marketing for a mobile arm. The money that decides this is not venture money, it is the automotive parts industry deciding what it wants to buy.
