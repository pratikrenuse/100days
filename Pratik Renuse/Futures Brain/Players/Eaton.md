# Eaton

#player
**Proves:** [[Energy is the Ceiling]]
**Awareness:** Building at the company level (a large, listed, well-covered industrial), Obscure at the business-line level (almost nobody outside the sector knows Eaton holds the single best measurement of how long the AI buildout actually is). Treat like [[TSMC CoWoS]] and Broadcom: a hidden business inside a known name.

## One line
The company that sells the electrical gear between the grid and the chip says America's data-centre order book is now fifteen years long.

## The chokepoint it owns
Grid-to-chip electrical distribution: switchgear, busway, transformers, uninterruptible power, the unglamorous boxes between a substation and a server rack. Nothing gets energised without them, and because Eaton quotes and books this equipment before a building exists, its backlog is a forward measurement of the whole buildout. That makes Eaton less interesting as a supplier than as an **instrument**. It is the most reliable public thermometer for the central claim of this thesis.

## Key facts (dated)
- **307 GW.** On the Q2 2026 earnings call, **August 7, 2026**, CEO Paulo Ruiz put the total US data-centre project backlog at **307 gigawatts, roughly 15 years of backlog at 2025 build rates**, up from **12 years** at the previous update. (Eaton Q2 2026 earnings call, August 7, 2026)
- **Only about 20% converts near term.** The majority translates into deliveries in **2028 and beyond**. This is the honest caveat: order visibility is not near-term revenue, and a backlog is a queue, not a promise. (August 7, 2026)
- **Profit outlook lifted to $13.50.** (August 7, 2026)
- **Growth step change.** Between 2024 and 2026 management expects to add **$10 billion to the top line, about ten times the growth recorded in the prior decade**. (August 7, 2026)

## Why it matters for the future
Every other number in this thesis is a forecast: how much electricity data centres will draw by 2030, how many gigawatts of gas will be built, how fast reactors arrive. The 307 GW figure is different because it is not a forecast at all. It is a count of projects that people have already committed enough money to for someone to quote the switchgear. Fifteen years of backlog at current build rates means the constraint is not demand and not capital, it is the physical rate at which an industry can pour concrete, wind transformers and connect substations. The 10-year read is that the gap between what has been ordered and what can be delivered stays open through 2035, which keeps pricing power with equipment makers, keeps campuses building their own generation to skip the queue (see [[SB Energy]], [[Lancium]] and the Energy Vault deal of August 7, 2026), and makes an already-permitted site more valuable than a cheque. The 20-year read is that the backlog eventually clears in one of two ways: capacity catches up, or a large share of those projects quietly never gets built. Watching the conversion rate against the stated 20% is the cheapest way to tell which.

## Awareness watch
Building at the company level. The 307 GW number is the thing to track: it moved from 12 years to 15 years of backlog in one update, and if it moves again in Q3 2026 the constraint is still tightening. If the conversion rate falls below the stated 20%, that is the first evidence of announced-but-never-built capacity, which would be the strongest counter-signal this thesis has ever had. Use Eaton as an instrument and a hook, not as a stock call.
