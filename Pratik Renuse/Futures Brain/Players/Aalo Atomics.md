# Aalo Atomics

#player
**Proves:** [[Energy is the Ceiling]] (the fission answer built for data centers, arriving on an AI timeline)
**Awareness:** Obscure (nuclear insiders know it; almost nobody else does, even after the July 4 criticality)

## One line
The Austin startup that took a brand-new reactor from groundbreaking to a live chain reaction in under eight months, on purpose, for AI data centers.

## The chokepoint it owns
Speed of deployment. Like [[Oklo]] and [[Helion]], Aalo is a ceiling-lifter rather than a chokepoint owner, but its wedge is different: factory-style, small (10 MWe) sodium-cooled reactors bundled into 50 MWe "Aalo Pods" designed to sit next to a data center. If the scarce thing in energy is how fast you can add firm power, Aalo's bet is to make reactors a manufactured product, not a construction project.

## Key facts (dated)
- July 4, 2026, 00:20 MT: Aalo's Critical Test Reactor (Aalo-X) achieved first criticality at Idaho National Laboratory, the fourth advanced reactor to go critical under the DOE Reactor Pilot Program, meeting the executive-order goal of at least three by July 4. (Aalo / DOE / ANS, July 4 to 6, 2026)
- Groundbreaking to sustained chain reaction in under eight months, described as one of the fastest reactor builds in roughly 80 years. (World Nuclear News / POWER, July 2026)
- The design: low-enriched uranium, sodium-cooled, full-scale core demonstrating the nuclear components of 10 MWe reactors, deployed in 50 MWe Aalo Pods aimed at AI data centers. (Aalo, July 2026)
- Next step already moving: Project Ascension, a second reactor beside Aalo-X at INL, a commercial-scale system intended to produce electricity and power an on-site data center "in the coming months." (Aalo / Businesswire, July 6, 2026)
- Context: selected August 2025 as one of 11 projects in the DOE Reactor Pilot Program (EO 14301, May 2025). Peers that also reached criticality: Antares Mark-0 (zero-power, June 4, 2026, first new non-light-water criticality in 40+ years). (DOE / powermag)

## Why it matters for the future
American nuclear's old law was ten to twenty years from paperwork to power. Aalo just demonstrated eight months from dirt to chain reaction inside the new federal fast lane. If the Pod model works, data centers stop negotiating with grids and start ordering power in 50 MW increments the way they order racks. The 10 to 20 year read: firm power becomes a product with a lead time, not a permit, and the "energy is the ceiling" era ends the way most ceilings end, from below, one small reactor at a time.
