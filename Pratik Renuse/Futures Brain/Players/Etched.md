## One line
The chip company that deleted every kind of AI except one, and got valued at ten billion dollars for the deletion.

## The chokepoint it owns
Specialisation at the exact moment the workload stopped moving. A general-purpose AI chip has to keep silicon area free for model shapes that might arrive. Etched bet that the transformer won, permanently, and spent that spare area on the transformer instead. If the bet holds, the economics of running models stop being set by the most flexible chip and start being set by the most committed one. If a new architecture displaces the transformer, the whole company is a paperweight. That is the cleanest version of the bottleneck argument there is: the layer beneath the famous one gets won by whoever is willing to be least flexible.

## Key facts (dated)
- $300M Series C at a $10.3B post-money valuation announced July 23, 2026, led by Sequoia, with a16z, Jane Street, SK Hynix and Diffusion participating. Reported as the highest valuation ever for a Sequoia-led Series C. (TechCrunch, Yahoo Finance, The Next Web, July 23, 2026)
- Valued at $5B in December 2025 on a $500M round. Roughly doubled in about seven months. (July 23, 2026)
- Product: Sohu, a transformer-only ASIC on TSMC's 4nm process. Company claims roughly 20x inference speedup versus Nvidia's H100. Company figures, no third-party benchmark published. (July 2026)
- Roughly $1B of prospective customer demand reported. 400 employees. Scaling production across an 80,000 sq ft California facility and a Taiwan factory. (July 23, 2026)
- Notable on the cap table: SK Hynix, the memory supplier, is now an equity holder in a chip that competes with the customer buying most of its memory. (July 23, 2026)

## Why it matters for the future
Every general-purpose computing era has ended the same way: the workload stabilises, and the money moves to a chip that does only that. It happened to graphics, to video encoding, to bitcoin mining. Etched is the bet that it is now happening to the transformer. The 10 to 20 year read is not "Etched beats Nvidia," it is that inference stops being a compute market and becomes an appliance market, priced per token rather than per hour, with the flexible chip pushed back up into research and training. Watch whether the labs that own their own models start ordering fixed-function hardware; that is the tell.
