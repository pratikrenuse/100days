# Lancium

#player
**Proves:** [[Energy is the Ceiling]]
**Awareness:** Obscure (a private Houston power developer; the campus it owns is famous, the company is not)

## One line
Nvidia just paid two billion dollars for a fifth of a company that does not make chips, does not make software, and owns a thousand acres of Texas with a power connection.

## The chokepoint it owns
Land with an interconnection position. Everyone can buy chips eventually. Nobody can conjure a site that already has grid capacity, water, permits and a community that will tolerate the noise. Lancium owns the 1,000-acre Lancium Clean Campus in Abilene, Texas, which is Stargate's first operational site. As the queue for grid connection lengthens (see the 307 GW backlog under [[Eaton]]), an already-connected campus stops being real estate and becomes an option on the next five years of AI capacity.

## Key facts (dated)
- **Nvidia is investing up to $3 billion.** Reported **August 10, 2026** by The Information: an initial **$2B for roughly a 20% stake**, with a further **$1B contingent** on conditions including grid hookups. (Reuters / Yahoo Finance / Techstrong, August 10, 2026)
- **Implied valuation.** Lancium's land and power connections carry an enterprise value of around **$10 billion**. (August 10, 2026)
- **What it owns.** The 1,000-acre Lancium Clean Campus in Abilene, Texas, the **first operational site of Stargate**, the SoftBank, OpenAI and Oracle data-centre programme committing up to $500B to AI infrastructure. (August 10, 2026)
- **Backing.** Blackstone-backed. An **IPO is being explored for 2027**. (August 10, 2026)
- **The pattern it completes.** In July 2026, Nvidia was reported in talks to guarantee roughly $250B of credit so OpenAI could lease [[SB Energy]]'s 10 GW Piketon campus, because OpenAI may lack investment-grade credit. That was the chip vendor lending its balance sheet to a power developer. Two weeks later the chip vendor bought equity in one instead. (July 27, 2026 and August 10, 2026)

## Why it matters for the future
The July version of this story was about credit: a chipmaker's rating standing behind a power project. The August version is about ownership, and ownership does not unwind. Nvidia is now a landlord. The 10-year read is that the AI supply chain vertically integrates downward into land and electricity, so the balance sheet that matters is not the model lab's or even the cloud's but the chip vendor's, and a single company underwrites the chips, the racks, the credit and increasingly the dirt. The 20-year read is the concentration risk that follows: if one vendor's rating is the collateral for the compute, the power and the campuses, then the sector's real single point of failure is not a fab or a turbine, it is a credit rating. Watch for rating-agency commentary on Nvidia's aggregate exposure, and watch whether AMD, Broadcom or a hyperscaler responds by buying its own power developer.

## Awareness watch
Obscure. Triggers to move it: the 2027 IPO filing, a rating agency naming Nvidia's power-asset exposure, or any mainstream coverage that leads with Lancium's name rather than Stargate's. The IPO is the retirement event; after it prices, this becomes context rather than a hook.
