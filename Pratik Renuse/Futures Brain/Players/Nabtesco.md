# Nabtesco

#player
**Proves:** [[The Physical World Gets Automated]]
**Awareness:** Obscure (a 1944-rooted Japanese precision components maker listed in Tokyo; industrial robotics engineers know it, humanoid coverage never mentions it)

## One line
Every humanoid robot needs a gearbox in its hip that can take the weight of the whole machine, and one Japanese company makes about sixty percent of them.

## The chokepoint it owns
Cycloidal reducers, known in the trade as RV reducers. A robot joint has a small fast motor and needs a slow strong output, so a gearbox does the conversion. In a humanoid, the leg and hip joints carry the entire body weight through every step, which needs a heavy-load reducer with almost no backlash and a long fatigue life. Nabtesco makes those. The smaller precision joints in arms, wrists and hands use a different device, a harmonic drive, where **Harmonic Drive Systems** holds roughly 85% of the world market. The two are **not fully substitutable**: precision at one end, load at the other. So the humanoid joint market is not one contestable market with a leader, it is two near-monopolies sitting in different parts of the same robot.

## Key facts (dated)
- **Roughly 60% of the global cycloidal (RV) reducer market**, used in leg and load-bearing joints. (404K SEMI-AI brief, August 10, 2026)
- **Operating profit rose 60% year over year** as humanoid and industrial robot orders grew. (August 10, 2026)
- The audited backing: FY2025 (ended December 2025) operating profit of **¥20.7B, up 60.3%**, on **¥307.9B of sales**. FY2026 guidance is **¥327B revenue**, up 6.2%, at an **8.5% operating margin**. (Nabtesco FY2025 results briefing, February 19, 2026)
- **Doubling RV-reducer capacity by 2026** for anticipated humanoid demand. (2026 disclosure)
- Target application: RV reducers for the **hip and leg joints** of humanoids, with the mass-production programmes at Tesla, Figure, 1X and Apptronik named as the opportunity. (2026)
- The twin: **Harmonic Drive Systems** holds roughly **85%** of the global harmonic-drive market, the zero-backlash precision reducers used in the smaller joints. (August 10, 2026)
- The neighbour: **Yaskawa Electric** posted operating profit **up 70% YoY** on automation and robotics demand, with acquisitions strengthening its portfolio. It is one of the few companies globally able to supply precision motion components at industrial scale. (August 10, 2026)
- The competitive pressure: Unitree cut its R1 humanoid price from nearly RMB 40,000 to under RMB 30,000, squeezing harmonic-drive and actuator suppliers. China's [[Leaderdrive]] is the only Chinese harmonic-drive maker with profitable mass production for humanoids, has passed Tesla supplier validation, and signed a joint venture with SKF on July 2, 2026 (SKF 60%) with operations from end-2026. (July 2026)

## Why it matters for the future
The humanoid story has been told entirely through robot brands: Unitree's listing, Figure's valuation, Optimus's delays. Underneath all of them sits a parts list, and the joints are the expensive, hard, slow-to-qualify part of it. Actuators run roughly 40% to 60% of a humanoid's bill of materials, and the reducer is the heart of the actuator. The 10-year read is that the cost curve which takes a humanoid from about $35,000 in 2025 to roughly $17,000 by 2030 runs through reducer volume, so the companies whose revenue compounds most reliably over the decade are the two joint makers rather than any single robot brand, in the same way that the smartphone decade paid Corning and TSMC more reliably than it paid most handset brands. The 20-year read is the risk in that position: an 85% or 60% share invites every customer to qualify a second source, which is exactly what Leaderdrive, Zhejiang Shuanghuan and SKF are being funded to become, and a design shift to direct drive would dilute both incumbents at once. High share is the reason to watch it and the reason not to assume it lasts.
