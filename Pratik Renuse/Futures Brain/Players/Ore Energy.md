# Ore Energy

#player
**Proves:** [[Energy is the Ceiling]]
**Awareness:** Obscure (three years old, Series A announced August 5, 2026, known inside European energy-storage circles and nowhere else)

## One line
A Delft spinout raised forty three million dollars to store electricity by rusting iron, and it holds it for a hundred hours instead of four.

## The chokepoint it owns
Duration. Every energy answer this brain has tracked so far is a generation answer: more turbines, more reactors, eventually fusion. Ore Energy is the first player here attacking the other half of the problem. A standard lithium grid battery discharges for about four hours, which is enough to shave an evening peak and useless for a data centre that must run every hour of every day. If a battery can hold 24 to 100 hours cheaply, solar and wind stop being intermittent in any way that matters to a buyer, and the gas turbine loses its only real advantage, which is that it runs when asked. The chokepoint is not the electron. It is the calendar.

## Key facts (dated)
- Series A of EUR 37.3M (about $43M) announced August 5, 2026, led by Plural and HV with Positron Ventures participating. Brings total raised to about EUR 53M (about $61M). (Energy-Storage.news / ESS News / EU-Startups, August 5, 2026)
- Founded 2023 in Amsterdam and Delft, a TU Delft spinout. Founders: Aytac Yilmaz (CEO), Rutil Ozdemir (COO), Yaiza Gonzalez Garcia (CSO). (August 5, 2026)
- The technology: grid-scale iron-air batteries that store electricity for **24 to 100 hours**, against roughly 4 hours for a standard lithium grid battery. The cell charges and discharges by un-rusting and rusting iron electrodes. (August 5, 2026)
- Materials are iron, water and air. No lithium, no cobalt, no nickel, so none of the mineral geopolitics tracked under [[Compute and Resources Become Geopolitics]] applies to it. (August 5, 2026)
- Positioned explicitly as renewable baseload power for the AI era, not as a consumer or vehicle technology. (AccessNewswire, August 5, 2026)
- Commercial-scale production targeted from 2028. (August 5, 2026)

## Why it matters for the future
The whole AI power argument has assumed that firm power means burning something. That assumption survives only while storage is measured in hours you can count on one hand. Multi-day storage does not add generation, it changes what existing generation is worth: a solar farm that can deliver at 3am is a different asset class from one that cannot, and it can be built in months rather than the four to five years quoted for a large transformer or the years-long queue for a turbine slot. The 10 to 20 year read is that the AI campus of the mid-2030s does not look like a power station with computers next to it. It looks like a field of panels with a warehouse of rusting iron underneath. Watch whether any announced campus pairs renewables with multi-day storage instead of on-site gas. That is the first crack in the turbine story.

## Awareness watch
Obscure. Triggers to move it: a signed offtake with a named data-centre developer, a first commercial installation, or a cost per kilowatt-hour figure that beats lithium on a multi-day basis. Iron-air has a long history of promising and under-delivering, so treat 2028 as the honest test date and do not front-run it.
