**Awareness:** Building (was Obscure; a CNN Business feature on July 7, 2026 plus the SpaceX and Orbital FCC filings made "data centers in space" a mainstream-media topic. The company is still little known, but the concept is no longer a secret.)

## One line
An Nvidia-backed startup that trained the first AI model in space, betting that the cheapest place to run a giant computer is not on Earth.

## The chokepoint it owns
Orbital compute. In the right orbit the sun never sets (solar up to 8x more productive, no batteries), and the vacuum is a free, infinite heat sink (no water, no cooling towers). It sidesteps the two walls boxing in AI: power and heat.

## Key facts (dated)
- Trained the first AI model in orbit (Dec 2025); valuation over $1B (2026).
- Next satellite (Starcloud-2) planned for October 2026 with ~100x the power generation of Starcloud-1; Starcloud-3 (200 kW, ~3 tons, sized for Starship's deployment system) is pitched as the first orbital data center cost-competitive with terrestrial ones. CNN Business feature aired July 7, 2026. (CNN / TechCrunch, July 2026)
- The orbit land-grab now has three filers: SpaceX (up to 1M satellites, 100 GW, filed Jan 30, 2026), Starcloud, and newcomer Orbital (100,000 satellites, 10 GW, filed June 24, 2026, reported July 8 to 9). (SpaceNews / DCD, July 2026)
- Filings describe orbital data centres measured in gigawatts with solar arrays kilometres across (ambition, not built).
- Peer: Google Project Suncatcher (TPUs in orbit, satellites linked by free-space optics; pre-print stage).
- SpaceX is now enabler and rival: it unveiled its own orbital data-center satellite (AI1) in June 2026 and filed with the FCC to launch up to ~1 million AI satellites; it projects space could be the lowest-cost place for AI compute within 2 to 3 years. (2026)
- Jeff Bezos: giant (gigawatt-scale) data centers in space within 10 to 20 years, eventually beating terrestrial cost. (Oct 2025)
- The economics hinge on SpaceX's Starship, targeting roughly 99% lower launch cost (~$100 to 200 per kg). Google's threshold for orbital data centers to pencil out is ~$200/kg. (2026)

## Why it matters for the future
The first slice of heavy industry to leave Earth for cost reasons, not as a stunt. In 20 years the most powerful AI may sit in orbit, beyond any single grid, government, or water table.

## Awareness watch
Currently sci-fi-sounding, which is the hook. Reliable, cheap launch (Starship cadence) is the domino to watch.
