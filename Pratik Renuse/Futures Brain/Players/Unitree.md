## One line
The Hangzhou company that ships more humanoid robots than anyone on earth, now approved to become mainland China's first major humanoid-robot listing.

## The chokepoint it owns
Volume. While Western humanoid firms burn venture money on pilots, Unitree already manufactures and sells walking robots at consumer-hardware scale and consumer-hardware prices (entry humanoids from roughly $16,000, some models under $6,000). Together with AgiBot it accounts for ~80% of China's humanoid shipments. Whoever gets the manufacturing learning curve first sets the cost floor for everyone else.

## Key facts (dated)

## Why it matters for the future
The humanoid story has been demos and promises; Unitree is the first company where it is revenue, margin, and now a stock ticker. A public price on a humanoid maker does for robots what listings did for EVs: it hands the sector a benchmark, forces disclosure, and pulls a capital flood into the supply chain (actuators, gears, magnets). The 10 to 20 year read: the winner of the robot race is decided on cost curves and factories, not on the smartest demo, and China is currently running that curve fastest.
