# Mazama Energy

#player
**Proves:** [[Energy is the Ceiling]] (primary)
**Awareness:** Obscure (geothermal and climate-tech trade press plus one TechCrunch piece; no general-audience recognition, and "superhot rock" is not a phrase most people have heard)

## One line
Every other answer to the AI power problem needs a fuel supply, a licence or a turbine slot. This one needs a drill.

## The chokepoint it owns
Depth, and the cost of reaching it. Geothermal's economics are almost entirely drilling economics: the heat is free and effectively infinite, and the bill is the hole. Conventional geothermal only works where hot water already sits near the surface, which is why it supplies a rounding error of global electricity despite being the cleanest baseload source available.

**Superhot rock** changes the arithmetic rather than the geology. Past roughly 374 C water becomes supercritical, which means it carries far more energy per unit of mass than steam. A well that reaches supercritical conditions can produce several times the power of a conventional geothermal well from the same hole, so the cost per megawatt falls even though the cost per well rises. The whole bet is whether drilling into very hot, very deep rock can be made routine.

What makes this a chokepoint worth tracking rather than a science project: the skill required is oil and gas drilling, and the two companies that just funded it are oil and gas companies.

## Key facts (dated)
- **The round.** **$135M** announced **September 17, 2026**, an oversubscribed **Series B led by Centaurus Capital** (founded by John Arnold) and **Doerr Capital**, with **ConocoPhillips and Shell Ventures** joining, and **Khosla Ventures and Gates Frontier** investing again. Also SiteGround Capital, H. Barton Asset Management, and the Jeffrey and Marieke Rothschild Foundation. (GlobeNewswire / TechCrunch / Bend Bulletin, September 17, 2026)
- **The record.** In **2025** Mazama reached **629 degrees F (331 C)** at **Newberry, Oregon**, the **highest temperature ever achieved in an engineered geothermal system**.
- **The number that actually matters.** Its second well, **Project Athena**, reached the same **10,350-foot depth in 15 days this month, roughly 80% faster** than the 2025 well, and is continuing about a mile deeper, targeting **750 degrees F (400 C)**. One well generation to the next, the time collapsed by four fifths. In an industry where cost is time, that is a learning curve appearing in public.
- **The commercial plan.** First electricity to the grid targeted **2029 at 50 MW**, expanding to **200 MW in 2030**.
- **The resource claim.** Targeting **independent certification of more than 10 GWe of resource potential at Newberry alone**, while evaluating other US and international sites. Note the wording: this is a target for certification, **not a certified resource**, and the note must say so wherever the figure is used.

## Why it matters for the future
The 10-year read. Every answer this brain has filed to the power ceiling carries a structural delay. Gas turbines are sold out to 2029 and beyond ([[GE Vernova]]). Fission needs a licence and a fuel chain, and even the fast ones ([[Oklo]], [[Aalo Atomics]]) have produced test reactors rather than commercial gigawatts. Fusion is a 2030s proposition ([[Commonwealth Fusion]], [[Helion]]). Solar needs storage to be baseload ([[Ore Energy]], [[Suniva]]). Superhot rock geothermal is the only one whose gating item is a **drilling rig and a permit to drill**, both of which already exist at industrial scale in the United States. If Mazama's drilling curve holds, geothermal stops being a regional curiosity and becomes the boring answer, which in this brain is usually the one that wins.

The 20-year read, stated as a bet. The heat is under most of the planet; only the depth varies. A technology whose cost falls with drilling speed rather than with manufacturing volume follows a different curve from solar or batteries, and a slower one, but it ends somewhere unusual: baseload power that is weather-independent, fuel-free, land-light, and sited by geology rather than by grid access. That last point is the one that matters for AI. A data centre next to a 400 C well does not need an interconnection queue position, which is the thing the [[Energy is the Ceiling]] note has spent two months documenting as the real constraint.

## Awareness watch
Obscure. Triggers to move it: Athena actually reaching 400 C; any signed offtake with a hyperscaler; a second operator publishing a comparable drilling-time improvement, which would turn one company's curve into an industry trend; or the 10 GWe certification landing.

## The honest risk
Four, and they are real. First, **2029 first power does nothing for the 2026 to 2028 crunch**, which is the window this thesis is actually about. Second, deep hot drilling has a long history of expensive failures: bits, casing and electronics degrade fast at 400 C, and the reason nobody has done this is not that nobody thought of it. Third, the 10 GWe figure is a target for certification and should never be quoted as a resource. Fourth, **induced seismicity** has killed engineered geothermal projects before (Basel, Pohang) and Newberry is a volcano, so the social licence risk is not hypothetical.
