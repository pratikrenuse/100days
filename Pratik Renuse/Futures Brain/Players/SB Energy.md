# SB Energy

#player
**Proves:** [[Energy is the Ceiling]], [[Compute and Resources Become Geopolitics]]
**Awareness:** Obscure (SoftBank's US energy arm; named in one week of coverage as the owner of the largest data-center project ever proposed, and almost nobody can say what it does)

## One line
The SoftBank energy subsidiary that would own the biggest AI campus ever proposed, and that is being financed on Nvidia's credit rating because the tenant does not have one.

## The chokepoint it owns
Speed to power, and the balance sheet that buys it. SB Energy develops and owns the land, the generation and the buildings, then leases the finished campus to an AI company. That structure exists because the two things a frontier lab cannot conjure are a grid connection and investment-grade credit. The Piketon plan solves both by not using the grid (9.2 GW of on-site gas) and not using the tenant's credit (Nvidia's guarantee). Whoever can assemble those two things at gigawatt scale becomes the landlord of the AI economy.

## Key facts (dated)
- Nvidia is in talks to provide roughly $250B in financing support so OpenAI can lease a planned 10 GW campus in Piketon, Ohio, developed and owned by SB Energy. (Wall Street Journal reporting via Qz, Al Jazeera, ABC6, July 27, 2026. Reported, not signed.)
- Site: a decommissioned uranium-enrichment plant about 50 miles south of Columbus, Ohio. (July 27, 2026)
- Full build-out: 10 GW of data-center capacity alongside 9.2 GW of on-site natural gas generation. Total cost including chips could exceed $500B, the largest data-center project announced to date. (July 27, 2026)
- The financing mechanism, as reported: OpenAI may lack an investment-grade credit rating, so SB Energy borrows against Nvidia's credit instead. Investment-grade credit makes the debt cheaper and easier to raise. (July 27, 2026)
- A parallel negotiation covers Nvidia helping fund OpenAI's chip purchases, reported as potentially reaching $350B. (July 27, 2026)
- First phase is roughly 800 MW, targeted for 2028, contingent on settling financing, generation and transmission. (July 27, 2026)
- Prior context: SoftBank and OpenAI committed $1B to SB Energy for AI data centers earlier in the Stargate build-out. (2025, standing fact, not this week's evidence)

## Why it matters for the future
This is the week the AI buildout stopped being financed like software and started being financed like a power plant. The tenant cannot borrow, so the chip vendor lends its credit; the grid cannot deliver, so the campus builds its own 9.2 GW. Both substitutions point the same way: the constraint is no longer intelligence or even chips, it is the ability to underwrite and energise ten gigawatts of concrete. In 10 years, the companies that look most like AI winners may have balance sheets that read like utilities, and the vendor guarantee will either look like the smartest structure of the decade or the clearest circularity risk in it.
