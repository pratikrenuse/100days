# Oklo

#player
**Proves:** [[Energy is the Ceiling]] (the 2030s answer to the turbine squeeze)
**Awareness:** Building, upper edge (updated August 7, 2026: Groves went critical on August 5 and the milestone was widely covered, so the "reactors are switching on" fact is now public. The distinction that is still not understood, that a low-power isotope test reactor is not a data-centre power source, is where the remaining edge sits.)

## One line
The Sam Altman-incubated reactor company racing to switch on America's first new-style small reactors, decades ahead of the old nuclear consensus.

## The chokepoint it owns
Like [[Helion]], Oklo is a ceiling-lifter, not a chokepoint owner. Gas turbines are sold out to 2029 and the grid queue is years long; small fast reactors that can be built next to a data center are the first fission answer that arrives on AI's timeline rather than nuclear's. Oklo's Aurora line targets compact liquid-metal-cooled fast reactors (~15 to 75 MW class) sold as power-as-a-service.

## Key facts (dated)
- July 1, 2026: the US Department of Energy approved the Documented Safety Analysis (the final safety basis) for Oklo's Groves Isotope Test Reactor in Texas under the DOE Reactor Pilot Program. Remaining gates: readiness review and startup approval, then fuel load. (Oklo / Businesswire, July 1, 2026)
- Oklo targets first criticality at Groves in July 2026, the month of this note. (Oklo, July 1, 2026)
- Context: the DOE Reactor Pilot Program aimed to have at least three advanced reactors reach criticality around July 4, 2026; early-July coverage framed Oklo among the leaders of that sprint. (July 3 to 4, 2026 coverage)
- July 23, 2026: the DOE granted **startup authorization** for Groves, clearing the last regulatory gate before fuel load and criticality. Groundbreaking (September 2025) to startup authorization took just over ten months. Groves is the first advanced reactor to clear a Documented Safety Analysis while sitting on privately owned land and running entirely on commercially sourced fuel, equipment and systems. Criticality still not announced as of July 28, 2026, with three days left in the July target. (Oklo newsroom, Interesting Engineering, July 23, 2026)
- **August 5, 2026: Groves reached first criticality at low power, in the evening.** Thirteen days after startup authorization and roughly ten months from groundbreaking on a greenfield site. It is the first reactor under the DOE Reactor Pilot Program to achieve criticality on privately owned land, built from the ground up rather than inside an existing national-lab complex. Groves is a low-power test reactor whose purpose is medical isotope production and generating operating data for Oklo's larger commercial designs, so this is an operational foundation, not a power source. OKLO stock slipped on the news. The July target this brain tracked for three consecutive weeks slipped five days and then landed. (Oklo newsroom / NucNet / Seeking Alpha, August 5, 2026)
- Status check July 14, 2026: Groves has NOT announced criticality yet; the pilot program's July 4 goal was met by others (four reactors critical, the fourth being Aalo-X on July 4, see [[Aalo Atomics]]). Oklo still targets criticality in July; remaining gates unchanged (readiness review, startup approval, fuel load). (Foreign Policy Journal, July 4 and 8, 2026)
- Broke ground on its first commercial Aurora powerhouse at Idaho National Laboratory in September 2025, eyeing late-2027 to 2028 first power. (World Nuclear News, Sept 2025)
- Sam Altman took Oklo public via SPAC in 2024 and chaired it until 2025; the company sells electricity, not reactors (build, own, operate, sign long-term offtakes with data-center customers).

## Why it matters for the future
The old rule was that a new US reactor takes ten to twenty years of licensing before concrete pours. The Reactor Pilot Program just compressed that to months for test units. If Groves goes critical this month and Aurora follows in 2027 to 2028, the [[Energy is the Ceiling]] thesis gets its first hard evidence that the ceiling can be raised by fission on an AI-relevant timeline. The 10 to 20 year read: data centers stop queueing for the grid and start ordering reactors like they order turbines today, and the "SMR is always a decade away"-consensus breaks in public.

## Awareness watch
OKLO is a heavily traded meme-adjacent nuclear ticker, so market awareness is high, but the concrete milestone (a DOE-approved reactor loading fuel in 2026) is barely understood outside the sector. First criticality at Groves, or first power at the INL Aurora, are the mainstreaming triggers. Peers on the same rung: X-energy, Kairos, NuScale, TerraPower.
