## One line
The company trying to replace the copper wires between chips with light, selling optical input/output chiplets that sit in the same package as the processor.

## The chokepoint it owns
Optical interconnect, the rung after scale-up switching. Once electrical signalling runs out of room (a copper link barely holds a high-speed signal a metre before it degrades), the data between accelerators has to move on light. Ayar's TeraPHY optical I/O chiplets and SuperNova light sources push terabits per second in and out of a package over fibre, at lower power than copper. If [[Astera Labs]] owns the wiring this generation, the optics layer is what the wiring becomes next.

## Key facts (dated)
- Raised a $500M Series E in March 2026, lifting total funding to about $870M and valuation to ~$3.75B, to mass-produce co-packaged optics and grow its Taiwan presence. (March 3, 2026, The Register / Optica)
- Unveiled the world's first UCIe (Universal Chiplet Interconnect Express) optical interconnect chiplet, aligning optical I/O with the open chiplet standard. (2026, SDxCentral)
- Reference designs with Global Unichip (GUC) using eight next-gen TeraPHY chiplets for 200+ Tbps of aggregate bandwidth per package. (2026)
- Context: at OFC 2026, interconnect was framed as core AI infrastructure, not a supporting part; co-packaged optics forecast to move from ~0.5% of AI optical modules in 2026 toward ~35% by 2030 (TrendForce, via the [[Astera Labs]] note).

## Why it matters for the future
The same pattern as memory and interconnect, one rung lower: when copper stops scaling, the firm that already ships the light engine collects the toll. The 10 to 20 year read is that "buying bandwidth" eventually means buying photons, and a handful of optical-I/O names become as strategic as the memory makers are now.
