# AXT

#player
**Proves:** [[The Bottleneck Slides Down the Stack]] (primary), [[Compute and Resources Become Geopolitics]] (secondary)
**Awareness:** Obscure (a small Fremont substrate maker, followed by compound-semiconductor specialists and almost nobody else, even as the material it sells becomes the tightest input in AI infrastructure)

## One line
Every laser that carries data between AI chips is grown on a crystal wafer, and the crystal is now scarcer than memory.

## The chokepoint it owns
Indium phosphide substrates. As data centres run out of copper headroom, the links between chips move to light, and light needs lasers. The lasers that carry data at data-centre speeds cannot be made on silicon; they are grown on indium phosphide wafers. AXT grows those wafers. The reason this is a real chokepoint rather than a temporary shortage is the input: indium is recovered as a by-product of zinc smelting, so the world's indium supply is set by how much zinc the world wants, not by how many lasers AI needs. No amount of AI capital expenditure changes the output of a zinc smelter. That is a category of constraint this brain has not tracked before, because every previous chokepoint (packaging, memory, interconnect, turbines) could eventually be relieved by spending enough money.

## Key facts (dated)
- **The shortage is now officially the binding one.** TrendForce, August 6, 2026: indium phosphide has emerged as the AI optical interconnect bottleneck. Lumentum CEO Michael Hurlston said the InP supply-demand gap has surpassed that of DRAM and NAND. Demand for electro-absorption modulated lasers exceeds supply by more than 30%, even with output expected to rise more than 50% from December 2025 to December 2026. (TrendForce / Tom's Hardware, August 6, 2026)
- **AXT's numbers turned on it.** Reported August 4, 2026: AXT returned to profit on record quarterly indium phosphide revenue of **$30.7M in Q2/2026**, against $13.6M the prior quarter and **$3.6M a year earlier**. That is roughly 8.5x in four quarters. (Semiconductor Today, August 4, 2026)
- **China controls the export.** Indium phosphide was added to China's export control list on **February 4, 2025**. Every overseas shipment needs a permit from China's Ministry of Commerce, and the permits are opaque and timing-volatile. AXT's Q4/2025 revenue was constrained by permit delays; Q1/2026 revenue grew 17% after more permits than expected were granted. AXT has roughly **$66M of Q3 2026 revenue** either already permitted or not requiring a permit. (Semiconductor Today, March and May 2026; company disclosure)
- **It is doubling capacity twice.** AXT completed a **$632.5M capital raise** to fund an expansion that doubles InP substrate capacity in 2026 and doubles it again in 2027, by repurposing existing facilities and leveraging a vertically integrated China campus covering raw materials, furnaces and recycling. Management argues this allows faster, lower-cost scale-up than competitors. (2026 disclosures)
- **The shortage is not just substrates.** It spans substrates, qualified manufacturing lines, fabrication equipment, yields and packaging. EML laser yields run **15% to 50%** depending on wafer generation and design complexity, and yields must be qualified through extended production runs before volume commitments can be met. (August 6, 2026)
- **Order scale changed by orders of magnitude.** Orders that once numbered in the hundreds for telecom customers have moved to hundreds of millions of devices, driven by Nvidia and hyperscale operators. (August 6, 2026)
- **Someone bought a fab over it.** Nokia signed a definitive agreement on **August 5, 2026** to acquire NXP's Chandler, Arizona semiconductor campus and convert it to indium phosphide production for optical components, leasing part of the site from early 2027 with full acquisition expected to close Q1 2029. The stated driver was the global InP shortage. Nokia Q2 2026: net sales EUR 4.815B, optical communications sales up 20% YoY, sales to AI and cloud customers up 105%. (Semiconductor Today / Nokia, August 5 to 7, 2026)
- **Relief keeps slipping.** LightCounting projected the InP laser shortage would ease from mid-2026 as new capacity came online. Analysts now expect the imbalance to persist beyond 2027. (August 6, 2026)

## Why it matters for the future
This brain's Astera Labs work already forecast the shift from copper to light, and named Ayar Labs as the rung after the wiring. What it did not have was the material underneath the light. The 10-year read is that the optics transition happens as forecast, roughly a third of chip-to-chip links running on light by 2030, but it happens at the pace indium allows rather than the pace capital wants, and the companies that matter are the ones holding substrate capacity and export permits rather than the ones designing transceivers. The 20-year read is stranger: if the binding input for the world's most valuable industry is a trace by-product of zinc mining, then zinc smelting economics become an AI variable, and recovery and recycling of indium becomes a strategically funded activity in a way it has never been. Watch for the first hyperscaler that signs an offtake for a metal rather than for a part.
