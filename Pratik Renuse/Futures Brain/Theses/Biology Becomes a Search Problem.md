# Biology Becomes a Search Problem

#thesis
**Awareness:** Obscure, moving, and **the node is live again after two blank runs** (September 22, 2026: a dedicated biology pass, run first as the September 15 changelog required, produced two in-window items where the general sweep had produced none for five weeks. A frontier lab has built its own wet lab, and the manufacturing rung under the loop got funded. Still badly under-discussed next to chatbots, so the hooks remain usable, but the "nobody is building this" framing has been wrong since August.)

## Claim
AI is turning the discovery of physical matter and medicine into a search problem, something you run at the speed of a computer instead of the speed of a career.

## Where it leads (2035 to 2045)
The next superconductor, the battery that ends the gas engine, cheap carbon capture, new catalysts, and many new drugs get deliberately searched for by machines rather than stumbled on by lucky researchers. Whoever owns the AI-plus-robot-lab loop could control the physical economy the way oil majors once did.

## Mechanism
Generative models propose candidate structures; autonomous robot labs synthesise and test them; another model checks the result. The human-speed trial-and-error bottleneck of 10,000 years breaks.

## Evidence for (dated)
- GNoME proposed 2.2M new stable crystals in ~17 days (~800 years of materials science); hundreds experimentally confirmed.
- 52,000 graphene-like (superconductor-candidate) compounds found vs ~1,000 known before; 528 new lithium-conductor candidates (25x prior).
- Berkeley A-Lab: 36 new compounds made autonomously in 17 days.
- Isomorphic Labs: first AI-designed cancer drug targeting Phase 1 by end 2026; ~$3B in Lilly/Novartis deals; IsoDDE doubles AlphaFold-3 accuracy.
- Design and wet-lab search became one loop: a Nature proof-of-concept (Krasnow et al.) combined AI protein design with laboratory evolution to reprogram botulinum neurotoxin proteases to cut a range of new protein targets, including ataxin-2, implicated in neurodegeneration. The model proposed sequences that folded to the same 3D structure as the natural enzyme with greater stability, and directed evolution then tuned them onto the new targets. The two halves of the search, propose and test, now run as one pipeline rather than two disciplines. (Nature via Phys.org, July 24, 2026)
- The loop stopped being a result and started being a company. **Jeff Dean is leaving Google after 27 years to co-found Discovery Loop**, announced August 5, 2026, with Sanjay Ghemawat, Oriol Vinyals and Quoc Le. It is a public benefit company whose product is the research loop itself: propose an experiment, run it, evaluate the result, iterate thousands of times. It begins by automating machine learning research with itself as first customer, then aims the same machinery at engineering, medicine, materials design and clean energy. Radical Ventures and Khosla Ventures co-lead the seed; the round is not closed and no valuation was disclosed; Google is a founding investor and cloud partner. Announced in the same breath as Demis Hassabis moving to Chair of Google DeepMind and Chief Scientist of Alphabet. (GeekWire / TechTimes / Radical Ventures, August 5, 2026. See [[Discovery Loop]] and [[Sources/Profiles/12 Jeff Dean]])
- And the public sector started building the same loop as shared infrastructure. The US National Science Foundation awarded **$24.9 million over six years on August 5, 2026** to build **ARM-MIP**, the Autonomous Robotic Metallurgist Materials Innovation Platform at Texas A&M University: the first national self-driving laboratory for metals, open to researchers across the country. Robots and models take over the repetitive labour of alloy discovery, proposing a composition, making it, testing it and learning from the result. A companion NSF facility, READINESS, does the same for two-dimensional materials, oxide semiconductors and diamond thin films, with human oversight retained at critical decision points. LLNL described a parallel push into AI, robotics and automation for materials and advanced manufacturing on July 29, 2026. Note what this means: the robot lab stops being one company's advantage and becomes a shared facility any researcher can book. (AIwire / HPCwire / Rice University / NSF, August 5, 2026. See [[ARM-MIP]])
- **A frontier lab stopped renting the wet lab and built one.** Reuters, **September 18, 2026**: Anthropic has set up a physical biology lab in the San Francisco Bay Area. Eric Kauderer-Abrams, its head of life sciences, confirmed the lab and said the aim is to explore how Claude can direct robotic systems (microscopes, liquid handlers, robotic arms) to run experiments with limited human intervention, while human oversight remains essential and the work stops short of clinical trials. It is tied to a **Model Hardware Standard (MHS)**, a specification that lets AI agents coordinate multiple instruments, adjust experiment parameters and recover from some hardware errors. The company describes itself as "in the very early innings". Anthropic is mainstream and owns no chokepoint, so this is thesis evidence, not a hook. What it means for the thesis: [[Discovery Loop]] was filed six weeks ago as the loop-as-a-product bet by people leaving Google. A frontier lab is now building the same loop in-house, with an **instrument interface standard** attached, which is the part that matters. A standard is what turns one company's robot lab into an industry's. (Reuters via Engadget / Gizmodo / Open Data Science, September 18, 2026)
- **The loop's output rung got funded.** [[Multiply Labs]] raised a **$75M Series B on September 17, 2026**, taking total funding past **$100M**. San Francisco, founded 2016. It builds robotic manufacturing clusters for cell and gene therapies and other advanced biologics, designed to work with existing pharmaceutical equipment and processes rather than replace the plant. Round joined by **AstraZeneca Ventures, Lingotto, Legend Biotech and Strange Ventures**, with **Casdin Capital, Lux Capital and Founders Fund** returning; capital goes to manufacturing capacity and to moving from clinical-stage deployments toward commercial-scale production. Company figures, flagged as company figures: **74% lower cost per dose** and **up to 100x the throughput** of manual manufacturing. Why it belongs in this thesis rather than only in robotics: a search problem is only as good as what you can build at the end of it, and a personalised cell therapy is hand-made today. If discovery runs at computer speed and manufacture runs at human speed, the constraint simply moves to the clean room. (The AI Insider / Crunchbase, September 17, 2026. See [[Multiply Labs]])
- Talent is reallocating toward AI-for-science: John Jumper (2024 Nobel, AlphaFold co-creator) announced he is leaving Google DeepMind after ~9 years to join Anthropic, followed by reports that Gemini researchers Jonas Adler and Alexander Pritzel are moving too. (June 19 to 24, 2026) Signal that the next competitive front is AI reasoning over hard science, not just chatbots.

## What would break it
- AI-designed candidates failing at the real-world / clinical stage (design is not yet approval).
- Synthesis and validation staying slow despite better generation.
- Tripwire: first AI-designed drug to clear Phase 2 efficacy is the real signal.
- **New tripwire (added September 22, 2026): the interface standard, not the robot.** Anthropic's Model Hardware Standard is the first attempt in this brain to define how a model talks to laboratory instruments generally, rather than to one bench. Robot labs have existed for a decade and stayed bespoke, which is exactly why they never compounded. If MHS or anything like it gets adopted by a second organisation, or by an instrument maker rather than an AI lab, then autonomous experiments become portable and the loop compounds across institutions instead of within one. Watch for any instrument manufacturer (Thermo Fisher, Agilent, Tecan, Hamilton, Opentrons) shipping support for a model-facing control standard, and watch whether [[ARM-MIP]]'s national facility adopts one rather than writing its own.
- **New tripwire (added September 22, 2026): the constraint moves to the clean room.** If discovery keeps accelerating while manufacture does not, the binding constraint on AI-designed medicine stops being the science and becomes the plant, which is the same motion [[The Bottleneck Slides Down the Stack]] tracks one industry over. [[Multiply Labs]]' 74% cost-per-dose claim is the number to test. Watch for the first AI-originated therapy whose public timeline slips on **manufacturing capacity** rather than on trial results, and watch whether any of the large pharmas that just invested (AstraZeneca, Legend Biotech) convert a pilot into a commercial line.
- **New tripwire (added August 7, 2026): does the loop close on itself first?** [[Discovery Loop]]'s stated plan is to automate machine learning research with itself as first customer before pointing the system at matter and medicine. That ordering is the test. If it produces a published, reproduced ML result that no human proposed, the thesis accelerates hard. If the company spends two years improving its own models and never reaches a wet lab, this is a compute story wearing a science costume. Watch for the first externally reproduced Discovery Loop result, and watch whether ARM-MIP publishes an alloy discovered end to end without a human in the proposal step.

## Outlook (10/20/30 curve, sourced)
Full deep-research curve (AI drug discovery + protein design; DNA synthesis/storage + synbio, with bear case): [[Signals/Biology - The Long-Horizon Curve (2026-07-02)]]. Headline: AI has cracked reading and designing biology (AlphaFold DB 200M+ structures, RFdiffusion de novo proteins, Insilico's rentosertib posted the first generative-AI Phase 2a in Nature Medicine, June 2025), but ~90% clinical failure persists and zero AI-designed drugs are FDA approved yet. The write-cost of DNA (~7-9 cents/bp, falling far slower than reading) and DNA storage (~$3,500/MB, ~50,000x too slow) are the walls. Synbio ~$24-26B (2025) -> ~$100-240B by 2035. Discovery is getting fast; proof in humans and cheap DNA writing are not.
