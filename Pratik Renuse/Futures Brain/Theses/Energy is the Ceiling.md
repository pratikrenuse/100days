# Energy is the Ceiling

#thesis
**Awareness:** Building (talked about, but the gas-and-turbine reality is not)

## Claim
Power, not chips, is the real constraint on how fast AI can grow. And the near-term answer is not the clean one everyone advertises.

## Where it leads (2035 to 2045)
Whoever generates, moves, and sells electrons captures a huge share of the AI economy. Electricity becomes the strategic commodity of the century, the thing nations hoard and fight over. The companies that make and move power spend a decade as the most important, least discussed businesses around.

## Mechanism
Data centres need power now, at a pace concrete can barely keep up with. Reactors (especially small modular ones) do not switch on this decade, so the marginal electron through ~2030 is natural gas. The true chokepoint is the gas turbine, and turbines are sold out for years.

## What would break it
- **Tripwire result, July 22, 2026: the SMR-softening check came back negative.** GE Vernova's Q2 raised the year-end gas backlog-plus-slots guide from 110 GW to 125 GW and posted record $176B backlog with orders up 88%. Four advanced reactors reaching criticality in July did not soften a single turbine order. Conviction on this thesis is raised: the ceiling is binding harder, and the fission answer is running in parallel rather than in substitution. Next check: GE Vernova Q3, and whether any 2027 turbine slot is released rather than added.
- **New tripwire (added July 28, 2026): on-site generation as the default.** The Piketon plan pairs 10 GW of compute with 9.2 GW of its own gas plant, and the National Grid / Joulent Project Kilby structure (July 1, 2026) does the same at 2.67 GW. If large campuses routinely build their own generation instead of queueing for interconnection, the grid stops being the binding constraint and the turbine order book becomes the only number that matters. Watch how many 2027 to 2028 campuses announce with dedicated on-site generation attached.
- **New tripwire (added July 28, 2026): vendor-financed power.** If Nvidia's ~$250B credit backstop for SB Energy is signed, the chip vendor's balance sheet becomes the collateral for the electricity. That is either the unlock that makes the ceiling liftable or the concentration risk that makes one credit rating the single point of failure for the whole buildout. Watch for rating-agency commentary on Nvidia's guarantee exposure.
- A real SMR or fusion timeline pull-forward. Status July 14, 2026: first spark, not yet fire. Four advanced reactors have now gone critical under the DOE Reactor Pilot Program (Aalo-X on July 4 was the fourth, built in under eight months); Oklo's Groves targets criticality this month. But zero-power test criticality is not commercial gigawatts; the claim only breaks when reactors displace turbine orders at scale (watch GE Vernova backlog growth for the first sign of softening; Q2 earnings ~July 22).
- **New tripwire (added August 7, 2026): duration, not generation.** [[Ore Energy]]'s iron-air cells claim 24 to 100 hours of storage against roughly 4 hours for lithium. If multi-day storage gets cheap before turbines get faster, the constraint stops being "how many gigawatts can you build" and becomes "how many hours can you hold," which would let existing renewable capacity serve baseload load and soften the gas-turbine case underneath this thesis. Watch Ore Energy's 2028 commercial-production date, and watch whether any announced AI campus pairs solar with multi-day storage instead of on-site gas.
- **Tripwire result, August 10, 2026: the vendor-financed-power tripwire fired, and harder than written.** The July 28 version asked whether Nvidia's roughly $250B credit backstop for [[SB Energy]] would be signed. Two weeks later Nvidia skipped the guarantee and bought equity instead, up to $3B in [[Lancium]] for about 20%. Debt support is reversible; ownership is not. The chip vendor is now a power landlord, which means the concentration risk the tripwire was watching for is real and growing, and the "who collects the money" answer for this thesis shifts one notch toward whoever holds the land and the interconnection queue position rather than whoever generates.
- **New tripwire (added August 11, 2026): permission, not power.** The binding constraint may stop being gigawatts and become consent. If local opposition converts into permitting delays, project audits or ratepayer-protection rules across several states, then the thesis's central claim (electricity is the ceiling) gets replaced by a political one (siting is the ceiling), and the winners change from generators to whoever holds already-permitted land and an existing interconnection position. That would upgrade [[SB Energy]], [[Lancium]] and Joulent and downgrade pure equipment plays. Watch: any state passing a data-centre-specific ratepayer or siting law before the November 2026 midterms, and whether Eaton's 307 GW backlog conversion rate falls below the stated 20%.
- **Tripwire result, August 5, 2026: Groves went critical, and the ceiling did not move.** The fission fast lane hit its milestone thirteen days after authorization, and Oklo's own stock slipped on the news. A low-power isotope test reactor is not a data-centre power source. The thesis holds. Next real check remains Aalo's Project Ascension and Oklo's INL Aurora first power (2027 to 2028).
- **Tripwire result, September 15, 2026: the "permission, not power" tripwire fired inside three weeks of being written.** It asked for any state passing a data-centre-specific siting or ratepayer rule before the November midterms. On **August 3, 2026** Governor Abbott paused **all** new Texas data-centre grid connections pending a PUCT and ERCOT audit, with projects now required to disclose ownership, financial commitments, water use and community impact before proceeding. That event sits outside this run's evidence window so it is logged as a tripwire result rather than as new evidence, but the direction is settled: consent is now a gating item in the single most important US market for this thesis, and the winners shift further toward whoever already holds permitted land and a queue position ([[SB Energy]], [[Lancium]], Joulent) and away from anyone whose plan starts with a new interconnection request. Next check: whether the Texas pause converts into a standing approval regime, and whether [[Eaton]]'s 307 GW backlog conversion rate falls below its stated 20%.
- **New tripwire (added September 15, 2026): the workload leaves the site.** If splitting one training or inference job across several geographically separate campuses becomes standard practice because no single site can be powered, then the ceiling stops being "megawatts per site" and becomes "megawatts per region plus the quality of the link between sites." That weakens the case for any player whose value is a single large permitted campus and strengthens the long-haul optical layer. Watch for a hyperscaler publicly running one training job across sites more than 100 miles apart, and watch [[Celero Communications]]' 2nm coherent DSP entering a named customer design.
- **Tripwire result, September 22, 2026: the "permission, not power" tripwire fired a second time, from the opposite direction.** The August 11 version watched for states gating access, and Texas supplied that on August 3. This week the **buyers** answered it: Emerald AI, Google and Nvidia founded the **AI Energy Management Alliance** on September 16 with an explicit flexibility-for-connection bargain, and nineteen named launch partners including Anthropic, National Grid, Constellation and NRG. A trade body exists because a negotiation exists. The constraint is now formally political on both sides, and the thing being negotiated is not price, it is queue position. This strengthens rather than breaks the thesis, but it moves the winner one more notch: away from whoever generates the most and toward whoever can **prove** they will not take power at the wrong moment.
- **New tripwire (added September 22, 2026): flexibility becomes a connection condition.** If any utility, ISO or state makes demand-side flexibility a **requirement** for a data-centre interconnection rather than a voluntary offer, then the ceiling stops being gigawatts available and becomes gigawatts available *at peak*, which is a much smaller and much more tradeable number. That would make curtailable capacity a priced asset, revalue every campus with on-site generation or storage ([[SB Energy]], [[Lancium]], [[Ore Energy]]) and put a software layer ([[Emerald AI]]) between the grid and the chips. Watch the six federal proceedings opened in June, any FERC or ISO order that conditions a large-load interconnection on curtailability, and whether AEMA's first regulatory filings ask for a standard or for an exemption.
- **New tripwire (added September 22, 2026): drilling time, not reactor licences.** [[Mazama Energy]] cut 10,350 feet from one well generation to the next by about **80%**. If superhot-rock geothermal keeps a drilling-cost curve like that, it reaches commercial baseload without a fuel supply chain, without a licensing queue and without a turbine slot, which are the three things that make every other answer in this thesis slow. That would not break the claim that energy is the ceiling; it would move the winner from whoever holds a turbine order book to whoever holds drilling rigs and hot geology. Watch whether Athena reaches 400 C, whether the 2029 50 MW date holds, and whether a second operator publishes a comparable drilling-time improvement, because one company's curve is a data point and two is a trend.
- A step change in performance-per-watt that softens demand.
- Tripwire to watch: turbine lead times starting to shrink.
- New tripwire: first criticality at Groves and first power at Oklo's INL Aurora (2027 to 2028 target). Each one shortens the credible gap between "power is the ceiling" and "the ceiling lifts."
- New tripwire (added July 14, 2026): Aalo's Project Ascension, the commercial-scale reactor beside Aalo-X built to power an on-site data center. A reactor actually feeding a data center is the first true "ceiling lifts" event, watch for its startup date.
- New tripwire (added July 14, 2026): whether federal emergency curtailment of data centers (July 2026 precedent) becomes routine each summer. Routine curtailment means the ceiling is binding harder, strengthening the thesis short-term while accelerating the SMR pull that eventually breaks it.

## Outlook (projections, dated and sourced; label speculation as such)
Used to build the 10 and 20 year outlook in posts. These are forecasts, not facts. Full deep-research 10/20/30 curve (demand, supply-answers, grid/transformers, with sources and bear case): [[Signals/Energy - The Long-Horizon Curve (2026-07-02)]].
- Demand: IEA "Energy and AI" (2025) sees data-centre electricity rising from ~460 TWh (2024) to ~945 TWh by 2030 and toward ~1,300 TWh by 2035 (Base Case; Lift-Off ~1,750 TWh in 2035). AI share of DC power ~5 to 15% recently, to ~35 to 50% by 2030.
- Mix: 2024 DC power was ~40% gas, ~24% renewables, ~20% nuclear, ~15% coal (IEA). To 2030 wind and solar meet ~half of the additional demand; by 2035 low-emissions sources are >half of US DC supply. New nuclear meets ~10% of the DC demand increase by 2035 (Deloitte).
- SMRs: first commercial units early 2030s (Oklo and X-energy targeting ~2030 to 2031); ~22 GW of SMR projects in development; AWS targets ~5 GW by 2039.
