# The Bottleneck Slides Down the Stack

#thesis
**Awareness:** Obscure (this is a strong, underused angle)

## Claim
As AI scales, the binding constraint keeps jumping from the famous layer to the boring one beneath it. Compute, then memory, then the wiring between chips, then advanced packaging, then power. Each time, the unglamorous supplier of the moment quietly wins.

## Where it leads (2035 to 2045)
The biggest, most durable profits accrue not to model makers but to whoever owns the current physical chokepoint. The roster of "most strategic companies on earth" fills with firms most people cannot name. The edge is spotting where the constraint moves next, before the crowd.

## Mechanism
Intelligence is commoditising. When the visible layer gets cheap and crowded, pricing power moves one floor down to whatever physics refuses to scale fast. You can raise a hundred billion dollars in a weekend. You cannot build a memory fab or a packaging line in one.

## Hidden players
[[SK Hynix]] (HBM today, now mainstreaming), [[TSMC CoWoS]] (advanced packaging), [[Astera Labs]] (the wiring between chips, the next rung), [[Ayar Labs]] (optical interconnect, the rung after Astera), [[GE Vernova]] (power), transformer makers next. Added July 28, 2026: [[Cerebras]] (the decode half of a split inference workload) and [[Etched]] (fixed-function transformer hardware). Both are a **sideways** slide rather than a downward one: the constraint is not moving to a lower layer this time, it is splitting one workload into two priced goods. Added August 7, 2026: [[OLIX]], the first player here that tries to go **around** the chokepoint, building a decode chip with no high-bandwidth memory and no advanced packaging. Added August 11, 2026: [[AXT]] (the indium phosphide substrate every AI data-centre laser is grown on) and [[Yageo]] (the ceramic capacitors now third in an AI server's bill of materials). Both sit **under** the semiconductor industry rather than inside it, which is a new position for this thesis. Added September 15, 2026: [[Positron AI]] (commodity phone memory instead of HBM) and [[Celero Communications]] (the coherent link between data centres, not inside one), both **around** motions; and [[Fab2]], which sits **behind** the industry rather than under it, in the tools that build the fabs. Added September 22, 2026: [[Cornelis Networks]], which is a sixth motion, **through**: rather than moving the constraint to another layer, it absorbs the work into the layer that used to only carry data.

## Evidence for (dated)
- HBM sold out across SK Hynix, Samsung, Micron through 2026, reserved further out.
- AI data centres consume ~70% of all memory chips made (2026 estimate).
- SK Hynix makes ~half the world's HBM; shortage may run to 2030 (SK Hynix chairman).
- Advanced packaging (CoWoS) repeatedly cited as a gating constraint on GPU supply.
- SK Hynix shipped 12-layer HBM4E samples (48GB, 16 Gbps/pin) and filed for a ~$29B US Nasdaq listing. (June 18 and 24, 2026) The listing is the awareness signal: the memory chokepoint is now a financial front page.
- The constraint is visibly starting to slide: TSMC's CoWoS supply-demand gap is seen narrowing from ~20% to ~10% by end-2026 as capacity ramps toward ~120k to 130k wafers/month. (TrendForce, June 15, 2026) As packaging eases, attention moves to the scale-up interconnect rung ([[Astera Labs]]).
- The market began pricing that next rung in real time: [[Astera Labs]] joined the Nasdaq-100 (effective June 22, 2026), and sell-side targets nearly doubled in a single session (BofA to $450 from $240, Stifel to $460 from $260, both June 23). Index funds are now forced to own the interconnect maker even though the public cannot name it.
- Nvidia's next platform confirms which rungs bind: Vera Rubin triples per-GPU memory bandwidth to 22 TB/s (HBM4) and doubles rack-scale interconnect to 260 TB/s (NVLink 6). The roadmap is engineered around memory and the wiring between chips, the exact two rungs this thesis tracks. (June 27, 2026, TechTimes)
- The awareness signal completed: SK Hynix's ADR debut was confirmed for July 10, 2026, the largest ADR listing in market history (past Alibaba's $21.8B), with the company's ~$1.2T market cap passing Samsung as Korea's number one. The memory chokepoint is now a retail-investor product. Proceeds go to a Yongin fab and advanced packaging, capital flooding two rungs at once. (June 30 to July 6, 2026)
- The debut itself: priced $149, raised $26.5B (largest US share sale ever by a foreign company, past Alibaba's $25B), ~7x oversubscribed, opened $170 and closed day one at $168.01, up 13%. The boring layer's coming-out party filled the order book seven times over. (July 10, 2026, Bloomberg / CNBC)
- The model layer started integrating downward: OpenAI and Broadcom unveiled Jalapeño, OpenAI's first custom inference chip, designed to tape-out in nine months (partly by using OpenAI's own models in the design loop), claiming ~50% cost savings vs current GPUs, deploying from late 2026. When the famous layer builds its own boring layer, it is conceding where the margin lives. (June 24, 2026, OpenAI / CNBC; caught late, logged 2026-07-07)
- The famous layer itself named the boring layer as the constraint: TSMC's record Q2 2026 (revenue $40.2B, up 36% YoY; net profit up 77.4%, a single-quarter record; gross margin 67.7%) came with CoWoS packaging explicitly called the primary bottleneck in AI chip production. Capacity is growing ~80% per year (~35k wafers/month end-2024, ~75k end-2025, target 125k to 130k end-2026), 2026 capex was raised to $60B to $64B, and on July 17 the Arizona commitment was lifted to $265B with four fabs aimed at packaging. The world's most important chipmaker is spending its record profits on the gluing step. (July 16 to 17, 2026, TSMC earnings / TechTimes)
- Compute scarcity is now rationed between giants: Google reportedly capped Meta's access to Gemini models after Meta requested more capacity than Google could supply, and Anthropic is reportedly in talks with Samsung for a custom Claude chip, the second custom-ASIC lane after OpenAI/Broadcom's Jalapeño. Reported, not company-confirmed. (July 14 to 15, 2026, aggregated dailies)
- The workload itself split in two, and the hardware followed: AMD and Cerebras announced a disaggregated inference solution on July 23, 2026, with AMD Helios rack-scale systems handling high-throughput prompt processing and the Cerebras Wafer-Scale Engine handling low-latency token generation, running as one workflow. Modelled at up to 5x higher tokens per second per watt versus Cerebras-only (company modelling, July 2026, not independently measured); available first through Cerebras Cloud in H2 2026. Reading a question and writing an answer are now different products with different suppliers. (AMD / Cerebras / Axios, July 23, 2026. See [[Cerebras]])
- Specialisation got repriced: Etched raised $300M at a $10.3B post-money valuation on July 23, 2026, led by Sequoia with a16z, Jane Street, SK Hynix and Diffusion, reportedly the highest valuation ever for a Sequoia-led Series C. The company was worth $5B in December 2025, so the valuation roughly doubled in seven months. Its Sohu chip is a transformer-only ASIC on TSMC 4nm claiming ~20x inference speedup versus an H100 (company figure, unbenchmarked), with ~$1B of prospective demand and production scaling across an 80,000 sq ft California facility and a Taiwan factory. Note the cap table: SK Hynix, the memory supplier, now holds equity in a chip competing with its biggest customer. (TechCrunch / Yahoo Finance, July 23, 2026. See [[Etched]])
- Someone funded the escape route. **OLIX**, a two-year-old London company, raised EUR 270.5M (about $312M) at a EUR 2.8B (about $3.3B) valuation on August 3, 2026, from Fundomo, Arm and Hudson River Trading, with Reed Hastings angel-investing and Nick McKeown joining the board. Its X-1 platform breaks inference into stage-specific chips instead of one general-purpose part, linked by a "slow and wide" optical interconnect that moves data between chips with light rather than copper. The first chip, DX-1, is a decode accelerator holding the model in on-chip SRAM, and it uses **no high-bandwidth memory and no advanced packaging**, stated explicitly as a supply-chain decision because those are the two components in shortest supply. First customer deliveries targeted H2 2027; over 10,000 tokens per second per user on 100B-parameter models is a company figure, unbenchmarked. (EU-Startups, August 3, 2026. See [[OLIX]])
- The chokepoints OLIX is avoiding got tighter in the same week. Q2 2026 DRAM contract prices rose 58% to 63% quarter over quarter and NAND rose 70% to 75%; Samsung, SK Hynix and Micron are reported effectively sold out of 2027 DRAM and HBM output, with buyers placing advance deposits ahead of formal agreements; AI servers and HBM absorb close to 70% of DRAM output; HBM consumes roughly three times the wafer capacity of standard DRAM per gigabyte (Micron). Samsung's stated view is that the shortage worsens before it improves, into 2027 and 2028. (Counterpoint via Sammy Fans, August 4, 2026; Tom's Hardware; Astute Group)
- The interconnect rung was vindicated on the numbers, not the narrative. [[Astera Labs]] Q2 2026 (August 4, 2026): record revenue $392.4M, up 27% sequentially and 104% year over year; non-GAAP EPS $0.80 versus $0.69 consensus, an eighth straight beat; gross margin 73.7% against 73% guidance. Q3 guidance of $540M to $560M lands against $418.2M consensus, with EPS guided $1.16 to $1.21 against $0.80. Scorpio-X passes Scorpio-P to become the largest product family in Q3, a quarter early. The six-week, roughly 40% drawdown this brain tracked from late June carried no company signal in it. (Astera Labs IR / GlobeNewswire, August 4, 2026)
- **The constraint fell out of the semiconductor industry and landed on a mining by-product.** TrendForce, August 6, 2026: indium phosphide has emerged as the AI optical interconnect bottleneck. Lumentum CEO Michael Hurlston said the InP supply-demand gap has now surpassed that of DRAM and NAND; demand for electro-absorption modulated lasers exceeds supply by more than 30% even with output expected to rise more than 50% from December 2025 to December 2026. The shortage spans substrates, qualified lines, equipment, yields (15% to 50% depending on wafer generation) and packaging. The structural point: indium is recovered as a by-product of zinc smelting, so its supply cannot expand on laser demand alone, it is bound to the economics of zinc. LightCounting had expected relief from mid-2026; analysts now expect the imbalance past 2027. (TrendForce / Tom's Hardware, August 6, 2026. See [[AXT]])
- Two companies moved on it in the same week. **Nokia** signed a definitive agreement on August 5, 2026 to acquire NXP's Chandler, Arizona semiconductor campus and convert it to indium phosphide production for optical components, leasing part of the site from early 2027 with full acquisition closing expected Q1 2029. Nokia Q2 2026: net sales EUR 4.815B, optical communications sales up 20% YoY, sales to AI and cloud customers up 105%. And **AXT**, the substrate supplier underneath all of it, returned to profit on record quarterly InP revenue of $30.7M in Q2/2026, against $13.6M the prior quarter and $3.6M a year earlier, while doubling substrate capacity in 2026 and again in 2027 off a $632.5M raise. (Semiconductor Today, August 4 and 7, 2026)
- **The cheapest part in the machine became the third most expensive line item.** Per the 404K SEMI-AI brief of August 10, 2026 and surrounding trade coverage: some customers are paying two to three times market price to secure multilayer ceramic capacitors from [[Yageo]], Murata and others, on agreements running six months to several years. MLCCs are now the third-largest line item in an AI server bill of materials, behind only GPUs and memory. The top five makers run at 90% to 95% utilisation; a new high-end line takes 18 to 24 months, and announced expansions from Murata, Samsung Electro-Mechanics and Taiyo Yuden will not deliver meaningful volume before late 2027. Yageo extended capacitor price increases to direct customers rather than distributors alone from July 1, 2026, official increases around 50%.
- **Memory is now most of the machine, and it is not the memory everyone watches.** UBS teardown, reported August 10, 2026: a Vera Rubin core unit of one Vera CPU and two Rubin GPUs costs roughly $38,900, of which memory is roughly $24,300, or 62%, up from 53% on the Blackwell Ultra GB300. SOCAMM2, the CPU-side module, is roughly $19,400 or 49.8% of the superchip cost; HBM4 is roughly $4,943 or 12.7%. Memory cost rose about 2.5x generation on generation against a 2.1x rise in total unit cost. The rung this thesis has tracked as "HBM" is now the smaller half of the memory bill. (Digitimes, August 10, 2026)
- Packaging capacity is now being bought secondhand. TSMC is negotiating to acquire two existing display-panel plants to expand CoWoS, potentially more than NT$30B (about $930M), on the logic that existing cleanrooms and power shorten the ramp. July 2026 revenue was NT$467.58B, about $14.5B, up roughly 45% YoY, with the 2026 dollar revenue growth outlook raised above 40%. (Reported August 10, 2026. See [[TSMC CoWoS]])
- [[Astera Labs]] moved onto the light rung itself: after acquiring aiXscale, its product scope now runs from electrical interconnect (PCIe, CXL) into optical I/O and co-packaged optics. The brain's own Astera post forecast this shift toward light; the company has now bought its way there. (404K SEMI-AI brief, August 10, 2026)
- **The escape route became a category. Three companies funded in one week to route around the chokepoint rather than supply it.** (September 8 to 10, 2026)
  - **[[Positron AI]]**, $875M at a $5B post-money valuation on September 10, 2026 (a $375M Series C plus a Series C-1 of up to $500M, led by James Clark and NEA, with the Qatar Investment Authority participating; total funding roughly $1.2B). Its Asimov chip is built around **commodity LPDDR5X**, the memory class used in phones and laptops, instead of high-bandwidth memory. Between 288 GB and 2,304 GB per chip, 2TB per accelerator, 8TB per Titan system, with bandwidth the company describes as comparable to Nvidia's Rubin GPU and more than 90% of available memory bandwidth actually achieved. Company figures, not independently benchmarked. Tape-out on TSMC N3P at end-2026, production targeted H2 2027. (SiliconANGLE / Quartz / DCD, September 10, 2026. See [[Positron AI]])
  - **[[Fab2]]**, $500M Series A at $3.7B on September 9, 2026, its first disclosed round, led by Fundomo with Maverick Silicon, StepStone, Paradigm, Duquesne Family Office and Naval Ravikant. Founded 2022 by Jim Keller and Sam Zeloof. It does not route around a chip shortage, it routes around the **factory queue**: a "fab fab", a factory that mass-produces small software-defined fabs and every tool inside them, from pumps and valves to lithography and vacuum chambers. Honest limit, stated in the reporting: direct-write electron-beam lithography is slow, so the throughput suits prototyping and low volume rather than commercial high-volume foundry work. (Dealroom / Crunchbase, September 9 to 11, 2026. See [[Fab2]])
  - **[[Celero Communications]]**, $275M Series C at $3B-plus on September 8, 2026, led by CapitalG, Atreides and Valor with Sutter Hill and Maverick Silicon returning, announced alongside validation of what it calls the industry's first **2nm coherent DSP silicon**, supporting 1.6T through 3.2T per port. It routes around the single-site limit: instead of fitting one bigger machine in one building, stitch buildings together over fibre spans of **more than 100 miles**. Coherent DSPs drive the transceivers, recover timing without a separate timing link, and add redundancy bits to repair errors from light dispersing over distance. Celero says the same part covers long-haul, scale-up and scale-out. (SiliconANGLE / Business Wire, September 8, 2026. See [[Celero Communications]])
- **The memory-wall answer inverted.** This brain's first published post was on [[SK Hynix]] and the memory wall. [[Positron AI]]'s bet is the memory wall answered by declining to buy the scarce memory at all. Note the pairing with [[OLIX]] (August 3, 2026): same **around** motion, opposite routes. OLIX went up-market into on-chip SRAM and photonics; Positron went down-market into the cheapest memory on the shelf. Both are funded, neither has shipped, and they cannot both be the right answer.
- **The wire started doing the arithmetic.** [[Cornelis Networks]] raised **$205M led by IAG Capital Partners on September 14, 2026**, announced together with its **Active Compute Fabric** architecture and a **strategic collaboration with Qualcomm**, both unveiled ahead of the AI Infra Summit. Cornelis is the **Omni-Path team spun out of Intel in 2020**. The mechanism is the point: instead of the network forwarding packets and letting the accelerators do all the maths, programmable compute sits inside the fabric and operates on data in transit. It assembles KV cache for disaggregated inference, coordinates expert dispatch for mixture-of-experts models, runs collective operations like AllReduce, and compresses gradients on the way. CMO Brandon Draeger: "The payload does not arrive the way it left." Company figures, flagged: up to **50% less network traffic** (pre-production simulations); accelerator utilisation in large AI deployments **commonly near half of installed capacity**; the network is about **15% of an AI system's cost** while determining the return on the other 85%. Built on open standards, Ethernet and UALink for scale-up and Ultra Ethernet for scale-out. CN5000 shipping at 400 Gbps; CN6000 multi-protocol at 800 Gbps, broader availability **Q4 2026**. (SiliconANGLE, September 14, 2026. See [[Cornelis Networks]])
- **China's answer to Nvidia still buys the chokepoint.** Alibaba unveiled the **Zhenwu V900** at its Apsara Conference on September 21 to 22, 2026: three times the performance of its predecessor, **216 GB of HBM**, 1.2 TB/s inter-chip bandwidth, FP8 and FP4, scaling to 500,000 chips per cluster, mass production targeted Q1 2027. Read against this thesis, the important number is the 216 GB of HBM. The chip built specifically to route around US export controls does **not** route around high-bandwidth memory. Whatever [[Positron AI]] and [[OLIX]] are funded to escape, China's flagship accelerator is still standing in the queue for it. (CNBC / Reuters, September 22, 2026)
- Exponential View's "State of the AI Economy" (June 25, 2026) quantifies the slide with build-cost data: memory is the single biggest mover in data-center build cost, from ~2% (2021) to ~18% (2026E), while chips' overall share of build cost rose 40% to 60% ("each new dollar buys more silicon and less concrete"). Value is also moving UP the stack toward apps and models (app+model revenue share grew ~2.95x in a year); labs hold pricing power only while they hold the frontier, and yesterday's frontier commoditizes fast into open weights (Google+OpenAI+Anthropic's OpenRouter token share fell 72% to 33%). The interconnect rung is now a real line item (DC network ~$1bn of the ~$7.9bn/yr cost of a 1 GW build, i.e. [[Astera Labs]]). See [[Signals/EV State of the AI Economy (2026-06-25)]].

## What would break it
- An efficiency leap that relaxes the constraint.
- Capital flooding a chokepoint fast enough to kill the scarcity (watch capacity build vs lead times). The CoWoS gap narrowing (TrendForce, June 15, 2026) is the first live example: when it happens, the constraint does not disappear, it relocates one rung down. Tripwire confirmed working as designed.
- **Sharpened July 28, 2026:** the thesis says the constraint moves DOWN. This week it also moved SIDEWAYS (Cerebras/AMD splitting inference into prefill and decode; Etched betting a fixed-function part beats a flexible one). The claim survives, because in both cases the money still moves off the famous general-purpose processor and onto the specific thing that is scarce. But the wording "one rung below" is now too narrow. Restate as: value accrues to whichever component is least substitutable for the current workload, wherever it sits.
- **New tripwire (added July 28, 2026):** if a post-transformer architecture gets real traction, fixed-function bets like Etched break instantly while flexible hardware is vindicated. Watch for any frontier lab shipping a production model that is not a transformer.
- **New tripwire (added August 7, 2026): designing around the chokepoint instead of buying it.** [[OLIX]] is the first funded company in this brain built to avoid the two current chokepoints rather than supply them, using on-chip SRAM instead of high-bandwidth memory and skipping advanced packaging entirely. If that works at scale, the chokepoint owner's pricing power is capped not by new capacity but by architecture, which is a different and faster breaker than the capacity-flood one above. If it does not work, it is the strongest evidence yet that the chokepoint is real physics rather than a supply queue. Watch for a benchmarked DX-1 result and for whether any incumbent copies the no-HBM approach. First real read: H2 2027 customer deliveries.
- **Sharpened August 7, 2026:** the thesis now has three motions, not one. Down (packaging to memory to interconnect). Sideways (prefill split from decode). And **around** (OLIX routing past HBM and packaging). All three obey the restated claim: value accrues to whatever is least substitutable for the current workload. The new question the thesis has to answer is whether a chokepoint can be made substitutable by redesign rather than by capacity.
- **New tripwire (added August 11, 2026): the constraint leaves the industry.** Every chokepoint this thesis has tracked so far sat inside the semiconductor supply chain, where enough capital eventually buys more capacity. Indium phosphide does not. Indium is recovered as a by-product of zinc smelting, so the quantity available is set by zinc demand, not by laser demand, and no amount of AI capex changes that. The same shape shows up one layer over in ceramic capacitors, where the binding input is high-end line time on an 18-to-24-month build cycle rather than a chip design. If the binding constraint sits in a market that does not care about AI, the usual breaker (capital floods the chokepoint) stops working, and the winner is whoever holds a permit or an existing line rather than whoever spends most. Watch AXT's quarterly China export-permit count, Lumentum's InP capacity conversion, and whether any hyperscaler signs a direct offtake for indium or for MLCC line time rather than for finished parts.
- **Tripwire result, September 15, 2026: the August 7 "design around the chokepoint" tripwire fired three times in one week.** It was written to watch whether a single company ([[OLIX]]) could cap a chokepoint owner's pricing power by architecture rather than by capacity. Seven days in September funded three separate escapes at three different layers: substitute the scarce component ([[Positron AI]], commodity memory for HBM), relocate the workload ([[Celero Communications]], across sites instead of inside one), and replace the queue itself ([[Fab2]], mass-produced small fabs instead of waiting for a big one). That is no longer one experiment. It is a category with roughly $1.65B of new capital behind it in a week. Restated tripwire: watch whether **any** of the three ships a benchmarked, customer-delivered result, because the thesis's core claim (the chokepoint owner wins) is only safe while all of them fail. First real reads: Positron H2 2027 production, Celero's 2nm silicon into customer designs, Fab2's throughput ceiling.
- **New tripwire (added September 15, 2026): the escape becomes cheaper than the chokepoint.** The capital-floods-the-chokepoint breaker assumed money chases the scarce thing. Money is now also chasing the way past it. If an escape route reaches production cost parity before the chokepoint's capacity arrives, the chokepoint owner's pricing power collapses without a single new fab being built. Watch for the first hyperscaler that publicly designs a workload around a non-HBM part, or that signs for capacity across two sites instead of bidding for one bigger one.
- **Sharpened September 15, 2026: the fifth motion is BEHIND.** [[Fab2]] does not sit under the semiconductor industry the way [[AXT]] does; it sits **behind** it, in the machines that make the machines. The constraint at that layer is not a material or a line, it is tool supply and cycle time. This is the ASML-shaped position, and it is the one layer where a shortage cannot be relieved by any of the previous four motions, because every one of them eventually needs a tool. Whether Fab2's throughput limit keeps it out of that position is the open question, and it is the most important thing in this thesis to watch next.
- **Sharpened September 22, 2026: the sixth motion is THROUGH, and it is the first one that argues the constraint is not a layer at all.** Motions one to five all keep the model intact: the binding constraint is a *place*, and it moves (down, sideways, around, under, behind). [[Cornelis Networks]]'s Active Compute Fabric says something different. If the network can run the collective operations, compress the gradients and assemble the cache while the data is already moving, then the work is not relocated to a scarcer layer, it is **deleted from the layer that was scarce** and absorbed into one that was already paid for. The number that makes this a thesis problem rather than a product detail: accelerator utilisation in large deployments commonly sits near **half** of installed capacity, and the network is only about **15%** of system cost. If a 15% line item can recover a meaningful share of a 50% waste, the cheapest new compute in the world is compute nobody has to buy. That is an efficiency leap, which is the first breaker listed in this section, arriving through the interconnect rather than through the model.
- **New tripwire (added September 22, 2026): idle time becomes the scarce good, not silicon.** Watch for any hyperscaler that publicly reports accelerator utilisation, or buys a network on a utilisation guarantee rather than on bandwidth per port. That would mean the industry has started pricing the gap between installed and used capacity, and the moment it does, the chokepoint owner competes not with a rival supplier but with the customer's own idle hardware. First real reads: CN6000 broad availability in Q4 2026, whether the Qualcomm collaboration converts into a named rack-scale design, and any independent measurement of the 50% traffic-reduction claim, which today is a company simulation.
- **Sharpened August 11, 2026: four motions now.** Down, sideways, around, and **under**. "Under" means the constraint drops below the semiconductor industry entirely into raw materials and passive components whose supply is governed by another industry's economics. The restated claim still holds (value accrues to whatever is least substitutable), but the practical hunting ground has changed: the next unknown winner is more likely to be a substrate grower, a smelter or a capacitor line than a chip designer. Corollary worth tracking: the same week showed the memory bill inverting, with CPU-side SOCAMM2 at 49.8% of a Vera Rubin superchip against HBM4 at 12.7%, so even inside a rung the money can move to the part nobody was watching.
