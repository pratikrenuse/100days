Questions founders ask me

How do I get more people to complete my sign-up form?

By Pratik Renuse · Updated 23 November 2026

Short answer

Match the form to the intent of the person who clicked. Someone who pressed "test for free" wants the product, not a questionnaire. Fit onboarding on one page, show a progress indicator, remove optional questions, rename "Submit" to what actually happens, and where you can, give new users direct access to the tool instead of a call.

The case

A B2B AI startup in the 2023 Techstars Berlin programme had a landing page with a "test for free" button that led to a multi-step onboarding form. People clicked, started the form and some dropped off. We went through it screen by screen.

Think like the person who clicked

Someone who clicks "test for free" is saying: just give me the product to use. Every question between that click and the product is friction. The form should feel like the fastest way into the tool, not an application.

Changes we made

  • Add a progress bar or step indicator, so people know how much is left
  • Fit the whole onboarding on one page if possible
  • Remove the last three optional questions. If they are optional, you do not need them now
  • Rename the button. Instead of "Submit", use a word that matches the intent, such as "Request" or the actual outcome
  • Reword field labels in the customer's language, not internal jargon

Give direct access

The founder was talking to every new sign-up before giving access. That slows everything down. Giving new users direct access to the tool shortens the cycle and makes their feedback more valuable, because they have actually used it.

Other fixes I have seen work

From other mentoring sessions with consumer startups:

  • A style quiz must end with a result. People who answer questions expect something back
  • A register page usually should not carry the full menu bar, which invites people to wander off
  • Look at quiz funnels from brands that do this well and copy the structure, not the words

Measure the steps

Track the click, the start and the completion of the form separately. Then you know whether the fix worked or the drop just moved to another step.
