Questions founders ask me

What success rate should I expect from growth experiments, and how long should each run?

By Pratik Renuse · Updated 23 November 2026

Short answer

At the start, expect about one in ten experiments to work. With practice and better data, that rises to about one in three. Run them on a five-week cycle: one week to set up, four weeks to run. Judge the programme by what you learn per cycle, not by each test, and log every result, including failures.

The expectation I set with founders

When I ran the Newchip growth programme with seed-stage startups in 2020 and 2021, the founders who struggled with experiments were not the ones with bad ideas. They were the ones who expected most ideas to work and lost heart after three failures.

So I set expectations early: a success rate of about one in ten at the beginning, and about one in three later.

Why early rates are low

  • You do not yet know your customers well, so your guesses are rough
  • Tracking is new, so some results are unclear
  • The team is still learning to design a clean test

Why it improves

Each experiment, win or lose, teaches you something. Logged learnings make the next guess better. Better tracking makes results clearer. After a few cycles, your ideas come from evidence instead of opinion.

The five-week cycle

  • Week 1: set up. Define the hypothesis, the success metric and the tracking
  • Weeks 2 to 5: run. Leave it alone long enough to get a real result

Shorter tests often end before the data means anything. Longer ones slow the learning.

Plan before you test

For a carpooling startup in a shrinking market in 2020, my advice was to create a strategy and a framework for running experiments, rather than starting to run experiments. Testing channels right away, without structure, burns money and produces noise.

Measure the programme, not the test

Track per cycle: how many experiments ran, how many won, and what you learned. A cycle with zero wins and three clear learnings is still progress.

Keep a learnings document

The log is the real asset. When a new team member joins or you revisit a channel a year later, the log stops you from repeating the same failed test.
