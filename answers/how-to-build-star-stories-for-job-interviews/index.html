Careers in growth

How do I build STAR stories for job interviews when I have little work experience?

By Pratik Renuse · Updated 23 November 2026

Short answer

STAR stands for Situation, Task, Action, Result. Build a bank of six to eight stories before any interview, one each for planning, attention to detail, creativity, teamwork, analysis, a strength, a weakness and a challenge you overcame. Mine them from study, side projects, volunteering and part-time work, not only jobs. Keep each under two minutes, with most of the time on your actions.

Why a story bank

Most interview questions are variations of "tell me about a time when". Graduates I coach often freeze because they think they have no stories. They have plenty. They have not collected them yet. So before any interview, we build a story bank.

The STAR shape

STAR is a well-known interview format:

  • Situation: one or two sentences of context
  • Task: what you had to achieve
  • Action: what you did and why, step by step. This is most of the answer
  • Result: what changed, and what you learned

The categories to cover

The model document I give coachees, built from my own interview answers, has a story for each of these:

  • Planning
  • Attention to detail
  • Creativity
  • Teamwork
  • Analysis
  • A strength
  • A weakness
  • A challenging situation you overcame

That last one comes from a career course homework I use with international students: write about a challenging situation you overcame. It almost always produces the strongest story.

Where to find stories

  • Study projects where something went wrong and you fixed it
  • Side projects, such as growing a social account or organising an event
  • Volunteering and student associations
  • Part-time and summer jobs
  • Your lifeline exercise: the chapters of your life often hold the best material

An example of mine: teamwork

Situation: at Bayer in 2017 I was the youngest person in a senior team running a global executive event. Task: earn trust quickly so the project could move. Action: I started lunch-and-learn invitations and organised a team evening. Result: the team got to know each other outside meetings, and working together on a long project became easier.

Strength and weakness

Be specific and honest. My strength is seeing the big picture while staying detail oriented, such as managing a six-month project timeline and guest logistics at the same time. My weakness is administrative tasks, and I explain how I manage them.

Practise out loud

Tell each story aloud and time it. Cut anything that is not your action. Then practise switching stories between questions, because one good story can answer several.
