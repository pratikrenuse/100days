Careers in growth

How do I break into growth marketing as a graduate?

By Pratik Renuse · Updated 23 November 2026

Short answer

Build proof, not just a CV. Learn the measurement basics first: Google Analytics, Tag Manager and ad pixels. Run a small project of your own, such as a side Instagram account or a tiny ad test, and document what you tried, what failed and what you changed. Then tell those stories in STAR format. Hiring managers trust experiments more than certificates.

Why I get this question

Many of the graduates I coach want a career in marketing, and growth marketing in particular. I have led online marketing at Infento, VU and Bueno and been ranked in MarketerHire's top 1% globally, so they ask how to get the first step.

Learn measurement first

Growth marketing is marketing that is measured. Learn the basics before anything else:

  • Google Analytics, including conversions and filters
  • Google Tag Manager
  • The Meta pixel and one other ad platform
  • A simple funnel: aware, acquired, activated, converted, retained, referred

It is closer to simple Excel than people think. A few hours of tutorials gets you started.

Run your own experiment

Nothing beats a small project you can talk about. Early in my career I ran a side Instagram account about watches. I used a public-data tool to find people who liked watch photos, noticed many had "tag for a repost" in their bio, and tagged a handful in each post. Followers grew several times over in a month. It was small, but it taught me audience research, testing and measuring, and it became an interview story.

Ideas for yours:

  • Grow a niche social account
  • Run a tiny ad test for a friend's business
  • Rebuild a landing page and measure the change

Think in priorities

When I set up marketing for a company with nothing in place, I did analytics first, then search ads, then Facebook. Show interviewers you think in order of impact.

Tell it in STAR format

Turn each project into a Situation, Task, Action, Result story. Interviewers remember a story about something you tried, more than a list of tools.

Find the right first role

Early-stage startups often let juniors touch every channel. Look for a team where someone senior will review your experiments.
